# Winning the Sillerman — strategy note

Related: [[WINNING SHORT STORY PRIZE-Story structure]]

---

## 1. Situation

You have 31 poems, 46 formatted pages, and twelve weeks until the 1 December deadline. The floor is 50 pages. You asked what *type* of poetry you must write to win.

**There is no type.** That is not encouragement, it is the finding. Evidence below.

---

## 2. Analysis

### 2.1 The evidence is inside your own manuscript

Five poems were cut or gutted in the humanize pass. Look at what they had in common — not their subjects, their surfaces:

| Cut poem | What it was doing |
|---|---|
| The Morning Breeze (prose) | five negative definitions, sections labelled *The First Verse … The Final Inversion* |
| The King's Last Course | invented Latin binomials, *"a melodic osteology"* |
| Shadows in the Moonlight | ten sections, each closing on a portable aphorism |
| The Word of the Ghawth | seven `Because…` anaphora, *"Nothing with horns… can be bound forever"* |
| An Ode to Time (untriaged) | *"The path may differ, but the destination never does"* |

**Every one of them is what "prize poetry" sounds like to someone imagining a judge.** Elevated diction, symmetry, wisdom delivered in portable units, a closing line you could print on a card.

And here is the tell that should decide this for you: **those five are the only poems in the book with no typos.** The polish and the emptiness arrived together. Writing toward an imagined judge is what produced them.

### 2.2 What the winners actually are

*Fact — verifiable.* Recent Sillerman winners: Clifton Gachagua, *Madman at Kilifi* · Ladan Osman, *The Kitchen-Dweller's Testimony* · Mahtem Shiferraw, *Fuchsia* · Safia Elhillo, *The January Children* · Bernard Farai Matambo, *Stray* · Tjawanga Dema, *The Careless Seamstress* · 'Gbenga Adeoba, *Exodus* · Michael Imossan, *All that Refuses to Die* (2024) · Jedidiah Mugarura (2025) · Afia Ansong, *I buried [her] in a field of lemongrass* (2026).

*Fact.* The judging panel is eight poets — Kwame Dawes, Chris Abani, Matthew Shenoda, John Keene, Gabeba Baderoon, Phillippa Yaa de Villiers, Aracelis Girmay, Mahtem Shiferraw. These are not people a pretty line gets past.

*Fact.* Judge Mahtem Shiferraw on the 2026 winner: **"I love this manuscript for what it can do, the possibilities it can open with each poem, which go beyond the page."**

Read that citation again. Not *beautiful*. Not *moving*. **What it can do.** She is praising a machine, not a surface.

*Interpretation — mine, and you should test it.* Those titles are not miscellanies. Each is one obsession pursued to the end of its rope: migration and water; women's labour; a diaspora childhood; burial and grief. **They are books. Not folders of poems.**

### 2.3 Your real problem is not style

Your manuscript is currently a folder. It contains, in this order: a Kigoma train journey, a Zanzibar burial, Windermere Avenue in North West London, macaroni and cheese, a Sufi discourse on the falcon, three separate ocean poems, cassava twice, and Zadie Smith twice.

**But there is a book in there,** and it is strong. The spine is already visible:

> Kalemie · Kigoma · the lake · the Indian Ocean · the Atlantic · Kilwa Kivinje · the coffle · the yokes · the river of bone · the mother's grave · the ropes · the return that is not an arrival

Call it what it is: **the water that keeps the dead, and the son who comes back up the line.** That is roughly 14–16 of your 31 poems. The rest is a different book, or no book.

This — not lineation, not diction — is the gap between where you are and a shortlist.

---

## 3. The type of poem you must write

Not a style to imitate. **The thing you already do when you are not performing.** Every line below is yours, and each one demonstrates a technique you can repeat deliberately.

### Technique 1 — Flat fact where the poem expects decoration
> The leg irons, he said, were the only truth that needed no poetry.

The line refuses its own genre. It earns everything around it.

### Technique 2 — The unglossed particular
> he rubbed dry sage into the head of a tilapia and carved a window into a wall of red clay
>
> *Nyie nenda kule kajamba nani* — the conductor's chant a key

Nobody generates this. Sage, tilapia, red clay, and no translation offered. **Do not translate the Swahili. Do not explain the ritual.** The judges are Dawes, Abani, Baderoon — they do not need the footnote, and the reader who does will lean in.

### Technique 3 — Consolation withheld
> I did not arrive. / I was delivered

Two lines. The second takes the first's dignity away and does not give it back.

### Technique 4 — The correction that lands as violence
> It was not a sob. It was a structural failure.

This is a negative definition — the exact figure cut five times elsewhere — and here it works, because the poem built the pillar/wall/mortar image for ten lines before spending it. **One is punctuation. Six is a habit.**

### Technique 5 — Let the error stand when it outperforms intention
> Sometimes, I am no one is there.

Do not fix this. It is the best line in the book and you did not mean to write it.

### And the flat statement that outruns the whole Ghawth
> I cocked my ears and listened, a man who loved a man.

Nine words, at the bottom of 1,500 words of scaffolding. **That line is the book's centre and it is currently buried on page 46.**

### What to stop doing
- No abstraction defined against another abstraction (*not X, but Y*) — you have 20 of these
- No abstract-of-concrete genitives (*the grammar of stems*, *a catalogue of densities*)
- No section that closes on transferable wisdom
- No Latin, no thesaurus register
- Never a word chosen because it sounds like poetry

## 4. Options

| Option | Gets you | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Submit close to as-is in December** | done, cheap | 46pp fails the 50pp floor; a folder, not a book; still four `[?]` holes |
| **B. Find the spine, cut hard, write into the gap** | a real book; clears 50pp honestly; the only version that can place | ~12 weeks of actual work |
| **C. Skip this cycle, target Dec 2027** | more time, better book | the prize runs annually; you can enter again either way |

**Trade-off that matters:** entry is free and you may submit more than one manuscript. There is no penalty for losing. The only real cost of submitting a folder is that you will conclude your poems are the problem, when the arrangement is.

---

## 5. Recommendation

**Option B.** And the 50-page problem is the opportunity, not the obstacle: you need roughly 8–10 new poems, and the book itself will tell you which ones are missing once the spine is named.

---

## 6. Action plan — 12 weeks to 1 December

### Weeks 1–2 · Find the book
- [ ] Name the obsession in one sentence. Not a theme — a question the book cannot answer
- [ ] Sort all 31 into **spine / satellite / other book**
- [ ] Accept that good poems will go in "other book." *The Green Bottle* may be one
- [ ] Answer the four `[?]` holes — they are all spine poems

### Weeks 3–4 · Read the competition
- [ ] Read three winners cover to cover: Adeoba's *Exodus* (water, migration — nearest your subject), Dema's *The Careless Seamstress*, Elhillo's *The January Children*
- [ ] For each, write down **how the book is ordered** and where its strongest poem sits
- [ ] Note: not one of them opens with its prettiest poem

### Weeks 5–8 · Write into the gap
- [ ] 8–10 new poems the spine demands. Where is the father? The crossing itself? What is on the other side of the lake?
- [ ] Mine the Kalemie material already on your disk — *The Lake at Kalemie Doesn't Give Up on Its Dead* is the same book
- [ ] Rule for every new poem: **one unglossed particular, no aphorism, no summary at the end**

### Weeks 9–10 · Sequence
- [ ] Three or four movements with part-title pages (adds 3–4pp legitimately)
- [ ] Opening poem: the one that teaches the reader how to read the rest. **Not the loveliest**
- [ ] Consider moving *"a man who loved a man"* out of page 46
- [ ] Confirm 50+ pages in the Sillerman format

### Weeks 11–12 · Finish
- [ ] Line-level pass on the 46 overrunning lines
- [ ] Strip every `[?]`; delete the acknowledgements page if nothing was published
- [ ] Re-verify: no name in the file, no name in the metadata
- [ ] Submit via Submittable before 1 December

---

## 7. Next step

**Name the book in one sentence.** Everything above waits on that, and only you can write it.
