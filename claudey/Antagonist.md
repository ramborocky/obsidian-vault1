## Contradiction

[What makes them complex?]

Jean Luc gets the biggest halo-effect reversal in the book: readers trust him early, then the ground shifts. The contradiction has to be one that _recontextualizes_ rather than _reverses_ — something that stays true even after guilt is revealed, so readers don't feel cheated.

Give him a genuinely good, verifiable trait in his first appearance — something we see him do when he thinks no one important is watching:

- Protective of a child or vulnerable character (ironic if it's the same child who discards the motherboard)
- Does something costly and selfless — takes blame that isn't his, gives up something valuable
- Other characters (Djamela, D'Juma, Morisho) independently vouch for him early — consensus, not just Daudi's opinion (bandwagon effect reinforcing the halo)

Pick traits like "protective" or "self-sacrificing" that can coexist with guilt — protective _and_ dangerous is complexity, not contradiction. Avoid traits that would require an outright reversal (e.g., "incapable of violence" if he turns out violent).

## Secret

[What do they hide?]

Jean Luc's guilt in the central mystery is the secret the whole book protects and misdirects from. The motherboard a child discards as random debris is the linchpin clue tying him to it — communications, evidence, or a device connected to whatever act (or cover-up) defines his guilt.

Critically: his lack of reaction when the motherboard first appears is itself part of the secret. He doesn't flinch, doesn't look twice — the "dog that didn't bark." That non-reaction needs to happen visibly on the page (not withheld from the reader) so a re-read confirms fair play.

Layer the reveal in three touches, spaced far apart, Panama-hat style:

1. **First touch:** the motherboard discarded as debris — buried mid-list among other unremarkable background details. Jean Luc present but unreactive.
2. **Second touch:** the object resurfaces somewhere incongruous. An expertise-holding character (D'Juma or Morisho) notices something technically _off_ about it without yet understanding why.
3. **Third touch:** full reveal — the motherboard connects directly to Jean Luc's guilt/motive.

## Shadow

[What is their dark side?]

Jean Luc's shadow shows up as rhetorical deflection rather than overt menace — tools that work on the _reader_, not just other characters, keeping his halo polished a beat too long:

- **Ad hominem:** When accused or challenged, he discredits the accuser's character rather than answering the substance of the claim ("who are you to say that, after what you did"). Readers side with him emotionally even as facts quietly stack up against him.
- **Appeal to ignorance:** He treats the absence of proof against him as proof of his innocence — leaning on "there's no evidence" as if that settles it.
- **Bandwagon reinforcement:** He may quietly encourage or benefit from the community's collective trust in him, letting consensus do the work of a personal defense.

At least one character should feel a flicker of doubt about him early — even if they dismiss it — so the eventual reveal reads as confirmation, not new information invented after the fact.

## Scene Tracker
#scene-template 
[Scene1]
[Scene 2]

Minimum scaffold for the red herring / halo arc — slot into actual scene numbers as the manuscript develops:

1. **Establishing scene:** Jean Luc's selfless/protective act, witnessed privately. Halo established.
2. **Motherboard, first touch:** Child discards it as debris, buried mid-list of background details. Jean Luc present, unreactive — plant the "dog that didn't bark."
3. **Suspicion-routing scene:** Another character (Djamela, D'Juma, or Morisho) gets an appearance-miscue moment — a dramatic or unexplained action that makes them look temporarily guilty, drawing suspicion away from Jean Luc.
4. **Consensus-building scene:** Other characters vouch for Jean Luc, reinforcing the halo through bandwagon effect rather than direct evidence.
5. **Flicker-of-doubt scene:** One character (ideally Daudi) has a fleeting moment of suspicion about Jean Luc that they suppress or explain away — this needs to be on the page for fair play.
6. **Motherboard, second touch:** Object resurfaces incongruously. An expertise-holding character notices something technically off without understanding its significance yet.
7. **Deflection scene(s):** Jean Luc uses ad hominem or appeal-to-ignorance rhetoric when challenged, keeping readers on his side a little longer.
8. **Motherboard, third touch / full reveal:** The object's true connection to Jean Luc's guilt is exposed.
9. **Recontextualization scene:** The established halo trait (protective, self-sacrificing) is shown to coexist with his guilt rather than contradict it — complexity, not reversal.
