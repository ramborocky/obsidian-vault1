# CLELAND — WRITE SENTENCES THAT WORK

> Certain brief sentences are peerless in their ability to give one the feeling that nothing remains to be said.

## The Twenty-Word Average, Not a Cap

- Aim for an *average* under twenty words per section, not a hard ceiling on every sentence — a paragraph of 30/16/4/42/8-word sentences still averages 20. The metric is a life ring against rambling, not a shackle against long sentences entirely.
- Long sentences (30–70 words) create mood and invite reflection; short ones (4–10 words) catapult readers into action. Five-plus long sentences stacked in a row is fog, not lyricism, regardless of how good any single one of them is.

## Every Sentence Must Move the Story

- Skip fillers, don't digress, and limit backstory to a sentence or two woven into current action — no info-dumps. Dialogue should always *do* something, not merely *say* something.
- Shorten sentences specifically at moments of heightened tension — the same content told in half the words, with concrete nouns and active verbs, reads as urgent where the long version reads as merely informative.

## Marry Fact and Emotion, Cut the Verb "To Be"

- The best sentences integrate what happened *and* how someone feels about it. Favor hot emotions (rage, devotion, despair, loathing) over lukewarm ones (annoyance, happiness) as the emotional charge in a sentence.
- Challenge every instance of *is/are/was/were/be/being/been* — not to eliminate all of them, but to test whether a more active verb would carry more.
