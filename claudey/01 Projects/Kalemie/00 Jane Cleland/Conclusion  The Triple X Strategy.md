# CLELAND — CONCLUSION: THE TRIPLE X STRATEGY

> Whatever is hidden behind the curtain must be revealed at last, and it must be at one and the same time completely unexpected and inevitable.

## The Triple X Strategy

- Whenever the draft raises something that needs resolving later, or contains a sentence you're unhappy with but don't want to stop and fix mid-flow, type `xxx` (a string that never appears naturally in English) so it's instantly searchable later.
- Keep a running plot-thread list (bottom of the manuscript or a separate doc) of every `xxx` and what it refers to — not page numbers, since those shift, but the *item* itself ("Kayla — shrimp allergy"). Review the list periodically so nothing is forgotten between drafting and revision.

## No Coincidence, No Contrivance, at the End

- Every primary and secondary plotline must resolve by the end without leaning on coincidence — anything that currently feels contrived needs a fact planted earlier, an incident added, or a character attribute adjusted so the ending reads as "unexpected and inevitable" (Atwood's phrase), not convenient.
- The Triple X Strategy exists specifically to make this achievable without holding the whole book in your head at once — you don't need total recall if you trust the tracking system.

## The Final Checklist (Figure 14.1, Compressed)

Has every plot thread paid off? Is coincidence present anywhere (eliminate it)? What's the average sentence length, and is it varied? Have banned labeling words been purged? Are red herrings planted and resolved? Is the narrative question answered? Does the ending offer a global reflection, not just an event?
