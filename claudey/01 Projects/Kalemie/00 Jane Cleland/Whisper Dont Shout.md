# CLELAND — WHISPER, DON'T SHOUT

> All of nature whispers its secrets to us through its sounds.

## Quiet Outperforms Loud

- A man screaming "There's a gun!" reads as panic, maybe as unstable. A man who goes silent, throat closed, and points with a trembling hand reads as real fear — and it recruits the reader (and other characters) to look where he's looking. Restraint is more contagious than volume.
- Silence is never fully silent, and noise inside silence (or silence inside noise) is one of the most reliable suspense-building juxtapositions available — a break in an expected soundscape signals danger before any character says so.

## Banned "Labeling" Words

- Cut: *look, see, hear, listen, feel, taste, notice, observe, recognize, know, realize, understand.* These words tell readers a perception happened instead of dramatizing the perception itself.
- "I heard them yelling" (distancing) → "They yelled" (immediate). The fix is almost always deletion, not substitution.

## Specificity of Sound

- Choose the noun/verb, not the adjective, to do the work: not "loud boom" but naming what *kind* of boom (paralyzing, mortifying, horrifying) based on what it means to *this* character specifically.
- Onomatopoeia (words that imitate their sound) add verisimilitude cheaply — but the real craft is matching the sound-word to the character's interior state, not just accuracy to the physical event.
