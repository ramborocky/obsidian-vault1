Why this exists

The lorry going back is empty. Your sack has nowhere to sit.

Every day trucks leave Dar loaded and come back with nothing, while a trader in Makambako pays whatever the one broker at the stand decides to charge. Neither of them can see the other. That gap is the entire business.

How it works

Four steps. No map, no address, no account needed.

Loads move town to town, not pin to pin. Say what you're sending and where — that's the whole form.

1

Post it

What it is, how heavy, from which town to which. Pick a vehicle or leave it open to anything.

2

They bid

Every transporter running that corridor gets it. They send you their price, their vehicle, and when they can lift.

3

You pick

On price, rating, or whether they're verified. Cheapest isn't forced on you and neither is nearest.

4

It moves

Money is held. The person receiving reads a code aloud. The money goes. Everyone rates everyone.

The trust mechanism

Six digits are why a stranger hands you a 500 km load.

When you send money into escrow, Zigo generates a code and gives it only to the person receiving the load. The transporter never sees it.

They cannot release your money. They cannot claim they delivered. The only way that payment moves is if somebody at the other end holds the goods and reads the number out loud.

If it never gets read, the money comes back to you. No contract, no bank guarantee, no court. That is the whole innovation, and it costs a flat TZS 3,000 — not a percentage, so it never costs more than a small consignment is worth.

Escrow isn't how we make money. It's what makes two strangers willing to trade at all.

For lenders and partners

Six months of payouts is a credit file that never existed.

A boda rider has no payslip, no invoices, and no way to prove what they earn. That's why asset finance on a second motorcycle is priced like a gamble, if it's offered at all.

A transporter with six months of settled Zigo payouts into CRDB or NMB has a verifiable, bank-visible income record — earnings, frequency, route consistency, counterparty ratings.

We're not asking a bank to fund a logistics app. We're offering an origination channel into a market of more than a million unbanked vehicle owners, with repayment history attached.

0%

Commission taken from any fare, ever. Revenue is subscription — the model Vahak used to reach roughly two million registered transport businesses and 25,000 postings a day in India.

The pilot

One corridor. Both directions. Then the next.

Depth beats a national map with nobody on it. We open where produce comes down and goods go up — and we don't open a second corridor until the first one has loads every day.
