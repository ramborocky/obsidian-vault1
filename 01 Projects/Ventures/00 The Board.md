## THE FOUR THINGS THIS BOARD ACTUALLY FOUND

**1 · Paid fiction markets pay better than prizes and better than KDP.**
Clarkesworld pays **$0.12/word**, Nightmare **$0.08**, The Dark **$0.05**. A single 4,000-word sale to Nightmare is **$320** — more than the entire modelled first-year profit of the KDP anthology. And these are **horror markets**, which is the genre already chosen in [[KDP Business Plan#PART 8 · THE GENRE, AND HOW TO SHELVE IT|Part 8]]. → [[Paid Markets]]

**2 · Kiswahili is a moat, and two prizes sit behind it.**
**Tuzo Nyerere** — TSh 10,000,000 first prize, **four categories**, Kiswahili, and the field is **Tanzanian, not global**. **Safal Kiswahili** — $5,000, 29 Jan – 31 Mar. The writers you compete with for the Commonwealth cannot enter either. **You have been competing in the hardest pool available while two easier and better-paying ones sit in your own language.** → [[The Prize Atlas — Tanzania, SADC and Africa]]

**3 · Grants dwarf prizes, and one email decides whether you can reach them.**
**Miles Morland pays £18,000** — a year's writing money, from home. **African Liberty** runs a paid fellowship entirely online. Both beat every prize on this board several times over. But serious grants want **a published sample and a book-length project**, which is the real argument for selling stories to magazines. And **Windham-Campbell and Civitella are nomination-only — you cannot apply, so stop looking at them.** → [[Grants and Fellowships]]

**4 · No investment beats the loan you already have.**
The Bank of Tanzania caps microloans at **4% per month ≈ 48% a year.** Paying that down is a guaranteed, risk-free 48% return. **Nothing guarantees 48%.** Every shilling into a volatile asset before the loans are clear is the worse trade, every time. → [[Debt First]]
