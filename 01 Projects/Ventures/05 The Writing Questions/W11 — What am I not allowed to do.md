# 11 · What am I not allowed to do?

The writing side has fewer legal traps than the accounting side, and more **rule** traps — and rule traps are worse, because there is no appeal and no second attempt.

Four to hold, in descending order of what being wrong costs:

**1 · Prior publication.** Online counts, except personal blogs, personal websites and personal Facebook pages. Where a public code repository falls is unconfirmed. → [[W04 — Is this published, and who decides]]

**2 · One entry per story, forever.** Rule 3g. Chantal has one attempt. This is known and worth restating in the same list as everything else, because a rule you know can still be forgotten when a deadline is close.

**3 · Authorship declaration.** Every entrant confirms the work is their own. In 2026 every shortlisted writer had to state personally that no AI was used, and the Foundation examined drafts to satisfy itself. **A true declaration you cannot evidence is a worse position than a true declaration you can.** → [[Provenance — The AI Question and the Public Repo]]

**4 · Eligibility of form.** Morland funds full-length adult fiction and non-fiction of at least 80,000 words. **Short story collections are explicitly not eligible.** The Village, The House, The Road is investigative narrative non-fiction and clears this easily — but the Mihogoni cycle would not, and if the proposal ever drifts back toward linked stories the whole application dies on a definition.

None of these is discovered by reading craft books. All are discovered by reading **the entry rules, once, slowly, with a pen** — an hour of work that nobody does because it produces nothing and feels like admin.

> **The question:** what are the rules of this particular game, which one is the whole eligibility resting on, and am I inside it by fact or by assumption?

The pattern repeats exactly on the other side of the vault: the accounting plan turned out to have a mentor requirement nobody had looked for. **Same question, different game.**
