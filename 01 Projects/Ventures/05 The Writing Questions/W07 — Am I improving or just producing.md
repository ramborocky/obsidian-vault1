# 7 · Am I improving, or just producing?

Rate of improvement is set by **loop length** more than by talent or hours, and the three routes differ by two orders of magnitude.

**Prizes: one signal a year, and it is a single bit.** A yes or a silence, twelve months after the work, with no explanation, decided by a panel whose composition changes. You cannot learn from it. You can only be selected or not selected by it.

**Paid markets: weeks.** A rejection is still one bit, but it arrives fast enough to act on, and it comes many times a year. Occasionally an editor writes a line, and that line is worth more than any prize result because it is specific.

**Readers: days.** The fastest loop available, and the only one that answers the question the other two dodge — does this hold a person who is not obliged to finish it?

The vault's apparatus is aimed almost entirely at the slowest loop. And it already contains the evidence that the slowest loop is also the least legible: **the 2026 winner scores 31/45 on your own rubric and beat 7,806 entries. Chantal scores 40.** Home.md reads that correctly as a warning rather than a win — the rubric rewards architecture and the panel forgives it.

That is a genuinely important finding and it should be followed one step further. **A rubric that disagrees with the outcome by nine points is not calibrated**, and the only thing that can calibrate it is more outcomes — which the annual loop cannot supply and the monthly one can.

> **The question:** what is the shortest loop available to me here, and am I in it? If the honest answer is that I am waiting rather than practising, that is the finding.

**The practical version:** send work to fast-answering venues while the slow ones are pending. Not instead — while.
