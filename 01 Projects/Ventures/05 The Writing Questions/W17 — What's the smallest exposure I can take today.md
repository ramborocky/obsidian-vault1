# 17 · What's the smallest exposure I can take today?

Everything here converges where the other seventeen did: the obstacle is not craft, information, or instruments. **It is that the exposing act goes second**, and the safe act — real, skilled, defensible work — goes first and absorbs the week.

The writing side has a specific version of this, and it is worth naming plainly. **Every instrument in the vault returns a verdict you generated.** Rubrics, diagnoses, cold-read prompts, craft bases, the Craft Atlas. All excellent, all internal. A reader, an editor, or a panel returns a verdict you cannot control — and that is the only kind that teaches you anything.

Today's list, in ascending order of exposure:

1. **Email the Foundation** the neutral rules question: does work in a personal public code repository count as previously published? Ten minutes. Removes a disqualification risk that currently sits under an assumption.
2. **Move `x-factor/` and the AI draft to a private repository.** An hour. Removes the adjacency problem without deleting anything or destroying the labelling.
3. **Write the provenance page** while it is a record rather than a defence.
4. **Send three readers the draft**, briefed with the cold-read prompts that already exist, with a deadline.
5. **Pick one paid market and send one story this month.** Not the best story. One story.

And the two paragraphs on the dashboard, which are not mine to write and never were.

Each of these is small. Each returns something the world decides rather than something you decide. **An act that cannot tell you you're wrong has not exposed you to anything**, however much work it contained.

> **The question, every morning:** what is the smallest thing I can do today that puts a verdict outside my own control?

Scored weekly: zero such acts is a week of hiding, whatever it produced. One is a working week. Three is a good one.
