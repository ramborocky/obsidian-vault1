# 4 · Is this published, and who decides?

The 2026 rules treat online publication as publication, **with an explicit exception for personal blogs, personal websites and personal Facebook pages.** Magazines, anthologies and other public platforms are not exempt.

So: is a public GitHub repository a personal website, or a public platform?

On the natural reading it is the former — it is your own space, not a venue that selects and presents work to an audience. **On the natural reading.** That is not a ruling, and rule 3g means Chantal cannot be entered a second time if the reading turns out to be wrong.

The asymmetry is the whole argument. Asking costs one email and some mild awkwardness. Not asking risks a story that has absorbed months, on a technicality, with no second attempt available. **A cost of near-zero against a loss of everything is not a close call**, and the only reason it stays unasked is that the answer might be inconvenient.

Ask it neutrally and early, without naming the story: *does work posted in a personal public code repository count as previously published under the entry rules?* A question from someone who reads the rules carefully is not a red flag. **A question asked in October, about a story already submitted, is a different conversation entirely.**

> **The question:** which rule am I currently satisfying by interpretation rather than by confirmation — and what does being wrong cost?

There is a general version worth keeping for any competition, grant or contract: **find the definition that the whole eligibility rests on, and check whether you are inside it by fact or by assumption.** It is nearly always one definition, and it is nearly always assumed.
