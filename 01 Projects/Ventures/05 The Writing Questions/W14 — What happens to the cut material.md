# 14 · What happens to the cut material?

An archive hook captures the pre-edit content of story prose to the Craft Log before any overwrite, and [[Collection Tracker]] states the principle: **cut material is trunk material for other stories in the cycle.**

This is the best structural decision in the vault and it is worth understanding why, because most writers get it exactly wrong.

Cutting normally feels like loss, and the felt loss is what makes writers defend weak passages. Convert cuts into inventory and the emotional economics invert: **a cut becomes a deposit rather than a deletion**, and the resistance to cutting largely disappears. That is a craft benefit disguised as a filing system.

The second effect is the one that compounds. Chantal is story one of a cycle set in Mihogoni. Every story after it inherits a world, a lake, a set of minor characters, a register, and now an archive of material that was too good to lose and wrong for the story it was written in. **Story five will cost a fraction of what story one cost**, and that fraction is the whole argument for a cycle over a set of unrelated stories.

Which points at something the plan has not fully drawn out. The vault says only one story should be in active drafting at a time, and that is right. But **the cut archive is already large enough to seed the next one**, and the next one is the thing that makes every route in [[W01 — Who is the payer, and is it a reader]] repeatable. One story cannot be sold to a paid market twice. A cycle can supply them indefinitely.

> **The question:** where does the discarded work go — and is it going somewhere it can be used, or somewhere it can only be mourned?

The general form: **a system that turns your losses into inventory changes what you are willing to lose.** That is true of prose, of failed pitches, of abandoned businesses.
