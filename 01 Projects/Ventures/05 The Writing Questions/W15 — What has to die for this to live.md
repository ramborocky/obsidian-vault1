# 15 · What has to die for this to live?

Steve Young, the day after retiring, having been known worldwide for one thing and waking up to find it gone:

> Everyone needs to treat it like a death — to mourn it and bury it, and actually have it as a place you can keep referring to, almost like a grave site. Because otherwise you carry it around and you never transition.

Roger Staubach's advice to him was one word: **run.** *The game will never leave you, but you need to leave it.*

Read carefully, nothing is given up. Young keeps returning to the grave site — the thing is buried and still there, and can be visited. What dies is not the football. It is the **primacy**: the arrangement in which one thing answers the question of who you are, and everything else is a hobby or an interruption.

Applied here without softening it, the arrangement to bury is not *writer*. It is:

> **"Writer — and one day the writing will pay, and until then the accounting is admin."**

That sentence has been running the allocation for months, and it explains the shape of the vault exactly: a nine-axis rubric for a 3,896-word story, and a credential file that moved once in six weeks.

The reordering is small and specific. **The writing stops being the plan and becomes the practice.** It gets protected hours rather than all of them, it stops carrying the household on its back, and — this is the part that is easy to miss — **it gets better as a result**, because work that has to pay the rent this year cannot take the risks that good work requires.

Suleika Jaouad supplies the rest. *Moving on is a myth.* You do not compartmentalise a passage and stow it in the past; you move forward with it, integrated. And she notes we have rituals for birth, marriage and death and none for the transitions between — so she built her own.

> **The question:** what arrangement has to be buried, with some ceremony, for the new thing to have room? **Nothing is lost. What ends is the ordering.**
