# 8 · Would I still want to be writing this in month nine?

**This one is yours, not mine.** It is already in [[Morland — What They Actually Fund]] and it is the best question in the vault:

> Could you write 10,000 words a month of it for twelve months? That is the actual undertaking, every month. **The idea that excites you least at month nine is the wrong idea, whatever it looks like now.**

And the sharper version in [[Morland 2026 — Due Diligence and Rerun]]: *you brought this subject yourself. You are angry about it. That anger is the reason the prose will still be alive at month nine.*

It is worth stating why this is such a good question, because the reasoning generalises.

Most selection is done at the moment of maximum enthusiasm, when everything is possible and nothing has been attempted. **Month nine is the honest test condition** — novelty gone, no applause, the difficulty fully visible, and the only remaining fuel whatever was intrinsically there. A choice made for month one will not survive it.

The scoring rubric in the proposal sheet has seven axes and gives a total out of 35. **Month nine is not one of them, and it should be the tiebreak.** Two subjects can score identically and only one of them will still be written in July.

Note also what it protects against: choosing the *impressive* subject. Impressiveness is a month-one property. It decays fastest of anything on the list, because by month nine nobody is watching and the only audience is you at a desk at six in the morning.

> **The question, for anything longer than a season:** not *is this the best idea* but **which of these am I still doing in month nine, on a bad day, with nobody watching?**
