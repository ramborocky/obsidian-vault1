# 2 · Can I prove I wrote this?

**In 2026 the Commonwealth Foundation investigated allegations of AI use in its own winning stories.** It declined to use detection tools, citing artistic ownership over unpublished work. Instead it examined **working drafts, time-stamped documents and notes**, consulted judges, and took personal statements from every shortlisted writer.

Read that method again, because it changes what an archive is for.

Detection tools are unreliable and everyone knows it. **Provenance is not.** A record showing a piece coming into existence over months — bad versions, abandoned turns, revisions with reasons attached — is something no generated text has, and cannot be manufactured after the fact.

You have one. Every draft of Chantal exists as a git commit, time-stamped, in order, with a message saying what changed. An archive hook captures pre-edit prose to the Craft Log before any overwrite. Six knowledge bases record the reasoning behind specific revisions, and a Pattern Log names the recurring failures.

**That is a stronger evidentiary record of human authorship than most professional novelists could produce**, and it exists because of an archiving decision made for entirely unrelated reasons.

The move is to make it legible before anyone asks. One page: how the story was written, which commits show what, where the Craft Log entries sit, and the plain statement that no AI wrote any of the prose. **Written now it is a record. Written after a question arrives it is a defence, and reads like one.**

> **The question:** if someone doubted this was mine, what would I show them — and does that thing already exist, or would I be assembling it under suspicion?

The general form applies well beyond prizes. In a world where output is cheap and identical, **the person who can show the work coming into being is in a different category from the person who can only show the output.**
