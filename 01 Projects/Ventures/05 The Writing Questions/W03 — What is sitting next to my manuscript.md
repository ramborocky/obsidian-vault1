# 3 · What is sitting next to my manuscript?

`origin` is public. Anyone can read it. Tracked in that repository right now, alongside the submission candidate:

- `Claude_s Version of Kalemie (AI Draft, Not Canon).md`, in the drafts folder
- the whole `x-factor/` directory of AI-written craft experiments

**Both are honestly labelled, and that labelling was the right call**, made before any of this was known. `x-factor.md` states plainly that every file in it was written by Claude and none of it is yours.

Now read it from outside. Someone follows a link and finds a publicly readable repository containing an AI-written version of the same story, in a drafts folder, next to the entry. The disclaimer is *inside* the file. **The filename is what a search returns.**

Nothing here is dishonest and the exposure is not to a finding of wrongdoing. It is to **having to explain** — in a year when the Foundation has said publicly that it is reviewing its processes, and when an allegation costs nothing to make and a great deal to answer.

The fix is not deletion. Deleting from a public repository does not remove anything; git keeps history, and a deletion is itself a fact in the record. Worse, it destroys the labelling that currently protects you. **Move the AI material to a private repository.** The public vault then holds only human work and its history; the AI files still exist, still labelled, still explainable.

> **The question, generalised:** what is adjacent to my work that I have never looked at from a stranger's angle? Not *is it defensible* — **is it explainable in one sentence to someone who is not inclined to be fair?**

The habit worth keeping: once per campaign, open your own public surface as an unfriendly reader and write down what you find.
