# 10 · What did the winning application actually contain?

**The vault already does this better than most agencies.** [[Reverse-Engineered — What a Winning Morland Application Contained]], [[Morland 2022 — The Five, Read Closely]], [[Oduah Read Closely — The Method Behind the Funded Book]], [[Morland 2025 — Post-Mortem]], and the 2026 winner read against your own rubric.

It is worth naming the method, because it is the strongest habit in this vault and it is not being applied everywhere it should be.

**The method:** do not read advice about how to win. Find the things that actually won, get the closest available artefact — the funded proposal, the winning story, the shortlist — and read them the way an engineer reads a competitor's product. What is present. What is absent. What order. How long. What the first paragraph does.

Advice is generalised from memory and is usually wrong in the specifics that matter. **The artefact is evidence.**

And it produces findings nothing else does. Reading the 2026 Commonwealth winner against your own instrument gave the single most useful sentence on the dashboard: **it scores 31/45 and beat 7,806 entries, while Chantal scores 40.** No amount of craft reading would have produced that. Only the artefact did.

Where it is *not* being applied: the paid markets. [[Paid Markets]] has the rates and the ladder, but the same treatment has not been run on the venues — read twenty published stories from The Dark, ask what they have in common, notice the length, the register, the kind of opening. **The magazines publish their whole back catalogue for free.** It is the same method, against a target that answers in weeks instead of a year.

> **The question:** what is the closest artefact to the thing I want, can I get it, and have I read it as evidence rather than as inspiration?
