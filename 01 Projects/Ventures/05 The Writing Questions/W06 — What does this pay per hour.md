# 6 · What does this pay per hour?

Every route in the vault is priced per outcome. None is priced per hour, and the hours are where the decision actually sits.

The Atelier has been running at roughly **25 hours a week.** Over twelve weeks that is **300 hours.**

- 300 hours aimed at the Commonwealth: an expectation of **£1.92.**
- 300 hours aimed at Morland: a proposal, a biography and a polished extract — **£90 of expectation, and a monthly wage if it lands.**
- 300 hours aimed at paid markets: perhaps a dozen finished submissions, at $200–480 each on acceptance.

The craft hours are not wasted in any of the three, which is exactly what makes this hard and why the choice has never been forced. Skill built for one transfers to all. **But the hours are finite, and only one of the three has a deadline in September.**

There is a second cost the per-outcome view hides. A free entry that consumes 25 hours a week is one of the most expensive things you own, and **the zero on the invoice is precisely what stops you noticing.** Price every opportunity in hours at their best alternative use, not in the fee it charges.

And a third thing, which is not arithmetic. At 25 hours a week on top of everything else, the question is not only what the hours return but what they cost the rest of the life they are taken from. Samin Nosrat shut down a thriving business because it was making her ill, and needed therapy rather than a spreadsheet to see it. **You cannot spreadsheet your way out of everything, nor onto everything.**

> **The question:** what is this paying per hour, at the hours I am actually putting in — and would I accept that number if someone offered it to me as a job?
