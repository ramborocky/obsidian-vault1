## A · REMOTE FREELANCE — the fastest money on the board

**The whole advantage is arbitrage: earn in dollars, spend in shillings.** It is the one structural edge in your position and it does not expire.

Open-market freelance writing runs **$15–40/hr**; finance and technical specialists command more. The January 2026 per-word average across 500 working freelancers was **$0.42**. **Generic content writing has fallen 18% since 2020 — specialist work has not.**

```
10 hrs/wk @ $20/hr  =  $800/mo    =  TSh 2,117,600
15 hrs/wk @ $25/hr  =  $1,500/mo  =  TSh 3,970,500
20 hrs/wk @ $30/hr  =  $2,400/mo  =  TSh 6,352,800
```

**An accountant who writes clean English is an uncommon combination.** Do not price yourself as a generic writer — that is a race against the whole world on cost, and you will lose it.

### The rules

**1 · Narrow beats broad.** Not "writer" — *bookkeeping and management accounts for small businesses*, or *finance writing for accounting-software companies*. Narrowness is what lets a beginner charge a specialist rate.

**2 · The first three clients are the hard part.** No reviews, no history. After three, it compounds.

**3 · Check the payment rail before anything else.** Getting paid from the US or UK into Tanzania is a solved problem but not an automatic one. Sort this first — it is the thing that stops people.

**4 · Ask NBAA whether this counts toward the ACPA logbook.** If it does, this work pays you *while* completing the qualification and the two best tracks merge. → [[Career Leverage]]

**Time to first client: realistically 4–12 weeks of active pitching.** Nothing else on this board pays a bill in 2026.

## THE REORDERING — read this before writing another anthology story

[[Paid Markets]] changes what this folder is for.

**A single 4,000-word horror story sold to Nightmare pays $320 — more than the entire KDP book's first year — and it lands eighteen months sooner.**

So the sequence inverts:

> **Old:** write nine stories → publish an anthology → hope it sells
> **New:** write stories → **sell them individually to magazines** → collect the paid, credited stories into the anthology afterwards

Same work, same genre, same nine stories. But each one is **paid on acceptance**, arrives at the book **already vouched for by an editor**, and builds the credit history that agents and grant panels actually read.

**Check rights on every contract.** You want first rights with reversion, or the story cannot go in the book.
