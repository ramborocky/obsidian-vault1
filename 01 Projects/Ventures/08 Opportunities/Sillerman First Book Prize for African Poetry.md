# 📖 SILLERMAN FIRST BOOK PRIZE FOR AFRICAN POETRY
### $1,000 and a university press book. **You are eligible and you have nothing to send.**

→ [[08 The Opportunities Board]]

---

## THE OFFER

- **$1,000** and **book publication through the University of Nebraska Press**
- Awarded annually to an African poet who has **not yet published a poetry collection**
- Judged by the Sillerman Editorial Team — Kwame Dawes, Chris Abani, Matthew Shenoda, John Keene, Gabeba Baderoon, Phillippa Yaa de Villiers, Aracelis Girmay, Mahtem Shiferraw
- Winner announced in **June**

## ELIGIBILITY — you pass every clause

- **"African writer"** means born in Africa, a national or resident of an African country, or with African parents ✅
- Must not have published a book-length poetry collection ✅
- **Self-published books count as a published collection if they were sold** online, in stores or at readings — worth knowing before you put anything on Kindle
- Editing an anthology of *other* writers' work does not disqualify you
- English only; translations accepted, with a share of the prize to the translator
- Not open to people at Nebraska Press, Akashic, Amalion or Brown University

## THE VERDICT

**Free, prestigious, and completely out of reach — for the only reason that matters: it needs fifty pages of poetry you have not written.**

This is on the board so that you stop half-looking at it. A first-book poetry prize is a five-year proposition for a prose writer, not a deadline. **It becomes real the year a manuscript exists, and not one day before.**

⚠️ **One clause here is live even though the prize is not.** Sillerman treats a **sold** self-published collection as a published book. Nothing you are planning touches poetry, so the Kindle route is unaffected — but the principle is worth carrying: **selling a book is a permanent status change, and different prizes count it differently.** → [[Morland 2026 — The Campaign]]
