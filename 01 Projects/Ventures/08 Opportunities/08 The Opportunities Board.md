## THE FIVE THINGS THIS DOCUMENT ACTUALLY TELLS YOU

**1 · Morland is the emergency, not NHIF.** £18,000, closes in 2 days, and [[Morland 2026 — The Campaign]] still shows the extract in revision. Whatever else this board does this week, that file gets checked and moved first. NHIF is worth doing, but it is free, it is new, and it is not the £18,000 clock. → [[Morland 2026 — The Campaign]]

**2 · The NHIF call is a five-day sprint on a different track, not a new one.**
It's a research concept note on Universal Health Insurance Coverage, free to enter, and it fits under Career Leverage (track 03) — claims fraud and audit are accounting problems, not an invented literary detour. **Eligibility for someone not enrolled at a university is unconfirmed; email both contacts today.** → [[NHIF Call for Abstracts — Research Competition 2026]]

**3 · Doek! is a trap, and it is worth reading twice.**
It looks like an open Pan-African fiction call. It is not. **Fiction and poetry are restricted to Namibian citizens and residents.** Only **nonfiction** and visual art are open to African writers generally. Submit fiction there and it is dead on arrival. → [[Doek! Literary Magazine — Issue 18]]

**4 · None of these solve the Morland gate.**
The Morland sample must be **published and offered for sale**, and the FAQ allows exactly one online medium: **Kindle**. Brittle Paper and Doek! are free to read, so publishing in either creates a byline but **not eligibility**. The Kindle route stays the only route. → [[Morland 2026 — The Campaign]]

**5 · Of what's left after Morland, Sillerman is poetry outright and Brittle Paper takes it among other things.**
**You are a prose writer.** Doek! is nonfiction-only and NHIF isn't literary at all, so Brittle Paper and the NHIF concept note are the only two calls left where fiction or accounting prose actually applies. → [[Prize Portfolio]]
