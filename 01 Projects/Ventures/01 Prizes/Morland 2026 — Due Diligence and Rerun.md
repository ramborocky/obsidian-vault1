# PART 1 · THE EXTRACT

## The honest position: **I have not read your stories and cannot judge them**

*Voices of Azania* is not in this vault or on this machine. **Any verdict I gave on whether the extract is "tight" would be invented**, and inventing it is worse than saying so. → **paste me the stronger story and you get a real read against the same nine-axis instrument the vault already uses.**

## What I can give you is the decision rule

**The extract is the only prose the first-round readers see.** Before the proposal is read, before the biography, before anything — this is the whole of your case that you can write. **It is not a formality. It is the audition.**

### The three tests, in order

**1 · The measurement test.** Both stories, counted in Word. The upload must be **2,000–5,000 words**. Longer story? Upload a 2,000–5,000 word section that stands alone.

**2 · The first-page test.** Read the first 400 words cold, aloud. **A reader working through 800 submissions decides in that first page.** If the first page explains rather than shows, the rest never gets read.

**3 · The honest-doubt test.** ⚠️ **You already told me the collection may not have been tight.** That instinct came from you, unprompted, a year after the fact. **Trust it.** Writers are wrong about their own work in both directions, but a doubt that survives twelve months is data.

### The decision

| If… | Then |
| --- | --- |
| One of the two stories genuinely holds up | **Use it. Change nothing. Ship early** |
| Neither does, and you have a stronger story in a drawer | **Add it to *Voices of Azania* and re-upload.** A re-upload is a day's work and the ASIN stays the same |
| Neither does, and you have nothing else | **Write one story, and only one.** Then stop |

> ⚠️ **The trap: do not let "improve the collection" eat the proposal.** The extract is the audition; the proposal is the job interview, and it is the thing that is not written yet. **If the choice is a merely-decent extract with a strong proposal, or a strong extract with a rushed proposal — take the first, every time.**

**And not the Mkwawa story as the extract.** It points straight at the proposal you are no longer making.

# PART 2 · IS THE BALE'S SUBJECT TAKEN?

## 🔴 The finding, and it is the reason for this note

**Partly yes. The field is more crowded than I implied this morning, and the crowding is recent.**

| Work | Who | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ***Clothing Poverty*** (2015) | Andrew Brooks | **The closest competitor.** Fast fashion and secondhand clothes, including the 1980s debt-crisis liberalisation that opened African markets |
| ***Secondhand*** (2019) | Adam Minter | The global used-goods trade, travel reportage |
| ***The Travels of a T-Shirt*** (2005) | Pietra Rivoli | One garment, followed down the chain — **the exact structural move** |
| ***Worn: A People's History of Clothing*** (2022) | Sofi Thanhauser | The wider cloth history |
| ***Dead White Man's Clothes*** | The Or Foundation | Large, well-funded, ongoing research and media project — **Ghana, Kantamanto, 120+ traders interviewed** |
| Long-form reportage | New Lines, McGraw Center | Recurrent magazine coverage of the same trade |

**A judge who reads widely in non-fiction will know at least two of these.** A proposal that reads as *the story of the global secondhand clothing trade* will be met with **"already done, twice, better funded."** That is a bad first reaction to earn on page one.

## 🟢 But the gap is real, and it is narrow and specific

**Every one of those books is either Ghana-centric or a global survey, and every one is written from the outside** — by academics or by American and British journalists following the goods *outward* from the donation bin.

**Three things nobody has taken:**

1. **East Africa, not West.** The literature's centre of gravity is Kantamanto, in Accra. **The Tanzanian trade is comparatively unwritten in narrative form**
2. **The ban itself as a story.** 2016: five countries agree to end it. 2017–18: the US lobby moves, AGOA is threatened, **four countries fold — yours among them — and Rwanda stands alone and pays a 30% tariff.** That is a political thriller with dates, and **no trade book has been built around it**
3. **The receiving end, in the first person, by someone from there.** Not a visitor. The person who wore it

> ### The correction: **The Bale is not a book about secondhand clothes. It is a book about the year East Africa tried to stop them and lost.**
>
> That is a different book, it is unwritten, and it fixes the crowding problem in one move.

## ⚠️ And it fixes a second problem you had not been told about yet

**"Follow the chain from the donation bin" requires standing in a sorting warehouse in England or America.** That is a visa, a flight and money, inside a twelve-month scholarship, from Tanzania, with a job.

**Narrowing to the ban solves it.** The book starts at the port in Dar and runs *inland and backwards* — the auction, the wholesaler, the bale, the stall, the body — with the upstream end reached by telephone, by document and by the trade's own paperwork. **Everything you must physically touch is inside your own country.** That is a proposal you can actually deliver, and deliverability is something a Foundation funding twelve months of writing thinks about hard.

# THE RECOMMENDATION

> ## **The Bale — narrowed to the ban.** And here is the honest reasoning, including the part that is not in the table.

**On the numbers, the Unfinished House wins by three points.** I am not recommending it, and you should know why: **its three points come from safety.** No crowding, no politics, easy to deliver. But it has **no event**, and every single project the Foundation funded in three years has an event. **A safe book that misses the strongest pattern is not safer.**

**The Bale, narrowed, is the only idea that scores high on the two patterns that decide first-round reading** — a dated wound and a named form — while being a book only you are positioned to write.

**And there is a factor no rubric holds.** You brought this subject yourself. **You are angry about it.** That anger is the reason the prose will still be alive at month nine, when the undertaking is 10,000 words a month whether you feel like it or not. **The Unfinished House is the better-scoring book and The Bale is the one you would actually finish.**

### Two conditions on that recommendation

**1 · Narrow it in the proposal itself, explicitly.** The first sentence must make clear this is the story of the ban, from the receiving end. **If it opens as "a book about the secondhand clothing trade," Brooks and Minter beat you before the second paragraph.**

**2 · Name the trade you already know.** One market. One wholesaler. One stallholder. The port. **Specificity is what killed the Mkwawa proposal and it is the cheapest thing on this list to fix.**

### And the fallback, stated plainly

**If you read the paragraph above about anger and it does not describe you — take Villages Moved instead**, and handle Nyerere as a family's story that politics happens to. It is the best *book* on this page. It is not the best *application*, and the two are different questions.
