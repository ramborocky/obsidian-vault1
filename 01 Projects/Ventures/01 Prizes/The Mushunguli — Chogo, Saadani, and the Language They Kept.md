# 🪶 THE MUSHUNGULI
### Taken out of Tanga in chains. **Walked back in 2003.** And they still spoke Kizigula when they arrived.

## 📜 THE ARC — verified

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| **1800–1890** | **25,000–50,000** enslaved people sold from Zanzibar to the Somali coast, to work plantations on the **Juba and Shebelle**. Among the main groups taken: **Zigua**, Zaramo, Yao, Makua, Nyasa |
| **From the 1840s** | Fugitives escape into the Juba valley forests and form their own communities — the **Gosha**, *people of the forest*. **~35,000 by the early 1900s** |
| **By 1900** | 🔴 **Kizigula is the language of about fifteen villages in the lower Juba valley.** They are called **Mushunguli** — from *Mzigula* |
| **1870s–1890** | Back home: **Bwana Heri bin Juma**, Zigua, rules **Saadani**. Refuses the Sultan of Zanzibar. Taxes the caravans. Joins the Abushiri revolt 1888 |
| **June 1889** | Saadani bombarded and taken. **Bwana Heri flees inland and builds a series of forts**, holding out until **April 1890** |
| **1991** | Siad Barre falls. The Bantu minority has **no clan protection** and is the most exposed group in the country |
| **early 1990s** | Flight to the Kenyan camps |
| **April 2003** | 🔴 Tanzania allocates **21 km² at Chogo**, Handeni district, **Tanga region.** Over **3,000** transferred from Mkuyu camp, 80 km away. **2.5 acres per family** |
| **2009** | **They are granted Tanzanian citizenship** |
| **Now** | Declich documents the language **shifting from Kizigula to Kiswahili** at Chogo |

## 🥘 THE ONE FACT THE BOOK IS BUILT ON

> ### **They held the language through six generations of slavery and exile. They are losing it now, at home, in freedom.**

**That is your *isi ewu***— the recurring object from [[Oduah Read Closely — The Method Behind the Funded Book|the Oduah reading]]. It appears three times and means something different each time:

1. **In the Juba forests** it is the thing that survives — fifteen villages speaking Tanga's language a thousand miles north
2. **At the border in the 1990s** it is the proof of the claim — *we are yours, listen to us speak*
3. **At Chogo today** it is going, replaced by Kiswahili, and nobody is doing anything wrong

**No argument required. The politics arrives entirely through a language and never once has to be stated.**

## ⚔️ AND BWANA HERI IS THE UNRESOLVED CENTRE

While Zigua people were being shipped north out of the coast, **a Zigua was holding a coastal port against the Sultan and then the Germans.**

**And Saadani — the port Bwana Heri ruled — was a slave-trading port.**

> **That is not a contradiction to solve. It is the centre of the book, and it must be left exactly as it is.** The Zigua leader remembered for fighting the Germans ran the harbour that shipped Zigua north. **Both true, at once, in one man, in one decade.**
>
> **This is the Oduah move precisely** — Aisha Wakil stops a bombing by putting her own daughter in the building, and the piece never adjudicates it. → rule 1 and rule 3, satisfied by the choice of subject rather than by careful wording.

⚠️ **And the sources disagree about how he died.** One account has him hanged at Saadani in April 1890. Others say Wissmann treated him as an honourable enemy and told him to rebuild the town; one has him ruling until his death in **1894**. **Do not resolve this either.** A documented uncertainty at the heart of a national resistance story is a gift — it is *do not solve the murder*, handed to you.

### The three axes that decide it

**🟢 Political exposure · 5.** No Nyerere. No CCM. No living party, no serving officials, no public holiday. **The antagonists are 19th-century slavers, the Sultan of Zanzibar, and the German colonial administration** — and Tanzania's own conduct in the story is *generous*: it gave land and then citizenship. **The single serious risk on the Sokoine book does not exist here**, and you are a serving government officer.

**🟢 Only you · 5.** **You are Zigula. You are from Tanga. Chogo is in Tanga region.** Pattern 6 asks the proposal to answer *why you* without being asked — here it is answered by the first clause of your bio. **No other applicant on earth has this.**

**🟡 Field crowding · 4, and the gap is precise.** **Francesca Declich** owns the academic ground — decades of it, the *Africa* article on the Chogo migration, *Gendered Narratives* in *History in Africa*. **Catherine Besteman** wrote *Unraveling Somalia* and then ***Making Refuge*** (Duke, 2016) — **but Making Refuge is about Lewiston, Maine.**

> **Besteman took the branch that went to America. Nobody has taken the branch that went home.** There is no trade narrative non-fiction book about the return to Chogo, and certainly none by a Zigula Tanzanian. **That is the gap and it is a real one.**
