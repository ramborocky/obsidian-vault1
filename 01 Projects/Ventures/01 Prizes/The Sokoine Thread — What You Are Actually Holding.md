## 1 · YOUR INSTINCT, CHECKED

**All of it holds.**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **5 April 1983** | Nyerere launches the crackdown on economic saboteurs under **the Economic Sabotage Act No. 9 of 1983** |
| **Who ran it** | **Sokoine.** It was his campaign |
| **What the law did** | **Reversed the burden of proof.** You had to explain where your wealth came from. **Special courts. No lawyers admitted. You defended yourself** |
| **What it felt like** | In Oysterbay and Upanga, **people threw televisions and bundles of money into the streets and gutters** out of fear of him. Police collected them and sent them to the Bank of Tanzania |
| **12 April 1984** | **Fourteen months later he is dead** on the Dodoma–Dar road at Dakawa |

> **Money in the gutters of Oysterbay.** That is your opening scene and you did not have to invent it.

**And yes — Nyerere's man, Nyerere's villages.** Sokoine was the enforcer of the same state that carried out **Operation Vijiji in 1973–76**. The two ideas you have been circling for two days are **the same state, eleven years apart.**

**A man who made the wealthy account for every shilling, in a country where the wealthy were inside the party, died in a road accident fourteen months into the job.** You do not need to allege anything. **You just put the dates next to each other and let the reader do it** — which, as it happens, is the entire method of the courtroom scene you already wrote.

## 2 · SO WHAT GENRE IS IT? — not speculative fiction

**The name for what you have written is *secret history*.**

> **Secret history keeps the record exactly as it happened — and invents the hidden cause.**
>
> Sokoine still dies on 12 April 1984. Dumisani Dube is still convicted. **Nothing in the public record changes.** What you add is a second car on that road, and a reason nobody was ever asked about it.

**That is a real, named tradition** — Forsyth's *The Day of the Jackal*, Ellroy's *American Tabloid*, most of the good conspiracy novels. **It is not speculative fiction**, which changes the outcome. **Yours is stronger than speculative fiction precisely because nothing changes.** The horror is that the official story stays intact and is still standing today.

**Add your dream sequences — Bibi Kivuli, the baobab, the ancestors at the moment of the crash — and the full description is:**

> ### **A secret-history political thriller with ancestral realism.**

**That is a *named form*, and naming the form is one of the six things every funded Morland project does.** → [[Morland — What They Actually Fund]]

### ⚠️ The 2025 date — now a real fork, not just a typo

**Door 1 · Fix it to 1984.** The book is secret history. Tight, frightening, and the version I would take.

**Door 2 · Commit to it.** A man crashes in 1984 and wakes in 2025 — into a Tanzania where the money that hid from Sokoine has long since won. **That is legitimate, and it is a different and much harder book:** you must plant it early, pay it off, and carry two Tanzanias for 80,000 words.

> **Do not leave it undecided. Undecided reads as a typo, and a typo is what it currently is.**

## 3 · THE THING TO NOTICE — three ideas were always one book

```
1973–76   Operation Vijiji        the state moves its people
1983      Uhujumu uchumi          the state opens its people's houses
1984      Dakawa                  the man running it dies on the road
```

**One arc: what a state does to its own people in the name of order — and what happens to the man who means it.** Villages Moved was not a rival to Sokoine. **It was chapter one.**

**This is a bigger and more coherent book than The Bale**, and it scores higher on every one of the six patterns: a dated wound, a named form, ferocious geographic specificity, and a writer nobody could substitute.
