# 🚗 "THE FASTEST CAR IN MAZIMBU" — COLD READ
### **This is the better story, and it is not close.** It also contains one number that breaks the whole thing.

## THE DECISION

> ## **This is the extract.** Fix it, re-upload, submit.

It beats *The Chants* on the two things that decide a first-round read: **it has a real event at its centre, and it has a premise a reader wants resolved.** *The Chants* has better individual lines. **This has a better book underneath it.**

---

## WHAT YOU HAVE HERE — and I do not think you have fully noticed

**You built a story on a true, unhealed national wound, and the facts check out:**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **12 April 1984** | Edward Moringe Sokoine, Prime Minister of Tanzania, dies in a road crash |
| **Where** | **Dakawa, Morogoro** — on the Dodoma–Dar road |
| **Who hit him** | A speeding Land Cruiser driven by **Dumisani Dube, a South African freedom fighter** |
| **Why it still matters** | Sokoine was Nyerere's anti-corruption man. **Forty years on, Tanzanians still do not believe it was an accident** |

**And your invention is genuinely good:** a second ANC courier, also South African, also Tanzanian, doing 180 on that same road at that same moment, carrying FRELIMO documents — who wakes from a coma to hear that *another* South African has been convicted of it. **Then he is released without a statement, and a man in a Kaunda suit and Nzoo's shoes drives away in a black Mercedes.**

**That is a real story.** A man may have killed the Prime Minister, and the state needs someone else to have done it.

### The five things that are simply good

1. **The FRELIMO document wrapped around the maandazi.** A classified ANC security assessment used as street-food paper. **That is the best single image in either story** — an entire liberation apparatus undone by a snack
2. **The two constables at the war memorial.** *"Who are you guarding at night, the British Embassy, not the poor in Manzese?"* Dialogue revealing class, exactly as the Constitution asks
3. **The cream Peugeot in the police impound**, which he assumes is someone else's and which turns out to be his. Quietly sinister
4. **The release with no questions asked.** You never explain it. **Don't ever explain it**
5. **The dream at Kalenga**, tying this story to *The Chants*. **The collection has a spine and you may not have realised it**

## 🔴 THE THING THAT BREAKS IT

> *"The newspaper was thrown on the floor, and it read that the accident had taken place on the **12th of April, 2025**."*

**Your first line says 1984. Sokoine died in 1984. The newspaper says 2025.**

**A reader hits that and one of two things happens.** Either they decide it is a typo — in which case they have just watched the author fail to notice a forty-one-year error in his own premise. Or they decide it is deliberate, wait for the time-slip to pay off, and it never does.

**Either way the story dies at that sentence.** Everything before it was working.

**Fix it to 1984 and the story stands up.** If the slip *is* deliberate — the amnesia, the visions, the coma — then it needs planting earlier and paying off later, and that is a different, harder story. **Decide, and write the decision down.**

## THE THREE REPAIRS

### 1 · The speed is impossible, and the fix improves the prose

**A Peugeot 504 does about 160 km/h flat out.** The story says *200 miles per hour*, then *160*, then *180*. At 200 mph you are in a Formula One car.

> **Change every figure to km/h.** *160 km/h on the new asphalt* is fast, true, and still reckless enough to kill a Prime Minister. **The error disappears and nothing is lost.**

### 2 · The dangling participle is a systemic tic — and it is everywhere

This is the **one stylistic fault** doing the most damage, and it repeats on nearly every page:

| As written | The problem |
| --- | --- |
| *"Coming out of the foyer, a man in a black Kaunda suit… Entered a black Mercedes"* | **The man in the suit came out of the foyer**, not the narrator |
| *"Having been told to sit on a wooden bench, there was nothing that could be done"* | **The bench was told to sit** |
| *"Putting the key on the car lock was the answer"* | keys do not answer |
| *"Calling at my house in Lilongwe, had only made things worse"* | no subject at all |
| *"Being alone, without anybody from Mazimbu, made me suspicious"* | reads as *they* were suspicious |

**Your narrator's own actions keep detaching from him.** In a first-person story about a man who cannot remember what he did, that is an unfortunate accident to have on every page.

**The fix is mechanical: give each of those sentences a subject.** *I came out of the foyer. A man in a black Kaunda suit got into a black Mercedes and took off.* **Two sentences, both clear, both better.**

### 3 · Copy-editing — the same problem as the other story

*Asphalat · Lilogwe / Lilongwe · Peguet / Peugeot · Skyscapers · Getrude · PauPau · Jasmin · next to Kin / next of Kin · Thought raced / Thoughts raced · **Bob Nester Marley** (Nesta) · **Oliver Thambo** (Tambo) · **"Patients!- subiri"** (Patience) · Sandimba Sapi / Sapi Sandimba.*

⚠️ **And one POV break:** *"Sandimba remembered being told, by Moses yesterday morning…"* — **third person, inside a first-person story.** One sentence, but it is the kind a judge circles.

## TWO CRAFT NOTES

**The ending is doing two jobs and only one of them lands.** The radio gives you Dube's conviction — which is your story's real ending, chilling and complete — and then swerves to a mother-in-law in Lilongwe suing over a white baby. **That swerve may be brilliant** (domestic absurdity flattening a political tragedy, and it points at Batuli) **or it may be a different story arriving in the last paragraph.** Right now nothing earlier prepares it. **Either plant it, or end on Dube.**

**The opening paragraph is the weakest thing in the piece.** Seasons, weeds, impregnating the earth, Ethiopia and Eritrea — **it is an essay, and it is holding the door shut.** Your actual first line is *"Africa Liberate Zimbabwe came from my Peugeot 504."* Or better: **the 1984 date, the road, and the speed.** The first page is where the reader decides. **Give them the car.**

## 📌 ONE OBSERVATION, AND THEN I WILL LEAVE IT ALONE

**Sokoine's death is the best non-fiction book idea to appear in this whole campaign.** An anti-corruption Prime Minister killed on a road in 1984, a South African freedom fighter convicted, and forty years of a country not believing it. **Dated wound, named form, ferocious geographic specificity, and a Tanzanian who can work in Kiswahili — it scores higher than The Bale on every pattern.**

⚠️ **And it carries the same flag I raised against Mkwawa, so I have to raise it here too: you have published fiction about it.** A non-fiction investigation is more separable from a short story than a narrative retelling was — but it is the same question, and **only the Foundation can answer it.** You already have a live thread with Mathilda.

**I am not reopening the proposal decision.** The Bale stands unless you want it moved. **But you should know what you are sitting on.**
