## 🎲 THE ODDS, HONESTLY — read this before the plan

**You asked for 90% shortlisted and 65% of winning. Neither is available, to you or to anybody, and here is the arithmetic rather than an opinion.**

```
4 scholarships ÷ ~800 entries = 0.5% base rate
```

**No preparation converts 0.5% into 65%.** Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. What preparation actually does is move you between two piles, and the piles are very unequal:

| Pile | Roughly | What puts you here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Removed before merit** | the majority of the 800 | Missing proof of sale · proposal for a collection · proposal for a work in progress · under 80,000 words · a political-writing flag · incomplete documents · late |
| **Actually read** | the remainder | Everything above is clean |
| **Shortlisted** | not published by the Foundation | Literary merit of the extract and the 700 words |
| **Funded** | **4** | Four people's taste, on a day you are not in the room |

> **This is the real lever, and it is genuinely large:** the eliminations are almost entirely mechanical, and mechanical things are the ones 42 hours can fix. **Moving from "removed" to "read" is the biggest single jump available to you, and it is bought with admin, not talent.**
>
> **What it is worth in numbers:** clean eligibility plus a strong dated-wound proposal plus a mechanically sound extract puts you in low single digits — call it **2–5%**, against a 0.5% start. **That is a fivefold to tenfold improvement and it is the truth.** It is also, on £18,000, an expected value of several hundred pounds for two days' work, which is the best-paid 42 hours in this vault.

**One more thing that is true and unwelcome: your odds are already better than the base rate before you start**, because the gate is cleared in writing, the subject is dated and unwritten in English, and you can read the Kiswahili archive. **Most of the 800 have none of those.** Do not spend the 42 hours acquiring advantages you already hold.

# BLOCK 2 · THE EXTRACT, MECHANICAL — three hours
### *Bricks 1–3 of [[Fastest Car — The Build Order]], and nothing else.*

**One brick at a time. Never two.** The reason the last revision left four spellings of one name is that mechanics and meaning were fixed in the same pass, and meaning always wins that fight.

- [ ] **The date. Ten minutes and it is worth more than the rest of the block.** `2025` → `1984`, everywhere. Door 1. Write the chosen year in the file header so it never reopens → [[The Sokoine Thread — What You Are Actually Holding]]
- [ ] **Every speed to km/h.** *200 mph* is a Formula One car; a Peugeot 504 does about 160 km/h. **160 km/h on new asphalt is fast, true, and still enough to kill a Prime Minister**
- [ ] **The participle hunt.** Search for sentences opening with *-ing · Having · Being · Coming · Putting · Calling · Thinking · Making*. **Give every one of them a subject.** This is the single biggest score on the board — sentence structure is 2 of 6
- [ ] **Names and spelling.** *Asphalat · Lilogwe · Peguet · Skyscapers · Getrude · PauPau · Jasmin · next to Kin · Bob Nester Marley · Oliver Thambo · Patients* for *Patience* · **Sapi Sandimba, one order, everywhere**
- [ ] **The one POV break** — *"Sandimba remembered being told…"* is third person inside a first-person story. **A judge circles that**

> **✅ Done when:** searching each character's name returns exactly one form, and no sentence leaves the reader guessing who acted. **Spellcheck will not catch *Patients* for *Patience* — read it.**

# BLOCKS 6, 8, 11 · THE PROPOSAL — three sittings, nine hours
### *This is the highest-value writing in the application. It is the only new prose the Foundation has from you.*

**A finished 665-word draft is ready at [[The Proposal — 42-Hour Final]].** Do not send it as it stands — five facts in it are about your life and I cannot verify any of them.

**Sitting one · H+18, three hours.** Write the whole thing **by hand, in your own words, without looking at the draft.** Then read the draft and take only what you actually agree with. If you copy it out, the judges get my voice, which is a worse bet than yours.

**The seven moves it has to make:**
1. **Open with an image, not a thesis** — the televisions in the gutters
2. **The spine** — the death, and the seventeen years inside it
3. **Name the form** — narrative non-fiction, ~85,000 words, three parts
4. **The three movements** — village, house, road
5. **The dates, plainly** — 1967 · 1973–76 · April 1983 · 12 April 1984
6. **Why you — shown, never claimed**
7. **One paragraph of real prose**

**Sitting two · H+22, three hours.** Cut to 700. **One continuous piece of prose — no headings, no bullets, no numbered sections.** That formatting is what killed 2025 → [[Morland 2025 — Post-Mortem]]

**Sitting three · H+35, two hours.** Read it aloud, whole, once. Then verify the five biographical facts and the *thirty-six years* arithmetic. Then run [[The De-Slop Pass — English Prose]].

> ### 🚧 The three boundaries, and they are not negotiable
> **1 · The book stops in 1984.** Nothing after it is named — not 2021, not October 2025. **A book that stops in 1984 is history; one that reaches 2025 is a political intervention, and political writing is excluded**
> **2 · No verdict on Nyerere.** A character with intentions, never a defendant. **People, not policy**
> **3 · Do not solve the murder.** You cannot prove it and you must not imply you will. **The country's doubt is the subject**
