# THE AUTOPSY — *The Real Black Panther: The Legacy of Mkwawa*

## 1 · The title is the single worst decision in the document

> **"The Real Black Panther"**

**You had a Tanzanian chief who annihilated a German column at Lugalo, held out for four years, shot himself rather than be taken, and whose skull sat on a European shelf as a trophy — and the title asks Marvel for permission.**

Think about what that does to the reader in the first three words:

- It says **Mkwawa is not interesting enough on his own** and needs Hollywood's authority to be legible
- It makes the book **derivative before page one** — a comparison, not a subject
- It is the **exact inverse of your own closing argument**, that African history *is* world history. A title that borrows an American superhero to explain an African king concedes the entire point
- It hands a lawyer a trademark question the Foundation does not want

**The fix:** the book is named after the object. *The Skull of Mkwawa.* Whatever you choose, **the title must be able to stand in a room with Hochschild's without flinching**, and *King Leopold's Ghost* does not mention a superhero.

## 2 · It is a grant application, not a book

Numbered headings. **"3. Why Me?" "4. Why the Morland Scholarship?" "5. Conclusion."** A bulleted list of travel plans.

**This is the format of a funding request, and they are not primarily funders — they are readers.** Look at what they funded: *a walking memoir tracing Bantu migration routes, 7,500 km*. *A journalist-turned-revolutionary in a Zimbabwe haunted by its resurrected dictator*. **Those read like books. Yours reads like a form.**

**The fix:** no numbered sections, no bullets, no headings at all. **One continuous piece of prose, 400–1,000 words**, that behaves like the opening of the book it describes.

## 3 · There is not one good sentence in it — and that is what they are judging

**This is the hardest thing on this page and the most important.** They say the work is judged **purely on literary merit**. The proposal is a thousand words of *your prose*. Here is what it gives them:

> *"an engrossing story that is pertinent to current discussions"* · *"a compelling argument for why African history is world history"* · *"vivid scene-setting, and character-driven storytelling"* · *"bridge the gap between scholarly history and popular biography"* · *"speaks powerfully to our current moment"*

**Every one of those is a phrase about writing rather than a piece of writing.** They could describe any book on any subject. Nothing in this document could only have been written by a Tanzanian who grew up with that name.

And in the middle of that inflated register sit basic breakages — ***"In The Real Black Panther's Comparing Mkwawa to the Black popular Marvel Hero"*** (not a sentence), ***"may not sorely be due to"*** (*solely*), ***"The book being a 95,000-word narrative will be structured"***. **Grand abstraction plus broken grammar is the worst possible texture** — it reads assembled rather than written, which is precisely the impression you cannot afford to give this year.

**The fix, and it is the whole game: give them one paragraph of real prose.** The skull on the shelf. The label. The room. **A judge who reads a great paragraph forgives a lot; a judge who reads nothing but claims has nothing to forgive with.**

## 4 · The book is buried in Part III

**Part I is 1880–1894 biography. Part II is the hunt. Part III is the skull.**

**Part III is the book.** A war trophy on a German shelf, an unobtrusive label, a hundred-year pursuit, a repatriation fight that is still live — **that is a thriller with an object at its centre**, and it is the only part of your outline that no academic has already written. Parts I and II are a competent history that a dozen people could produce.

**The fix: open with the skull and let the life arrive through it.** Same instinct as [[The Bale — Working Sheet|The Bale]] — one object, followed. You already have this instinct; you buried it at position three.

## 5 · The best idea in the document is thrown away in a broken sentence

> *"not sorely be due to military might, but also African science, in the fields of War Strategy as well as other forms of battle such as African magic"*

**African science and African magic as instruments of war** — that is a genuinely original claim and it is the only sentence here nobody else would have written. **It gets nineteen words and a typo**, then never returns.

**The fix: either it is a spine of the book, developed properly, or it is cut.** ⚠️ And if kept, handle it carefully — the Foundation excludes *works of special interest such as religious writings*, so it must be **how the Hehe understood their own war**, not a metaphysical argument. Historical, not devotional.

## 6 · It names nothing

*"identifying key archives and sources"* — **which ones?** *"potentially to Germany"* — **potentially?** *"Tanzanian oral histories"* — **whose?**

Baraka named **Shakahola**. Saio named **7,500 km**. Kwachou named **thirteen**. **Specificity is the cheapest credibility there is and this proposal spends none of it.** The Bundesarchiv. The Museum. Iringa. A living descendant with a name. One archivist who has already replied to your email.

## 7 · "Why Me?" should never appear as a heading

*"My connection to this material is both personal and intellectual."* **That sentence contains no information.**

The pattern in every funded proposal: **the reader concludes you are the only person who could write this, without ever being told.** You cannot assert your way there. One concrete detail — where you first heard the name, from whom, how old you were — does more than the entire section.

## 8 · The last day

You said it yourself. **A borderline-final-day submission is read in the last, worst tranche.** Free to fix: **target 1 September.**

# WHAT THIS MEANS FOR 2026

## ⚠️ Do not resubmit Mkwawa

Three reasons, and the third is the one that matters:

1. **The Foundation says so.** *"We would prefer new submissions and book proposals rather than ones that have been submitted previously."* Permitted, but you would be starting behind
2. **A reader may remember it** — with the Marvel title attached
3. 🔴 **You have already published fiction about it.** *"The Chants Began a Whisper"* — the skull of Chief Mkwawa returning home — **is a story inside your own Kindle book.** A proposal for a Mkwawa book, from a writer with a published Mkwawa story, invites the reading that it is **a work in progress.** That is an explicit exclusion, and you would be handing them the evidence yourself

> **Mkwawa is not dead. It is your second book, written from strength, when you are not asking a stranger for £18,000 to start it.**
