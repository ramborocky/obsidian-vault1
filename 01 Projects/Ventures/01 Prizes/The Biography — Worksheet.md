# 👤 THE BIOGRAPHY — WORKSHEET
### 200–300 words. **Four movements, each with a word budget and a question.**

→ [[Morland 2026 — The Campaign]] · [[Morland — What They Actually Fund]]

> [!note] Why there is no sample bio in this file
> **The biography is one of the five submitted documents.** A sample written by me becomes the sentence pattern you reach for at 21:00 tomorrow, and the Foundation is reading it for a voice. **The scaffold below does more for you than a sample would — it tells you what each paragraph has to accomplish and leaves the sentences to you.**

---

## WHAT THE FOUNDATION ASKED FOR

> *"Please also tell us in 200 – 300 words something about yourself and your background."*

**That is the entire brief, and its vagueness is the test.** Everyone sends a CV. **The bio's actual job is pattern 6 from [[Morland — What They Actually Fund]]:**

> ### **Make the reader conclude you are the only person who could write this book — without ever saying so.**

**It is not a covering letter. It does not sell the book — the proposal does that.** By the time they reach the last line, the proposal should feel like something you were always going to write.

---

## THE FOUR MOVEMENTS

### 1 · WHERE YOU ARE · ~50 words
**Question: where are you, exactly, and what does a day there look like?**

Not "Tanzania." **The town. The lake. The road.** The reader has never been there and will remember one detail if you give them one. → pattern 5, *ferocious geographic specificity*: nobody has ever been funded to write about "Africa."

---

### 2 · THE DOUBLE LIFE · ~70 words
**Question: what do you do between six in the morning and six at night, and what do you do after?**

**You are an accountant who writes.** ACPA, an employer, a ledger — and a story published under your own name. **That is not a weakness to be explained away and it is not a hobby. It is the most distinctive fact in your application**, and it is the reason a scholarship that buys a year of writing time means something concrete in your case rather than something general.

⚠️ **State it. Do not apologise for it, and do not dress it as a struggle.** The facts are heavier than the adjectives.

---

### 3 · WHAT YOU HAVE ACTUALLY WRITTEN · ~80 words
**Question: what exists, where, and under what name?**

***Voices of Azania***, published, on sale, two stories, your name on it. Anything else that is real. **Facts and titles, no adjectives.** Never *compelling*, *vivid*, *evocative*, *powerful* — those are the words that made the 2025 proposal read as assembled. → [[Morland 2025 — Post-Mortem]]

**If a fact is small, give it plainly.** A small true fact beats a large claim every time, and this panel has read eight hundred large claims.

---

### 4 · THE LINE THAT MAKES THE BOOK INEVITABLE · ~60 words
**Question: what is the one thing about your life that means this book is yours?**

**One fact, not an argument.** Where you first heard about the village that was moved, or who told you, or what your family lost, or what you were doing in April 1984 — or that you were not yet born and grew up among people who were.

**Do not write "this is why I am the right person."** Write the fact and stop. **The reader does the concluding, and a conclusion a reader reaches themselves cannot be argued with.**

## 🚫 THE SEVEN TRAPS

| | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| **"I have always loved writing"** | True of every one of the 800 |
| **A CV in prose** | Schools, jobs, dates. They asked about *you*, not your record |
| **Adjectives about your own work** | *Compelling, vivid, deeply moving.* Praise from the author counts for nothing |
| **"Aspiring writer"** | You have a book on sale. **You are not aspiring** |
| **Explaining the book** | That is the proposal's job. Doing it twice wastes both |
| **Asking for the money** | They know why you applied |
| **Apologising for the day job** | It is your best material, not your handicap |

---

## THE THREE TESTS — apply them before you send

**1 · The substitution test.** Take your name out and put in any other Tanzanian writer's. **Is it still true?** If yes, you have written nothing.

**2 · The aloud test.** Read it to someone who has never read your work. **Watch for the moment their attention leaves.** That sentence is the problem.

**3 · The last-line test.** Cover everything but the last sentence. **Does it make you want to read the proposal?**
