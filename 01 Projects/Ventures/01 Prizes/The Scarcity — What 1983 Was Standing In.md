# 🌾 THE SCARCITY — WHAT 1983 WAS STANDING IN
### You said the Act was not all that happened. **You are right, and the missing layer is the book's motive.**

→ [[The Village, The House, The Road — Proposal Sheet]] · [[The Proposal — Drafting Session]] · [[The Sokoine Thread — What You Are Actually Holding]]

> [!important] The one-line version
> **Empty shelves → somebody must be hoarding → search the houses.** Without the scarcity, Part II is a story about tyranny. **With it, it is a story about a broke, hungry state doing something that felt legible at the time** — which is a better book, and it is also the cleanest possible route to rule 1, *no verdict on Nyerere.* **You do not have to defend or prosecute a man if you show what he was standing in.**

## 🔴 THE FIND — Nguvu Kazi, and it closes the architecture

> ### **The Human Resources Deployment Act 1983 — *Nguvu Kazi*.**
> It registered all employees, and **rounded up unemployed men in Dar es Salaam and repatriated them to rural areas.**
>
> **It was defined and driven by the Office of the Prime Minister. Sokoine's office.**

**Read that against Part I.**

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1973–76** | **Operation Vijiji** | The state puts you on a lorry **to** the village |
| **1983** | **Nguvu Kazi** | The state puts you on a lorry **back to** the village |
| **1983** | **Economic Sabotage Act** | The state searches what is in your house |
| **12 Apr 1984** | **Dakawa** | The man whose office ran two of the three dies on a road |

**Ten years apart, the same journey, and by 1983 the same office.** The three parts were already the right three parts. **Nguvu Kazi is the hinge that makes them one motion instead of three episodes** — and it costs nothing, because it is the same year and the same man you were already writing about.

## 📋 THE VERIFIED SPINE OF THE COLLAPSE

**Everything here is sourced. Dates in this table are the ones to build on.**

| When | What | Why it matters to the book |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1977** | The **East African Community collapses** | The regional economy the country was built inside disappears |
| **1978–79** | **The Kagera War.** Tanzania removes Amin — **cost ≈ $500 million**, plus **≈$100m** propping up Uganda's new administration | **A poor country bought a war and a neighbour's government.** This is the bill everything after is paying |
| **1979** | **Floods — ≈$100m.** Then **drought**, at similar cost | |
| **1979** | **The IMF demands** major devaluation, fiscal adjustment, cuts to parastatal deficits. **The government refuses** — it would work against the socialist strategy | The standoff that defines the next six years |
| **Nov 1980** | **TANESCO orders major industrial consumers to shut down for an entire month** | **A whole country's industry switched off for thirty days.** This is an image, not a statistic |
| **25 Mar 1983** | **The Economic Sabotage (Special Provisions) Act** — brought in by Presidential Order, ***retroactive*** to this date. In force twelve months, extendable six | ⚠️ **See the date note below** |
| **1983** | Special tribunals. **No bail. No legal representation. Thousands detained for months** | |
| **1983** | ***Nguvu Kazi*** — the Human Resources Deployment Act | The find above |
| **1984** | The Act is **repealed and replaced** by the Economic and Organised Crimes Control Act | **The emergency law lasted about a year and was quietly turned into permanent law** |
| **1985** | **Nyerere retires** rather than implement IMF conditions | He leaves rather than sign |

> [!warning] ⚠️ A date to check before it goes in the proposal
> [[The Village, The House, The Road — Proposal Sheet|The proposal sheet]] says the Act was **"launched 5 April"** 1983. The legal record says it was brought into force **retroactive to 25 March 1983.**
>
> **Both can be true** — an announcement in April, retroactivity to March. **But the retroactivity is the better fact and you should confirm which is which**, because *a criminal law that reaches backwards in time* is a sentence a judge does not forget. **Verify against the Gazette before using either.**

## 🧺 YOUR THREE MEMORIES — where each one lands

**These came from you, unprompted, and they are the reason you can write this book. Treat them as leads, not yet as facts.**

### 1 · The rags
Imports were choked, foreign exchange had gone, and there was no *mitumba* trade to fall back on. **The secondhand clothing flood arrives only after liberalisation from the mid-1980s.**

> 🔗 **And this is worth noticing: [[The Bale — Working Sheet|The Bale]] is the other end of this same story.** People wore rags in 1983 because nothing came in; the market drowned in dead men's clothes a decade later because everything did. **The earlier subject is not wasted — it is the sequel.**

### 2 · The yellow maize
US and Canadian food aid, and **the colour is the detail that matters.** People knew it was not their maize by looking at it. **A country that had been told it would feed itself queuing for somebody else's grain, in the wrong colour** — that is the Writing Constitution's *objects carry emotion*, handed to you by history.

### 3 · The queues — *foleni*
Sugar, soap, cooking oil, cloth. **The queue is the scene the entire crackdown was addressed to.** Sokoine's logic is only intelligible standing in one: *these goods exist somewhere, and somebody is holding them.*

**Verification, in this order, and all of it is in-country:** *Daily News* and *Uhuru*, 1980–84 · Bank of Tanzania annual reports · National Milling Corporation records · **and the people in the queue, who are alive.**

## ⚖️ HOW IT ENTERS THE BOOK — and the trap

> ## **It is motive and weather. It is not a fourth part.**

**Do not add THE SHOP or THE QUEUE.** Every project the Foundation has funded attaches to **a specific nameable event.** *"Tanzania's crisis decade"* is a **survey**, and surveys do not get funded — [[Morland — What They Actually Fund|the neo-colonialism assessment]] already established this at length.

**Where each piece goes:**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Move 1 · The image** | Unchanged. **The televisions in the gutters.** Do not trade it for a queue — the gutter is stranger |
| **Part I · The Village** | Add nothing. Vijiji stands |
| **Part II · The House** | **This is where the scarcity lives.** Open the part in a queue, not in a statute. Then Nguvu Kazi, then the Act, then the tribunals |
| **Part III · The Road** | Unchanged |
| **The war** | **One sentence, early, and never explained again.** *A poor country bought a war and then a neighbour's government, and everything after 1979 is that invoice.* Then drop it |

⚠️ **The trap this material sets.** The economics is genuinely interesting and you are an accountant — **which is exactly why it could eat the book.** The moment a paragraph is about balance of payments rather than about a person in a queue, you have written [[Morland — What They Actually Fund|the excluded thing]]. **The IMF standoff is one clause of context, not a chapter.**
