## 🇹🇿 THE FINDING — TUZO NYERERE

> **The Mwalimu Nyerere National Award for Creative Writing.** Government-run, Ministry of Education, coordinated by **Taasisi ya Elimu Tanzania**. Established 2022/23.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **1st prize** | **TSh 10,000,000** ($3,778) |
| 2nd | TSh 7,000,000 ($2,645) |
| 3rd | TSh 5,000,000 ($1,889) |
| **Categories** | **Four** — fiction, poetry, children's literature, drama |
| **Language** | **Kiswahili** |
| **Field** | **Tanzanian. National, not global** |
| **Extra** | 1st place manuscripts are **published and distributed to schools and national libraries** |

**Why this outranks everything else on odds.** The Commonwealth put you against **7,806 writers worldwide**. Tuzo Nyerere puts you against **Tanzanian writers working in Kiswahili**. You are a Tanzanian who writes Kiswahili. That is not a marginal edge — it is a different competition.

**And it has four categories**, where the Commonwealth allows one entry a year, full stop. Whether one person may enter more than one category is the first thing to ask.

**In household terms:** first prize is **4.9 months** of your running cost. A Commonwealth regional win is 4.1 months. **The "smaller" prize is the bigger one for you**, and it is the only prize here where a win also puts your book in every school library in the country.

**Unknowns — the 2027 cycle isn't published yet.** Ask directly:
- [ ] When do entries open and close?
- [ ] **May one person enter more than one category?**
- [ ] Entry fee, if any? Word or manuscript length?
- [ ] Is it manuscript-length or short-form?

**Contact: Taasisi ya Elimu Tanzania · Wizara ya Elimu, Sayansi na Teknolojia.** The 2026 ceremony ran in April, so entries likely open late in the preceding year.

## THE ONE THAT NEEDS EXPLAINING — THE CAINE PRIZE

**£10,000, the most prestigious short story prize in African writing. And you cannot enter it.**

> **Entries must be submitted by a publisher.** Not the author. Self-published and unpublished stories are ineligible. The story must have been published by a journal, magazine, anthology or collection **within the previous five years**.

- Length **3,000–10,000 words** · African national or a parent African by birth ✅
- **Submissions 1 December – 6 March**
- A publisher may submit multiple stories but **only one per author**
- One shot per story — no resubmission, ever

**So the Caine is not an entry. It is a consequence.** Sell a story to Lolwe, Isele, The Dark, Nightmare or Adda, and that magazine can submit it. **This is the single strongest argument for [[Paid Markets]] on the whole board** — the $200–480 sale is not the prize, it is the ticket to a £10,000 one you otherwise cannot reach.

### 🔑 Safal Kiswahili Prize — the real numbers

Founded 2014 by Dr Lizzy Attree and Dr Mukoma wa Ngugi, funded by Safal Group through Mabati Rolling Mills and **Alaf Tanzania**. **Unpublished manuscripts. Three categories.**

| Category | 1st | 2nd |
| --- | --: | --: |
| **Riwaya** (novel) | **$5,000** | $2,500 |
| **Ushairi** (poetry) | **$5,000** | $2,500 |
| **Hadithi fupi** (short stories) | **$2,500** | — |
| **Total pool** | **$17,500** | |

**The 2026 round drew 230 manuscripts from seven countries.**

```
5 prizes ÷ 230 entries        = 1 in 46 chance of placing
$17,500 ÷ 230                 = $76 expected value per entry

Commonwealth: 1 in 1,561      = $2.46 expected value per entry
```

> **31 times the expected value. 34 times the odds of placing.** Same effort, same craft, different pool.

**And in 2026, four of the five winners were Tanzanian** — Salim Hussein Ng'azi and Dickson Damas Mtalaze (novel), Ali Hilal Ali and Leonard Barnaba Mtesigwa (poetry). Only the short-story winner was Kenyan.

**Entries ~29 Jan – 31 Mar.** → [kiswahiliprize.org](https://kiswahiliprize.org/)

**Afritondo Short Story Prize** — ~$1,000–1,400, closes ~23 December. **Publishes, so it burns rule 3f.**

**IHRAM · Writing Climate Resilience** — poetry, stories, narratives and essays **from the SADC region specifically**. 2026 round closed 15 May. Worth watching for a 2027 call — the SADC-only field is the attraction.

### Country prizes worth watching

**South African Literary Awards (SALA)** — annual since 2005, multiple categories. Largely for South African writers; check nationality rules before planning around it.

**Namibia (Doek!) · Botswana (Bessie Head) · Malawi (Kalemba) · Lesotho (Ba re e ne re) · Zimbabwe (NAMA)** — all exist, most are **nationality-restricted to their own country**. Confirm eligibility before spending time. As a Tanzanian your regional openings are the pan-African ones plus the Kiswahili ones.

## 📚 KISWAHILI MARKETS — and the honest finding

**There is no Kiswahili equivalent of Nightmare or Clarkesworld.** No per-word paying Kiswahili short fiction market turned up. In English you can sell a 4,000-word story for $200–480; in Kiswahili that market does not appear to exist.

**Kiswahili money is in books, not stories.** Specifically, in **school set books** — a title adopted onto a national curriculum sells for years in tens of thousands of copies, and it is where East African literary writers actually earn. That is a different game from magazine sales: slower, larger, and won through publishers rather than editors.

| Publisher | Where | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mkuki na Nyota** | Dar es Salaam | Founded 1991 by Walter Bgoya. Tanzania's major independent. Kiswahili fiction is central to its mission — **the first door to knock on** |
| **East African Educational Publishers** | Nairobi | One of the largest in Africa. Creative works and school texts in Kiswahili |
| **Longhorn Publishers** | Nairobi | Publicly listed. Heavy in Kenyan set books |
| **Oxford University Press East Africa** | Nairobi | Open manuscript route via *Publish with OUPEA* |

**Institutions — not markets, but gateways:**
- **BAKITA** — Baraza la Kiswahili la Taifa. The national council; standardises and promotes Kiswahili, coordinates other Kiswahili bodies. Worth being known to
- **CHAUKIDU** — Chama cha Ukuzaji wa Kiswahili Duniani. Publishes *Jarida la CHAUKIDU* annually, but it is **academic**, not fiction

> **The structural point: Kiswahili does not pay per story. It pays per book adopted.** And **Tuzo Nyerere's first prize is distribution to schools and national libraries** — which is a door into exactly that market, opened by winning rather than by pitching.
>
> **Unverified:** I have no royalty figures for set-book adoption. Ask Mkuki na Nyota directly what a Kiswahili title earns and how adoption works.
