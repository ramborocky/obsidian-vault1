## THE DRAFT

I am a Tanzanian writer and accountant. I was born in Dar es Salaam in 1977, the year Chama cha Mapinduzi was founded, and at home they called me Chama for it. I write `[when — the hour, and the room]`.

My father had served close to Julius Nyerere, and in 1986 he was posted to London as Tanzania's ambassador. I was schooled there and came home for A-levels at Azania. In 1990 my father took me to visit Mwalimu in a hospital in London. Nyerere asked my name. My father explained the nickname — born the year the party was born — and Mwalimu laughed. There were Christmas cards at our house with his signature on them.

I have published two books, both in English and both offered for sale: *Voices of Azania*, a collection of stories, and `[poetry title]`, a collection of poems. `[A shortlist or placing, if you have one — the prize and the year, nothing else.]`

I am a CPA(T) with an MBA from the Institute of Accountancy Arusha, and for `[X]` years I have worked in public service, currently as a senior officer with the National Health Insurance Fund.

I live in Kigamboni with my wife of twenty years and our six children. In 2024 I ran the Safee Marathon for autism awareness, and in 2025 the half at Kilimanjaro, for childhood cancer.

I was named after a ruling party and raised inside the state's own household. The book I want to write is about what that state did to its villages, and to the man who tried to make it honest.

## WHY IT IS BUILT THIS WAY

**The last two sentences are the whole document.** Everything above them is evidence; those two are the verdict, and they are what makes the proposal inevitable without ever saying *this is why I am the right person*. → pattern 6 in [[Morland — What They Actually Fund]].

> **A man nicknamed after the ruling party, raised in the ambassador's house, who made Nyerere laugh at nine years old — proposing to investigate what that state did to its own villages.**
>
> **Nobody else in eight hundred applications has that sentence.** It converts closeness to power into standing to ask, which is the single hardest thing a book like this has to establish, and you establish it in a line.

**The Nyerere hospital scene is left as action, not as feeling.** He asked, my father answered, he laughed. **No adjective anywhere near it** — the original WASIFU has *macho yakametameta*, and in English that register would read as reverence and cost you the reader. The facts are heavier.

**The accountancy is stated the way Kalimamukwento states her law:** a qualification, an institution, and nothing else. No struggle, no balance, no apology. **It is also two lines shorter than it was** — the diplomas were doing repetition, not work.

**The running is in because it is the only thing in your WASIFU that is purely yours** — not inherited, not credentialled. Both races kept are ones you ran for other people's children. **That says something a paragraph of self-description could not.**

**What was cut, and why:** the closing paragraph of the WASIFU about *uongozi wa haki, utu kamili na haki ya kijamii*. **True, and written for a Nyerere prize.** To this panel it reads as a political position rather than a writer's, and the exclusion on political writing is live.

## 🔴 THREE THINGS TO SETTLE BEFORE YOU SEND

### 1 · `[when — the hour, and the room]` — the most important placeholder on the page
**One concrete detail beats the sentence it replaces.** *I write between four and six, at the kitchen table, before the house is up* tells a judge more about whether this book will exist than every qualification below it. **This is the sentence that says you are already a writer rather than a man who would like to be funded to become one.**

### 2 · The shortlist slot — fill it or delete it cleanly
**Any prize longlist, shortlist or placing, any year, any country.** It outranks the MBA on this page. If there is none, **delete the bracket entirely** — do not write *"I have submitted to…"*. An intention is not a credential.

### 3 · ⚠️ The last line and rule 1
> *"…and to the man who tried to make it honest."*

**It is the best sentence in the bio and it is also a verdict**, which is the one thing the proposal's own rules forbid. *Tried to make it honest* implies the state was dishonest and that Sokoine was the honest one — a reading the book is supposed to leave to the reader.

**And [[The Scarcity — What 1983 Was Standing In|today's research]] gives you a defence and an alternative.** The state was **broke and hungry**, not simply dishonest: a war it could not pay for, a drought, empty shelves, and a Prime Minister who concluded the goods must be somewhere. That is a truer sentence and it takes no verdict.

**Two defensible calls, and it is yours.** Keep it, because it is the line that makes a stranger want the book. Or replace *honest* with the shortage, and lose the best sentence to buy safety. **I would keep it** — it is a verdict on an apparatus, not on a president, and it is the last thing they read.
