# 🏝️ ZANZIBAR 1964 — ASSESSED, AND WHY NOT NOW
### Pitched as: *the Revolution as a genocide against Arabs*, against [[The Village, The House, The Road — Proposal Sheet|the Sokoine book]].

→ [[Morland 2026 — Due Diligence and Rerun]] · [[Morland — What They Actually Fund]] · [[Two Campaigns — The Master Calendar]]

> [!danger] The short answer
> **It is a real subject with real scholarship behind it, there is a genuine gap in the literature, and there are at least three good books inside it.**
>
> **It scores 31/50 — below all four candidates the rerun measured — and you should not switch to it.** The reason is not the subject. It is that *"genocide"* is a verdict in the premise, and the Morland exclusion is written against verdicts.

## THE SUBJECT IS SOUND — this is not a brush-off

**12 January 1964.** A month after independence, the Sultanate falls in about nine hours. What follows is mass killing, rape, expropriation and expulsion, directed at Arabs and South Asians.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Death toll** | Wildly contested — **several hundred to 20,000.** Petterson ≈5,000. Okello's own count: 7,994 Arabs, 1,417 Africans. A government member: 13,000 |
| **Scale** | Glassman: Zanzibar lost roughly **a quarter of its Arab population** by the end of 1964 |
| **The word** | **Serious scholars do use it.** Petterson: *"in parts of Zanzibar, the killing of Arabs was genocide, pure and simple."* A Cambridge chapter treats it as a **denied** genocide. Others prefer ethnic cleansing, massacre, pogrom. **The contestation is itself part of the story** |

**And the literature gap is real.** What exists is an American diplomat's memoir (Petterson), Okello's own out-of-print account (1967), and academic work (Glassman, Burgess). **Nobody has written the trade narrative from inside the country.** That is the same shape of gap the Bale had.

### 🔴 1 · Political exposure · 1 — and this is the one that ends it

**12 January is Mapinduzi Day — a national public holiday.** The Revolution is the founding event of the ruling party's Zanzibar half, and the Union is the most sensitive constitutional question in the country.

**The Morland exclusion:** *"works of special interest such as religious or political writings."*

> **The Sokoine book survives that exclusion because of rule 1 — no verdict on Nyerere.** Its politics can be moved entirely into people and facts.
>
> **This book cannot.** *Genocide* is an adjudication, and it is in the title of the pitch. Strip the verdict and it stops being the book being proposed. **That is a structural difference, not a matter of careful wording.**

And [[The Village, The House, The Road — Proposal Sheet|the proposal sheet]] already records the personal side, which has not changed: **you live there, you are a senior officer in a government agency**, and that was written nine months after a crackdown in which people who spoke were documented as disappearing. **The Sokoine book gestures at a dead Prime Minister. This one puts a verdict on a public holiday.**

### 🔴 2 · Only you · 2 — Gurnah holds this ground

**Abdulrazak Gurnah is Zanzibari.** He left in 1968 as a refugee from this exact aftermath, has written it across six novels — *Memory of Departure*, *Admiring Silence*, *By the Sea*, *Gravel Heart* — and **won the Nobel Prize for it in 2021.**

A mainland Tanzanian proposing Zanzibar's atrocity enters a room where the judges' first mental reference is a living Nobel laureate **who was there.** Pattern 6 asks the proposal to answer *why you* without being asked. **Here the question gets asked out loud, and the answer is thin.**

### 🔴 3 · Deliverable in 12 months · 2

**The surviving community is in Oman, the UK and Dubai.** The protectorate archives are in London. That is a visa, a flight and money, inside a twelve-month scholarship, from Tanzania, with a job — **the exact constraint that forced the Bale to be narrowed to the ban.** Sokoine's witnesses are forty minutes away.

## ✅ THE THREE BOOKS THAT ARE ACTUALLY IN THERE

**Strip the verdict and the subject survives beautifully.** Banking these — they are the reason this note exists rather than a one-line no.

### A · ⭐ THE FOOTAGE
> **Two Italians in a helicopter filmed an African atrocity for a shock documentary, and their reel became the most-seen evidence of it.**

*Africa Addio* (1966) — Jacopetti and Prosperi shot aerial footage over Zanzibar in January 1964. The film was later accused of racism and of staging scenes. **A book about the reel:** what is on it, who shot it, what it was sold as, who has used it since, and what it means that the primary visual record of this was made by two men selling tickets.

**One object, followed the whole way** — the Bale's structure, a named form, and **the politics arrives entirely through the object without the author adjudicating anything.** Best of the three.

### B · JOHN OKELLO
> **A Ugandan bricklayer took an island in a night and was erased from its history within a month.**

Declared himself Field Marshal, broadcast in an apocalyptic register, was sidelined within weeks by Karume, deported, and vanished under Amin. **He wrote his own account in 1967 and it is out of print.** A person, not a thesis — the violence sits inside the story and the book never has to rule on it. **Rules 1 and 2, satisfied by the choice of subject.**

### C · THE FORCED MARRIAGES
Karume's regime compelled girls from Persian and Arab families into marriage with revolutionary officials. **Specific, people-not-policy, badly underwritten.** Also the hardest access and the heaviest ethical load. **Not a first book.**
