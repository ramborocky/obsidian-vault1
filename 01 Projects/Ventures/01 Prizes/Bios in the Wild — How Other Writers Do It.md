# 🔬 BIOS IN THE WILD
### Real writers, including Morland scholars. **What they do, in what order, and what none of them do.**

→ [[The Biography — Worksheet]] · [[Morland — What They Actually Fund]]

---

## FIRST, THE FINDING THAT SAVES YOU TIME

> **The Foundation does not publish applicants' 200–300 word biographies.** They are private. **There is no corpus of winning Morland bios to study, and anyone who tells you otherwise has not looked.**

What *is* public is two different things — and neither is the document you are writing:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **MMF announcements** | One sentence per scholar, about **the book**, not the person. 20–40 words |
| **The writers' own author bios** | Third-person, 50–90 words, written for magazines and book jackets |

**So study the second. The moves transfer even though the length does not** — and the gap between 90 words and your 300 is exactly where your life goes.

---

## 🎯 THE ONE TO STUDY HARDEST

### Mubanga Kalimamukwento — Zambia · **Morland scholar**

**Read hers first, because her shape is your shape.** The facts, in her order:

1. Zambian writer
2. **Her novel, named** — *The Mourning Bird* — and what it is about, in a clause: Zambia's AIDS crisis
3. **Her collection, named** — *Obligations to the Wounded* — and who it centres: Zambian women and girls
4. **LLB, Cavendish University Zambia. LLM, University of Minnesota**
5. **She practised law in Zambia until 2019**

> ### **A lawyer who writes. You are an accountant who writes.**
>
> Look at how the day job appears: **a qualification, a jurisdiction, and a date.** No struggle, no apology, no *balancing my career with my passion*. It is stated the way you would state it on a form, and that is exactly why it lands. **The reader does the arithmetic about what it cost her. She never asks them to.**

→ [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mubanga_Kalimamukwento) · [Laxfield Literary](https://laxfieldliterary.com/our-authors/mubanga-kalimamukwento/)

## THE OTHER TWO

### Yvette Lisa Ndlovu — Zimbabwe · **2024 Morland scholar**

**The move worth stealing is her first six words.** She does not call herself a Zimbabwean writer. She calls herself a Zimbabwean **sarungano** — the Shona word for a storyteller — and lets the English gloss follow.

**A word from her own language, used as her job title, in the opening clause.** It tells a reader where she stands before a single fact arrives.

Then: the university post, the BA, the MFA, the collection named with its publisher, and the prizes listed flat — won one, shortlisted for three, **no adjective anywhere.**

→ [her site](https://www.yvettelisandlovu.com/) · [Simon & Schuster](https://www.simonandschuster.com/authors/Yvette-Lisa-Ndlovu/260212434) · [AALBC](https://aalbc.com/authors/Yvette-Lisa-Ndlovu)

### Rémy Ngamije — Rwandan-born Namibian · **founder of *Doek!***

**Identity by accumulation, then by what he built.** He opens with a stack of nouns — author, editor, publisher, photographer, literary educator, entrepreneur — then the novel with its publisher and two awards, then the thing that actually distinguishes him: **he founded the Doek Arts Trust and Namibia's first literary magazine.**

**Not what he feels about writing. What exists because of him.**

→ [his bio](https://remythequill.com/bio/) · [MMF page](https://milesmorlandfoundation.com/remy-ngamije/) · [Simon & Schuster](https://www.simonandschuster.com/authors/Remy-Ngamije/175687434)

## 🧩 THE SEVEN MOVES, EXTRACTED

**1 · Nationality first, in the first four words.** Zimbabwean. Zambian. Rwandan-born Namibian. **Nobody buries it and nobody says "African."**

**2 · A self-chosen noun.** *Sarungano.* A word from your own language, doing the work of a job title. **You have Kiswahili and nobody else in that pile of eight hundred has your Kiswahili.**

**3 · The day job as a plain fact, with dates.** *Practised law in Zambia until 2019.* **This is the single most transferable line on this page.**

**4 · Titles named, publishers named, prizes named.** *Voices of Azania* goes in. So does Kindle. **A small true fact beats a large claim.**

**5 · Zero adjectives about their own work.** Not one of the three writes *compelling*, *powerful*, *moving*, *vivid*. **The prizes are allowed to be impressive; the sentences are not allowed to say so.**

**6 · No origin story.** Nobody says when they started writing, or that they always loved books, or that stories chose them. **Not one.**

**7 · Institutions and places, not feelings.** Cornell. UMass. Cavendish. Minnesota. Windhoek. **Proper nouns are what a stranger remembers.**

## ⚠️ THE TWO DIFFERENCES YOU MUST HANDLE

**1 · These are 50–90 words. You have 200–300.**

**Do not pad with more credits — you do not have more credits, and listing thin ones makes them look thinner.** The extra 150 words are for **the thing these bios leave out: the life.** The town. The lake. What you do between six and six. The fact that makes the book inevitable. → [[The Biography — Worksheet]] movements 1, 2 and 4.

> **The public author bio is a credential document. The Morland bio is a person document.** Take the *discipline* of these — flat facts, no adjectives, proper nouns — and spend the extra room on what a jacket copy has no space for.

**2 · These are third person. The Foundation asked you a question.**

*"Tell us something about yourself and your background"* is addressed to you, and **first person is the natural answer.** Third person is the convention for jackets, not for applications. **Either can work; choose deliberately and stay in it** — a bio that starts *I* and drifts into *he* is the same fault as the third-person slip already flagged in your story. → [[The Fastest Car in Mazimbu — Cold Read]]

## THE TEST

**Take Kalimamukwento's bio and swap in another Zambian lawyer-writer.** It stops being true at *The Mourning Bird*.

**Take Ndlovu's and swap in another Zimbabwean.** It stops at *sarungano*.

> **Yours must break the same way, and early.** → the substitution test in [[The Biography — Worksheet]].
