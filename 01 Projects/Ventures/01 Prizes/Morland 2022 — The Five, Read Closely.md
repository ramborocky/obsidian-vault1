# 🧪 MORLAND 2022 — THE FIVE, READ CLOSELY
### The year [[Morland — What They Actually Fund]] marked *projects not published.* All five recovered, and they do not say quite what the six patterns say.

→ [[Morland — What They Actually Fund]] · [[Morland 2026 — The Campaign]] · [[The Village, The House, The Road — Proposal Sheet]]

> [!info] **Why this note exists**
> The roll in *What They Actually Fund* runs on **twelve projects across 2023–2025**, because the Foundation stopped publishing descriptions before that. 2022 was reachable after all. **The evidence base is now seventeen projects across four years** — and the extra year is the one that breaks things, which is exactly what a fourth year is for.
>
> **This note does not reopen the subject decision.** That closed on 8 August and the subject is fixed. Everything below is either confirmation of the choice already made or instruction for how to write the proposal.

## 🔧 THE SIX PATTERNS, TESTED AGAINST A YEAR THEY WERE NOT BUILT FROM

**Three hold. Three need correcting.** The corrections are the point of the note. **Numbers below are the canonical ones from [[Morland — What They Actually Fund]]**, not a fresh list.

### ✅ 5 · Ferocious geographic specificity — **holds absolutely. 5 / 5**
Bakau and the Gambian diaspora. Ngoni country. A Zimbabwean village. The Nigerian northeast. Lagos. **Still nobody funded to write about "Africa."** Strongest pattern in the whole base — seventeen for seventeen.

### ✅ 6 · The writer is visibly the only person who could write it — **holds. 5 / 5, and harder than before**
Kejera is Gambian and made his own transcripts of the truth commission. Oduah relocated and got exclusive access to the heartland. Komba is writing southern Tanzanian history as a southern Tanzanian. **The 2022 cohort earns "why you" through labour, not just biography** — two of them did primary research nobody else had done.

### ✅ 3 · A hook you can say in one clause — **holds. 5 / 5. This is the law.**
> *Haunted by a dictator who isn't dead. A woman general in Maji Maji. A girl grows up inside the guerrilla war. A journalist walks into Boko Haram's heartland. The sexual miseducation of two boys.*

Every one survives compression with its strangeness intact — **including Otaiku's, which has nothing else going for it on this list.** Seventeen for seventeen. If a project can't do this, nothing else it has will save it.

---

### ⚠️ 1 · "A dated wound, not a mood" — **4 / 5. First counter-example on record.**

Four of five are nailed to a datable catastrophe: Maji Maji (1905–07), the 2nd Chimurenga (1966–79), the Jammeh years and the TRRC, Boko Haram (2009– ).

**Otaiku's is not.** Male friendship, sexual miseducation, a father and a son. No event, no date, no history. **It is the mood-and-relationship novel the pattern says does not get funded, and it got funded.**

> **Corrected reading:** the dated wound is the *reliable* route, not the *only* route — it does the work of making a book feel necessary, which is why most winners use it. **A book without one has to be carrying its weight somewhere else entirely, at the sentence.** Sixteen of seventeen took the reliable route. Take it.

### ⚠️ 2 · "Half of it is non-fiction, and that half looks winnable" — **overstated.**

2022 ran **1 non-fiction in 5 (20%)**, against 5 in 12 (42%) for 2023–25.

| | Non-fiction | Total | Share |
| --- | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 2023–2025 | 5 | 12 | 42% |
| **2022** | **1** | **5** | **20%** |
| **Four-year base** | **6** | **17** | **35%** |

**Still a thinner field than the fiction queue, and still the better door — but 42% was one favourable stretch, not a standing rate.** Plan on roughly a third, and on the 18-month extension being discretionary rather than expected.

### ⚠️ 4 · "A named formal device" — **weakest pattern in the set. ~2 / 5.**

2023–25 was full of them: AfroSurrealist fable, walking memoir, oral storytelling technique, climate fiction. **2022 has almost none.** Kejera has one real conceit — the *eidolon*, a living man's ghost. Oduah has reportage-memoir. Komba, Muchemwa and Otaiku are, formally, just novels.

> **Corrected reading:** a named device is a *finishing move*, not a gate. **It sharpens a project that already has a subject and a hook; it cannot substitute for either.** Do not go hunting one at the cost of clarity.

## 🎫 THE CREDENTIAL QUESTION — what actually got each of them through the door

This is the axis *What They Actually Fund* does not cover, and it is the one worth having. **Entry requires a previously published excerpt of 2,000–5,000 words, published and offered for sale.** That is a turnstile. After it, the stated criterion is **literary merit alone.**

| Scholar | What they had going in |
| --- | --- |
| **ML Kejera** | *Strange Horizons*, **adda**, Café Irreal, The Outline, Pangyrus, The Nation, **Lolwe** · **Commonwealth Short Story Prize shortlist, 2020** — first Gambian · Caine Prize nominee |
| **Neema Komba** | **Etisalat Prize for Literature, flash fiction — won it in 2014** · *Safe House* (Commonwealth Writers, 2016) · *Payback* anthology · **adda** · Index on Censorship · Stack Awards shortlist 2019 · a poetry collection and a biography |
| **Chido Muchemwa** | Baltimore Review, Bacopa, Canthius, Humber Literary Review, Tincture, Apogee · **Short Story Day Africa shortlist, twice** · 2nd, Humber Emerging Writers 2020 · MFA, Wyoming |
| **Chika Oduah** | NYT, National Geographic, BBC, Al Jazeera, CNN, The Atlantic, Foreign Policy, VICE — **a working foreign correspondent, not a literary CV** |
| **Lanre Otaiku** | **Deborah Rogers Foundation Writers Award, 2021** ("HERESY") — thinnest public record of the five |

### Four things fall out of that table

**1 · Not one of them arrived with a book deal or a major press.** Small magazines, open calls, one journalism career. **The level of the credit is modest; the volume is not.** Nobody bought their way in.

**2 · Two of five came in on a *shortlist*, not a win.** Kejera and Muchemwa. A shortlist is a credential. **This is the single most under-valued fact on the page** — losing well, publicly, twice, is a track record.

**3 · 🎯 adda published two of the five.** Kejera and Komba. **adda is Commonwealth Writers' own magazine**, and it feeds directly into the same ecosystem that runs the story prize.

> [!warning] ⚠️ CORRECTED 2026-08-12 — this said "free to submit" and told you to add it to the calendar. **Both were wrong.**
> **adda does not accept unsolicited submissions.** Pieces are commissioned, or selected through specific calls announced to its mailing list. **You cannot submit to it and it does not belong on a submissions calendar.**
>
> **The route in is the prize itself** — Commonwealth shortlisted stories are published in adda. Which makes [[Reverse-Engineered — What a Winning Morland Application Contained|Kejera's ladder]] tighter than first described: **Commonwealth shortlist 2020 → published in adda → Morland 2022.** The magazine is a *consequence* of the shortlist, not a separate door.
>
> **What to do instead:** join the mailing list for the calls, and treat [[The Year — Submissions Calendar|Brittle Paper]] as the venue that is actually open all year.

**4 · 🔥 Kejera's route is your two campaigns, in your order.** **Commonwealth Short Story Prize shortlist in 2020 → Morland scholarship in 2022.** Same sequence, two years apart, first Gambian at both. Whatever else is uncertain, the ladder [[Two Campaigns — The Master Calendar]] is built on is one a real person has climbed.

> **So: the anthology did not win it for anyone.** Publication is the turnstile — and [[Morland 2026 — The Campaign|*Voices of Azania*]] already cleared it, confirmed in writing. **Stop pricing the CV. Nothing past the turnstile is credential-weighted.**

## 🚨 THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST FOR THE LIVE PROPOSAL

The proposal sheet scores **political exposure at 2 / 5** and treats the Foundation's exclusion of *"works of special interest such as religious or political writings"* as the book's one serious risk. **The 2022 cohort says that risk is real but badly mis-priced.**

Three of these five are about **state violence committed by a named, real government** — and two of those are about governments that still exist:

| | The politics in it | Funded anyway |
| --- | --- | :-: |
| **Kejera** | **Yahya Jammeh by name.** A former head of state, alive, in exile. A sitting truth commission whose testimony is the book's source material | ✅ |
| **Oduah** | The Nigerian government's active war. Boko Haram. **Ongoing, contested, dangerous** | ✅ |
| **Komba** | A colonial administration she calls **illegitimate** | ✅ |

**The exclusion is not a bar on writing about states, presidents, or atrocities. It bars *argument*.**

Kejera wrote a **haunting**, not a verdict on Jammeh. Oduah wrote **her own witness**, not a policy critique of counter-insurgency. Komba wrote a **general**, not a thesis about German colonialism. Every one of them put the politics in the facts and the people, where it cannot be excluded — **which is precisely rules 1–3 of the risk section already written into the proposal sheet.**

> **Confirmation, not a reopening: the fixed subject is well within what this Foundation funds, and the three handling rules are the correct handling.** *No verdict on Nyerere. People, not policy. Do not solve the murder.* They were right when they were written and the cohort proves it.

### 📖 Read *Daughters of Bloody Soil* before writing the proposal

Oduah is **the closest precedent in the entire seventeen-project base** to *The Village, The House, The Road*: non-fiction, a state and an atrocity, the reporter's own footwork, **first person where the reporting is hers**, and ordinary women carrying the history. If one funded project should be studied for how to pitch this book, it is that one — not Komba's.

### One caveat on Komba, corrected

Earlier framing had her as the safe-history model — dead empire, no living party. That still describes her book, **but the cohort makes it the wrong lesson to draw.** Kejera and Oduah show that living politics is fundable; safety came from *stance*, not from the subject being old.

## 🪞 ONE THING THAT CUTS AGAINST THE VAULT'S OWN SCORING

Kejera 2022 — *a woman haunted by the living ghost of a dictator.*
Yvette Ndlovu 2024 — *a fictional Zimbabwe haunted by its resurrected dictator.*

**Near-identical premises, funded two years apart by the same Foundation.** The Bale was dropped partly on a **2 / 5 for field crowding**. That scoring is sound against *published trade books*, which is what it was measuring — but it should not be extended to mean the judges want novelty against their own roll. **They demonstrably do not.**
