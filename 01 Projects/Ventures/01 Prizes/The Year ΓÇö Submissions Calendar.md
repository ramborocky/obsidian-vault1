# 01c · THE YEAR
### Everything worth entering, when it opens — and the rule that stops this becoming a treadmill

→ back to [[00 The Board|The Board]] · sits with [[Prize Portfolio]] and [[Grants and Fellowships]]

---

## READ THIS BEFORE THE CALENDAR

**You are right that craft is the multiplier.** It is the only variable you control. Judges, panels and slush readers are not persuadable; the prose is. Everything on this page is worthless without it.

**But a calendar invites the exact opposite behaviour.** A list of thirty deadlines produces thirty thin submissions, and thirty thin submissions is the surest way to spend a year writing and place nothing. **The entries that win are not the ones that were entered most often.** *The Serpent in the Grove* was one story.

> **The rule for this page: it is a filter, not a to-do list.** Most months you enter nothing. You enter when a *finished* story or essay happens to fit an open call — never the other way round.

**And one correction worth keeping straight.** Grants have the biggest headline numbers on this board — £18,000 dwarfs everything. That is not the same as being the highest-paying track. **A big number multiplied by a small probability is a small number.** The reliable money is still [[Career Leverage|the qualification]] and [[Publishing|the freelance work]]; this page is the lottery you play with hours you were spending on craft anyway.

## ⚠️ THE COLLISION, EVERY TIME

**Anything published anywhere kills that piece for the Commonwealth, permanently (rule 3f).** Essays, anthologies, magazines, KDP — all of it.

**So: one story is the prize story. Everything else is for sale or for entry.** Chantal is protected. Nothing else is. → [[12 The Rules — Source of Truth]]

## THE THREE FORMS, HONESTLY

### Short fiction — **your form. Go deep here.**

Six craft knowledge bases, a nine-axis rubric, a Chair Test, an Atelier, a Craft Atlas. **Nobody entering these calls has more apparatus than you do.** That is the edge and it only exists in one form.

**And it is the only form that also sells for cash**, at $200–480 a story. → [[Paid Markets]]

### Essays — **the real second front, and underrated**

Essay calls are far less competed than fiction, they recur constantly, and **the African Liberty fellowship pays for them monthly and runs entirely online.** Themed calls — holidays, place, memory, family — come round every year.

**Why this actually fits you:** a working accountant in Tanzania writing about money, class, debt and work has a subject almost nobody in the market has, and it needs no invention. **The essay is where your life is the material rather than an obstacle to it.**

**And essays build a byline faster than fiction** — which is the published record grants and agents ask for.

### Poetry — **be careful here**

Poetry pays badly and, more to the point, **you have not built the craft.** Everything in this vault is prose: McKee, Gardner, Barrett, Cleland, Kiteley. There is no poetry base, no reading programme, no instrument.

Free entry is not free — **it costs the hours the fiction and essays need.** Anthology calls are the most tempting item on this page and the least likely to pay.

> **Enter poetry only if you are writing poetry seriously anyway. Otherwise skip every single one of these, all year.**

## HOW TO KEEP THIS CURRENT WITHOUT LIVING ON IT

Specific deadlines rot. These publish new calls monthly, free, and are worth more than any static list:

- [African Writer Weekly](https://africanwriterweekly.substack.com/) — the best of them, monthly roundups
- [Writing Africa](https://www.writingafrica.com/) — monthly opportunities for writers and poets
- [Funds for African Writers](https://www.fundsforafricanwriters.com/) — grants and funded calls
- [Pawners Paper](https://www.pawnerspaper.com/) — annual competition lists

**Twenty minutes a month.** Read the roundup, note anything a *finished* piece fits, ignore the rest. **Do not read them weekly** — that is procrastination wearing a productive face.

## THE OPERATING RULES

1. **Never pay an entry fee** without recomputing the odds. Grants never charge; be suspicious of any that do
2. **One story is the prize story.** Everything else is free to publish
3. **Submit finished work to open calls** — never write to a prompt because a deadline exists
4. **Simultaneous submissions are normal** unless a market forbids them. Withdraw on acceptance
5. **Track it** — piece, where, sent when, response due. Twenty in flight is normal
6. **Depth beats breadth.** One story good enough to win beats twelve good enough to enter
