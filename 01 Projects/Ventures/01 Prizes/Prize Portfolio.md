# 01 · THE PRIZE PORTFOLIO
### Prizes are not a business. Here is the arithmetic, and here is what to do anyway.

→ back to [[00 The Board|The Board]]

---

## WHY THIS IS NOT A BUSINESS

**The Commonwealth pays £15,000 in total** — £5,000 to the overall winner, £2,500 to each of four regional winners — **across 7,806 entries.**

```
£15,000 ÷ 7,806  =  £1.92 expected value per entry
```

Free to enter, so that £1.92 is positive. It is still £1.92.

**And you cannot fix this by entering more prizes**, because the rules forbid the portfolio:

| Rule | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **3d** | One entry per writer per year |
| **3f** | The story must be unpublished — **until 1 May following the cycle** |
| **3g** | A story that has been entered is **never** eligible again |

**Afritondo and Brittle Paper publish.** So a story they take is burnt for the Commonwealth, permanently. These prizes are not a diversified portfolio; they are mutually exclusive doors.

> **The resolution, and it is the only one:** one story is the prize story and everything else is for sale. **Chantal is the prize story.** Nothing else needs protecting. → [[Paid Markets]]

## 🔑 THE FINDING — SAFAL KISWAHILI

> **Up to $5,000, and you are competing in Kiswahili.**

The Commonwealth put you in a pool of **7,806** — odds of about **1 in 1,561** for any prize at all. The Kiswahili literary field is a small fraction of that, and you have something almost none of the Commonwealth entrants have: **it is your language and your country's literature.**

**This is the highest expected value of any prize on this page, by a wide margin, and it is not mentioned anywhere else in this vault.**

Entries open **29 January**. That is after the Commonwealth deadline, so **the two do not collide on time** — and a Kiswahili story cannot be the same story as Chantal, so they do not collide on rule 3d either.

**Action:** find the 2027 guidelines when they publish, confirm the fee and the word count, and decide whether the next story is written in Kiswahili. → [kiswahiliprize.org](https://kiswahiliprize.org/)

## 💰 THE MILES MORLAND SCHOLARSHIP — the biggest number here

**£18,000**, paid monthly across 12 months. Roughly **$23,000 ≈ TSh 61M** *(approximate — GBP rate not fixed here)*. That is not prize money, it is **a year's salary to write a book**, and it dwarfs everything else on this board.

**Requirements:**
- Born in Africa, or both parents born in Africa ✅
- Writing in English ✅
- The project must be a **full-length book, 80,000+ words** — fiction or non-fiction
- **A 2,000–5,000 word excerpt from work already published AND offered for sale**, with evidence
- **Deadline 22 September**

### ✅ RESOLVED — 5 August 2026. The KDP route works

**This section previously carried a blocker: that short story collections might be excluded as the published sample, so only a self-published novel would create eligibility. The MMF FAQ does not support that reading.**

> *"Being published on Kindle is acceptable; it is the only online medium that is."* — [MMF FAQ](https://milesmorlandfoundation.com/faq/)

Kindle is an **explicitly approved** route. Free publication does not qualify — the price tag is the qualification. Poetry, plays and film scripts are excluded as samples; **prose is required, and short fiction is prose.** The exclusion of short story collections governs the **proposal**, not the excerpt.

**So a £0.99 Kindle short story opens the gate in 72 hours.** Still worth one confirming email, but the plan no longer waits on it.

## THE HONEST POSITION

Prizes stay on this board for one reason: **the free ones cost nothing but a submission**, and the payoff isn't money, it's a **credential** — an agent, a publication, a route to a book. Nazir won and went to Granta.

**But budget nothing on them.** Not one shilling of the household's plan should assume a prize.

**Maintenance, not programme.** A submission when a story is finished. The 25 October Commonwealth entry stands because that work is already bought and there is no second attempt.
