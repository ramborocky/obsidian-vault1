## 🔑 THE ONE FINDING BEHIND ALL FOUR

Three weeks of instruments, winners and scholarship research keep returning the same result from unrelated directions:

> ## **Enact it. Never state it.**

| Where it turned up | The evidence |
| --- | --- |
| **[[23 Four Scored — Gurnah, Dite, Serpent, mihogoni-3g\|Gurnah]]** | Puts his position inside a speech the character **declines to make** — *if she had gone in there and told them.* 44/45 |
| **🏆 Nazir** — won from 7,806 | Scores its only **5** on Leakage. Wins on world, loses on architecture |
| **🏆 Lubwama** — Africa 2025 | The only **5 on Position** in the set. Never states it |
| **Pandit**, NW14 | Eight pages of a comic verbal tic, detonated once as literal |
| **Komba** — Morland 2022 | Funded for **a general**, not a thesis on German colonialism |
| **Oduah** — Morland 2022 | Funded for **her own witness**, not a critique of counter-insurgency |
| **The Morland exclusion itself** | *"works of special interest such as religious or political writings"* — **the rule literally pays for enactment and punishes argument** |
| **[[Morland — What They Actually Fund\|Idea 5, assessed harshly]]** | The neo-colonialism essays failed on exactly this. *Stop arguing and start reporting* |

**Both prizes are asking for the same thing, in different forms, and it is the thing the vault's Constitution already says:** *we write scenes, not explanations.*

**All four writers below are extreme cases of it.** None of them works in theory vocabulary. Not one would use the word *post-colonial* on the page.

# 📖 COMMONWEALTH — for *Chantal*

## The fault being fixed, stated as a number

[[15 The Chair Test — Scoring the Hidden Factor|The Chair Test]] puts Chantal at **16 / 25** against a prize band of 21–24. **Nine of the eleven missing points sit in two axes:**

| Axis | Chantal | Band it needs |
| --- | :-: | --- |
| **5 · The World** | **2** | *Six or more items of true surplus, including at least one person with a life the story does not follow* |
| **4 · The Person** | **3** | *Two or more people want something the plot does not need — and one refuses their category* |

**Neither is a rewrite. Neither cuts a word** — which matters, because [[Home]] forbids cutting for tightness again.

## 1 · ⭐ EDWARD P. JONES — the primary model

**Read: *Lost in the City* (1992).** Start with **"The Girl Who Raised Pigeons"** and **"Marie."** Then *All Aunt Hagar's Children* (2006).

**Why him and not a more obvious name:** his single signature technique is the exact repair for the two axes above, and it is **portable, cheap and additive.**

### The move — the biographical overshoot

Mid-scene, a minor character is handed their entire future in a subordinate clause — often including **when and how they die, decades after this story ends** — and then the scene simply resumes as though nothing happened.

The effect on a reader is not sadness. It is **vertigo**: the sudden knowledge that the story is a window cut into a world that was already running and will keep running after the last page. Every person in the frame acquires a life the narrative has no intention of following.

**That is the Chair Test's World band 5 and Person band 4 in one grammatical habit.**

### Why it is the right model *for this panel*

- 🏆 The 2026 winner scored its only **5 on Leakage.** This is the technique that produces leakage on purpose.
- The vault's own [[The X-Factor]] already isolated *surplus* as the winning variable. **Jones is the writer who does it most systematically.**
- He writes one city — Washington DC — with total specificity and never once explains it to an outsider. **That is the standing answer to NW14's *tourist fiction* charge.**

### The exercise

Take the four named people around Djamela — **Morisho, Alicia, the mother, Jean Luc.** The Chair Test note already records the verdict: *the mother is furniture, Jean Luc has no object, Morisho is a function, Alicia is a device.*

**Give each of them one sentence of life the plot does not need.** Not backstory, not motive — a fact that runs parallel and is never picked up again. Morisho already half-has one: he gave her a coin off her fare. **Three more like that is the difference between World 2 and World 4.**

## 2 · BESSIE HEAD — the secondary model

**Read: *The Collector of Treasures and Other Botswana Village Tales* (1977).** The title story first.

**Why:** it is the closest thing in world literature to what *Chantal* is doing, and it is fifty years older. A village. A woman who has been used. **A community that knows exactly what happened and arranges itself around not saying so.** Told backwards from a prison cell, with the other women as chorus, and **it never once argues.**

### The two things to take

**1 · The chorus.** Head's villages have a collective voice that knows everything and acts on nothing. That is Chantal's controlling idea — *inaction is complicity* — **carried by a crowd instead of asserted by a narrator.** It is also the cheapest available route off a **Position score of 3**.

**2 · The way out of the saturated child.** [[21 NW14 — The Editors' Statement and Three Stories|NW14]] found *"the dominant perspective was that of the child"* across a thousand submissions from forty-three countries — **the adolescent narrator is the field's default, not a clever choice.** Head writes the adult world *around* a young woman rather than from inside her head. Djamela stays first person; what she needs is **a second engine underneath the naivety**, and Head's is the community that watches.

> **Do not take her endings.** Head resolves more than this panel wants. Take the chorus and leave the closure.

# 📕 MILES MORLAND — for *The Village, The House, The Road*

## The fault being fixed

The proposal sheet scores **political exposure 2 / 5** and names one serious risk: *a careless proposal reads as a political argument and is excluded before literary merit is considered.*

[[Morland 2022 — The Five, Read Closely|The 2022 cohort]] proved the exclusion bars **argument, not subject** — Kejera named a living ex-president, Oduah wrote an active war, both funded. **What is missing is a method for writing seventeen years of state violence with no verdict in it.** Two writers have solved exactly that.

---

## 3 · ⭐ SVETLANA ALEXIEVICH — the primary model

**Read: *Secondhand Time* (2013) first — then *Voices from Chernobyl*.** Nobel Prize 2015, *"for her polyphonic writings, a monument to suffering and courage in our time."*

**Why she is the answer to your single biggest risk:** Alexievich has written five books entirely about a state's violence against its own people, assembled from hundreds of interviews with ordinary citizens — **and there is no argument anywhere in any of them.** She almost completely disappears. The voices carry it. **The position is unmistakable and never once stated.**

> **That is the Gurnah conditional, run at eighty-five thousand words.** A book that is undeniably *about* the Soviet project and contains not one sentence adjudicating it.

### What transfers directly

| Her method | Your book |
| --- | --- |
| Hundreds of ordinary witnesses, decades after the event | **Operation Vijiji survivors are alive.** People who threw televisions into the gutters of Oysterbay in 1983 are alive. **Forty minutes away** |
| No verdict on the regime — only what happened to people | **Rule 1: no verdict on Nyerere** |
| The state appears only through what it did to a kitchen | **Rule 2: people, not policy.** *A policy has no scenes; a person does* |
| Grief is never named; it arrives in the objects the speaker mentions | The Writing Constitution, unchanged |

**The practical consequence: start interviewing now, before the proposal is written.** Alexievich's books are unfakeable because the voices are real, and **the Foundation's shortlist asks for a 3,000–4,000 word sample chapter fifteen days after the call.** A chapter built from two real interviews cannot be matched by a chapter built from reading.

---

## 4 · PHILIPPE SANDS — the structural model

**Read: *East West Street* (2016).**

**Why:** it is the shape you have already proposed, executed at the highest level. Sands braids **his own family's search** with the two lawyers who invented *genocide* and *crimes against humanity* — all of it converging on **one city and one trial.** First person where the reporting is his; archive where it is not.

Your spine is the same architecture: *a death, opened up, that turns out to contain seventeen years.* **Sands is the proof that a personal investigative thread can carry a continental history without the book becoming either memoir or lecture.**

**Take specifically:** how he moves between the present-tense reporting trip and the historical panel **without a seam**, and how he handles the moment the documents run out — he says so, on the page, and does not fill the gap with inference. **That is rule 3: do not solve the murder.**

> **Third, if you read one more:** **Katherine Boo, *Behind the Beautiful Forevers*.** Three years' reporting in a Mumbai settlement, and she removes herself entirely — plus an author's note at the back that shows every bit of the working. **Her book contains a death that is never resolved and she refuses to resolve it.** Read the author's note even if you read nothing else; it is the best short statement of this method in English.
