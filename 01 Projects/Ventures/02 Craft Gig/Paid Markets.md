# 02 · WHERE CRAFT BECOMES AN INVOICE
### The surprise of this whole exercise: **selling stories pays better than prizes and better than KDP.**

→ back to [[00 The Board|The Board]]

---

## THE ARITHMETIC THAT CHANGES THE PICTURE

Professional fiction markets pay **per word, on acceptance.** Not a lottery. Not a royalty. An invoice.

| Market | Rate | A 4,000-word story pays | In TZS |
| --- | --- | --: | --: |
| **Clarkesworld** | $0.12/word | **$480** | **1,270,560** |
| **Nightmare** | $0.08/word | **$320** | **847,040** |
| **The Dark** | $0.05/word | **$200** | **529,400** |
| African literary magazines *(Lolwe, Isele, Adda)* | "a modest token" | ~$50–150 | ~130,000–400,000 |

Now put those against the rest of the board:

```
One sale to Nightmare          $320   ← more than the KDP book's ENTIRE first year
KDP anthology, year one        $280
One Commonwealth entry         £1.92  expected value
```

> **A single 4,000-word horror story sold to one magazine out-earns the whole nine-story anthology's first year.**
>
> And it does it **eighteen months sooner**, with no cover to buy, no tax interview, and no 60-day payment lag.

---

## AND IT COMPOUNDS WITH EVERYTHING ELSE

**These are horror and dark-fiction markets** — exactly the genre already chosen for the anthology in [[KDP Business Plan#PART 8 · THE GENRE, AND HOW TO SHELVE IT|Part 8]]. That was decided for other reasons. It turns out to be the reason this track works at all.

So one story does four jobs:

1. **It gets paid** — $200–480
2. **It becomes a published credit** — which is what agents, editors and grant panels actually look at
3. **It goes in the anthology later** — most of these markets buy first rights only, and rights revert. **Check each contract**
4. **It may satisfy Miles Morland's "published and offered for sale"** — the open question in [[Prize Portfolio]]

**Nothing else on this board does four jobs at once.**

---

## THE HONEST PART — the odds

These are the best short fiction markets in the world and they are savagely competitive. Clarkesworld receives well over a thousand submissions a month and accepts a handful. **Realistic acceptance is around 0.5–1%.**

**But compare properly:**

```
Clarkesworld       ~1 in 100–200      and it PAYS when it lands
Commonwealth        1 in 1,561        for any prize at all
```

**Ten to fifteen times better odds, and the payout is cash rather than a chance at cash.** Also: rejection costs nothing but the submission, there is no annual limit, and you may have twenty stories in flight at once. None of that is true of prizes.

---

## THE LADDER — start at the bottom, not the top

Do not open with Clarkesworld. Build a credit history.

| Step | Where | Why |
| --: | --- | --- |
| **1** | African literary magazines — **Lolwe, Isele, Adda** | Your material is native to them. Payment is small, but a credit is a credit. Adda is the Commonwealth Foundation's own magazine |
| **2** | **The Dark** — $0.05/word | Genuinely reachable, pays real money, exactly your register: quiet, literary, dark |
| **3** | **Nightmare** — $0.08/word | A professional-rate credit. This one changes how your submissions get read |
| **4** | **Clarkesworld** — $0.12/word | Aim here once 2 and 3 have landed |

**Submit up and down the ladder simultaneously.** A story rejected at Nightmare goes to The Dark the same day.

---

## THE RULES OF THE GIG

**1 · One story is the prize story. Everything else is for sale.** Publication burns Commonwealth eligibility permanently (rule 3f). Chantal is protected; nothing else is. → [[Prize Portfolio]]

**2 · Never pay a reading fee.** Professional markets pay you. Money flows toward the writer.

**3 · Check rights on every acceptance.** You want **first rights** with reversion, so the story can go in the anthology later. If a contract takes exclusive rights forever, that story is out of the KDP book.

**4 · Track submissions properly.** Which story, where, sent when, response due. Twenty in flight is normal and unmanageable from memory.

**5 · Read the magazine before submitting to it.** This is the single most common reason for rejection, and it doubles as the Atelier's Block A.

---

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE ATELIER

**The Atelier is currently producing disposable Block D drills that get thrown away.** The [[KDP Business Plan|KDP plan]] already redirects them into `Seeds/`.

**This changes the target.** A drill is not a seed for an anthology two years away. **A drill is a draft of a story that can be sold in six months** — and the anthology is what you assemble afterwards from stories that have already been paid for and already carry credits.

> **That reordering is the most valuable thing on this page.** Same work, same stories, same genre. Paid eighteen months earlier, and each one arrives at the anthology having already been vouched for by an editor.
