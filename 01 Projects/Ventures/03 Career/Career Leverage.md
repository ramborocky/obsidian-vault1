# 03 · CAREER LEVERAGE
### You are employed. That is an asset, and it is currently doing nothing for you.

→ back to [[00 The Board|The Board]]

## THE POSITION

**Employed, carrying debt.** That combination is better than it feels — a salary is the most reliable thing on this entire board, and it is the only track here that already works. The problem is not that you lack income. **The problem is that the income is fixed and the debt is compounding.**

Two levers, and only two: **raise the income** or **become harder to replace.** ACPA does both.

---

## 🎓 ACPA — THE ONLY REAL LEVERAGE HERE

**A professional accountancy registration is a legal gate, not a market.** You do not have to beat competitors or persuade customers. You have to satisfy a board. That makes it the most predictable investment available to you and the one with the highest ceiling.

### What it pays

Accountant salaries in Tanzania run roughly **TSh 462,000 – 3,957,000/month**, junior roles clustering **411,000 – 1,624,000**. Registered and qualified sits in the upper half; Dar pays materially more than Arusha.

```
TSh 1,200,000/mo  =  $453
TSh 2,500,000/mo  =  $944
TSh 4,000,000/mo  =  $1,511
```

*Sources disagree — some conflate monthly with annual. Treat as indicative.*

### What it needs

1. Registered **Graduate Accountant** status
2. **Three years** of logged practical experience
3. **Three logbooks**, one per year
4. **Two ACPA/FCPA referees**
5. Application via **MEMS**

## THE POWER QUESTION — asked for, answered straight

You wanted the *48 Laws of Power* / Machiavelli treatment. Here it is, crudely.

**Most of that genre is entertainment.** It is written to be read, not applied, and its case studies are courts and cartels, not a Tanzanian firm where you will see the same twenty people for a decade. In a small professional market, **reputation is the only durable asset**, and reputation is destroyed by exactly the manoeuvres those books recommend.

**But the underlying idea is sound, and it is one idea:**

> **Power is scarcity. Be the person the work cannot route around.**

That is genuinely Machiavellian, and it is what ACPA does — not because it makes you clever, but because it makes you **legally necessary**. An unregistered accountant is substitutable. A registered one signs things nobody else in the room can sign.

### What actually moves a salaried person

| Works | Doesn't |
| --- | --- |
| **A scarce credential** — legal gates beat charm | Manoeuvring against colleagues in a small market where everyone knows everyone |
| **Owning one process end to end** so the firm feels its absence | Being generally hard-working and hoping it's noticed |
| **Written evidence** — a record of what you delivered, kept by you, not by HR | Assuming your manager remembers |
| **A credible outside option** — the only thing that gives a raise request weight | Asking for more because costs went up |
| **Being the one who writes clearly** — rare in accounting, and it's how people above you learn your name | Waiting for the annual review |

**That last row is not filler.** You write well. Almost nobody in a finance department does. The person who writes the board memo is the person the board knows. That is real leverage, available immediately, costing nothing.

## THE RAISE — how it actually happens

A raise is not granted for need. It is granted when replacing you is more expensive than paying you.

1. **Build the record now**, in writing: what you own, what you delivered, what it saved
2. **Get the credential moving** — registration in progress is itself an argument
3. **Establish the outside option** — one live conversation elsewhere, or a freelance income, changes how you negotiate more than any script
4. **Ask once, with a number**, tied to the credential and the record, not to the debt

**Never disclose the debt at work.** It converts a negotiation about value into a request for charity, and it prices you *down* permanently.
