# 05 · DEBT FIRST, THEN EVERYTHING ELSE
### You asked about Bitcoin. The answer is arithmetic, and it starts with the loans.

→ back to [[00 The Board|The Board]]

---

## THE NUMBER THAT SETTLES IT

The Bank of Tanzania caps digital loans and microloans at **4% per month.** Commercial bank lending averages around **16% a year.** Non-bank finance companies sit between, and often near the cap.

```
4% per month  =  48% a year simple
              =  ~60% a year compounding
```

**Paying down a loan at that rate is a guaranteed, risk-free return of 48–60% a year.**

Not projected. Not expected. **Guaranteed** — the moment the balance falls, the interest stops, and no market, government or counterparty can take it back.

> **There is nothing available to you that guarantees 48%.** Not Bitcoin, not property, not a business, not a prize. Nothing.
>
> **So every shilling put into any investment before those loans are cleared is mathematically the worse trade.** Not "risky" — *worse*, arithmetically, against a certain alternative. That holds whatever Bitcoin does next, because the comparison is certainty against uncertainty and certainty wins at 48%.

## ⚡ FIRST ACTION — you don't yet know your own numbers

**Everything above assumes rates I inferred rather than read.** Get the actual figures for both loans:

- [ ] **Interest rate** — monthly or annual, and which
- [ ] **Outstanding balance**
- [ ] **Monthly instalment and remaining term**
- [ ] **Any early-settlement penalty**

Phone both lenders, or read the agreements. **One afternoon.** Until this is done, the whole folder is estimate.

**Then rank them by rate, highest first.**

---

## HOW TO CLEAR THEM

**The mathematically correct method — avalanche.** Every spare shilling to the **highest-rate** loan, minimum payments on the other. Fastest, cheapest, provably.

**The method people actually finish — snowball.** Smallest balance first, for the momentum of closing one. It costs more in interest but has a better completion rate, and **a plan you abandon returns 0%.**

**Pick one and don't switch.** If the two loans are close in size, use avalanche.

### The move most people miss

> **Refinance the expensive debt with cheaper debt.**

If one loan is near 4%/month (~48%/yr) and a **salary-backed loan through your employer or a commercial bank** is available at 16–20%/yr, moving the balance saves **~28–32 percentage points a year on that money** — with no extra income, no new work, and no risk. In Tanzania, employer-facilitated and salary-deducted loans are routinely far cheaper than microfinance.

**Being employed is what makes this possible.** It is the most valuable thing on this board that you can act on this week.

- [ ] Ask HR or your bank what a salary-backed consolidation loan would cost
- [ ] Compare its rate against each existing loan's real rate
- [ ] Refinance only if the new rate is genuinely lower **after fees** — check for the trap where a lower monthly payment hides a longer term and more total interest

## 🪙 THE BITCOIN QUESTION — straight, not a lecture

You asked. Here are the facts, and then the comparison. **The decision is yours; I don't give investment advice and I'm not qualified to.**

### What's true in Tanzania

- **Not legal tender**, and there is no specific law regulating crypto trading — it sits in a **legal grey zone**
- The Bank of Tanzania **warned against it in 2019**, and in 2026 is **finalising a regulatory framework** for crypto, stablecoins and virtual assets. **The rules are about to change and nobody knows into what**
- A **3% withholding tax on digital asset transactions** was introduced in the Finance Act 2024 — you start each transaction 3% down
- A stablecoin sandbox was approved in May 2026, so the direction is regulate-and-tax, not ban

### What's true about the asset

Bitcoin has had **multiple drawdowns exceeding 70%** — 2011, 2014, 2018, 2022. Anyone holding through those needed either years of patience or money they did not need. **It is not a quick-buck instrument**; the people who made money in it held for years through losses that would have been catastrophic if the money was owed to someone else.

### The comparison, which is the whole point

| | Your loan | Bitcoin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Return | **48–60%/yr, guaranteed** | Unknown. Has been −70%+ | 
| Certainty | Absolute | None |
| Costs | None to pay it down | 3% withholding per transaction, spread, fees |
| Legal position | Settled | Grey zone, rules being rewritten |
| Time needed | Works instantly | Years |

**Paying the loan wins on every row.**

> **The honest version:** "quick buck" and "borrowed money at 48%" are the two ingredients of every story that ends badly. The debt is a guaranteed loss running against you every month. Stopping a certain loss is the highest-return move available, and it is available today.

### When the question becomes live

**When the loans are gone.** Then you have surplus that is genuinely yours, and it becomes a real question worth researching properly — allocation, horizon, how much you can lose without it mattering. **Ask me then and I'll lay out the landscape.** It's a bad question now only because the answer is dominated by something better.

## THE ORDER

1. **Get the real rates** — one afternoon
2. **Refinance the worst loan** if a salary-backed option is cheaper
3. **Avalanche the rest** — every spare shilling to the highest rate
4. **Build one month of expenses in cash** before anything else. Without it, the next emergency becomes a third loan at 4% a month, and that is how people go round again
5. **Then** ask the investing question
