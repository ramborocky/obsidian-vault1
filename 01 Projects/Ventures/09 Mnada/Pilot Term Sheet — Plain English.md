# THE PILOT DEAL, IN PLAIN WORDS

→ [[MNADA — Due Diligence and Gap Analysis]] · [[Pilot Term Sheet]] (the formal version) · [[00 The Board]]

> [!info] What this is
> The same deal as [[Pilot Term Sheet]], written so you can read it without a lawyer next to you. **Where the two disagree, this one is right** — it carries the two corrections the due diligence found. Rebuild the formal version from this, not the other way round.
>
> Nothing here is a credit offer or investment advice. Numbers are illustrations until a bank, a feedlot and an insurer put their own in.

---

## THE WHOLE DEAL IN SIX LINES

1. A feedlot wants to buy 200 cattle, fatten them for 45 days, and sell them.
2. It does not have the money. A bank does.
3. The bank lends **TZS 118m** — 80% of what the cattle are worth today.
4. The cattle are the security. **You** count them, photograph them, and tell the bank weekly whether they are still there.
5. On day 45 the cattle are sold. The money goes **straight to the bank first**, not to the feedlot.
6. The bank gets **TZS 120.3m** back. The feedlot keeps whatever is left. **You earn nothing on this one** — that is on purpose.

---

## WHO IS WHO

| Who | What they do | What they risk |
|---|---|---|
| **The bank** | Puts up TZS 118m | Losing it |
| **The feedlot** | Owns and feeds the cattle, owes the money | The cattle, plus a personal guarantee |
| **You (MNADA)** | Count, photograph, value, report, and run the settlement | Your name. Nothing else on this deal |
| **The custodian** | Physically holds and feeds the cattle *(the feedlot itself, for the pilot)* | Its licence |
| **The insurer** | Pays if cattle die | The mortality claim |

**Why you earn nothing:** if you cannot afford to prove the idea at your own cost, you have no business asking a bank to prove it at theirs. Your fee is **1.20%** on every deal after this one.

## THE ONE NUMBER THAT MATTERS

Not the loan. Not the advance rate. **The price the cattle must fetch for the bank to get every shilling back.**

When the cattle are sold, three people are paid before the bank sees anything:

| Order | Who | How much |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Government — cess and levies | 1.5% of the sale |
| 2 | The custodian — feed and care | TZS 2,200 per head per day |
| 3 | **The bank** | 120,327,671 |
| 4 | You | *waived on this deal* |
| 5 | The feedlot | whatever is left |

The custodian is paid before the bank because **an unpaid custodian does not open the gate**, and a fight over that destroys more value than the ranking costs.

So the sale must raise:

```
Day 45:  cess + 45 days of feed + the bank's 120.3m
      =  TZS 142,261,595   →   TZS 711,000 per head
```

**The cattle enter at 742,000. The line is 711,000. That is 4.2% of room on day one** — before any weight is gained.

## CORRECTION 1 — THE LINE MOVES EVERY DAY

> The formal term sheet has one fixed trigger at 108%. **That is wrong, and a credit officer will find it in ten minutes.**

Feed costs and interest pile up daily. Every day that passes, the sale has to raise more just to stand still:

| Day | Feed + interest so far | Sale must raise | Which means cover of |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | ~0.5m | ~TZS 120.3m | **~102%** |
| 22 | ~10.4m | ~TZS 131.8m | **~112%** |
| 45 | ~22.1m | **TZS 142.3m** | **~121%** |

**A single 108% trigger is safe on day 1 and already underwater on day 45.**

So the deal uses a moving line instead:

| If cover is | Then |
|---|---|
| **110% or more** | Fine. Keep counting. |
| **102% to 110%** | Margin call. The feedlot adds cash or cattle within 5 working days. |
| **Below 102%** | Sell the lot now. |

**Cover** = what the cattle are worth today ÷ what the sale must raise today.
**100% means the bank breaks even.** Below that, it loses money.

*The app now shows this number on its front screen, and it recalculates it every day. That was the point of building it.*

---

## CORRECTION 2 — PRICES CAN FALL IN A STEP, NOT A SLOPE

> The formal term sheet says the lot can fall 21% and the bank is still fine. **That is only true if the fall is gradual.**

It is not always gradual. Sesame — a crop with a public price and a national auction — went from **TZS 3,144/kg to 2,329/kg in a single season. A 26% drop.**

Apply that to these cattle:

```
200 head at 549,080 (down 26%)      TZS 109,816,000
less cess                           −   1,647,240
less 45 days of feed                −  19,800,000
Available to the bank                  88,368,760
Bank is owed                          120,327,671
SHORTFALL                           TZS 31,958,911   =  27% of the loan
```

The margin call is supposed to catch this long before day 45. **It will — if the fall is gradual.** Cattle prices in Tanzania move on rainfall, disease outbreaks and festival demand. Those move in steps.

**Say this to the bank yourself.** A lender who discovers it alone stops believing everything else in the document. A lender who is told it up front prices it and moves on. The formal term sheet already does exactly this with the legal risk in §14, and that is the most credible thing in it.

---

## CORRECTION 3 — DROP THE "RECEIPT"

The old plan invented a **Livestock Deposit Receipt** — a new kind of ownership document for animals. Tanzania's warehouse receipt law is written for grain sitting in a licensed warehouse. **A live animal in a pen is not grain in a warehouse**, and stretching that law to cover one needs Parliament, not an argument.

**You do not need it.** What the bank actually needs is four things, and the term sheet already builds all four:

1. A registered legal claim over these specific, identified animals.
2. Control of the sale money, so it cannot go anywhere else.
3. A custodian who has signed to say it is holding them for the bank.
4. The insurance policy assigned to the bank.

Kenya's movable-property registry took **34,638 livestock registrations** in one year. Ethiopia's central bank lists cattle, camels, sheep and goats as eligible collateral. Zimbabwe, Namibia and South Africa all lend against cattle. **None of them invented a receipt.**

> **The question for your lawyer is one sentence:** *"Can a security interest over individually identified livestock be created and registered in Tanzania today — and if so, where?"*
>
> **Yes** → this deal can be signed this year.
> **No** → everything waits on new legislation, and that is 5–7 years.
>
> This is the cheapest and most valuable question in the whole folder. Ask it first.

## BEFORE ANY MONEY MOVES

The bank pays out only when all seven are true:

1. The custodian's holding-ground licence is current, and both the bank and you have walked the site.
2. All 200 cattle are tagged, photographed, located and counted — **by you, with the bank watching**.
3. Mortality insurance is live, premium paid, bank named as the one who gets paid out.
4. The three agreements are signed: collateral management, custody, and the settlement instruction.
5. The escrow account is open at the bank, in MNADA's name, marked as a trust account.
6. A vet certifies the lot is free of notifiable disease and the district is not under movement restriction.
7. **Your lawyer has answered the question in Correction 3 in writing.**

*The old list had an eighth: a signed MoU with the Ministry. Drop it. It is a 12-month wait for something this deal does not need.*

## WHAT YOU DO EVERY WEEK

| What | How often |
|---|---|
| Walk the pen, touch every tag, mark present / missing / dead | **Weekly** |
| Weigh at least 20 head | Day 1, day 22, day 45 |
| Re-value the lot and report cover to the bank | Weekly |
| Report any death | Within 24 hours |
| Let the bank turn up unannounced | Whenever it likes |

**The app does this walk and produces the file.** One export, one number, one chain of hashes the bank can check itself.

---

## WHEN THE DEAL IS IN TROUBLE

Any one of these and the bank can call the loan:

- More than **1%** of the cattle unaccounted for, unexplained for 48 hours *(on 200 head, that is 2 animals)*
- Deaths above the insured limit, or the insurer refusing to pay
- The custodian losing its licence
- Any animal leaving the holding ground without the bank's consent
- A margin call not fixed within 5 working days
- Not paying on day 45

---

## HOW YOU KNOW IT WORKED

All five, or it is a data point rather than a proof:

| | Test | Why it matters |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Every tag accounted for on day 45 | Your register is real |
| 2 | Deaths within 2% | Custody is good enough |
| 3 | Repaid on or before day 45 | The loan works |
| 4 | Money moved through escrow with nobody intervening | The rail works |
| 5 | **Sale price within 5% of your valuation** | **Your number can be trusted** |

**Number 5 is the one that changes the bank's business.** The first four prove one loan got repaid. Number 5 proves the bank can value cattle it has never seen — and that is the difference between a favour and an asset class.

---

## IF IT FAILS

You pay the bank's out-of-pocket costs, and you publish the full failure analysis to them — counts, logs, everything. **A quiet failure teaches nobody anything and poisons the second attempt.**

## THE HONEST LIST — WHAT IS STILL A GUESS

Do not let these reach a bank as though they were facts:

| Number used here | Where it must come from |
|---|---|
| TZS 2,200/head/day feed | A written quote from the actual custodian |
| TZS 900,000/head expected sale | A named buyer's forward indication |
| TZS 742,000/head entry value | Your own index — the bank will test it against real auction prints |
| 16% interest | The bank's own pricing |
| Insurance terms and premium | UAP, Howden or ACRE |

**Five numbers, five phone calls.** Until they are made, this is a well-built model and not a deal.
