## ⭐ Why this arrived at exactly the right moment

**All three are about punctuation carrying meaning that the words refuse to carry.** That is Chantal's central formal problem, and until now the vault had only rubrics on it.

### 1 · Harris is a direct defence of Chantal's unquoted dialogue

[[The NAPLAN Floor — Ten Mechanical Criteria|The NAPLAN floor check]] scored Chantal **3/5 on punctuation**, because the guide *"strongly emphasises punctuation that is conventionally correct, even at the higher score levels."* I said overrule it. **This essay is the argument I did not have.**

Alice Notley's *The Descent of Alette* puts quotation marks in all the wrong places, on purpose, for four books. Readers complained enough that she added an explanatory note: the marks exist to **make the reader slow down and articulate the phrases at the pace and stresses she intends.**

And the lineage Harris lists is not marginal: **Dickinson's dashes · Bishop's parentheses · Ashbery's commas · Bidart's caps lock · Kapil's colons.**

Susan Howe on Dickinson — and this is the sentence to keep:

> Dickinson abolished conventional punctuation to *"subtract arbitrary authority."*

**Chantal removes quotation marks from a story about a girl whose account of herself is constantly being overruled by adults, police and a man with a car.** Removing the marks that assign and bound speech is not a lapse. It is the same move Notley makes against a tyrant who *"owns form."*

> [!success] What to do with this
> Nothing to the prose. **This is ammunition, not an instruction.** If a cold reader or an editor queries the unquoted dialogue, the answer is now a tradition rather than a shrug — and knowing the answer changes how confidently the choice survives revision.

### 2 · Armstrong is Djamela's exact rhetorical move

Armstrong's thesis: *lol* is **a way of asking for something — attention, reassurance, care — while pretending not to ask for anything.** She calls her own texts *near-explicit desperation to connect*, and eventually, *a bit manipulative.*

Now read the two most protected lines in the vault:

> *I am not asking to be forgiven for that. I am asking for it to be counted correctly.* — Chantal
> *I am not asking anybody to make that all right. I am saying it is what the job is.* — Beat of My Hurt

**Both narrators disclaim the request they are making.** [[13 The Worked Examples — Concepts Against Stories|The worked examples note]] already flagged this as *"either a signature or a tic"* and said it was worth deciding which. **Armstrong names the device and shows it is a real one people use in life** — which pushes it toward signature. It is [[Point of View — The Reluctant I|Kiteley's reluctant I]] with a mechanism attached.

### 3 · Millner reframes what a white space is

> *The line break is not the absence of notation but rather the notation of a specific, echoing absence.*

Chantal's most praised move is *I am not going to describe the hotel* — a withheld scene, [[Atlas — The Withheld Fact|seven for seven]] across the board. Millner's formulation says the gap is **notated**, not empty: Levertov measures a line break at roughly **half a comma**; Baker likens the pause to a quarter rest, *a place where you tap your toe without speaking.*

**The operational point for prose:** the paragraph break before and after a withheld thing is doing measurable work. In Chantal the refusal sits in its own one-line paragraph, with white space on both sides. **That is the notation. It was done by instinct, and it is right.**

## The one line worth stealing outright

Allen Grossman, quoted by Millner, on the line break:

> *a piece of the world broken off from the world in the interest of the perception of the world*

That is the best available description of what a short story is, and it belongs beside [[The Isolation Principle — One Emotion, One Incident|Barrett's unities]] — which argue the same thing from the opposite end, and much less beautifully.
