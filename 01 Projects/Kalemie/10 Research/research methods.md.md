# MCKEE — RESEARCH METHODS

## The Three Methods

> Generally, a story needs all three: **memory, imagination, and fact.**

---

## 1. MEMORY

> Lean back from your desk and ask, "What do I know from personal experience that touches on my characters' lives?"

### How to Use Memory

**Step 1:** Identify the emotion or experience in your story
**Step 2:** Search your own life for a parallel
**Step 3:** Relive the memory
**Step 4:** Write it down in vivid detail
**Step 5:** Apply it to your character

**Example:**
> You're writing about fear. Slowly, memory takes you back to the day your mother locked you in a closet. Bring back those long, fright-filled hours when the dark smothered you. Write it down. Now with the bile of fear in your belly, write an honest, one-of-a-kind scene.

### For Kalemie
- What do I know about loss?
- What do I know about watching someone leave?
- What do I know about feeling powerless?
- What do I know about the smell of a lake?
- What do I know about rain on a tin roof?

---

## 2. IMAGINATION

> Lean back and ask, "What would it be like to live my character's life hour by hour, day by day?"

### How to Use Imagination

**Step 1:** Sketch how your characters shop, make love, pray
**Step 2:** Draw yourself into the imagined world
**Step 3:** Let it feel like déjà vu
**Step 4:** Write down the scenes you envision

### For Kalemie
- What is Daudi's morning routine?
- What does he eat for breakfast?
- What does he see when he walks to school?
- How does he feel when he finds a new piece of debris?
- What does Djamela's room look like?
- What does the police station smell like?

---

## 3. FACT

> You're blocked because you have nothing to say. Your talent didn't abandon you. You can't kill your talent, but you can starve it into a coma through ignorance. Do research. Feed your talent.

### How to Use Fact

**Step 1:** Identify what you don't know
**Step 2:** Go to the library, the internet, interview people
**Step 3:** Take notes
**Step 4:** Let the facts inspire new ideas

### For Kalemie

**Geography:**
- Lake Tanganyika: depth, width, history
- Kalemie: population, economy, neighborhoods
- The road between the lake and the mines

**Politics:**
- DRC: colonial history, war, corruption
- Belgian Congo: Leopold, exploitation
- Current government structure

**Social Structure:**
- Class divisions in Kalemie
- Education system
- Police system

**Culture:**
- Food (ugali, dagaa)
- Language (Swahili, French, Lingala)
- Religion (St. Augustine Parish)
- Music (Fally Ipupa)

**Everyday Life:**
- Dala dala system
- Markets
- Schools (Monseigneur André Kaseba, Ecole Secondaire de Kitumaini)
- Lakeside hotel

**Objects:**
- Toyota Camry, BMW X5
- Louis Vuitton
- Cartoon lion fob
- Krupp ring (from "Escape from New York")

---

## The Research Cycle

> Creation and investigation go back and forth, making demands on each other, pushing and pulling this way or that until the story shakes itself out, complete and alive.
