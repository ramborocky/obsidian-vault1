## 📖 CRAFT CHEAT SHEET — READ THIS EVERY TIME YOU OPEN THIS NOTE

*Three sources, everything below traces back to one of them: **McKee** (structure/plot), **Cleland** (sentence-level craft), or **Gardner** (the underlying theory — why the McKee/Cleland rules work at all: the [[The Vivid and Continuous Fictional Dream|vivid and continuous fictional dream]], and why [[Interest, Free Will, and the Active Protagonist|a passive protagonist kills interest]]). Full detail in [[Scene_structure.md]], [[05 Plot Engine]], and [[01 John Gardner/The Art of Fiction by John Gardner|the Gardner index]].*

**STRUCTURE & PLOT (McKee)**
- Inciting incident happens **onscreen** — the reader watches it, doesn't hear about it after the fact.
- Every scene must **turn** — someone's situation changes by the end of it, or cut the scene.
- **The Gap**: character expects X, gets Y. Bigger gap = scene works harder.
- Controlling Idea (Kalemie's): *"Inaction is complicity because choosing not to act is still a choice."*
- Causality, not coincidence — every event should cause the next one.
- Arc = what the protagonist believes at the start vs. what they believe (or refuse to) by the end.

**SENTENCES (Cleland — the 20-word rule)**
- Average sentence length **under 20 words**.
- Long (30–70w) = mood/interiority. Short (4–10w) = action/shock. Fragments = rupture — use rarely.
- Never stack 5+ long sentences in a row. That's fog, not lyricism.

**SHOW, DON'T TELL — BANNED WORDS (Cleland)**
- Cut: *saw, looked, heard, listened, felt, noticed, observed, realized, understood, knew.*
- Replace with the physical action or image that **is** the perception.

**SOUND & SILENCE (Cleland)**
- High emotion → go quiet. One factual detail beats three adjectives.
- Plant a sound early, echo it later under different conditions.
- Silence inside noise = dread. Noise inside silence = danger.

**METAPHOR (Cleland)**
- Must come from the character's own vocabulary (Daudi = physics: entropy, gravity, refraction).
- Metaphor (X *is* Y) beats simile (X *like* Y). Earn it, don't decorate with it.
- Kalemie's governing metaphor: **the lake** = entropy / memory / women / death / colonial history.

**GRAMMAR**
- Minimize "to be" verbs (was/were/is) — they train passive voice.
- Local language (Swahili/French/Lingala) is never italicized — it's Daudi's native register, not a foreign flourish.

## Writing Constitution
*We write scenes, not explanations. Objects carry emotion. Dialogue reveals class. Every page must surprise. Every scene changes somebody. Readers infer more than they are told. The setting is a character. Local language is never italicized. Every symbol evolves. Beauty emerges from specificity. Cut abstractions. Trust the reader. Earn every metaphor.*
