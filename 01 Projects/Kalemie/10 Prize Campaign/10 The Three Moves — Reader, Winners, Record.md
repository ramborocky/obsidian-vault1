# 10 · THE THREE MOVES

> Not instruments. **Actions.** Three things that would do more for this campaign than every rubric in the vault combined, and none of them involve scoring anything.

---

## MOVE 1 · Find one cold reader — *the biggest hole in the whole campaign*

**Every serious rule in this vault depends on a human being who is not you, and there is nobody.**

| Rule | Requires |
| --- | --- |
| [[The Three Gates — Unified Instrument\|Gate 4]] | A stranger, 72 hours, three questions |
| [[The Cold Read Protocol]] | Somebody to give the brutal read |
| The seven-day rule | Somebody to hold the draft while you stay away |
| [[The Underground Rubric — Risk, Afterlife and the Room\|Afterlife]] | Somebody who still remembers it on Friday |

Four instruments, one missing input, and it is the same input every time. **Chantal has been scored on four rubrics by two entities, and read by no reader.**

**What "cold" means, precisely:** not a writer, not a relative, not somebody who wants you to be happy. Someone who reads fiction, does not know the story's history, and will not soften. **One is enough. One is the whole requirement.**

Where to look: a reading group, a librarian, a secondary-school literature teacher, a colleague who reads novels on the commute, the Kigoma or Dar literary WhatsApp groups. **[[House - Airbnb Writers Retreat|The retreat idea]] is a reader-finding machine you have already half-built** — that connection is worth more than the retreat itself.

**Ask exactly this and nothing else, or you will get politeness:**

> *Don't tell me if it's good. Tell me the moment you were bored, and the moment you were lost.*

**This week's version of the task:** name one person. Write the name in [[00 Campaign HQ]]. Do not send the draft yet — just name someone.

## MOVE 2 · Read the actual winners — *the target is currently unknown*

[[02 Comparison Board]] measures two placed stories and **flags the prize as unidentified for both.** Giorgis at 5,179 words cannot be a Commonwealth entry at all. So the board is calibrated against stories that placed *somewhere*, and the panel you are actually writing for has never been read.

That is a strange gap for a campaign this thorough. **You are aiming at a target you have not looked at.**

The Commonwealth Foundation publishes its winning and shortlisted stories free online in its own magazine — **adda** — and the regional winners are the closest available evidence of what this specific panel rewards. *(Verify the current URL; I cannot confirm it from here.)*

**The task, and it replaces four weeks of Reconstructions:**

> Read the last five **Africa regional winners**. Not about them. Them.
> Then run [[01 The Six — Analysis Instrument]] on two of them and put them on the board.

**Why this beats another rubric:** the board's most consequential finding is that *these panels forgive architecture and do not forgive voice, and Chantal is the inverse shape.* That conclusion currently rests on two stories from unidentified prizes. **Five actual Commonwealth winners would either confirm it or destroy it**, and it is the finding governing your final pass.

## MOVE 3 · Submit something smaller, now — *stop putting one story on one door*

Chantal is one story aimed at one prize with one deadline, and the entire emotional weight of a year of work is sitting on a single envelope. **That is a bad structure regardless of how good the story is.**

**Three things a smaller submission buys you:**

1. **Real rejection data.** A rejection is information you cannot get any other way, and it costs nothing but an email
2. **A publication record.** Prize panels are blind, but editors and agents are not, and a story in print somewhere is a fact about you that no rubric can generate
3. **The weight comes off Chantal.** A story that carries a whole year is a story you cannot revise clearly

**Where:** African and international literary magazines that take unpublished short fiction. **Check current open windows and payment terms yourself** — submission calendars change constantly and mine is out of date. Rules to hold: never pay a reading fee to an unknown market, and check simultaneous-submission policy before sending anywhere twice.

**Start a submission log.** One line per send: story · where · date sent · reply · what they said. **Rejections with a sentence in them are worth more than silence, and a log is the only way to see the pattern.**
