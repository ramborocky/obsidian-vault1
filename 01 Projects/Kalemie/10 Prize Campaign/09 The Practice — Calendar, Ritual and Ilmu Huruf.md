# 09 · THE PRACTICE — CALENDAR, RITUAL AND ILMU HURUF

> [!warning] What this does and does not do
> **Nothing on this page changes what a panel does with your story.** No lunar phase, planetary position or letter-square moves a judge. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.
>
> What it does do is real: **it puts hard dates on the cooling-off periods this vault already requires and cannot enforce.** [[The Cold Read Protocol]] says score cold after seven days. [[The Three Gates — Unified Instrument]] says Gate 4 needs 72 hours and a stranger. [[02 Comparison Board]] says two full passes were spent chasing a rhythm number that turned out not to exist.
>
> Every one of those failures is the same failure: **touching the draft when you should have been away from it.** A calendar you actually believe in solves that. That is not superstition, it is scheduling with teeth.

---

## Part 1 · The Moon as Scheduler

The moon is used here for one reason: it produces fixed, unarguable dates that arrive whether or not you feel ready, and you can see it from the road without opening a laptop. That is the entire mechanism.

> [!caution] These dates are computed, not looked up
> Calculated from the mean synodic month (29.53059 days) against a known reference. **Accurate to about ±1 day, and I have not verified them against an ephemeris.** Check before you build anything irreversible on them. Times are near enough for East Africa (UTC+3) but the day can shift.

| Phase | Aug 2026 | Sep 2026 | Oct 2026 | Nov 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **New moon** | ~12–13 | ~11 | ~10–11 | ~9 |
| **Full moon** | ~27–28 | ~26 | ~25–26 | ~24 |

### The four stations

Assign one job to each. **The rule is that no other work happens on that day**, which is what makes it useful.

| Station | Job | Why this one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **New moon** — dark | **Cut only.** Deletions, no additions. Open a draft, remove, close | [[The Five Passes]] says never polish a sentence in a scene you might cut. The dark moon is the cutting moon |
| **Waxing** — the fortnight after | **Write only.** New material. No scoring, no rubrics, no comparison board | This is the period [[03 Brian Kiteley\|Kiteley]] is for. Diagnosis is banned here — that is the standing warning in [[Learning Curve]] |
| **Full moon** — light | **Read aloud.** The whole draft, out loud, once, no pen in hand | Catches every register lurch and every unglossed word. It is the cheapest instrument in this vault and the only one that needs no notes |
| **Waning** — the fortnight after | **Score and hand over.** Run the gates. Give it to a cold reader | Gate 4 needs 72 hours and someone who is not you |

**The hard rule this buys you:** a draft is never scored in the same lunar phase it was written in. That single constraint would have prevented the 85–90/93 scorecard in [[The Underground Rubric — Risk, Afterlife and the Room]], which was produced the same day as the draft it praised.

### Against the prize calendar

The Commonwealth window has historically been **1 September to 1 November**. **Verify this for the current cycle before planning against it** — I cannot confirm the dates from here.

If it holds:

| When | Moon | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **12–13 Aug** | New | Final cut pass on Chantal. Deletions only |
| **27–28 Aug** | Full | Read Chantal aloud, whole, once |
| **~1 Sep** | Waning | Entries open. Gate 1a hygiene run. Hand to cold reader |
| **~4 Sep** | Waning | Gate 4 answers collected — 72 hours after handover |
| **11 Sep** | New | Last cut. **After this date nothing is added, ever** |
| **26 Sep** | Full | Final read-aloud. Submit within the week |
| **10 Oct** | New | Hard stop. If not submitted by now, the story is not going this year |

**Three weeks of margin before the deadline is deliberate.** Every year people lose to a website that falls over on 31 October.

## Part 2 · Ilmu Huruf

**ʿIlm al-ḥurūf** — the science of letters — is a real and old tradition, and on the Swahili coast it is ordinary rather than exotic: *hirizi* written by a *mwalimu*, numbers taken from names, squares folded into leather. It reached Kalemie and Kigoma the same way *dagaa* and secondhand words did, across the lake.

It works on **abjad** values, where every Arabic letter carries a number:

| | | | | | | | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ا 1 | ب 2 | ج 3 | د 4 | ه 5 | و 6 | ز 7 | ح 8 |
| ط 9 | ي 10 | ك 20 | ل 30 | م 40 | ن 50 | س 60 | ع 70 |
| ف 80 | ص 90 | ق 100 | ر 200 | ش 300 | ت 400 | ث 500 | خ 600 |
| ذ 700 | ض 800 | ظ 900 | غ 1000 | | | | |

A name is summed, and the total is read — reduced, factored, matched against a square.

### Where this actually belongs: inside the story

> [!success] Your prose is already doing this, and it is one of its real strengths
> Look at what is already load-bearing in the two drafts. **None of it is decoration.**
>
> **Chantal:** thirty-four days, counted twice, and the second count is bigger. Twenty seconds, counted, because *I count everything*. Four ships that have not moved in her lifetime. Forty-one likes and none of them asked her anything. Ten thousand, which is a school skirt. Two vaccinations. Eleven, the age of the auntie's neighbour.
>
> **Beat of My Hurt:** eleven beats, of which he knows nine. Beat four, beat two, beat six, the tenth. Eight seconds until the room breaks. Fifteen thousand and fifteen thousand. Four hundred times his mother has watched him warm the drum. Eleven years a woman sold cups for five francs.
>
> **Both narrators are counters, and both stories are built on arithmetic that will not come out even.** *It was not shame. It was arithmetic.* The instinct that sent you to ilmu huruf is the same instinct that wrote those lines. It is already in the work.

**Three ways to use it as craft, not as luck:**

**1 · The number audit.** Go through Chantal and list every number on the page. Ask of each: *is this number doing work, or is it furniture?* Djamela counts because counting is how she survives — so a number that means nothing is a wasted beat in her voice specifically. This is a real pass and it is cheap.

**2 · Name values as a consistency check, not a prophecy.** Sum the characters' names in abjad and see whether the numbers agree with what the story says about them. Where they clash, you have learned nothing about fate — but you have been forced to state, in one number, what you think each character *is*. That is the same discipline as the one-sentence core in [[The Underground Rubric — Risk, Afterlife and the Room|Gate 3]].

**3 · Give it to D'juma.** He already lays hands on people in the afternoons. He is a fruit seller, a healer and a philosopher, and [[KALEMIE-Draft_with_AI_Questions|the master draft's own AI question]] asks what single detail would make him three-dimensional. **This is the detail.** A man who counts letters over a sick person in the afternoon and stacks watermelons in a pyramid in the morning is one man, not two — and the pyramid takes him twenty minutes and nobody has ever bought a watermelon because of it. That is already a ritual performed for a result it does not produce. **The story is about that.**

> [!important] The strongest version of this
> Kalemie's controlling idea is that inaction is complicity. A man performing a ritual instead of acting is the purest possible image of it — and it would be **cruel and true** rather than mystical, because D'juma is not a fool and the story knows it. *He was handed a town that had been emptied out twice before he got to it, and he has nothing to give me except proverbs and fruit.*
>
> That line already exists in the draft. Ilmu huruf gives it a third object.

## Part 3 · The Rituals Already In This Vault

You have more of these than you think. They are written as engineering, which is why they do not feel like practice.

| Existing rule | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Archive the old text before touching it | The pre-edit hook, `CLAUDE.md` |
| Never polish a sentence in a scene you might cut | [[The Five Passes]] |
| Score cold, after seven days, never the same day | [[The Cold Read Protocol]] |
| Measure before writing the table, never after | The Draft 3 craft audit |
| The ending you plan to write is a 0 until it exists | [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes]] |
| Three numbers, never one | [[The Three Gates — Unified Instrument]] |

**Add one more, and it is the only one on this page that is not scheduling:**

> **Before submitting, write out by hand the name of the person the story is for, and do not put it in the story.**

Djamela wrote *Daudi* in the dust and wiped it off, *because a name in dust is evidence*. Ilunga's mother is never named once in the whole of Beat of My Hurt. **Both stories already know this rule.** Keeping the name off the page and somewhere else entirely is how the story stays a story and not a letter.
