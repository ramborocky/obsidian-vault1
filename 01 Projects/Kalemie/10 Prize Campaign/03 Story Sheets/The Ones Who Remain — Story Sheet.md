### The argument for scoring axis 5 a 2, and why it fails

Two moments deny the reader something: Amina leaves the perfect cluster of cloves unpicked (~57%), and at **39.5%** the grandmother reframes the absent mother as herself a version of remaining, waiting rather than staying. Both are good.

Neither is a gap. **A gap is what opens between what a character expects from an action and what the action returns.** The unpicked cluster is a choice with no consequence; the reframe is a line of dialogue. No action in this story returns anything other than what it was reaching for. The 1 stands.

### Axis 7 — the tree, at 64.8%

The most expensive sentence in the story. Axis 7's 5 is *never stated, impossible to miss*. This states the controlling idea, in a dream, in the mouth of the central symbol, and then supplies a simile that explains the symbol on top of it. Everything the story had built to that point is spent in one sentence.

## 🔧 COMPETENCE — **N**

Does the narrator know how to *do* something? The clove trade is the obvious candidate and it is described entirely from outside — the climbing kit at **5.2%**, and the drying-and-selling clause in the same paragraph, which gestures at money without ever naming any.

No price. No weight. No grade. No count. A yield of two baskets is given once, at 3.7%, and the number is never converted into anything.

Compare the two clean `Y`s in [[02 Comparison Board]] — both **economic or procedural, under pressure**: Vishnu pricing rice against wet cocoa and knowing what the gap leaves a man; Marsha peeling vine in her hands to feel whether it will hold a woman. ## ↔️ AGAINST THE THREE

### vs *Dite* — the motif, and it is the sharpest comparison on this sheet

[[Dite — Story Sheet|Dite]]'s finding: **one body part, six times, never explained, is enough to carry a controlling idea.** The throat is pressed by a cane, a code of conduct, a lover and a colonial ledger, and the reader assembles the argument without being handed it once.

Here the title phrase appears **ten times**, and the word *remain* in some form **seventeen**, in 3,974 words. And it is explained — by the grandmother, by Mzee Hamid twice, by the tree, by Amina, by the narrator, and by the title.

**Dite proves a motif works by being unexplained. This is the controlled experiment for the inverse, and it comes out the way Dite predicts.** The mechanism is not repetition; it is repetition plus silence.

**Dite's second finding — untranslated language should be rationed, not sprinkled.** Dite withholds Creole almost entirely, spends it three times, and makes the last word of the story *dite*. Here the Swahili is atmosphere: nine terms, none load-bearing, none costing anything to say. Gate 1 passes on this and the story is poorer for the reason it passes.

**Dite's third finding — instruction-as-epigraph.** Nothing here does that work. A clove buying-price list, a 1964 registration form, a school rule, a shipping manifest — any flat procedural block over a section would have carried the argument the prose is currently making in dialogue.

### vs *The Serpent in the Grove* — the winner, and the closest structural rhyme

Nazir loses **economy · close · controlling idea** — architectural, all three — and wins on **voice · interiority · scene**. Doughty called it *poetic, original, deeply moving*. All voice.

## 🚩 FLAGS — outside the rubrics

**1 · The story is about winning the Commonwealth Short Story Prize, and names it.** The decision to enter is spoken aloud at **49.3%**; the shortlist letter arrives at **59.8%**; the win at **72.8%**.

This is the largest single issue on the sheet and it is not a craft score. Three separate problems, and they compound:

- **It flatters the panel.** The classroom passage at **11.9%** puts the Foundation's own promotional framing — the Commonwealth, English as a shared language, authorship as self-representation — into a teacher's mouth and hands it back to the people who wrote it.
- **It stages its own reception.** The judges are being asked to award a prize that the story has already awarded itself, to a character whose story is this story. There is no reading position left for them that the text has not already occupied.
- **It makes the ending unfalsifiable.** The story's proof that Amina's writing is true is that it wins. A judge who does not think it is true has been written out of the story.

**2 · Two verifiable errors about the prize itself, in the one subject the panel knows better than anyone.**
- Amina is **seventeen** (stated at 2.1%) and enters. The Commonwealth Short Story Prize is open to entrants aged **18 and over** — this is not recorded in [[12 The Rules — Source of Truth]], so **check it against the 2027 rules PDF before relying on it**, but if it holds, the story's premise is ineligible inside its own plot.
- She **posts it** (54.5%) — an envelope, an address, a walk to the post office. Entries are uploaded through the Foundation's online portal (rule 3k, *"uploaded in a PDF document"*). There is no postal entry.

**3 · The chronology needs one pass with a pen.** The grandmother is *seventy-something*; Amina's mother was born two years after January 1964, so 1966; Amina is 17. If the present is roughly now, the grandmother was about sixteen when she gave birth, her sister already had a five-year-old, and Amina's mother was **forty-three** when Amina was born — then left when Amina was nine, so at fifty-two. None of it is impossible. All of it is tight, and a reader who does the arithmetic (this story invites it, by dating the photograph) will feel the strain before locating it.

**4 · Run [[The De-Slop Pass — English Prose]] on this one.** The standing exemption in that note covers Chantal, Found Things, Beat of My Hurt and the master draft *by instruction*. This draft is not on that list, and the counts are what the pass exists to find:

| --- | --: | --- |
| Em-dashes | 16 | < 13 (1 per 300 words) |
| *the way X* frame | **14** | Chantal D3 cut this to **4**, each survivor load-bearing |
| *the colour of X* frame | 6 | — |
| Paragraphs ending on a landed, resolved beat | **near all 70** | *not all of them* |
| Antitheses | 2 | 1 per story, at the peak |

**Law 5 does not apply here.** The character-trait exception protects a tell when it is a true fact about the person speaking — Chantal's composure *is* Djamela. Amina has no such trait; the composure is the narrator's, and the narrator is not a character. Nothing is being protected.

**5 · The point of view gives away the vault's strongest asset.** Chantal, Beat of My Hurt and Found Things are all first person, and the voice is the reason they score where they do. This is third person limited with an unmarked literary narrator, and axis 3 falls from a 4 to a 2 the moment that choice is made. The measured winners all win on voice — [[02 Comparison Board]] is five for five on it. This is the one draft in the vault that does not have one.

**6 · Nobody in the story is at fault, and 1964 is not a subject that permits that.** At 20.9% the perpetrators are *the men* and nothing else — no name, no side, no reason, no consequence. The story asks the reader to mourn without asking them to think about who, and G1 and G2 both drain out through that hole. The grandmother's position at **45.5%** — that keeping quiet was the only protection she had to pass down — is a real position and the story's best idea, and it is never allowed to win an argument, only to be gently retired.

**7 · The stated word count on the manuscript is wrong** — 3,509 against a measured 3,974. See COUNTED above.
