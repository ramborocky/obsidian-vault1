## 🔑 THE FORM — instruction-as-epigraph

Five sections, each opened by a **flat block of instructions in the trade's own voice**, and the instruction is never commented on:

> *Bring water, loose leaf tea, five cardamom pods, and one crushed lemongrass root to boil in a small deksi.*
> *Bring water, tulsi leaves, and ginger slices to a boil. Add this water to your own waters, for the sake of your unborn child.*
> *Pour hot water over tea sachet and let steep for five minutes. Enjoy with a cucumber sandwich or scone.*
> *Feast your eyes on the authentic vista of green tea bushes and local women picking tea…*

**The four registers are the story's whole argument and it is never made in prose.** Family recipe → folk medicine → New England tea room → colonial tourism brochure. Nobody says *colonialism*. The epigraphs say it by changing voice.

The frame is stranger still: the story opens narrated by **the teas themselves**, plural — *we grew into uneven rows of boxes and tins* — and returns to them at the close. An object-chorus that watches a woman for thirty years.

## 🔑 THE MOTIF — one body part, six times, thirty years

**The throat.** It is the only thing carrying the controlling idea and it is never explained.

| Where | What |
| --- | --- |
| Primary school | *holding in my cries until my throat hurts more than the welts* |
| Secondary school | *feeling no pain at all except for the strange one in my throat* |
| In bed with Nigel | *I turn my face and my aching throat to the wall* |
| The break-up | *the painful knot in my throat alchemises the words* |
| The indenture record | *A painful knot forms in my throat when my eyes see before I can understand* |
| The release | *the pleasure in my groin moves upward and explodes on my tongue, suddenly unsilenced* |

A cane, a code of conduct, a lover and a colonial ledger all do the same thing to the same organ, and the reader assembles the argument without being handed it once.

**Paired with it: *tall*.** Mama *standing tall and silent* in the doorway. The narrator *standing tall and silent* through the lashings. Dadi bent to three feet at the end. Then the indenture papers: *Height: 3 feet 2 inches* — a six-year-old — and *she is three feet five inches. She is tall, taller even than him.* **The motif detonates inside an archival document**, and *tall* stops being a posture and becomes an inheritance.

## 🔑 THE LANGUAGE POSITION

Creole is the forbidden tongue — Rule 2, French only — and the story withholds it almost entirely, then spends it three times: *bour to sime ale*, *Me mo bann tifi edike*, and the sentence Vandana teaches her. **The last word of the story is *dite*.** Tea, in Creole.

The story's subject is a woman who cannot say what she wants in the language she wants, so **it enacts that at the level of which words appear on the page.** Not a theme. A distribution.

## → WHAT THIS CHANGES

**1 · The rhythm metric is finished as a target.** Three accomplished stories, two profiles, opposite directions. Chantal's 14.8 is not a defect.

**2 · Instruction-as-epigraph is a portable device and it is cheap.** A recipe, a tariff, a form, a rule — any flat procedural block placed above a scene will carry irony the prose then never has to state. Used in [[MABANGA (New Story — AI Draft, Not Canon)]] as a price list.

**3 · One body part, six times, unexplained, is enough to carry a controlling idea.** Axis 7 asks for *never stated, impossible to miss.* This is how it is done, and it is a distribution problem, not a sentence problem.

**4 · Untranslated language should be rationed, not sprinkled.** Withhold the mother tongue until it costs something to say.
