## 🔑 THE FORM — packing as un-writing

The structural idea is genuinely good and it is the reason to have read this at all.

Ten numbered sections. The odd-numbered frame is present tense, second person: a man stands in his doorway while his lover packs to leave. **The packing is the plot.** Section by section the relationship is dismantled in reverse order of assembly — wallpapers off the wall, CDs out of the rack, wardrobe emptied, bathroom cleared — and each removed object triggers the past-tense section that explains what it was for. The apartment is being returned to the silence described in section II, on the page, in front of you.

That is a portable device and it costs nothing: **an inventory run backwards is a plot.** It is stealable, and it is the first third of this story only. From section V the frame is abandoned for four straight sections of backstory and never recovers its own engine.

## 🔑 THE ONE MOTIF THAT WORKS — and is spent as a grace note

Smell. Section II invents a whole perceptual system for the narrator: people have distinct smells if you listen harder. The lover is *balsamy*; the mother, on the phone from miles away, of cherries.

It pays out twice, both times beautifully, both times on an object:

- **§IV — the towel he does not take.** Everything else in the bathroom goes; the towel stays. The narrator, in three unanswered questions, works out that it may have been left so it can be pressed to a face. **This is the best move in the story** and it is [[All the Small Things — Grief Through the Minor Object]] executed at full strength by someone with no connection to this vault.
- **§VIII — the blanket.** He wakes on the kitchen floor wrapped in it, holds it to his nose, and finds it is the only evidence anyone was there.

Two objects, one sense, no explanation. Then it stops.

> [!danger] Compare *Dite*: the throat, six times, across thirty years
> [[Dite — Story Sheet]] established that **one body part, repeated six times and never explained, is enough to carry a controlling idea** — the axis-7 five, *never stated, impossible to miss*.
>
> Halim has the same device, of the same quality, and uses it **twice**. It is decoration where it should have been the spine. The story's controlling idea gets stated in the title instead.
>
> **This is the same law proved from the other side.** Two sheets now, one positive and one negative. It should go on [[04 Craft Laws — Reference Card]] as settled.

The third instance is also where the system breaks: the sister smells of excrement because she has just broken up with her girlfriend. It is a joke, it is the only joke, and it tells the reader the device is not serious about eight lines after it was introduced.

## 💥 THE FAULT THAT MATTERS — the unmetabolised assault

This is not a craft quibble and it is worth the space.

At **83.0%**, the narrator throws a mug at his lover, takes his head in both hands and beats it against a wall until blood is warm and watery in his palms, then — *stepping back to look at him* while the man lies sobbing and unmoving — unscrews a fluorescent tube and breaks it across his face.

At **93.8%** that man is back, smiling, with keys to a flat he has rented for them both. At **99.8%** the story's last line asks the reader to find him adorable.

**Nothing in the intervening 17% refers to the assault.** Not the victim, who returns cheerful. Not the narrator, who committed it and whose head we are inside for the entire story. It is not forgiven, or excused, or unresolved-on-purpose. It is *dropped*, and the story then requests a warm ending on the strength of material it has stopped acknowledging.

Two craft consequences, in the vault's own terms:

- **Gardner's sentimentality, in its precise sense** — emotion claimed at a cost the story has not paid. The closing warmth is drawn against an account that was emptied at 83%.
- **The single line of interiority given to the violence is a justification, and the cheapest available one:** *he wanted you to kill him, you thought.* The narrator supplies his victim's consent. The story does not examine the sentence, and it is the only explanation offered.

Then he wakes wrapped in the man's blanket, and the man has made tea and left bread. **The story does not know what it is saying there**, which is the difference between a story that depicts something ugly and a story that is complicit in it. [[The Underground Rubric — Risk, Afterlife and the Room]] is the note for the version of this that works — risk taken *knowingly*. This is risk taken absent-mindedly.

> [!question] The venue explains it — and that makes it worse, not better
> Brittle Paper filed the post under **Love story**. The ending is the beat that tag requires: the couple leaves together, warmly, on a joke.
>
> So the close is **generically motivated rather than story-motivated** — it satisfies the category the piece was published in, and abandons the material the piece actually built. That is a sharper diagnosis than *the writer forgot*, and a more dangerous one, because it is the failure mode of writing toward a known destination.
>
> **Chantal's exposure is the same mechanism at a different address.** A prize has an expected register too — the serious, the resonant, the closing image that lands. Writing the ending a Commonwealth panel is imagined to want is the identical error to writing the ending a love-story tag wants. [[The Funhouse Mirror — AI Output as Cliché Detector]] is the note for this: **whatever the destination generates by default is the cliché.**

> [!success] The direct application to Chantal
> **The most loaded act in a story must be metabolised on the page by the consciousness narrating it, or the close cannot land — no matter how good the close is.**
>
> Chantal already passes this and should be checked once more against it deliberately. Its climax at 78.4% *is* metabolised — *it was not shame, it was arithmetic* — which is precisely why [[02 Comparison Board]] can instruct **end on the four ships and add nothing.** Halim ends on the four ships equivalent and then adds a wink.

Three of these are load-bearing, and one is decisive:

**The pronoun collapse at 73.3%.** The most important event in the backstory — a father dying at his son's bedside having spent his last breath on the marriage demand — is unparseable on first reading. This is [[The NAPLAN Floor — Ten Mechanical Criteria|criterion 6]] exactly: *the reader is told what they need to know at the right moment.* It is the same class of fault as Chantal's *shemeji* and Found Things' Swahili block, and it is **larger than both**, because those withheld information the reader could recover later and this one collapses the sentence itself.

**The timeline break.** Five months at 58.2%; *all these years* at 97.4% — in the reversal paragraph, load-bearing on the reader's sense of what is being given up. Compounded earlier by wallpapers described as old, dog-eared and steeped in the flat's staleness, in a flat he moved into five months ago.

**And *dry of saliva* is at 2.7%.** [[The First-Page Cut]] is about the first page; this is the first *paragraph* of prose. **The single most expensive fault in the story is not the assault — it is that a first reader meets a vocabulary error before they meet the second character.**

Nine axes would score all thirteen of these at zero cost. This is the clearest demonstration in the campaign of why the NAPLAN pass is run at all, and it is on somebody else's story, which makes it cheap to learn from.

## 🎯 COMPETENCE — N

The narrator works from home; it is mentioned once, at 93%, because the plot requires him to be relocatable. He does not know how to do anything.

What he has instead is **brand taxonomy**: Man U, Gucci, Adidas, Calvin Klein, Converse, Timberland, and a CD rack listed by artist across two decades of pop. Inventory is doing the work scenes should, and it does not survive the vault's own constraint — *objects carry emotion.* These objects carry branding. The two objects that do carry emotion, the towel and the blanket, have no brand and no list.

**Board tally moves to 3 Y / 3 N.** The kill criterion (N eight times in ten) remains unmeetable, and the sharper rule from [[02 Comparison Board]] survives intact: the competence that counts is **economic or procedural, deployed under pressure**. There is none here, and this is the weakest story measured.

## 🧊 THE RAISED-AND-DROPPED THIRD PARTY

At 76% the fiancée gets one paragraph, and it contains **the best-observed human being in the story**: educated, her eyes done in a careful, seemingly overlaboured perfection — and she can see him flinch when she takes his hand. She knows. In one clause.

She is never mentioned again. The story ends with the two men leaving town together and does not spend a word on the woman whose life the plot has just decided.

Barrett would say cut her — one incident, two speaking characters. He would be right. **But raising a character to that level of observation and then dropping her is worse than either keeping or cutting her**, because the reader has been shown that this writer can see her and is then asked to stop.

> [!question] The test to run on Chantal
> Is there anyone in Draft 3 observed that precisely who then disappears? One paragraph of real seeing is a promise. Djamela is already flagged in [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes|the master-draft scoring]] as *seen only from outside* — the opposite fault, and the cheaper one.

## ☑️ COVERAGE PASS — three gaps this sheet had, and one of them qualifies the verdict

Added 2026-08-02 after running [[The Annotation Checklist — Coverage Pass]] over the analysis above. A fifteen-category student checklist found three categories this sheet never touched. **One of them changes how fairly the ending has been judged.**

### Setting — annotated as a mechanism, never as a place

The flat is the **only location in the present-tense frame**, and the sheet spent 500 words on what happens *in* it without once treating it as a place. It should have: the story gives it a prior state — before him, the flat *folded itself and crept into a silence* — and the entire packing sequence is the room being **returned to that state on the page.** Walls stripped back to their cracks, rack emptied, wardrobe cleared, bathroom stripped. The setting is not a backdrop to the plot; it is the thing the plot is doing.

**And the real gap: there is no Nigeria on the page until 63%.** The family, the uncle, the demand for an heir all arrive in § VII. Benin City is named at 93.8%. For a story whose central conflict is **only intelligible through its society**, that society is absent from the first two-thirds.

### Conflict, typed — and this is the finding

Naming the conflicts by type, which the sheet never did:

| Type | Where | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Character vs. character | narrator vs. the boyfriend | surface only; resolved by § X |
| Character vs. self | the intention/speech split in § I | **raised twice, then dropped** |
| **Character vs. society** | **the real one** | **no resolution available** |

A closeted only son, a dead father, an uncle demanding an heir, a fiancée already chosen. **Inside this story's world there is no exit** — he cannot marry the narrator, and he cannot stay and refuse. He can only leave the country. Which is precisely what § X proposes.

> [!warning] This qualifies the sheet's verdict on the ending — it does not overturn it
> The sheet called the close **generically motivated** — the beat the *Love story* tag requires. **That stands, and it is still the fault.**
> But the *flight itself* is not a genre cliché. **It is the only move the social conflict permits.** Two men leaving Nigeria together is the single realistic resolution available, and the story earned it across § VII.
> **So the failure is narrower and more precise than first stated: the ending's *content* is earned by the conflict. Its *tone* is not.**
> Leaving together is right. Leaving together **on a wink, ninety words after the assault, with the violence unmentioned**, is what breaks it. The problem was never the destination — it was the register the story arrives in.
> **The corrected charge is lighter and lands harder.**

### Tone and mood — named separately, which the sheet conflated

The sheet scored **register** (prose control, axis 2) and stopped there. Named properly:

- **Mood** — claustrophobic, and genuinely well built. The present-tense frame never once leaves the flat. Every escape is a memory.
- **Tone** — the narrator's attitude to his own material, and **it cannot settle.** Elegiac in § III, wry in § IV, clinical in § VIII, flirtatious in § X. The register faults already scored are the *symptom*; this is the cause. **A story that doesn't know how it feels about its own subject will lurch, sentence by sentence, and no amount of line editing fixes it.**

## → WHAT THIS CHANGES

**0 · The board gains a floor, which is the thing it was missing.** Every other row won or placed, so the instrument could only ever measure downward from the top. This row is a verified magazine publication at **22/45**, and the nine-point gap to a competitive score sits entirely in **controlling idea, close and something to say.** Magazines buy a voice and a subject; panels want a position and an ending. That is a calibration no winner could have supplied, and it arrived from a story nobody would have thought to measure.

**1 · Unquoted dialogue is confirmed in the wild, and the risk flag comes down.** [[The NAPLAN Floor — Ten Mechanical Criteria|Finding A]] kept unquoted dialogue but held a warning that it is *a live reader-risk with anyone*. Here is a published outside writer running the identical convention across an entire story, with no apparent trouble. **The flag can be lowered from a risk to a style choice.** Keep the reasoning; drop the anxiety. → also fix the script's dialogue detector, which has now failed on two of five texts.

**2 · A stated controlling idea scores 1 even when it is a good idea, and *especially* when it is the title.** *Leaving you is the only way I can love you* is a real thought and would have been a strong story. Placed in sentence four it stops being an argument the story makes and becomes an announcement the story then has to live up to — and this one contradicts it 96% later. **Kalemie's controlling idea is *inaction is complicity*. That sentence, and every paraphrase of it, must never appear on the page.** *Dite* proved the law positively with the throat; this proves it negatively. Promote to [[04 Craft Laws — Reference Card]].

**3 · The close must be paid for out of the account the climax emptied.** Not a rule about endings — a rule about arithmetic between two positions in a story. Applies directly to the standing instruction to add nothing after Chantal's four ships.

**4 · Rhythm is now available to bad writing, and the metric should be struck rather than retired.** 10.3 / 55% / 4% — the winner's profile, on a 22/45. Two passes were already spent chasing this number. There is no third.

**5 · The backwards inventory is stealable and this story only half-uses it.** An object removed, then the scene that explains what it was for. It is a complete engine for a story about an ending, it costs no exposition, and it is abandoned here at section V. Worth an [[x-factor]] experiment at full length — **in the sandbox, and not near the manuscript.**

## The honest column

A dissection that only convicts is useless. Four things here are better than anything in the campaign's own drafts at the equivalent moment:

- **The towel.** The best single object measured on this board, including the winner's.
- **The invitation card, discovered rather than confessed** — left in a bag, dug out by the person it destroys, and afterwards left in the dining room under a coat of dust while ten sections happen around it.
- **Walking the streets on tiptoe, afraid the earth would break if he pressed too hard.** One image, and the whole condition of the closeted son is in it.
- **The mother's grief.** *Her husband*, she says — as if the man were not also his father, as if the two of them had not kept small secrets and laughed at everyone else for not having them. It is the only passage where love and violence are shown to be the same organ, which is what the entire story is about. **Note where it sits: it is the boyfriend's story, in reported third, and the second-person narrator is entirely absent from it.** The prose gets better the moment the *you* leaves the room. That is a diagnosis of the device, not a compliment to the paragraph.

**The second person earns nothing.** Its two justifications are dissociation and accusation. There is one line of dissociation, unpaid; there is no accusation, in a story whose narrator commits a felony. Swap every *you* for *I* and the story is unchanged, which means the device is costing the reader and returning nothing.
