## 🔑 THE ENGINE — the reader knows and the narrator does not

**Nothing in this story is stated.** Lubwama never writes that Fatima sells sex, that Coach is coercing her, or that the mother's rage is shame. Every one of those is assembled by the reader out of things a ten-year-old reports accurately and does not understand.

| What Musa reports | What the reader assembles |
| --- | --- |
| Used condoms are excellent balloons, endlessly stretchy, and don't burst on grass | The opening line, and it is an obscenity the child cannot see |
| Every shopkeeper is out of stock of that colourless balloon | He has been to all of them asking |
| *waited like I had when it was Dan, like I had when it was Hassan, waited like I had when it was the electrician* | A list a child would genuinely keep |
| *She hadn't told me to wait when it was Sumaiyah* | And the one clause that tells you he has been counting them |
| *You'll tell her to reconsider. She'll understand* | Coach, to a ten-year-old, as a courier |
| Mother never helped with homework, never spoke English at parent evenings | *And then it started falling into place, everything* |

**The most instructive single move:** the mother reads as the antagonist for 80% of the story. She screams *stupid boy*, she humiliates herself in a stranger's compound, she has her son puncture his father's tyres. Then Fatima says *illiterate and jobless*, and *something had shrunk inside of Mother*, and **every earlier scene re-reads at once.** She was never the antagonist. She was a woman without literacy trying to hold a boy inside a school.

The title does the work the prose refuses to do. It is the only place the story says what it is about, and it says it in five words.

## 🔧 COMPETENCE — **Y**

Musa is expert at exactly one thing and the story lets him be: **the economics of being a child with no money.** Inventing a torn shoe to extract cash from an absent father. Pricing two weeks of washing utensils against a football. Trading a Jabulani to a cobbler for boots three sizes too big. *Who taught you the ways of a merchant?* is the mother's line and it is the character in six words.

## ⚔️ GATE 3 · Leakage — **5**

Jjaja Reagan on the porch she never leaves. Makumbi's dogs. The driving school that eats the football field and then negotiates a truce over free car washes. Pinto's femur. The headmistress's *temporary*. The cobbler's unkempt white beard. Ronaldo accepting a friend request and replying in Luganda.

**None of it serves the plot.** Pinto is run over, ruins the narrator's sleep for weeks, and never returns. That is a whole life crossing the frame and leaving.

## → WHAT THIS CHANGES

**1 · A child narrator is a structural device, not a voice choice.** The gap between what the narrator can report and what they can interpret is a machine for irony that costs no words. Nothing on the board exploits it.

**2 · The mother-reversal is the move worth stealing.** One line of dialogue from a third party, at ~25%, retro-actively rewrites every scene before it — and the narrator says only *something had shrunk inside of Mother*. **He never names what it was.**

**3 · Comedy is not a break from the subject, it is the delivery.** This story is funny on nearly every page and it is about a woman's public humiliation and a child being groomed as a go-between. **The two African winners on this board are both funny. Neither Chantal nor Beat of My Hurt is.**

**4 · End on a search that fails.** A thousand Fatimas, none of them polytechnic. The last image is the title, and the title is the loss.
