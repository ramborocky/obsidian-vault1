**Opening authority — 2.** It opens by announcing that it is about to be boring: *"I will begin with a brief background."* Two paragraphs of exposition follow before anything happens, and the story's real first line does not arrive until **19.7%** — *"It was on the way home that I heard the first gunshot."* A fifth of a prize story spent clearing the throat.

**Prose control — 3.** The Nigerian English register is superb and consistent — the *Me, I…* construction, *wahala*, *gragra*, *sef*, *sweating like Christmas goats*, *collapsed like a sack of Dangote cement*. But it lurches repeatedly into borrowed literary diction it has not earned: *the sky wept*, *the house of the dead*, *nature's part in the cycle of sorrow*. Two voices, and only one of them is the boy's.

> [!quote] What was forgiven
> **A throat-clearing opening and a redundant political monologue** — the mortuary attendant delivers the same secessionist argument the father already gave, at length, to a boy who only nods.
> Forgiven for **the largest gap in anything measured so far** — he expects a corpse or a prisoner and gets his father in bed with his mother's friend — and for a voice so specific it survives its own bad sentences.
> **The prize rewarded voice and reversal, and forgave architecture.**

### Axis 5 is the outlier — why the gap is a 5

The reversal operates on three levels at once: the **personal** (a father is not dead but faithless), the **political** (the martyrdom was theatre; he never marched), and the **retrospective** (the mother's lonely death is re-explained by the same fact). One image at 97% reorganises every earlier scene. No other story on the board does this.

## 🔧 COMPETENCE — **N**

**0 for 2.** Closer than Giorgis, though.

The fifty naira is real economics: given for lunch, spent entirely on the keke fare to the police station, leaving him to walk home. That is a child costing out a day and running out of money — the one place the story contains a fact rather than an observation.

But nobody is shown practising a trade. The gala hawking is observed **from outside**, by a boy ashamed of it, and the shame is the subject rather than the work. What the narrator has instead is **social taxonomy** — the nickname economy (*Nwanyi Akara*, *nwa gala*), Uncle-the-Law's mythology of police stations measured against the flimsy real door, the reading of a neighbour's tone. Sharp, but it is knowledge of a *place*, not of a *procedure*.
