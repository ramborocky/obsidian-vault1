# ❓ FINAL QUESTIONS — CHANTAL

> [!abstract] The rule, from [[Q and A — Delete the Questions|Kiteley 200]]
> **Answer in Djamela's voice. Never in analysis. Never as a reply to me.**
> ~150 words each. **Then delete the question.** What remains is the work.
> Then run Cleland: no *saw / looked / felt / knew / noticed.*
>
> These are **fiction questions only** — every one produces prose. Critic questions (*does it land? is it too neat?*) are excluded by design; that was the fault that killed the old 53.

---

## 🔴 A · THE ONE DECISION — not a question

**Pick the controlling idea.** It is the last red item on the board and it governs which sentences survive any further pass.

- *A child can see the machine perfectly and still be standing inside it.*
- *She was not fooled. She did the deciding. That is the worse thing and the truer one.*
- *Being chosen and being taken are the same event, described by different people.*

The draft's own candidate, and the front-runner:

> **Not that I was fooled. That I did the deciding.**

Choose one and write it at the top of the draft. Do not put it *in* the draft.

## ✍️ B · EIGHT FICTION QUESTIONS

*Each one aims at something the draft has planted and never picked up.*

**1 · The vaccinations.** *Two vaccinations, low on the arm, near the elbow, not where they usually put them.* Who took her, what did she tell her mother the appointment was for, and what did she do with the card afterwards?

**2 · The ten thousand.** He put it under the phone number. She says it was not the money. **So what did she buy with it?** She counts everything; she knows exactly.

**3 · Alicia.** She takes Djamela's arm on the Tuesday and told a whole row in Primary Six that their house smelled of fish. **Does Alicia know?** What was the last thing Alicia asked her, and what did Djamela say back?

**4 · Her father, at the end.** He gets the watermelon and the paragraph where she is fair to him — and then he is gone from the story. **Where is he the afternoon she hides the fob?** What is he doing while she stands on that board?

**5 · The mother's four days.** She knows now that her daughter was counting on the wash. **What does she do with the next four days?**

**6 · The fob.** She had a photograph and a policeman refused it. She hid a plastic lion under a board instead. **What does she think Daudi will do with it that the police would not?** ← *still the story's engine, still unexplained*

**7 · The date in her head.** She foresees Morisho's arrest — *I have known that since the twenty minutes.* **Does she foresee her own?** What does she think the date is?

**8 · The one thing in the hotel room.** She refuses to describe it, and the refusal must stand. **But if she were made to name one object in that room, what would it be?** Answer it, keep the answer somewhere, and do not put it in the story. Knowing it will change the sentences around it.

## 🧩 C · THE MESS QUESTIONS — new, and the most important set

> [!danger] Why this section exists
> Read cold, Chantal's flaw is not a weak axis. It is that **nothing in it is accidental.** Every image returns. Every line lands. That immaculateness reads — faintly, unnameably — as a performance of a person rather than a person, and it is the single widest gap between this draft and a story like [[Uwazuruike — Looking for Papa]], which is worse in every measurable way and feels more human.

**Answers to these must go into the draft and must never come back.** If a detail from this section pays off later, cut it and use another.

**9 · Name four things in her classroom she has never once thought about.** Use one.

**10 · What did Alicia actually say about somebody's hairdo?** Write the sentence. It should be uninteresting.

**11 · What is broken in her house that nobody is going to fix and that has nothing to do with anything?**

**12 · What bores her?** She is alert to everything and has never once been bored on the page. **Boredom is the most human thing there is** and this narrator has none.

**13 · What is she wrong about?** One small thing. She never finds out. Nobody corrects her.

> [!tip] The instruction that makes this work
> Let **one paragraph end without landing.** Let one sentence be flat. Let one observation be slightly wrong in a way that characterises her rather than the world.
> You are not writing worse. You are removing the evidence that a system, rather than a person, put this on the page.
