## 1 · "Him they will call as a witness, if they call him at all."

**Not a grammar error.** That is object fronting — a legitimate English construction that throws the stress onto *him*. It is doing work: the paragraph is about who gets taken and who does not, and the inversion enacts the sorting. It also sits directly after the officer "reciting" the penal code "in the voice the priest at St Augustine uses", so the lifted, quasi-legal register reads as deliberate echo.

**But the stumble is data.** He read attentively and this is the sentence he stopped on. Being right and being tripped over is still a cost in a submission.

**Open:** keep it as marked syntax, or unmark it. Not an error either way — a decision.

## 2 · A glossary for the non-English words

**Recommend against, and the Writing Constitution agrees:** *local language is never italicized — it's the native register, not a foreign flourish.* An appended glossary does what italics do — marks the words foreign and walks the reader out of the story to a back page. Commonwealth judges read multilingual English as a matter of course.

**The real test is whether each term self-glosses in context.** Most do:

| Term | Carried by |
|---|---|
| dala dala | "the packed up minibus", same paragraph |
| délestage | "their windows stay yellow" while ours do not |
| mama alisema | **Jean Luc translates it in dialogue** — the best handling in the story |
| khanga · boda boda · mama ntilie · mia tano mia tano | surrounding action |

**Two do not:**

- **shemeji** — and this one matters most, because it carries Morisho's innocence in the closing pages: *"He gave me a coin off my fare once and called me shemeji. That is the whole of what he did."* A reader who does not know it is a familiar/in-law term loses the sentence.
- **mihogo**

**Open:** no glossary; let context carry those two instead.

## 3 · Commas — confirmed, and measured

Counted against **Nazir, *The Serpent in the Grove*** — the confirmed Commonwealth overall winner, source text already in [[00 Campaign HQ|the Prize Campaign folder]].

| | Words | Commas | Density | `", and"` | % of *and*s taking a comma |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| **Chantal** (2026-09-07) | 4,170 | 255 | 1 per 16.4 words | **76** | **44%** |
| 🏆 **Serpent** — Nazir | 3,416 | 164 | 1 per 20.8 words | **9** | **9%** |

**Seventy-six against nine**, in texts of comparable length. Not a stylistic difference — a habit.

**The caveat:** `", and"` *is* the voice. "and both of these are jobs, and neither is the kind you say out loud at school" needs both commas. Polysyndeton is load-bearing here. But at 76 instances the device has stopped registering as a device.

**Open:** triage the instances where the comma joins nothing. Those are habit, not rhythm.

## 4 · Flow, flashback, suspense

Worth logging rather than waving off. **The retrospective frame is the riskiest structural choice in the draft**, and an unbriefed reader named it as the thing that worked — independent confirmation of the axes that already score highest.

## 5 · "What is the main theme?" — the one that matters

An attentive reader finished the story and could not name what it is about.

**This is direct external confirmation of the weakest axis on the vault's own instrument.** [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes|Axis 7, controlling idea]]: scored **4** in [[Chantal — Prize Plan|the Prize Plan]]'s cold pass, and **3** in [[17 Chantal Complete — Scored Against the Three|note 17]] against the three comparison texts — the lowest score in that set, and the one note 17 records as not having moved.

He arrived there with no access to any of it. Two instruments, independent, same finding.

### A mechanical reason

The controlling idea was decided 2026-07-28 as ***"Not that I was fooled. That I did the deciding."*** In this draft, every thesis statement sits in the middle of the story:

| Line | Position in text |
|---|--:|
| "Not that I was fooled. That I did the deciding." — *the decided controlling idea* | **38%** |
| "it was a collar with a good buckle" | 45% |
| "I am not asking to be forgiven for that. I am asking for it to be counted correctly." | 49% |
| "It was not shame. It was arithmetic." — *the engineered late reframe* | **86%** |

The controlling idea fires at 38% and is **never restated**. What a reader carries off the last page is *arithmetic* and *four ships that have not moved and will not move in his lifetime* — which argues nearer futility than agency.

The Prize Plan already logs *"closed aphorisms: 5 — miscalibrated, both placed stories evaluate harder."* That note and Riziki's question are the same problem from opposite ends: **the story states its idea early and often, then closes on an image that does not carry it.**

### Added 2026-09-09 — part of it is a lost plant, not a design fault

Re-measuring against [[2026-07-28 — Chantal (Draft 3, Prize Pass)|Draft 3]] turned up something that bears directly on this. Draft 3 placed the four ships **three** times — *stated* at 21% ("the four rusted ships in it that have not moved…"), *shortened* at 87%, *restored in full* at the close. **This draft has only the last two.**

So the closing image — the one a reader carries out and the one that reads as futility — arrives having never been planted. The restoration restores something the reader was never given. The same pass dropped the school-gate paragraph that made the opening line concrete (see [[2026-09-07 — Chantal (as sent to Riziki Mwandia) — Copy & Continuity List|the copy list]], item 3).

That does not settle axis 7. But it means part of what Riziki felt may be two missing paragraphs rather than an unclear idea — and both exist in Draft 3, in your own words.

**Still open, and still an axis-7 decision, the author's alone.** Nothing here proposes a line.
