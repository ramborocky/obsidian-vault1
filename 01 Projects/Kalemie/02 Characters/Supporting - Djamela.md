## Identity
- **Name:** Djamela (renamed **Chantal** by Jean Luc — see Arc; the renaming is the story's actual transaction)
- **Role:** Deuteragonist. Not "supporting" — she carries the emotional arc, and in [[2026-07-26 — Chantal (Djamela POV Draft)|Chantal]] she carries the narration
- **Age:** 13 (turns 13 onscreen in the birthday scene; dead within the year)
- **Occupation:** School student, Ecole Secondaire de Kitumaini

## Core Wound
Being made to know she is poor before she was old enough to have an opinion about it. A girl at school told a whole row that her house smelled of fish, and everybody laughed — and Djamela laughed too, because that is the fastest way to stop being the thing being laughed at. The house does smell of fish. That is not the part that stayed. **The part that stayed is that she laughed**, and understood in the same second that she would trade almost anything not to be on that side of the joke again.

## Lie They Believe
That the arithmetic of Kalemie can be beaten from inside it — that a girl with no money and no connections can convert attention into status, status into safety, and safety into a life, if she is only clever enough about the exchange rate. She is not naive. She has the mathematics almost right. **She has only got the sign wrong**: she thinks she is the one making the trade.

## Need
Someone to tell her the truth about the world before she has to learn it by experiment. A father who says *I am afraid for you* instead of a proverb. To be seen once as fully herself — which the story denies her from every direction, including, for one costly night, from Daudi.

## Want
To be one of the ghost kids from Rue Saint Michel. Concretely: nails done Friday, a European register, French over Swahili, "anchovy" instead of dagaa, second-hand designer labels worn as if new, a phone with something on it worth showing. To be chosen visibly, in the road, in front of people who have watched her not be chosen.

## Contradiction
She is the most numerate person in the story and she walks into it anyway. She notices Jean Luc's French is worse than he thinks it is — and **decides not to have noticed**. Her tragedy is not blindness; it is a clear-eyed girl doing a cost-benefit calculation with incomplete information nobody would give her, and being right about every term except the one that mattered.

## Fear
Her parents finding out about the pregnancy. Being seen as poor. And beneath both, the one she can only say in her own narration: **that her body is deciding things faster than she can decide them**, and that the deciding started before she was consulted.

## Secret
The pregnancy — carried alone for weeks while she does arithmetic on her fingers behind the school gate. Failing marks she hides at home. And the older knowledge underneath it: she has understood what men are buying since she was eleven, when an adult let his hand rest too long on the back of her chair while a room full of adults agreed together not to see it. She has been managing that knowledge, unassisted, ever since — which is where the harm-reduction arithmetic comes from, and why she reads Jean Luc correctly and goes anyway.

## Speech
Talks at length and at speed about social media, gossip, who has what. Reaches for French to sound classier and falls back to Lingala and Swahili to belong. **Never italicised** — the code-switching is her native register, not a flourish. Her sentences get short and flat exactly when the subject is serious, which is the inverse of how she sounds when she is happy.

## Habits
Nails on Friday, off Monday before school — living between two rule sets like someone crossing a road that never stops. Counting. Counting days, coins, what things cost, what she is owed. Borrowing a neighbour's phone for social media. Leaving certain clothes at the bottom of the wash basket for four days so the perfume goes out of them before her mother gets to them.

## Objects
The **motherboard** — she found it in a heap behind the internet café and gave it to Daudi years ago because he was the only person she knew who would understand a thing could be beautiful after it stopped working. *He thinks he found it.* The **lion key fob**, cartoon plastic, third floor of the lakeside hotel, kept in the left pocket where the seam has gone. Second-hand designer clothes. Her phone. The watermelon with a candle in it, standing crooked.

## Color
Red. Gold — the ring in the photograph, which is not real gold and is worn like a crown. The green-and-gold of the motherboard, little roads going nowhere. Black cars.

## Smell
**Shalis by Remy Marquis** — Jean Luc's, not hers, and it gets into the weave of a khanga and will not come out with soap, only with time. Otherwise: the lake, fish cooking at home, the flat smell of not having sanitary pads when she needs them.

## Religion
Muslim by birth, doesn't pray, no hijab, assumes a general future forgiveness she has never examined closely. Says "amen" at the end of a birthday wish for a television personality without registering it as strange — which is the whole story in one word, and Daudi should not gloss it.

## Dream
To be Kim Kardashian. Stated exactly that plainly, at thirteen, with her eyes shut over a watermelon. Underneath it: to be *solid, deeded, worth building around* — her own words in [[2026-07-26 — Chantal (Djamela POV Draft)|Chantal]] — the way Rue Saint Michel is.

## Shadow
She uses people and knows it. She warmed her hands on Daudi's hurt while Jean Luc's window was still down, because a girl who has just been chosen wants a witness. She let Daudi believe the twenty seconds in the rain were the beginning of something. Her own verdict on herself is harsher than the story's: **it cost her nothing and it did not cost him nothing, and she let the difference stand.**

## Arc
**Wanting to be chosen → being priced.**

Starts rehearsing a European register like a costume that hasn't been fitted. Crosses the threshold when she answers to *Chantal* — renamed before Jean Luc has given her anything else, so that everything after is only the account settling. Becomes pregnant, exposed, and takes it to the one institution meant to protect her, where her disclosure is converted into leverage for a bribe rather than evidence. Sits in a back room while three men agree out loud what she is worth, and does the arithmetic along with them, and finds the number smaller than the one she had been carrying.

**Dies off-page.** Discovered the way Daudi discovers everything — as an object among objects. The story's insistence: she is never once seen as fully herself by anyone in it.

Her single act of agency is the last one. She hides the fob where only Daudi will find it, and erases his name from the dust inside her locker so that no one can use it against him — the only thing she ever does for him that costs her something, and the only one he will never know about.

## Relationship Web
- **[[Protagonist - Daudi]]:** The boy who keeps broken things. She gave him the motherboard and never told him. She knows exactly what he thinks the rain meant and lets him think it. She saves him at the end by subtraction — taking her own handwriting back out of the world so it cannot be found next to his name.
- **[[Antagonist - Jean Luc]]:** Not a man to her. A door. She sees a way into Rue Saint Michel; he sees an opportunity. Neither sees a person, which is exactly why the transaction works — until the pregnancy, the one consequence he had not budgeted for.
- **[[Supporting - D'Juma]]:** Her father, who loves her in proverbs and refusals — no cake, no Kim Kardashian, that's a western thing — believing refusal will protect her from wanting. It only drove the wanting underground. She has no way to tell him this and he has no way to say he is afraid.
- **[[Supporting - Morisho]]:** Gives her a free coin off the fare and calls her shemeji. Half-protective, half-claiming, in the same breath. She reads him accurately and files him under harmless-for-now — and he is the one arrested for her death, because he is poor and visible and cannot afford an envelope.
- **Anitha:** Best friend, three months older, gets the two-thousand note for school fees. The friend she envies for the smallest possible things.

## Craft Notes
- **Metaphor register: money, counting, debt, price, change.** Daudi's is physics. Neither may borrow from the other — that asymmetry is the story's engine. He measures her in entropy; she measures herself in accounts receivable.
- Per [[All the Small Things — Grief Through the Minor Object]], the motherboard must stay **unweighted** on first appearance. No adjective may attach to it before the detonation.
- Per [[The Funhouse Mirror — AI Output as Cliché Detector]], the two defaults to refuse are: *the girl who does not understand what is happening to her*, and *the boy who rescues her*. She understands throughout, and nobody rescues anybody.
