# GARDNER — THE FICHTEAN CURVE

> All meaning, in the best fiction, comes from — as Faulkner said — the heart in conflict with itself.

## The Shape

- Energeic plots (see [[Profluence — Resolution, Logical Exhaustion, and Emphasis vs Function]]) fall into exposition, development, and denouement — not divided into equal thirds on the page, since raw exposition has no profluence and readers quit after a few pages of it. Aristotle's fix: begin in the middle of things, feed exposition in as needed.
- The Fichtean curve: line *a* is the safe, stable course the character would take if he cared only about safety; line *b* is the course he actually takes, rising through a series of increasingly intense climaxes as forces both help and oppose his will; the peak of *b* is the climax; what follows is denouement.
- **Internal + external conflict, both required.** Pressure has to come from outside the character *and* from within, "because otherwise the conflict involves no doubt, no moral choice, and as a result can have no profound meaning." External-only conflict is an obstacle course; internal-only conflict is navel-gazing. Both together is the heart in conflict with itself.

## Dispositio — Where to Start

- Starting too early (before the character's stable situation destabilizes) is slow and tedious. Starting too near the climax looks gimmicky and self-regarding. Gardner's model is Homer: the *Iliad* doesn't open with the start of the Trojan War, it opens with the Achilles–Agamemnon argument — the exact point where the underlying conflict (Achilles' idealism vs. Agamemnon's cynicism) becomes visible and irreversible.

## For Kalemie

- "Ending missing" and "Character Arc unclear" on the Dashboard are, on the Fichtean curve, the same gap: the curve has no peak defined, so there's nothing for the denouement to fall from. The planned Crisis Decision (confront Jean Luc or do nothing) is meant to be the peak of line *b* — but per [[The Fichtean Curve]]'s requirement, it only carries weight if forces are shown pushing Daudi toward safety (line *a* — silence, self-protection, the system's indifference) *and* forces pushing him toward the harder choice, both external (what would actually happen if he spoke) and internal (what he privately believes he owes Djamela).
- The inciting-incident fix already underway ("Move Inciting Incident onscreen — Daudi learns Djamela is dead. We see it happen") is the exact moment the Dashboard needs to establish the *destabilization* the Fichtean curve requires: before that scene, Daudi's situation must read as stable (line *a* available and comfortable) so that the reader feels the ground shift when it happens, the way Achilles' stable position collapses the moment Agamemnon claims Briseus.
- Gardner's dispositio principle argues the current Scene 1 placement (D'juma's Words) is defensible only if that's genuinely where the underlying conflict — inaction vs. complicity — first becomes visible and irreversible for Daudi, the way the Achilles/Agamemnon argument is for the *Iliad*, rather than simply the earliest point in the story's raw chronology.
