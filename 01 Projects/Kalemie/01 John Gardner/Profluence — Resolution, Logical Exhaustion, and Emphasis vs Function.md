# GARDNER — PROFLUENCE: RESOLUTION, LOGICAL EXHAUSTION, AND EMPHASIS VS FUNCTION

> By definition and aesthetic necessity a story contains profluence — our sense, as we read, that we're "getting somewhere."

## Profluence Needs a Direction, Not Just Movement

- A story is a sequence of events the reader casts forward from, page to page, wondering what comes next. The moment a reader stops caring where the story is going, the writer has failed. Profluence can come from causal sequence (strongest — "energeic," Aristotle's *energeia*, actualizing the potential in character and situation), from an argued sequence of logical positions (weaker — feels like a lecture), or from picaresque/symbolic juxtaposition (weaker still — an intellectual structure the writer already knows the answer to, easier to build but lacking "the power of inexorable process"). Homer, Shakespeare, and Tolstoy differ *in kind*, not degree, from Dante or Swift, because their plots are energeic rather than intellectual.
- A causal sequence can end only two ways: **resolution** (no further event can occur — the murderer caught, the diamond restored) or **logical exhaustion** (we recognize we've reached infinite repetition; the character is trapped in an empty ritual). Resolution is more satisfying because it gives the thing pursued real weight, achieved or thwarted. Logical exhaustion "satisfies us intellectually but often not emotionally" and risks revealing that the character's exercise of free will was illusory all along — which reads, to the reader, as the writer never having cared about the characters in the first place.

## Emphasis vs. Function

- **Emphasis** = how much narrative time/attention a detail gets. **Function** = the actual work that detail does in the story. When emphasis outruns function — dwelling on a curtain a character merely ducks behind — the moment becomes either a dull spot or a misleading promise of significance that never pays off. Proportion the sentence-time spent on a detail to what that detail is actually *for*.
