# GARDNER — THE VIVID AND CONTINUOUS FICTIONAL DREAM

> Fiction does its work by creating a dream in the reader's mind — vivid because we must be clear what we're dreaming, continuous because a repeatedly interrupted flow of action has less force than one carried straight through.

## The Central Concept

- All three major genres (realistic story, tale, yarn) persuade by the same inner strategy regardless of outer strategy (verisimilitude, suspended disbelief, the visible lie): a steady stream of closely observed, concrete detail that proves, moment by moment, that what's said to be happening is really happening.
- **Vivid** = enough concrete sensory detail that the reader is never unclear who/where/what/why. Abstraction ("creatures," "hostile maneuvers") kills vividness; concrete Anglo-Saxon words (thrash, coil, hiss) build it.
- **Continuous** = nothing breaks the dream once it's running: not clumsy syntax, not authorial throat-clearing, not a slip in logic, not the writer's own ego showing through the style. "It is as if a playwright were to run out on stage, interrupting his characters."
- Metafiction is the deliberate exception — a different mode that interrupts the dream *on purpose*, using the reader's expectation of an unbroken dream as the material it comments on. Kalemie is not metafiction; every technique in this knowledge base is in service of keeping the dream unbroken.

## Direct Presentation vs. Filtering

- "Turning, she noticed two snakes fighting in among the rocks" is weaker than "She turned. In among the rocks, two snakes were fighting" — which is weaker still than "two snakes whipped and lashed, striking at each other." Every layer of *she noticed / she saw / she heard* between the reader and the image is a pane of glass the dream has to pass through.
