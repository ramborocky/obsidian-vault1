# GARDNER — THE RESONANT CLOSE

> The denouement is not simply the end of the story but the story's fulfillment. Read the story over and over, at least a hundred times — literally — watching for subtle meanings, connections, accidental repetitions.

## Elements, Once Introduced, Demand Return

- The writer is "more servant than master" of the story once details are planted — a nightmare, a love scene, a minor character given real weight all call out to recur, because association works both ways: encountering one thing near another means the second thing forever partly evokes the first (Gardner credits Coleridge and Hartley here). A story's ending gets its power not from summarizing what happened but from the accumulated pull of everything the reader has already been quietly trained, by repetition, to associate.
- Model: the closing of Joyce's "The Dead," where every image converging at the end is an image of death, each one dragging its earlier associations behind it.

## Two Failure Modes

- **Overdone**: pushing the meaning so hard the ending feels contrived, "workshop," self-conscious — the reader sees the machinery.
- **Underdone**: so timid, from fear of sentimentality or obviousness, that "not even the angels aflutter in the rafters" can hear the resonance.
- The fix for both is the same: "oonch" implications toward the surface through subtle repetition, a clarifying metaphor, or closer proximity between related images — not through statement.

## For Kalemie

- The "cyclical ending" already decided on the Dashboard — "Daudi finds the envelope, nails it, smiles" — is a resonant-close structure by design, and Gardner's principle sharpens exactly what it needs: the nailing and the smile only land if they pull an *already-planted* association behind them. If nailing something (to a wall? a board? recalling the motherboard's circuitry, or D'juma's lake, or an earlier physical gesture of Daudi's) hasn't been set up earlier in the story, the ending will read as a new image introduced at the last second rather than as fulfillment.
- This gives a concrete revision task: audit the draft for every image the ending is meant to resonate with — the motherboard, the lake, the envelope, entropy, D'juma's words, Djamela's absence — and confirm each one has been planted with enough specific, repeatable texture (not just mentioned once) that its return at the end carries real weight, the way Joyce's death-images throughout "The Dead" earn the final snow.
- Watch the two failure modes specifically: given the Dashboard's own instruction to cut D'juma's lake speech and keep the ending's Controlling Idea implicit rather than stated, the draft is already leaning toward the underdone risk — Gardner's warning about the "angels aflutter in the rafters" not hearing it is the check to run once the cuts are made: does the resonance survive the cutting, or has the story cut past the point where the pattern is still legible?
