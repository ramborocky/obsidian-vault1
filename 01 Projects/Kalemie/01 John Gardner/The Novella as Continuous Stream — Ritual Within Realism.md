# GARDNER — THE NOVELLA AS CONTINUOUS STREAM: RITUAL WITHIN REALISM

> Notice, by the way, how thoroughly realistic all this is, for all its symbolic freighting. The details have the sharp-edged vividness of Edward Weston photographs or realistic painting. Yet nearly every detail works symbolically as well as literally.

## The Model

- Gardner walks through William Gass's novella *The Pedersen Kid* as the ideal short form: one continuous stream of action, one focal character, moving through a series of climaxes that each intensify the last, ending with the character's world "radically changed." The novella's chief beauty is "its almost oriental purity, its elegant tracing of an emotional line" — closer to a tone poem than a symphony.
- The technique that makes it work: ordinary domestic action (a mother reviving a half-frozen boy on the kitchen table) is rendered with total realistic precision — and simultaneously carries full ritual weight (the boy's whiteness recalls flour, the kneading recalls breadmaking, the whiskey recalls eucharistic wine) without the prose ever stepping outside plain, concrete description to announce the symbolism. The realism and the ritual are the same sentences, not two layers stacked on top of each other.

## For Kalemie

- This is the single most useful structural model in Gardner's book for Kalemie's actual form — a short story built as one tight, escalating stream centered on Daudi, moving through a small number of intensifying climaxes (the discovery, the birthday, the BMW, the rain, the police station) to a world radically changed for him by the end. The 7-Day Scene Schedule is already, without naming it, building a Pedersen-Kid-shaped novella-in-miniature.
- The lake is Kalemie's clearest opportunity for exactly this technique. D'juma's line — "some women resemble the lake... it remembers everything" — risks staying stated wisdom unless a scene actually *does* what Gass does with the kitchen table: render an ordinary, physically precise lake-adjacent action (washing, fishing, a body of water glimpsed from the dala dala) with total realism, while the surrounding pattern of images (memory, depth, cold, what sinks and doesn't resurface) quietly does the ritual work without the prose ever leaving plain description.
- The motherboard's three-touch reveal, already planned in Jean Luc's file, could take on the same double life Gardner describes in the whiskey/wine substitution: an ordinary piece of discarded electronics doing real, literal plot work (evidence) while also, through its placement and recurrence, accruing the same kind of unannounced ritual charge — something extracted from a device, disassembled, kept, the way Ma "kneads" the frozen boy on the breadboard.
