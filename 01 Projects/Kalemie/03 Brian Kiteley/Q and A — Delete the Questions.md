# KITELEY — Q AND A (EXERCISE 200)

> You will have twenty answers to hidden questions.

## The Exercise

Write a fragment about something in your story that bothers you **like a bad tooth**, in the form of twenty questions and answers.

1. Write the **questions first**, and spend real time on them — make them tough, probing, unsettling, unusual, unexpected. Kiteley wants them "lovely, strange, evocative, and part of the process of this story."
2. Then write the answers, carefully, over time. Honestly and imaginatively. Do not answer by slyly reproducing the question.
3. **Then delete the questions.**

*450 words.*

## Why It Works

His gloss is the whole justification for the book:

> How often do writers supply unseen questions to the sentences they're piling up on each other? This is the essence of the exercises in this book — unseen questions behind the narrative momentum.

The deletion is the move. Twenty answers to invisible questions reads as fiction with pressure behind it — prose that is clearly responding to something the reader cannot see. That is indistinguishable, on the page, from a narrator with an interior life.

It also solves a specific problem: a question in a manuscript is a note to yourself, and notes to yourself do not become prose by being answered politely. Deleting the question forces the answer to stand on its own, which is the only test that matters.

## For Kalemie

**This exercise was built for this draft, and the fit is almost uncomfortable.**

[[KALEMIE-Draft_with_AI_Questions|The master draft]] is named for its questions. It carries roughly forty of them in `[!AI]` callouts, one block under every scene, plus eight Structural Notes questions at the end. [[The Cold Read Protocol]] already found them unusually good — *"what single line in this scene is the weakest and why?"*, *"is withholding Djamela's name effective dread, or does it feel like a writer's trick?"*, and the best one in the vault, *"the draft cuts here — what is being avoided, and should it be faced?"*

**So step 1 is already done.** The questions exist, they are strange and probing, and they took real work. What has never happened is steps 2 and 3.

**The instruction is therefore exact:**

- Take the seven `[!AI]` blocks. Answer every question **in prose, in Daudi's or Djamela's voice** — not in analysis, not in a reply to me.
- Delete the questions.
- What remains is roughly forty fragments of narrated material generated by the sharpest reading anyone has done of this story.

**Two callouts to start with**, both of which are asking for prose and have been receiving commentary:

> *"Djamela's pregnancy reveal is devastating and matter-of-fact. Does Daudi react to this in the text? He should. What does he feel that he doesn't say?"*

Answer that in Daudi's voice, forty words, then delete the question, and the missing beat [[Character by Salient Detail|Barrett flags as the sawdust-doll fault]] is written.

> *"The draft cuts here — what is being avoided, and should it be faced?"*

Answer that one honestly and it is the same fragment [[What You Are Censoring|exercise 108]] would produce. Two books, arriving from opposite directions, at the same missing page.

**The structural warning.** A draft that carries forty unanswered questions is a draft that has converted its problems into a to-do list, and a to-do list is comfortable in a way an unwritten scene is not. Kiteley's deletion step is what stops that. **The questions are not the work. The answers with the questions removed are the work.**

## Questions for AI

1. Take the opening's `[!AI]` block. Answer every question as prose in Daudi's voice, delete the questions, and show me only what remains.
2. Do the same for Scene 7's block, starting with his unspoken reaction to the pregnancy reveal.
3. Of the forty questions in the draft, which are genuinely questions for the fiction and which are questions for a critic? Separate them; only the first kind get answered this way.
4. Write twenty new questions about the ending that does not exist yet, then answer them, then delete them.
5. After this pass, how many of the `[!AI]` blocks can be removed from the draft entirely because their material is now in the prose?
