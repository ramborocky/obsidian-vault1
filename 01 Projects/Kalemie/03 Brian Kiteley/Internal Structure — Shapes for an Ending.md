# KITELEY — INTERNAL STRUCTURE

> Probably no one but you will notice this structure. So what?

## The Idea

Stories have an imaginary shape the writer supplies, consciously or not. Henry James thought *The Ambassadors* was an hourglass; Woolf wrote *To the Lighthouse* around a shape she drew.

Kiteley's point is not that shapes are meaningful to readers — he concedes they usually are not — but that **holding a shape in mind while writing changes what you produce.** It is a constraint like any other. And his target is the default: linear fiction assumes time moves steadily forward with occasional flashbacks, which is faithful to clocks and not at all to memory.

## The Five Shapes

**185. A Maze** — send the narrator into a situation with connecting passageways that is hard to get out of. Physical, emotional or intellectual. Don't worry about the trick for escaping mazes; evoke *the sensation of being caught in one*. *500 words.* Most good murder mysteries are mazes; the key words in the dictionary definition are *intricate* and *interconnecting*.

**186. An Iceberg** — write a scene in which **most of the actual story is not told**. Let the reader feel the mass bobbing underwater without seeing it concretely. Every narrative leaves a great deal out; do it consciously. *500 words.*

**187. Two Parallel Lines** — two characters or two ideas that by definition never meet. They may see each other; they never know they are on similar tracks. *500 words.* Note the deliberate refusal of the usual payoff, where the lines converge and the reader goes *ah-ha*.

**188. A Circle** — a tale that begins and ends at the same place or idea. *500 words.*

**189. The Infinity Symbol** — a fragment that moves in a horizontal figure-eight, meeting itself endlessly. *500 words.*

## For Kalemie

**The ending has been specified as a shape since 22 July and never written as one.** [[Dashboard|The Dashboard]] calls it "the cyclical ending — Daudi finds the envelope, nails it, smiles." That is **exercise 188** described in the Dashboard's own words. It has stayed unwritten through six weeks and six knowledge bases.

Kiteley's version is a 500-word exercise with no requirement that it be good. That is a materially different task from "write the ending of your Commonwealth submission," and the difference is the entire reason it might get done.

**The Iceberg is the shape the story is already reaching for and has not committed to.** [[2026-07-26 — Chantal (Djamela POV, Draft 2 — Barrett Pass)|Chantal]] does it twice and successfully — *I am not going to describe the hotel*; *Nothing happened to my body in that room*. The master draft does it once, superbly, with the withheld name in the opening, and then abandons the technique. **Exercise 186 run on the police station** would test whether Kalemie's central scene survives having most of itself submerged — and [[The ELIZA Effect and the Vestments of Plausibility|the reader-does-the-work principle]] says it would gain.

**Two Parallel Lines is the diagnostic for the story's real structural problem.** [[The Unities in Miniature|Barrett found Kalemie running two actions]] — the mystery and the memory — and the Scenario's ending pays off one and abandons the other. Exercise 187 is that situation turned into a deliberate form: two lines that never meet. Writing it on purpose, once, would answer whether the doubling is a flaw or the story's actual shape.

**The Maze is worth noting for what it says about the mystery line.** Kalemie has a detective structure — found objects, a wrongly arrested man, a fob key that leads somewhere. Kiteley's note is that a maze is about *the sensation of being caught*, not about the trick for getting out. Kalemie's mystery currently promises an exit it does not provide. Reconceived as a maze, it would not owe one.

**One caution.** Kiteley's shapes are generative constraints, not structural solutions. Do not let the story acquire a sixth planning artefact. **Run 188 first, at 500 words, badly** — then look at whether what comes out can be cut into the draft. See [[Prescriptions for the Live Draft]], Day 5.

## Questions for AI

2. Run exercise 186 on the police station: rewrite it with most of the story underwater. Compare against the current version.
3. Write Kalemie's two actions as exercise 187 — parallel lines that never meet. Does the story lose or gain?
4. What shape is the master draft currently in, whether or not I intended one? Draw it in words.
5. After the circle exercise: how much of those 500 words can be cut straight into the draft, and how much was scaffolding?
