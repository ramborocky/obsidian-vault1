# THE THREE GATES — UNIFIED INSTRUMENT

> The floor asks whether anything is broken. The make asks whether it is well built. The fight asks whether anyone will care.
> Three questions. Three instruments. **Never one number.**

## Why Not Just Add Them Up

Three rubrics now exist in this vault: [[The NAPLAN Floor — Ten Mechanical Criteria|NAPLAN]] (47), [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes|the Nine Axes]] (45), [[The Underground Rubric — Risk, Afterlife and the Room|the Underground Rubric]] (93). The obvious merge is to sum them into a 185-point instrument. **That would be worse than any of the three.**

Three reasons, and they are the whole design:

**1. They overlap heavily.** Underground criterion 2 (*first sentence gravity*) and axis 1 (*opening authority*) are the same measurement. Underground 8 (*earned ending*) is axis 8. Underground 9 (*resistance to meaning*) is axis 7. NAPLAN criterion 1 is axes 1 and 9 at lower resolution. Summing scores the same virtue three times and makes a story with one good opening look like a story with three separate strengths.

**2. They are not commensurable.** A missing ending costs 2 points of 4 on NAPLAN, 4 points of 5 on the Nine Axes, and 5 points of 5 on Underground 8. Adding them means the ending is worth 11 points and the spelling is worth 6, which nobody decided and nobody would defend.

**3. Judges do not average.** [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes|The Nine Axes]] already says it: *judges eliminate on faults, not averages.* A single fatal flaw ends a story regardless of what else is on the page. A summed rubric cannot express that. **Gates can.**

So: three gates in order. **A story that fails a gate does not proceed, and its scores at later gates do not count.**

---

## GATE 1 · THE FLOOR — pass / fail, no score

*Is anything broken? Source: [[The NAPLAN Floor — Ten Mechanical Criteria]] criteria 5–10, plus the vault's hard constraints.*

Binary. Every line must pass. No partial credit, no total.

> [!warning] Split 2026-07-29 — this gate was wrong as first written
> The word ceiling was originally listed beside spelling and cohesion as one pass/fail set. Run against the field in [[08 Three Gates — Field Comparison]] that collapsed at once: **Baldwin fails at ~12,000 words and is the best story measured; Giorgis fails at 5,179 and placed.** Neither failure says anything about the writing.
>
> Two different things were being called one gate. They are now separate, and **only 1a is a judgement about quality.**

### 1a · Hygiene — a failure means the prose is broken

| Check | Passes when |
| --- | --- |
| **Spelling** | No non-words. *Squirmish* and *désvu* are the standing examples of failure |
| **Sentence structure** | Errors are slips, not errors — the writer knows what is wrong |
| **Name consistency** | Every proper noun spelled one way. *D'jamela/Djamela* is the standing failure |
| **Tense control** | No lurch between past and present narration |
| **Cohesion — the right-moment test** | Every unglossed term either recovers from context or is glossed by the next sentence. **No plot-critical fact is held in a language the reader may not have** |
| **Punctuation** | Consistent *system*, whether or not it is conventional. A style decision passes; a comma splice does not |
| **Paragraphing** | No block that should be split, no break that should be joined |

### 1b · Eligibility — a failure means wrong door, not bad story

| Check | Passes when |
| --- | --- |
| **Word ceiling** | Prose is 2,000–5,000 words for the Commonwealth. Measured, not estimated |
| **Genre and form** | Single short story, not an extract |
| **Deadline and rules** | Entry window open, entrant eligible, story unpublished per the panel's terms |

**A 1b failure never lowers a score.** It only means this story cannot be posted to this prize. Two of the four stories on [[08 Three Gates — Field Comparison|the field board]] fail 1b, and one of them is the ceiling.

**Run 1a last chronologically, first logically.** Never run it on prose that might still be cut — that is the error [[The Five Passes]] exists to prevent. But nothing that fails here should be scored at Gate 2 at all, because polishing the argument of a story with a typo on page one is the wrong order of worry.

## GATE 2 · THE MAKE — /45

*Is it well built? Source: [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes]], unchanged.*

The nine axes stand exactly as written — opening authority, prose control, character interiority, scene turn, the gap, structural economy, controlling idea, resonant close, something to say. Each 1–5.

**Unchanged rules carry over.** A single 1 on any axis caps the story regardless of total. Score only what is on the page today; the ending you plan to write is a 0 until it exists.

This gate is the largest because execution is the largest part of the work, and because it is the only gate that measures *control* — which is what separates a risk at Gate 3 from a mess.

---

## GATE 3 · THE FIGHT — /25

*Will anyone argue for it? Source: [[The Underground Rubric — Risk, Afterlife and the Room]], five criteria only.*

The Underground Rubric has twenty-one criteria. Seven cannot be honestly scored by the author. Nine more duplicate Gate 2 or Gate 1. **Five survive** — the ones that are both genuinely new to this vault and genuinely assessable from the page. Each 1–5.

| # | Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | **The dangerous ingredient** | Completely safe | Consistent risk-taking | A risk that makes the story necessary rather than merely bold |
| G2 | **Ugly honesty** | Flawed narrator, fully redeemed | Flawed, no full redemption | The reader is made complicit in the ugly |
| G3 | **The slide** | The shift is announced (*then I realised*) | Clean slide, barely noticed | The reader does not notice until after it has happened |
| G4 | **The leakage** | Everything contained and tidy | Multiple deliberate leaks | Leaks that make the world larger than the story |
| G5 | **Defensibility** | One weak argument for it | Multiple strong arguments | A story a judge would fight for out loud |

**Why these five and not the others** — the working the merge is built on:

| Dropped | Because |
| --- | --- |
| Voice specificity, first sentence gravity | = Gate 2 axes 1 and 2 |
| Trust differential, the unanswered question | = Gate 2 axis 5 (the gap) |
| Resistance to meaning, one-sentence core | = Gate 2 axis 7 (controlling idea) |
| The earned ending | = Gate 2 axis 8 |
| The sentence that hurts, shareable sentence | Reader-response, not page-assessable |
| Physical verbs ratio | = Gate 1, and already measured in every draft's craft audit |
| The hands principle | Subset of Gate 2 axis 3 |
| Room temperature, slow burn, judge's private fear | **Unknowable to the author.** Gate 4 |
| Writer stopped caring | Unfalsifiable by anyone |
| The ultimate test | = G5 defensibility, worded harder |

**G2 is the one that justifies the whole gate.** Axis 9 asks whether the story has a position. G2 asks whether the reader is implicated in it. Nothing else in this vault scores that, and it is the difference between a story about exploitation and a story that makes the reader feel how exploitation gets agreed to.

## GATE 4 · THE AFTERLIFE — not a score

*What survives three days? Source: Underground section F. **Cannot be self-assessed. Ever.***

The author cannot score whether a stranger still thinks about the story on Thursday. Neither can I. So this is a **protocol, not a number** — hand the draft to a cold reader, wait 72 hours, ask exactly three questions and write down the answers verbatim:

1. Without looking at it — what do you remember?
2. Is there a line you could say back to me?
3. If this story did not exist, would anything be missing?

Answers go in [[Craft Log]] with the date and the reader's name. See [[The Cold Read Protocol]] for how to ask so that you get an honest answer instead of a kind one.

**A story with no Gate 4 evidence is unscored at Gate 4, not full marks at Gate 4.** This is the single most common way a rubric gets used to flatter a draft.

## Reading The Result

Report as three values, never one: **`FLOOR: pass · MAKE: n/45 · FIGHT: n/25`**

The combined 70 is only meaningful once the floor has passed.

| MAKE + FIGHT | Verdict | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **56–70** | Competing | Gate 4 protocol, then submit |
| **42–55** | Real draft | The work is refinement, not rebuilding |
| **Below 42** | Structural | Stop polishing. Something is missing, not weak |

**Caps override totals, always:**

- Any Gate 1 failure → the story is not scored. Fix and re-run.
- Any Gate 2 axis at 1 → capped regardless of total.
- **Gate 3 at 20+ with Gate 2 below 30 → the most dangerous result on this instrument.** It means a story with real risk and no control, which reads as raw courage to the writer and as a mess to a judge. The fix is always at Gate 2.
- Gate 2 at 36+ with Gate 3 below 12 → competent and safe. The classic longlist-and-no-further story.

**Chantal G3 — the slide, 3.** The lowest single score of the three finished drafts, and it comes directly out of Chantal's greatest strength. The narration is declarative and in control from the first line — *I want to tell this part exactly*, *I am not asking to be forgiven*. A voice that announces its own retrospection cannot slide, because it never pretends to be discovering anything. Gate 2 rewards that control with 40/45. Gate 3 charges for it. **Both are correct, and the tension between them is the most useful thing this instrument has produced.**

**Chantal G2 — ugly honesty, 5.** *The hurt came off him like heat off a wall and I stood in it and warmed my hands.* A thirteen-year-old using a boy who loves her, describing it accurately, asking for nothing. The reader is not permitted the comfort of pitying her.

**Found Things — 21, but Gate 1 fails.** The score does not count. The three unglossed Swahili sentences carrying the inciting incident fail the right-moment test, and this instrument does not score a draft that fails the floor. Fix that one paragraph and the 21 becomes real. G2 is 4 rather than 5 because the narration is gentle with Daudi — the reader is made *superior* to him, not complicit with him, which is a different and easier effect.

**Beat of My Hurt — 25/25 at Gate 3, and no Gate 2 score at all.** *I have had that feeling over a child. I had it, and then I ate the food, and then I slept.* That is G1 and G2 at once: a risk that could get it rejected, and an honesty that hands the reader their own professional satisfaction at a wrong moment. G3 is the textbook case — *And I saw the fire in our own yard. Not remembered. Saw* — a slide the reader is inside before noticing. G4 is everywhere: the railway that ends, the chair man with two hundred chairs who has never attended a funeral, the three men whose names are not said.

## How To Run It

1. **Gate 2 first, while the story is being made.** Monthly, per [[Learning Curve]].
2. **Gate 3 when a draft is complete.** It is cheap — five criteria, twenty minutes.
3. **Gate 1 last**, after [[The Five Passes]], never on prose that might be cut.
4. **Gate 4 always by somebody else**, 72 hours after they read it.
5. Log all three values and the date in [[Craft Log]]. Never log the sum alone.

## Questions for AI

1. Score the current draft at all three gates independently, without reading the standing scores. Report every gate where you differ, and defend it. If Gate 1 fails, stop and say so rather than scoring the rest.
2. Run Gate 2 on [[The Beat of My Hurt (Draft 4 — Free)|Beat of My Hurt]] — all nine axes, scored cold and unkindly. This is the single largest hole in the vault's evidence and the only thing standing between a 25/25 and a decision.
3. Gate 3 criterion G3: find the exact sentence in [[2026-07-28 — Chantal (Draft 3, Prize Pass)|Chantal]] where a slide could be built without breaking the declarative voice. If there is no such place, say so — that is a real answer and it means the 3 is permanent, not a fault.
4. The dangerous-result rule says Gate 3 ≥ 20 with Gate 2 < 30 is the worst outcome on this instrument. Find a published prize-winning story that would score that way, and explain why it won anyway. If the rule survives that test it stays; if it does not, rewrite it.
5. Which single gate, raised, most changes the submission decision? Name it, and name what it would cost in words.
