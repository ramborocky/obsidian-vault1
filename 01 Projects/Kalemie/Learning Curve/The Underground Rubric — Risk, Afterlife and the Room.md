# THE UNDERGROUND RUBRIC — RISK, AFTERLIFE AND THE ROOM

> "Assessed on courage, not competence."
> — Section E header, as received

## Provenance — Read This Before Using It

This arrived as a document titled *The Underground Judging Rubric*, marked **"Official Use Only — Judges' Internal Assessment"** and presented as the internal instrument of the Caine Prize, Commonwealth Short Story Prize, O. Henry and the BBC National Short Story Award.

**It is not.** Those four prizes share no common scoring instrument, none of them publishes one, and no such internal document is in circulation. The insider framing is invented.

This is not a reason to throw it away. [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes|The Nine Axes]] is also home-made — built in this vault from Doughty's chair statement and the craft bases — and it is useful because it is honest about what it is. Same rule here. **Kept for its criteria, stripped of its costume.** Nothing in it should ever be cited as what judges do.

Its real value is that it names three things neither [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes|the Nine Axes]] nor [[The NAPLAN Floor — Ten Mechanical Criteria|the NAPLAN Floor]] contains at all:

- **Risk.** Both existing rubrics score execution. Neither asks whether the story was worth the danger of writing.
- **Complicity.** Axis 9 asks whether you have a position. This asks whether the reader is implicated in it.
- **The room.** Prizes are decided by one judge arguing for a story in front of other judges. Nothing else in this vault scores *whether an argument can be made.*

## The Twenty-One Criteria

Seven sections. Criteria 1–18 score 0–5, criteria 19–21 score 0 or 1. Maximum 93.

### A · First Contact — /15 · assessed in the first 60 seconds

| # | Criterion | 0 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Voice specificity** | Generic, could be anyone | Strong, consistent | Changes how I hear language |
| 2 | **First sentence gravity** | No pull | Must read more | A sentence I'll tell someone about |
| 3 | **Trust differential** | Explains everything | Trusts the reader with some mystery | Refuses to explain the right things |

### B · Visceral Impact — /15 · during reading and 24 hours after

| # | Criterion | 0 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | **The sentence that hurts** | None memorable | One that stopped me | One that changed something in me |
| 5 | **The slide** | No shift from observation to being | Clean slide, barely noticed | Slide I didn't notice until after it happened |
| 6 | **Room temperature change** | No physical reaction | Put the manuscript down once | Had to stop and process |

### C · Structural Integrity — /15 · full reading; penalties for visible construction

| # | Criterion | 0 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | **The unanswered question** | Everything answered | One significant question unresolved | A question that changes the reading |
| 8 | **The earned ending** | Comes from nowhere | Earned but expected | Earned, surprising, changes the rest |
| 9 | **Resistance to meaning** | Announces its theme | Resists meaning in one place | Makes meaning the reader's job |

### D · Craft Underground — /15 · the invisible technical work

| # | Criterion | 0 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | **Physical verbs ratio** | >80% thinking/feeling verbs | ~50% | <30%, grounded |
| 11 | **The hands principle** | No hand or body description | Hands described meaningfully once | Hands carry the unspoken story |
| 12 | **The leakage** | Everything contained, tidy | Multiple deliberate leaks | Leaks that haunt the reader |

### E · Risk Factor — /15 · courage, not competence

| # | Criterion | 0 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 13 | **The dangerous ingredient** | Completely safe | Consistent risk-taking | Risk that makes the story necessary |
| 14 | **Ugly honesty** | Good person learning a lesson | Flawed, no full redemption | Makes the reader complicit in the ugly |
| 15 | **The moment the writer stopped caring** | Performing throughout | Consistent authenticity | The whole thing feels necessary |

### F · Afterlife — /15 · assessed 24–72 hours after reading

| # | Criterion | 0 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 16 | **The slow burn** | Forgotten immediately | Still thinking about it next day | Changed how I see something |
| 17 | **The shareable sentence** | No quotable lines | Three lines I'd say aloud | Lines I'll quote in my head forever |
| 18 | **Defensibility** | No reason to advocate | Multiple strong arguments | A story I will fight for in the room |

### G · Final Intangible — /3 · score 0 or 1, no middle ground

| # | Criterion | 0 | 1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 19 | **The one-sentence core** | Cannot say what makes this matter | Can say exactly why this story exists |
| 20 | **The judge's private fear** | Didn't linger | Made the judge afraid of missing it |
| 21 | **The ultimate test** | Would not matter if it disappeared | Would matter if it didn't exist |

### Bands

| Total | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| 75–93 | Winner — defensible, memorable, necessary |
| 60–74 | Shortlist — usually Risk or Afterlife needs strengthening |
| 45–59 | Longlist — competent but safe; needs a dangerous ingredient |
| 30–44 | Honourable mention — proficient but forgettable |
| Below 30 | Reject — performing, not telling the truth |

## Seven Criteria The Author Cannot Score

This is the most important section on the page.

**Criteria 6, 16, 20 measure a stranger's nervous system.** *Room temperature change* asks whether a reader put the manuscript down. *The slow burn* asks what they still think about three days later. *The judge's private fear* asks whether they were frightened of missing it. **The writer cannot know these. Nor can I.** Any number placed there is invented, and inventing it inflates the total by up to 11 points out of 93 — enough to move a story two whole bands.

**Criteria 2, 4, 17 are reader-response dressed as craft.** *A sentence I'll tell someone about* is a fact about the reader, not the page. The author's guess is worth something, but it is a guess and should be marked as one.

**Criterion 15 is unfalsifiable.** *The moment the writer stopped caring* cannot be verified from the text by anyone, including the writer, who has every reason to believe they were sincere throughout.

That leaves **fourteen criteria** that can honestly be scored from the page. Those fourteen are where the value is, and [[The Three Gates — Unified Instrument]] takes the five best of them that nothing else in this vault already covers.

> [!warning] The self-benchmark that arrived with this document
> The rubric came with a filled-in scorecard rating [[The Beat of My Hurt (Draft 4 — Free)|Beat of My Hurt]] at **85–90/93**, followed by: *"Submit it. Don't wait. Don't revise again."*
>
> Set aside whether the story is good — it may well be. The scorecard scores *Slow Burn* 5 and *Defensibility* 5, two of the criteria listed above as unknowable to the author, and it was produced the same day as the draft. [[The Cold Read Protocol|The Cold Read Protocol]] exists in this vault specifically to prevent that, and the standing rule is **score cold after seven days, and not on one voice alone.** A rubric that tells you to stop revising, on the day you finished writing, is not a judging instrument. It is applause.
>
> The Gate 3 scores in [[The Three Gates — Unified Instrument]] are lower and are defended line by line. Use those.

## Overrule It On Two Things

**1. The authority claim.** Covered above. Never cite this as prize practice.

**2. "Risk that could get it rejected" as a virtue in itself.** Criterion 13 scores danger, and danger is not automatically merit — [[The Art of Fiction by John Gardner|Gardner]] would call an unearned one sentimentality wearing a leather jacket. The criterion is only useful with the [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes|Nine Axes]] beside it, because control is what separates a risk from a mess. **This is the strongest single argument for the merged instrument rather than this rubric on its own.**
