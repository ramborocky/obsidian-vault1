# CREATIVITY AMPLIFICATION — VINGE'S IA AND WHAT SURVIVES OF IT

> Source note for the [InGAME / University of Dundee repository](https://github.com/IngameDundee/CreativityAmplification), Lynda Clark, Research & Development Fellow for Narrative and Play. Read this one to know what the source actually is — and what in it has expired.

## The Theory

In 1993 Vernor Vinge gave a paper at NASA's Vision-21 conference predicting machine superintelligence within thirty years. The part that got attention was the threat. The part Clark builds on is the alternative Vinge offered alongside it: **Intelligence Amplification** — improving human capability by improving access to information and the means of communicating it. His question, which is the seed of the whole repository: we have programs that play chess far better than people, but how much work has been done on how a person might *use* that power to reach something better?

Clark's contribution is to run that question through creative writing practice and rename the result **Creativity Amplification** — technology used to extend a writer's own capabilities rather than to replace them.

Her formulation of the correct posture is the most quotable thing in the repository, and it is worth holding onto:

**AI alone is not better than you. Used as a tool, it is better than you alone.**

Her repository defines Creativity Amplification as necessarily exploratory, experimental, playful and open-ended — applying skills the writer already has, requiring no coding, and either enabling new forms of expression or letting existing ones develop faster or with more complexity.

## What Has Expired

Honesty about the source matters more than reverence for it. **The four toolkit PDFs are substantially obsolete.** They were written around 2020–21 and their practical recommendations are dead:

- **Zo**, Microsoft's chatbot and the centrepiece of Clark's most interesting collaboration, was taken offline in 2019.
- **Mitsuku**, the fallback, is a keyword-matching bot from 2005 whose failure modes fill several pages.
- **GPT-2**, **Write with Transformer**, **TextSynth**, **talktotransformer.com** — the exercises are built on capability levels that no longer describe anything current.
- The exercises themselves (*generate a face on thispersondoesnotexist and answer five questions about them*; *ask a chatbot what it would do if it lost a wedding ring*) are block-breaking prompts that any current model makes trivial.

**Do not work the exercises.** They were designed around scarcity — the difficulty of getting a machine to produce anything usable — and that scarcity is gone.

Plus one process idea worth stating here rather than in its own note:

**Keep the transcripts.** Clark could only analyse her chatbot conversations years later because she had kept the logs of exchanges that seemed disposable at the time. Her reflection is that this changed how she takes notes generally. For Kalemie this argues for keeping the AI sessions that produced material — not the outputs, which are already in the draft, but the *exchanges*, because what you asked and what came back is evidence about your own defaults that is unavailable any other way.

## The Honest Assessment

This repository was not written for a literary short-story writer. It was written for writers moving into games, interactive TV and AI-driven narrative, by a researcher whose own practice is largely interactive fiction. Its case studies are about Twine pieces and voice-assistant narratives. **Roughly 80% of it does not apply to Kalemie.**

The 20% that does apply is unusually good, and it is good precisely where Clark stops writing as a toolkit author and writes as a practising short-story writer reflecting on her own losses and her own clichés. That is the material in the four notes above.

## For Kalemie

The relevant application is not "use AI to write Kalemie." It is that **you are already doing Creativity Amplification**, and this repository gives it a name and a discipline.

The vault has a `09 AI Laboratory/` folder, an `[!AI]` callout under every scene in the master draft, a file explicitly quarantined as *Claude's Version of Kalemie (AI Draft, Not Canon)*, and a Dashboard section of ready-made AI prompts. That is a functioning CA practice built without the theory, and the quarantined-draft file in particular is a better instinct than anything in the toolkits — it keeps the machine's output structurally separate from the canon rather than letting it seep in.

Three adjustments the theory suggests:

**1. The role you are using me in is too narrow.** Clark catalogues several: AI as challenger (proposing what the story might be), as lead creative (producing raw material for a human editor), as student (carefully steered toward a target output), and as analyst (examining a corpus for patterns). Kalemie's `[!AI]` callouts use me almost exclusively as **challenger** — asking whether things land. The role that is entirely unused is **analyst**, and it is the one with the most to offer here: pattern-finding across the whole draft, which is what produced every measurement in [[Kalemie — Standing Diagnosis]].

**2. Never use me as lead creative on this story.** *Claude's Version of Kalemie (AI Draft, Not Canon)* is correctly named and correctly quarantined. Kalemie's value is a specific voice in a specific place — a boy who thinks in entropy, in a port town, code-switching without italics. That is the least probable thing in the draft and therefore exactly what a model dilutes. See [[The Funhouse Mirror — AI Output as Cliché Detector]].

**3. The playfulness clause is a real instruction, not decoration.** Clark's process begins from exploration with no agenda before exploration with purpose. [[Dashboard|The Dashboard]] currently has 17 tracked fixes, a 7-day schedule, and a progress table showing 0 of 17 complete. That is entirely purpose and no play — and a draft with two openings and no ending is a draft that needs one afternoon of writing something that does not have to be kept.

## Questions for AI

1. Act as **analyst**, not challenger: find three patterns across the whole draft that no per-scene question could surface.
2. What is Kalemie's least probable feature — the thing least likely to appear in any other Commonwealth Prize entry? How is the draft currently protecting or diluting it?
3. Review the `[!AI]` callouts and classify each by role (challenger / lead creative / student / analyst). Report the distribution.
4. Give me a no-agenda exercise for Kalemie's world that produces material I am explicitly not allowed to put in the story.
5. What in this repository have I not applied that I should have? Answer only if there is something real — say "nothing" otherwise.
