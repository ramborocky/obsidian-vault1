# THE FIVE PASSES

> Never polish a sentence in a scene you might cut.

## The Reason Revision Feels Endless

Most writers revise by opening the draft at page one and improving whatever they meet. This mixes structural decisions and comma decisions in the same hour, and the result is predictable: hours are spent perfecting the rhythm of a paragraph that gets deleted three weeks later, and the writer concludes revision is bottomless.

It is not bottomless. It is **five passes, in a fixed order**, and the order is the whole technique. Each pass may only make decisions at its own altitude. When you notice a lower-altitude problem mid-pass, you write it on a list and keep going.

**The order is non-negotiable because each pass destroys the work of the passes below it.** A structural cut deletes line edits. A line edit breaks a sound pattern. Running them in the wrong order is not inefficient — it is self-erasing.

## Pass 1 — Structure (the axe)

Only three verbs: **cut, merge, reorder.** No writing.

- Does the story start in the right place? Does it start twice?
- Does every scene turn? A scene that does not turn is merged or cut, never improved.
- Is anything unwritten? An unwritten ending is a structural fact, not a later problem.
- Word budget against the submission ceiling.

Finish the pass with a scene list, not a draft.

## Pass 2 — Scene (the gap)

Scene by scene, one question each: **what does the POV character expect, and what do they get?** Widen the distance. This is where new writing happens, and it is the *only* pass where the draft is allowed to get longer.

Also here: continuity. Ages, dates, names, the order events are learned in.

## Pass 3 — Line (the sentence)

Now, and not before, the sentence rules apply. Average length. Perception verbs replaced by the physical action that *is* the perception. "To be" verbs. Metaphors checked against the character's own vocabulary. Register spikes flattened to the surrounding altitude.

Mechanical, checkable, fast — if passes 1 and 2 are genuinely finished.

## Pass 4 — Sound (read aloud, standing up)

Out loud, on your feet, at reading pace. The mouth catches what the eye forgives: repeated word-shapes, sentences that land on the wrong beat, dialogue no throat would produce, unintentional rhyme. Mark where you stumble; the stumble is always real even when the cause takes a while to find.

Also the pass for deliberate sound work — planting a noise early and echoing it later under changed conditions.

## Pass 5 — Cold (the stranger)

Minimum **seven days** of not looking at it. Then read it once, at speed, start to finish, making no edits — only marks where attention slipped.

Attention slips are the only reliable proxy you have for a judge's experience of your own work. You cannot get this pass by wanting it; the seven days are the mechanism.

## For Kalemie

The draft is **mid-Pass-1 and has been treated as if it were in Pass 3.**

[[Dashboard|The Dashboard]]'s craft cheat sheet — read every session, by design — is almost entirely Pass 3 material: the 20-word rule, banned perception verbs, "to be" minimisation, metaphor sourcing. **The Dashboard's 7-day one-scene-a-day schedule mixes all five passes into every single day.** Day 1 alone asks for an inciting-incident move (Pass 2), hashtag cleanup (Pass 3), a person-tense fix (Pass 3) and making the motherboard meaningful (Pass 1 — it is a structural plant). Four altitudes in one sitting, which is exactly the condition this note exists to prevent.

**The repair is cheap:** run one clean Pass 1 across the whole story first — cut, merge, reorder, and write the ending. Estimated cost: three deletions and ~400 new words. Then the seven-day scene schedule becomes what it was designed to be, a Pass 2 sweep, and the sentence rules on the Dashboard become genuinely useful instead of premature.

## Questions for AI

1. Run Pass 1 on the full draft and return only a scene list — every scene marked keep / merge / cut / reorder — with no prose edits and no encouragement.
2. For each scene marked keep, state in one sentence what the POV character expects and what they get. Flag any scene where those are the same thing; those are the scenes that do not turn.
3. I am about to line-edit a scene. Confirm first whether that scene survives Pass 1 — refuse the line edit if it does not.
4. Re-sequence the Dashboard's 7-day schedule so that each day operates at exactly one pass altitude, keeping the same total work.
5. After Pass 1 closes, re-check word count against the 5,000 ceiling and report headroom before any Pass 2 expansion begins.
