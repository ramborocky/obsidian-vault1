# ☑️ THE ANNOTATION CHECKLIST — COVERAGE PASS

> "Think of annotations as *showing your work* while you read, just as you sometimes show your work in a math problem… **If you can't articulate your thoughts, then you have to question if you know what you're thinking.**"
> — Mrs. Kody

## What this is, and what it is not

A **fifteen-category checklist** for annotating a short story, written for students. Characterization, setting, plot, conflict, themes, point of view, dialogue, literary techniques, tone and mood, foreshadowing, character relationships, title significance, irony and symbolism, author's style, and open questions.

**It is a comprehension instrument, not a craft instrument** — and this vault already knows exactly what to do with one of those.

> [!abstract] It stands to [[The Appreciative Read — Shaylin's Method|Shaylin's method]] as [[The NAPLAN Floor — Ten Mechanical Criteria|NAPLAN]] stands to [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes|the Nine Axes]]
> | | The deep instrument | The floor |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | **Literary judgement** | [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes\|Nine Axes]] | [[The NAPLAN Floor — Ten Mechanical Criteria\|NAPLAN Floor]] |
> | **Reading another writer** | [[The Appreciative Read — Shaylin's Method\|Shaylin]] | **this note** |
>
> Shaylin goes **deep, in sequence, on whatever catches her eye** — and by construction, she can miss a whole category without noticing. Mrs. Kody goes **shallow and exhaustive** and cannot miss one.
> **Neither is a substitute. The checklist's entire value is that it finds the thing you didn't look at.**

## The one idea worth keeping outright

**"Showing your work."** It is the best justification for annotation in any of the three sources this vault now holds, and it is a stronger claim than it first looks:

> **Thinking is a word-based activity, not a nebulous puff of energy. If you can't articulate it, question whether you know it.**

That is the argument for this entire folder. It is also the argument against reading a prize winner and coming away with *"it was really good."* → [[Learning Curve]] was built on the same premise without ever stating it this cleanly.

## Overrule it on three things

Same treatment [[Short Story Writing by Charles Raymond Barrett|Barrett]] and [[The NAPLAN Floor — Ten Mechanical Criteria|NAPLAN]] get, for the same reason.

**1 · "Annotate everything" produces coverage, not insight.** Fifteen categories applied to every passage yields a fully highlighted story and no thinking. **Run the list once, as an audit, after the real reading — never as the reading itself.** Shaylin's forty minutes contain perhaps twenty observations, and each is worth more than a marked-up page.

**2 · Its plot vocabulary assumes a shape most good stories don't have.** *Exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, resolution.* Shaylin rejects this explicitly as a reading lens — *"I'm not thinking, where's the inciting incident and does it fall [here]"* — and [[Ling Ma — Los Angeles|the story she chose]] turns at its midpoint, while [[Dite — Story Sheet|*Dite*]] runs on instruction-epigraphs and an object-chorus. **Use the categories; discard the arc.**

**3 · It has no category for *what should I steal?*** Every one of the fifteen is comprehension-facing — understand, analyse, discuss. **Not one is production-facing.** For a writer preparing a submission, that is the only category that pays. Add it as a sixteenth and treat it as the one that matters.

The checklist's job is to find what was missed. **It found four things.**

**Four real gaps, and one of them changes a verdict.** All four have now been written into the two sheets:

- **Halim · setting** — the flat is the only location in the story, it is being emptied in real time, and the emptying *is* the plot. Annotated at length as a structural engine and never once as a *place*.
- **Halim · conflict, typed** — and this is the one that matters. Naming the conflict types exposes that the story's central conflict is **character vs. society**, that it is genuinely unresolvable inside the story's world, and that this is a **more sympathetic explanation of the ending** than the one the sheet gave. It does not overturn the finding. It does qualify it, and the sheet now says so.
- **Ling Ma · title significance** — *Los Angeles* names the setting, and the story's one flat statement about that setting is *there is no pattern, there is no meaning.* The title is the diagnosis. Missed entirely.
- **Tone and mood as distinct from register** — register was scored on both; mood was never named on Halim.

> [!success] The instrument did its job, and it did it in the one way that counts
> A checklist built for schoolchildren found a **verdict-qualifying gap** in an analysis written with six craft bases and a nine-axis rubric.
> **That is exactly what happened when [[The NAPLAN Floor — Ten Mechanical Criteria|NAPLAN]] was run on Chantal** — a rubric that "will flatter this project every single time" produced three findings, one of which (*shemeji*) the Nine Axes had no mechanism to see.
> **Cheap, shallow, exhaustive instruments catch what deep ones step over.** Two independent confirmations now. Stop treating a low-level rubric as beneath the work.

## For Kalemie

**Run the sixteen categories against Chantal Draft 3 once, as an audit, after [[The Five Passes]] and beside [[The NAPLAN Floor — Ten Mechanical Criteria|the NAPLAN pass]].** Not as a reading — as a coverage check on the analysis that already exists.

Three categories are worth predicting in advance, because the vault's own notes suggest they're where a gap will be:

1. **Character relationships.** [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes|The rubric]] already records that *Djamela is seen only from outside.* A category that asks specifically about relationships — not characters — may locate why.
2. **Title significance.** *Chantal* is a person's name, and the story is narrated by Djamela. **Nothing in the vault has ever asked what the title is doing.** → [[07 Final Questions — Chantal]] does not cover it.
3. **Conflict, typed.** The Controlling Idea is *inaction is complicity*, which is a **character vs. self** claim. If the draft's live conflict is actually character vs. society, that mismatch is worth knowing before the final pass.
