# THE FIRST-PAGE CUT

> "Confidence of tone, announcing themselves from the very first line."
> — stated criterion, Commonwealth Short Story Prize judging panel

## The Arithmetic Nobody Tells Entrants

The Commonwealth Short Story Prize longlists **200** stories. An international panel of **six** judges — one per Commonwealth region — reduces that to roughly **25**. Then five regional winners, then one overall.

Seven in eight longlisted stories are eliminated by people who have already read a great deal that week. These are not hostile readers. They are *saturated* ones, which is worse.

**A saturated reader is not looking for a reason to keep reading. They are looking for permission to stop.** Every unearned sentence on your first page is that permission.

## The Six Permissions

In rough order of how often they end a story:

1. **The double start.** The story opens, then opens again. Two first pages means the writer cannot judge their own work — a verdict about the writer, not the page.
2. **The throat-clear.** Scene-setting, weather, geography, or history before anything is at stake. The reader is being asked to bank information against a promise of future interest.
3. **The unearned abstraction.** A large claim (about death, colonialism, memory) made before the story has bought the right to make it.
4. **The register spike.** One sentence reaching visibly higher than the ones around it. It reads as the writer performing rather than the narrator speaking.
5. **The continuity slip.** An age, a date, a name that contradicts itself. Costs the writer their authority permanently — every later ambiguity now reads as accident.
6. **The competent nothing.** No error at all, and no reason to continue. The most common death by far, and the hardest to diagnose from inside.

## What Buys A Page

Only two things reliably, and they are the same two Gardner and McKee arrive at from opposite directions:

- **A specific, physical, unexplained detail** that implies a world the reader has not been told about.
- **A gap** — the narrator expects one thing, the page delivers another — opened *before* the reader knows enough to be comfortable.

Note what is not on that list: beautiful prose, an interesting setting, an important subject. Judges see hundreds of important subjects a week.

## For Kalemie

Kalemie's Option C opening **buys its page** — and then spends the credit immediately.

**What buys it:** the crowd with their backs turned; *"the founder of dead things"*; the boda boda boy who will not meet Daudi's eyes; and above all the withheld name — a whole neighbourhood knows who died and the narrator will not write it down. That is permission #6 defeated outright. Nobody stops reading there.

**What spends it:** three of the six permissions are live on the current first two pages.

- **Permission 1, the double start** — the opening closes on D'juma's *"some women resemble the lake"* (`line 67`), and Scene 1 immediately re-opens on the same line, unpacked across three explanatory paragraphs (`line 86`). See [[Kalemie — Standing Diagnosis]] fault #1. This is the one that will actually end the story in round one.
- **Permission 5, the continuity slip** — *"corpses aged twelve to seventeen, yet not much younger than me"* (`line 33`) against a thirteen-year-old Djamela and her peer narrator. Caught on page one. Authority gone.
- **Permission 3, the unearned abstraction** — the first sentence is a 34-word construction in which *"rotten carcasses hidden under the guise of mineral mines"* asks the reader to hold a colonial thesis before meeting a single person. The second line (*"the founder of dead things"*) is far stronger and does the same work by implication. The story's actual best first line is currently its third.

**The test to run:** cover everything after *"In our neighbourhood they called me the founder of dead things."* Does the story lose anything by opening there? If not, that is the opening.

Kalemie is not at risk of the competent nothing. It is at risk of the three faults that make a saturated reader decide the writer is not yet in control of their own material — while holding, three sentences in, one of the better opening lines in the pile.

## Questions for AI

1. Read only the first 200 words of the draft, as a saturated judge on story 140 of 200. State the exact sentence at which you would stop, and why — no encouragement, no summary of strengths.
2. Test the opening with the first sentence deleted, so the story begins on *"In our neighbourhood they called me the founder of dead things."* Report what is genuinely lost, if anything.
3. Which of the six permissions is live on the current first page that this note has not named?
4. Rewrite the first sentence so the colonial claim is carried by one physical detail instead of an abstraction — under 20 words, in Daudi's own register.
5. After the double start is cut, re-read the first page cold and score it on [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes]] axis 1 (Opening Authority) alone.
