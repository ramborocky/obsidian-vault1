# THE EXPLAINING SENTENCES — CHANTAL D3

> [!quote] The rule
> **We write scenes, not explanations.** A sentence that explains the sentence before it is doing the reader's job for them, and the reader can feel it being done.

> [!danger] The prose is the author's. Every fix here is the author's.
> This note **locates** and **asks**. It contains no replacement sentences and never will — see [[12 The Rules — Source of Truth]] rule 3e. Where a line needs to go, the question in the right-hand column is the tool; the sentence that replaces it is yours.

**Line numbers refer to [[2026-07-28 — Chantal (Draft 3, Prize Pass)]].** Work top-down: Tier 1 is free, Tier 4 is a decision you have already made once.

> [!tip] Why there are no checkboxes in this file
> `Home.md` runs a global `tasks not done limit 8` query. Thirty checkboxes here would fill it and hide the real blockers. Mark the **Done** column instead.

## TIER 1 · The narrator explaining her own story to a listener

**These do not gloss a gesture. They step outside the story and address someone.** This is the device the [[The Underground Rubric — Risk, Afterlife and the Room|Underground Rubric]] scores as **the slide** — *a shift from observation to being* — and Chantal caps at **3** on it. A reader who is being addressed stays outside the person addressing them.

| Done | Line | The sentence | The question |
| :-: | :-: | --- | --- |
| ☐ | 47 | *I am telling you about the Tuesday first because everybody wants to start with the man. The man comes later.* | The order of your paragraphs already does this. What is left if the explanation of the order goes? |
| ☐ | 113 | *I want to tell this part exactly, because everybody who has heard it since has decided they already know.* | Who is she defending herself to, and does the story need that person in the room? |
| ☐ | 191 | *That was true, and I want you to know that one of the things I said that year was true.* | The fact is strong. What does *I want you to know* add to it? |
| ☐ | 251 | *Here is the thing I have worked out and cannot put down.* | An announcement before a thought. Does the thought need announcing? |
| ☐ | 123 | *I have thought about that smile more than about anything else.* | The rest of the paragraph shows exactly this. Is the first sentence a label on it? |

## TIER 2 · The gloss after the gesture

**Pattern: one sentence shows, the next explains what it showed.** In every row below the first half is doing its job. Test each by covering the second half and reading the paragraph again.

| Done | Line | Shows → then explains | The question |
| :-: | :-: | --- | --- |
| ☐ | 55 | *Everybody laughed, and I laughed too* → *because that is the fastest way to stop being the thing people are laughing at* | A thirteen-year-old laughing at her own house. Does the reader need the strategy named? |
| ☐ | 57 | *Our house does smell of fish.* → *That is not the part that stayed with me.* | The second sentence tells the reader where to look. Can the paragraph point instead? |
| ☐ | 59 | *we look in and they do not look out* → *Not because they are proud. Because we are not there.* | **The hardest call in Tier 2** — it is also the best line in the paragraph. Does *we are not there* earn its explanation, or is *they do not look out* already the whole thing? |
| ☐ | 61 | *He meant it as a warning* → *and I heard it as a price list* | This one may survive. It is not a gloss, it is a second event — her mishearing. Test it, then decide. |
| ☐ | 101 | *It was green, with little gold roads on it going nowhere* → *I gave it to him because he was the only person who would understand that a thing can be beautiful after it stops being useful* | The object says it. Does the reason need saying? |
| ☐ | 107 | *call you beautiful in a way that is not a compliment but a notice* → *a way of saying that everyone has agreed you are available for comment now* | *A notice* is the image. What does the second clause add except certainty? |
| ☐ | 137 | *The hurt came off him like heat off a wall and I stood in it and warmed my hands* → *because a girl who has just been chosen wants somebody there to see her be chosen* | The best image in the story followed by its own footnote. |
| ☐ | 149 | *I had heard him say it eleven times.* → *I asked because I wanted him to be the person who knew something, for once, in front of me.* | *Eleven times* already tells us she is not asking for information. |
| ☐ | 161 | *I watched a boy … hold completely still* → *because moving might have cost him the version of me he kept in his head* | Does the stillness need a motive attached, or is the stillness the fact? |
| ☐ | 181 | *Fingernails, done every Friday and taken off every Monday before school* → *because at school there are rules and at the hotel there are different rules, and I live between the two of them like somebody crossing a road that never stops* | **The strongest single candidate in the file.** The inventory works because every other item is inert. This is the one entry that editorialises. |
| ☐ | 225 | *something went across his face and was gone, a flicker, like a bulb deciding whether to hold* → *He had planned for envelopes and not for consequences.* | The flicker is doing it. Is the sentence after it a translation? |
| ☐ | 235 | *Daudi was there when I came out* → *and I can tell you what he thought, because he is fifteen and good and he thinks shame is the only thing that can bend a person* | Note the next two lines — *It was not shame. It was arithmetic.* — are protected and land harder if less has been explained before them. |

**These are not glosses. They are the narrator delivering a character analysis**, and each sits where a scene could stand instead. They are also the best-written explanation in the draft, which is what makes them hard.

| Done | Line | What it is | The question |

| ☐ | 91 | *I was thirteen years and one hour old and I believed all of it … a town where the people with cars had taken things and the people without cars had been taken from* | This is the story's politics, stated by the narrator. The ghost kids and the *délestage* enact it four paragraphs earlier. **Which one does the reader believe more?** |

## How to run the pass

**Fifteen minutes, one tier at a time, top-down.** Do not skip to Tier 3 because it is the most interesting; Tier 1 costs nothing and Tier 3 costs a paragraph.

1. **Cover the explaining half with your hand** and read the paragraph aloud from the line above it.
2. If nothing is lost, it goes. If something is lost, **name what** in the margin — that is the note, and it is worth more than the cut.
3. Log the fix in [[Craft Log]]'s Pattern Log with the McKee or Gardner principle. Recurring failure modes only become visible if they are written down.
4. **Re-measure after each tier, never inside one.** → [[The Five Passes]]

> [!warning] This is not the tightness pass and must not become one
> [[Home]] has forbidden cutting Chantal for tightness since the Nazir finding: *nothing in Chantal gets cut for tightness again.* **This pass removes explanation, not length.** If a cut is being made because it saves words, it is the wrong cut. Word count is not the instrument here and should not be quoted as a result.
