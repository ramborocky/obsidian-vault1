# A DOUBLE-EDGED INHERITANCE — APPLIED COLD READ

> Hannah Giorgis, "A Double-Edged Inheritance" (Shiromeda). A prize-winning story, read cold against [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes|the nine axes]] and the four-section protocol: **Revelation, Evaluation, Forgiveness, Competence.**

Related: [[The Cold Read Protocol]] · [[The First-Page Cut]] · [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes]] · [[Kalemie — Standing Diagnosis]]

**Text used:** the version to hand, which breaks off mid-sentence in the final movement (`shopkeepers and tourists alike pausing`). Estimated ~3,500 words. Percentages below are proportional estimates, not exact counts.

---

## REVELATION

*The three most significant facts the story withholds.*

### 1. That the beating was a state execution attempt ordered by Robel's father

The story withholds the agent of violence for the entire hospital section, letting the nurse's contempt, the doctors' questions, and Almaz's lie stand in for it.

First partial release, **~48%**:

> She couldn't lose this baby, not to the swarm of uniformed men who'd descended upon her.

Full attribution, next section, **~56%**:

> But when Robel ran up three flights of stairs at Tikur Anbessa and saw the girl he loved fighting for two lives Girma had tried to extinguish, his resolve ossified.

**The withholding is soft.** "Fix. It." / "Or else I will." at ~36% has already told any attentive reader what is coming, so the reveal lands as confirmation, not shock.

### 2. That Robel is alive, that he bargained, and that Meskerem's paternity is a manufactured story

This is the story's true secret. Released **to the reader** at **~64%**:

> Let me fix it. Let me keep her safe. I will sacrifice my happiness to keep her alive.

Sealed at ~73% — "Tigist resolved then that she'd never tell this dangerous miracle of a child about her father."

**Never released to Meskerem, and never confirmed to the reader either.** We are never told what Robel actually traded, or to whom, or whether he is living.

What stands in for it — the parenthesis in the classroom scene, **~84%**:

> a woman named Almaz Gessesse had found a way for Meskerem's mother to come to America after her husband had died mysteriously (in the war?)

The question mark is the whole withheld fact compressed into punctuation: the daughter presenting her family history to a room, sourcing the one load-bearing lie to a guess. **This is the strongest single move in the story.**

### 3. That Tigist is dead

The story runs its entire second half on the assumption Tigist is alive, then disposes of her in a subordinate clause inside a sentence about someone else, **~76%**:

> Almaz comforted Meskerem when the girl asked why God had let her mother die alone in a car accident while she had been away taking classes earlier that year.

The protagonist of the first two-thirds dies offstage, in a dependent clause, in a car accident with no relation to the causal chain the story has been building.

### Never released

What the general is walking toward Meskerem to do or say. The text stops mid-sentence, so the story's terminal revelation does not exist on the page. **What stands in for it is the title.**

## EVALUATION

*Sentences where the narrator stops narrating to say what something means, what it is worth, or why it matters.*

**Count: 34.** At ~3,500 words: **~9.7 per 1,000 words** — an evaluative intrusion roughly every 100 words.

**Excluded:** all dialogue, all sensory description, thoughts explicitly tagged to a character ("Tigist reflected").
**Included:** free indirect discourse — the judging voice there is still the narrator's grammar.

### The three strongest

> Powerful men were dangerous, their lust for dominion more potent than any love they might claim to feel for a woman.

An aphorism with no owner. Not Almaz thinking it — the story stating its thesis in the third person present-eternal, and it arrives *before* the story has dramatized it.

> Meskerem was a brilliant, preternaturally insightful child.

A pure assertion of a quality the story never once shows in action. Meskerem's only demonstrated cognitive act is a college presentation built on a fact she has wrong.

> He knew she would be enraged at the sight of him. He knew she had a right to be.

The second sentence is a moral ruling handed down by the narrator through the mouth of the character being ruled on. It absolves the reader of deciding.

**Total: 29 / 45**

### Lowest — Resonant close (1)

The story does not have one; it has a cessation, mid-clause, at the moment of maximum unearned suspense. Even granting the intended ending, the design is a cliffhanger — a man walks toward a woman with a gun implied — which converts a story about inherited silence into a story about whether someone gets shot, and the fact that this won means the ending was judged on the promise of its final image rather than on any delivered effect.

### Second lowest — Structural economy (2)

Eight sections, four of them opening with a fresh biographical backfill ("Girma Woldemariam never wanted…", "Almaz was a literature professor…"), so the narrative repeatedly stops to install a character rather than revealing them through action; the Robel-at-the-bedside section exists almost entirely to have Almaz say a curse aloud. And the story spends its most expensive real estate — the last fifth — on a nostalgic gabi-shopping flashback that duplicates, beat for beat, the gabi-shopping scene that opened Tigist's story: a rhyme the story can afford at 2,000 words and cannot at 3,500 with an ending still unwritten.

### What the prize forgave

**An unfinished story.** It forgave a manuscript that has not reached its own last scene, plus a baggy expository architecture and a narrator who states her themes before dramatizing them — in exchange for genuine cultural specificity, a well-built dramatic irony (the presentation scene, the parenthetical "(in the war?)"), and a subject the judging pool wanted to see on the page. **The prize rewarded the premise and the access, not the execution.**

## COMPETENCE

*Does the narrator know how to do something, such that the story contains information a general reader would not have?*

**N.**

What the narrator has instead is **native cultural and toponymic fluency** — Amist Kilo, Siddist Kilo, Meskel Adebabay, Shiro Meda, Tikur Anbessa, Kidane Mehret; *tsom*, *netela*, *gabi*, Ge'ez inscriptions, the *kremt* rains, and the Ethiopian calendar fact that makes September 11 Meskerem 1. That is real knowledge a general reader lacks, and it is deployed accurately. But it is **setting, not procedure**: it tells you where you are, never how anything is done.

Every professional domain the story names goes undemonstrated:

| Named | Demonstrated |
| --- | --- |
| Tigist — engineering prodigy | Never solves anything |
| Robel — final-year law student | Never reasons legally |
| Almaz — literature professor | Seen holding Baldwin |
| Girma — the army's most respected general | Rifles on a wall, honors on a door |

The hospital chapters contain no medicine. **Nobody in this story is ever shown at work.**

### The nearest miss

The gabi: that a new one smells strange and dirty, that a household's are washed each season, that softness is the measure and softness means home. Domestic craft knowledge, correctly observed, doing load-bearing work twice. **It is one paragraph's worth, and it is the only thing in the story the narrator knows how to *do*.**

## What this is doing in Learning Curve

Three takeaways for [[Kalemie — Standing Diagnosis|Kalemie]]:

1. **A 29/45 won.** The bar is not 45. But note *which* axes were forgiven — economy and close — and note that Kalemie is currently exposed on exactly those two. Forgiveness is not transferable; it was bought here with specificity and subject.
2. **The parenthetical is the technique to steal.** `(in the war?)` does in three words what the story spends two sections doing elsewhere. The gap sits inside punctuation.
3. **~9.7 evaluations per 1,000 words is the ceiling to stay under, not a target.** Every one of the three strongest quoted above is a place the story tells the reader its verdict before earning it. See [[The Funhouse Mirror — AI Output as Cliché Detector]] — the aphorism with no owner is the default move, which is exactly why it reads as default.
