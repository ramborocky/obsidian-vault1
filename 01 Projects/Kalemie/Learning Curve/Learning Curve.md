# LEARNING CURVE — THE CRITIQUE ENGINE

> The fourth knowledge base. The other three teach you how to write. This one teaches you how to be *read* — by a stranger, in a stack of two hundred, with no goodwill toward you at all.

## What This Is

[[Story structure by robert mackee|McKee]], [[Mastering Suspense by Jane Cleland|Cleland]], [[The Art of Fiction by John Gardner|Gardner]] and [[Short Story Writing by Charles Raymond Barrett|Barrett]] are **generative** — they tell you what to build. Learning Curve is **adversarial** — it tells you what gets cut, and by whom, and on which page.

**And a warning this base has earned the right to give itself.** Diagnosis has a ceiling. Kalemie has been comprehensively diagnosed since July and still has no ending. When the problem stops being *what is wrong* and becomes *nothing is getting written*, close this base and open [[The 3 AM Epiphany by Brian Kiteley|Kiteley]] — specifically [[Prescriptions for the Live Draft]], which maps every fault named here to an exercise with a word count. Judgement and generation are different tools; this one is not the second.

Barrett is the closest ally of the four. His chapter on beginnings states [[The First-Page Cut|the first-page cut]] in 1900 — the editor reads no more than the first page of three manuscripts in four — and his chapter on endings adds the test this base had missed: **the editor then turns straight to the last page** to see whether the promise was kept. Read [[Climax and Conclusion — The Story in Six Words]] alongside [[The First-Page Cut]]; together they are the whole of how a story is actually judged.

It has two halves, and they came from two different places:

**Half One — The Critique Engine.** Built from the three existing craft bases plus the published criteria of the [Commonwealth Short Story Prize](https://www.commonwealthfoundation.com/short-story-prize/), which is [[Dashboard|Kalemie's stated target]]. This is the professor/judge apparatus: what dies on page one, how a story is actually scored, the order the edit passes must run in, and how to take a hard verdict without collapsing.

**Half Two — Creativity Amplification.** Deconstructed from Lynda Clark's [InGAME/University of Dundee research](https://github.com/IngameDundee/CreativityAmplification) — a repository on writing *with* machines. Its toolkits are obsolete (they recommend chatbots that were switched off in 2019). Its two academic papers are not, and two ideas in them are worth more than the rest of the repo combined: the **funhouse mirror** (whatever the machine writes by default is the cliché) and **all the small things** (grief arrives through the minor object, never the major loss).

## The One-Line Version of Each

- **First-Page Cut** — a judge is not looking for a reason to keep you; they are looking for permission to stop.
- **Nine Axes** — you cannot fix what you have not scored.
- **NAPLAN Floor** — a top score here means no fault found, never that the story is good.
- **Underground** — a rubric that tells you to stop revising is applause, not judgement.
- **Three Gates** — three numbers, never one; and the fourth gate is not yours to score.
- **Five Passes** — never polish a sentence in a scene you might cut.
- **Cold Read** — "what's the weakest line" is a better question than "is this good".
- **Funhouse Mirror** — if the machine writes it without being pushed, it is a cliché by definition.
- **Vestments of Plausibility** — leave the reader room and they will furnish it better than you could.
- **Small Things** — nobody weeps at the funeral. They weep at the handwriting.
- **Obfuscated Openness** — bury the true detail in nine invented ones.
- **Vinge** — the machine is not better than you; it is better than you *alone*.

## How To Actually Use This

1. Open [[Kalemie — Standing Diagnosis]]. It is the live verdict.
2. Before any writing session, read [[The First-Page Cut]] once. It is short by design.
3. When a scene is drafted, run it through [[The Five Passes]] — in order, no skipping.
4. When you want a hard read from me, use the prompts in [[The Cold Read Protocol]] verbatim. Vague requests get polite answers, and polite answers are worthless.
5. Re-score on [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes]] monthly. Track the numbers in [[Craft Log]].
