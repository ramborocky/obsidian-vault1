# THE JUDGING RUBRIC — NINE AXES

> "An ability to take their readers by the hand and lead them into a world where the characters are utterly believable, the prose assured, and the author has something important to say."
> — Louise Doughty, Chair, Commonwealth Short Story Prize

## Why Score At All

Doughty's sentence contains three separable demands — **believable characters**, **assured prose**, **something to say** — and a story can be strong in two and lose on the third without the writer ever noticing, because unscored impressions average themselves into "it's pretty good."

Scoring forces the disaggregation. You cannot fix what you have not scored.

Nine axes, each 1–5. The last column is what separates a 3 from a 5 — the distinction is almost always **control**, not ambition.

| # | Axis | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Opening authority** | Throat-clearing, double start | Competent, no reason to stop | Announces itself; first line is the best line |
| 2 | **Prose control** | Register lurches | Clean, occasional spike | Every sentence at the same altitude, deliberately varied |
| 3 | **Character interiority** | Reported feelings | Consistent voice | The reader knows what the narrator won't say |
| 4 | **Scene turn** | Nothing changes | Most scenes turn | Every scene turns, and the turn is never the expected one |
| 5 | **The gap** | Expectation met | Gap present, small | Gap so wide it reorganises the reader's understanding |
| 6 | **Structural economy** | Scenes justify themselves | Nothing wasted | Cutting any scene breaks two others |
| 7 | **Controlling idea** | Absent or stated | Implied, occasionally nudged | Never stated, impossible to miss |
| 8 | **Resonant close** | Stops | Resolves | Fulfils — every closing image drags an earlier one behind it |
| 9 | **Something to say** | Subject mistaken for theme | Real position | A position the reader cannot dismiss and did not expect |

**Reading the total:** below 27, structural work. 27–35, the draft is real and the work is refinement. Above 36, you are competing. A single 1 anywhere caps the story regardless of total — judges eliminate on faults, not averages.

## The Two Traps

**Scoring ambition instead of execution.** Axis 9 is where writers overscore most. Having an important subject is not having something to say. Kalemie's subject is the sexual exploitation of a child by a man with diplomatic cover; its *position* is the Controlling Idea — that inaction is complicity. Those are different, and only the second is scoreable.

**Scoring the story you intend.** Score only what is on the page today. The ending you plan to write is a 0 until it exists.

| 8 | Resonant close | **1** | The ending does not exist. Not a criticism — a fact, and it caps the story |
| 9 | Something to say | **4** | The thin envelope says more about institutional betrayal than a page of argument could. This is the story's strongest axis |
| | **Total** | **24 / 45** | |

**Read this correctly.** 24 is not a bad score for a draft with no ending. Strip axis 8 (unwritable until the ending exists) and the story sits at 23/40 — and three of the losing axes (1, 4, 6) are fixed by *deletions* already identified.

The pattern is unmistakable: **every high score is at sentence and image level (2, 3, 9). Every low score is structural (1, 4, 5, 6, 8).** This is a writer whose prose has outrun their architecture — which is the good problem, because architecture is learnable and the other thing is not.

**Axis 7 is the one to watch.** It is the only axis where the fix is neither a deletion nor a scheduled scene. Getting from 3 to 5 requires Daudi to *choose* not to act, onscreen, and for the story to know that choosing is what he did. The Dashboard has this as a High Priority item ("Let Daudi make ONE active choice: He chooses NOT to act"). It is actually the difference between a shortlist and a win.

## Questions for AI

1. Score the current draft on all nine axes independently, without reading the scores above. Report every axis where you differ by two or more points, and defend it.
2. Axis 8 is capped at 1 until the ending exists. Draft three different endings under 400 words each, then score each on axes 7, 8 and 9 only.
3. For axis 7: find the exact moment in the existing draft where Daudi comes closest to *choosing* his inaction rather than merely failing to act. Can that moment carry the weight, or must a new one be written?
4. Which single axis, raised by two points, most raises the total? Which is cheapest in words? If they differ, say so — that is the real revision decision.
5. Re-score after the fixes in [[Kalemie — Standing Diagnosis]] priority order and report the new total. Log both numbers and the date in [[Craft Log]].
