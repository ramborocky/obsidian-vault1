# STANDING DIAGNOSIS — KALEMIE

> Measured against [[KALEMIE-Draft_with_AI_Questions|the master draft]] as it stands. Line numbers are live. Re-run the measurements after any significant rewrite.

## The Headline

**The line craft is already passing. The structure is not.** This inverts what [[Dashboard|the Dashboard]] is currently chasing.

The Dashboard's cheat sheet spends most of its space on Cleland's sentence rules — the 20-word average, the banned perception verbs, the "to be" minimisation. Measured against the actual prose, those rules are **already being met**. Meanwhile four structural faults, none of which appear on the Dashboard, would each independently end this story in the first round of judging.

Chasing sentence rules that are already satisfied is how a draft stays in revision for a year.

This is the most useful number in this note. Every "add a beat here" instruction is now priced. If a scene expands, another must contract. Track it or the story disqualifies itself on a technicality after all the craft work is done.

## The Four Structural Faults

### 1. The story starts twice — and the second start is the weaker one

`Line 67` closes the opening on D'juma's line: *"some women resemble the lake."* Then `line 86` opens Scene 1 by quoting the same line again, this time unpacked across three paragraphs of explanation.

The opening (Option C) is genuinely accomplished — the withheld name, the crowd with its back turned, grief managed by list-making, *"the founder of dead things."* Scene 1 then stops the story dead to explain a metaphor the opening had already landed by implication.

Gardner's [[The Vivid and Continuous Fictional Dream|fictional dream]] does not survive being restarted. A judge who has read a superb first page and then hits a second, slower first page concludes the writer cannot tell which of their own openings is better — and that is a judgment about the writer, not the page.

**This is the single highest-leverage structural fix, and it is a deletion.** The Dashboard's own instruction to "cut D'juma's lake speech to ONE LINE" is correct but under-scoped: the line is already in the opening. Scene 1's version is the one to cut, and most of Scene 1 goes with it.

### 2. Daudi's age contradicts itself

`Line 33`: *"corpses aged twelve to seventeen, yet not much younger than me."* This places Daudi at seventeen or older.

`Line 172`: the birthday scene turns on D'jamela becoming **thirteen**, with Daudi present as her peer. `Line 133` has Anitha as a thirteen-year-old schoolgirl in the same cohort.

A seventeen-year-old narrator and a thirteen-year-old protagonist are a different story from two thirteen-year-olds — different in what Scene 6 means, different in what Daudi's passivity means, different in what the reader is being asked to witness. The draft has not decided.

A judge catches this in the first two pages and it costs the story its authority permanently. Every subsequent ambiguity now reads as carelessness rather than craft.

### 3. Scene 6 does not control what the reader concludes

Scene 6 is, by the author's own note, the story's emotional core. It is also the scene where two characters the draft establishes as thirteen are alone on a mattress, and the text goes deliberately opaque at the decisive moment: *"Tangent and sweet it felt at first. Then perfect and seamless"* → *"It took only twenty seconds."* → *"I gasped for air."*

The reader will conclude something. Right now the text does not govern **what**. The available readings run from a first kiss to something considerably further, and the prose supplies evidence for more than one.

This is not a content objection — child sexual exploitation is the story's actual subject, and the police-station scene handles it with real control. It is a **craft** objection, and a serious one: ambiguity that the writer has chosen reads as restraint; ambiguity the writer has not resolved reads as evasion. At present it reads as evasion, because the surrounding prose spikes into its least controlled register precisely where the meaning is least clear (*"the bioluminescent stream of pure everything"*).

Gardner's term for the failure of nerve that produces exactly this is [[Three Faults of Soul — Sentimentality, Frigidity, Mannerism|frigidity]] — the writer declining to look at the thing the story is about. Decide what happens. Then write it at the same level of control as the thin envelope.

### 4. Tense and diction slips inside a retrospective narration

- `Line 111`: *"D'jamela **is** probably the closest person that I **have been** next to"* — present tense inside a past-tense retrospective told by a narrator who already knows she is dead. The tense makes her alive. This is the one error in the draft that actively undercuts its own premise.
- `Line 84`: *"have rung in my mind... I'm beginning to understand"* — defensible as retrospective present, but it clashes with the opening's clean past tense and compounds fault #1.
- `Line 352`: ***désvu*** — not a word in French or English. If it is a coinage (on *déjà vu*), it needs to be legible as one. If it is a slip for *déjà vu*, it is a typo in italics at the story's emotional peak. Either way, an editor stops here.

## What Is Genuinely Working — Do Not Touch These

Stated plainly, because a diagnosis that only lists faults produces a writer who revises the strengths out.

- **The opening (Option C).** Withholding Djamela's name while the whole crowd knows it is real dread, not a trick. *"The founder of dead things"* is the kind of line judges quote in citations.
- **The thin envelope.** The story's central betrayal delivered as a change in an object's thickness, with no comment. This is the best writing in the draft and the model for everything else.
- **"Chantal had already taken over her."** Djamela lost before she is lost.
- **Morisho's "those children are ghosts to you."** Thesis line that does not read as thesis.
- **The entropy motif.** Load-bearing, not decorative — it belongs to Daudi's actual vocabulary, exactly as Cleland requires of metaphor.
- **Unitalicised Swahili/French/Lingala.** Confident, and correct. Commonwealth judges explicitly value "unheard voices"; the code-switching is an asset, not a risk.
- **The sentence rhythm.** 42% of sentences under ten words. That is a deliberate-sounding music most drafts never achieve.

## Priority Order — Corrected

The Dashboard's "5 Critical Fixes" are all sound, but they are sequenced as though the draft's problem is content. It is not; the problem is that two structural faults make the draft unreadable-as-serious before those fixes can register.

| # | Fix | Cost | Why first |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Cut Scene 1's lake speech; absorb what survives into the opening** | −250w | Removes the double start. Frees budget. Deletion, not writing |
| 2 | **Fix Daudi's age** — one number, decided once | ~0w | Everything downstream depends on it, including #3 |
| 3 | **Resolve Scene 6** — decide what happens, write it with control | ±100w | The story's core currently cannot be read confidently |
| 4 | **Fix line 111 tense; resolve *désvu*** | ~0w | Two-minute fixes that currently cost the draft its authority |
| 5 | Then the Dashboard's five criticals, in its own order | ~+650w | These are real, and they land properly only once 1–4 are done |

Fixes 1, 2 and 4 cost roughly **nothing in words and about an hour in total**, and together they move the draft from "promising but unserious" to "competitive." That is the highest-return hour available on this project right now.

3. Rewrite Scene 6's decisive twenty seconds twice: once at maximum restraint (the reader knows exactly what happened and is shown almost nothing), once at maximum directness. Compare which better serves the story's [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes|controlling idea]] that inaction is complicity.
4. Re-run the word count after each fix and report remaining headroom against the 5,000 ceiling. Flag immediately if headroom drops below 300.
5. Audit the draft for `looked` (12 instances) and replace each with the physical action that *is* the perception — then confirm no replacement pushed a sentence over 30 words.
