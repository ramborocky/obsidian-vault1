# MCKEE — SINGLE VS MULTIPLE PROTAGONISTS

## The Distinction

> **SINGLE PROTAGONIST:** A single character drives the story.

> **PLURAL-PROTAGONIST:** A group of characters share the same desire, mutually suffering and benefiting.

> **MULTIPROTAGONIST:** Multiple characters pursue separate desires, suffering and benefiting independently.

---

## Single Protagonist (Archplot)

**Characteristics:**
- One character drives the story
- Dominates screentime
- Star role
- Clear object of desire

**Examples:**
- **The Fugitive:** Harrison Ford's protagonist drives everything
- **Die Hard:** Bruce Willis is the single focus
- **Thelma & Louise:** Two protagonists, single desire (escape)

---

## Plural-Protagonist

**Characteristics:**
- A group of characters share the same desire
- They mutually suffer and benefit
- If one succeeds, all benefit
- If one fails, all suffer

**Examples:**
- **The Seven Samurai:** All share the goal of protecting the village
- **The Dirty Dozen:** All share the goal of the mission
- **The Battleship Potemkin:** An entire class of people (the proletariat)

---

## Multiprotagonist (Multiplot)

**Characteristics:**
- Separate characters with separate desires
- They suffer and benefit independently
- Connected thematically, not through plot

**Examples:**
- **Pulp Fiction:** Multiple stories with different protagonists
- **Hannah and Her Sisters:** Separate characters, separate desires
- **Short Cuts:** Many stories woven together
- **Parenthood:** Multiple family members, separate struggles

---

## For Kalemie

**Recommended:** Single Protagonist (Daudi)

**Why:**
- Daudi's internal journey is the heart of the story
- His passivity is the central conflict
- All other characters orbit around him

---

## What to Avoid

### 1. False Plural-Protagonist
Characters who share screen time but don't share a desire

### 2. Unclear Protagonist
When the reader doesn't know whose story it is

### 3. Disconnected Subplots
When subplots don't connect to the protagonist's journey
