# MCKEE — GENRE CONVENTIONS

## Definition

> **GENRE CONVENTIONS are specific settings, roles, events, and values that define individual genres and their subgenres.**

---

## The Audience Knows

> The audience enters each film armed with a complex set of anticipations learned through a lifetime of moviegoing.

**The Challenge:**
> You must not only fulfill audience anticipations, but you must lead their expectations to fresh, unexpected moments.

**Without genre knowledge, you can't do this.**

---

## Examples of Conventions

### Love Story
| Element | Convention |
|---------|------------|
| Setting | Anywhere lovers meet |
| Events | Boy meets girl → Lose girl → Get girl |
| Roles | Lovers, blocking characters |
| Values | Love/Hate, Together/Apart |
| Blocking Force | What stops them? |

### Crime Story
| Element | Convention |
|---------|------------|
| Setting | Anywhere |
| Events | Crime committed → Detective investigates → Criminal caught |
| Roles | Criminal, Detective, Victim |
| Values | Justice/Injustice |
| Point of View | Who sees the crime? |

### Disillusionment Plot
| Element | Convention |
|---------|------------|
| Protagonist | Optimistic, holds high ideals |
| Pattern | Repeated negative turns |
| Ending | Deep cynicism, disillusionment |
| Examples | The Conversation, The Misfits, La Dolce Vita, Lenny |

### Comedy
| Element | Convention |
|---------|------------|
| Setting | Anywhere |
| Event | Nobody gets hurt (permanently) |
| Tone | Laughter |
| Subgenres | Parody, Satire, Romantic, Screwball, Farce, Black Comedy |

## Conventions in Art Film

> **Art Film has become a traditional genre with its own conventions.**

**External Conventions:**
- Absence of stars
- Production outside Hollywood
- Generally in a language other than English

**Internal Conventions:**
- Celebration of the cerebral
- Smothering strong emotion under a blanket of mood
- Enigma, symbolism, unresolved tensions
- Unconventionality (Minimalist and/or Antistructure)

## For Kalemie

### Genre Conventions to Fulfill

**Literary Fiction:**
- [ ] Passive protagonist
- [ ] Internal conflict
- [ ] Open or ironic ending
- [ ] Consistent reality
- [ ] Linear time
- [ ] Focus on character over plot

**Education Plot:**
- [ ] Daudi changes from naive/passive to wise/active
- [ ] He learns something about the world
- [ ] He learns something about himself

**Social Drama:**
- [ ] Identifies a social problem (police corruption, class structure)
- [ ] Dramatizes its effects on characters
- [ ] Doesn't preach the remedy

### Conventions to Subvert or Reinvent
- [ ] Passive protagonist becomes... (what?)
- [ ] The system remains unchanged (subverting Justice convention)
