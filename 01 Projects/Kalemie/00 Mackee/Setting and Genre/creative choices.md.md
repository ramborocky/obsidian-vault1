# MCKEE — CREATIVE CHOICES

## The Principle

> **CREATIVITY means creative choices of inclusion and exclusion.**

> The craft demands the invention of far more material than you can possibly use, then the astute selection from this quantity of quality events, moments of originality that are true to character and true to world.

---

## The Ratio

> Creativity is five to one, perhaps ten or twenty to one.

**Example:**
> Actors try twenty ways to play a moment, then choose the one perfect moment. The same is true for writers.

---

## How to Make Creative Choices

### Step 1: Generate
- List five, ten, fifteen different ways to do it
- Don't judge yet. Just generate.

### Step 2: Evaluate
- Which is truest to the characters?
- Which is truest to the world?
- Which has never been done before?

### Step 3: Choose
- Pick the one that works best
- Throw away the rest

---

## The Singles Bar Example

### The Cliché
> "A singles bar! That's it! They meet at P. J. Clarke's!"

### The Creative List
1. Singles Bar (Cliché, but don't throw it away yet)
2. Park Avenue: Tire blows out on his BMW. She helps him fix it.
3. Toilet: She's drunk, throwing up. He finds her. He helps her.
4. [Continue listing until you have ten or fifteen]

### The Choice
> Which scene is truest to my characters? Truest to their world? And has never been on screen quite this way before?

### The Next Step
> If singles bar is still the right choice, make a new list: a dozen different ways to meet in a singles bar. Research this world until you know the singles bar scene like no writer before you.

## For Kalemie

### The Clichés to Avoid

**Meeting Scene Clichés:**
- A rich man in a BMW picking up a poor girl (done)
- A teenager wanting to be famous (done)
- A corrupt police station (done)

### The Creative List

**How Does Djamela Meet Jean Luc?**
1. BMW pulls up beside the dala dala (current version)
2. Jean Luc is a customer at D'juma's fruit stall
3. Jean Luc is a patient of D'juma's traditional healing
4. Jean Luc is at the school gate
5. Jean Luc is connected to Morisho
6. Jean Luc is the father of a ghost kid
7. Jean Luc owns the lakeside hotel where Djamela's mother works
8. Jean Luc is the real estate agent for the family
9. Jean Luc is at the market, he sees Djamela
10. Jean Luc is from the church (St. Augustine Parish)

**The Choice:**
> Which is truest to the characters? Truest to the world? Which has never been done before?

## The "Meeting at the Watermelon" Alternative

**What if:** Djamela meets Jean Luc at the fruit market when she's buying a watermelon for her birthday?

**Why This Works:**
- D'juma works at the market
- Jean Luc is a customer
- The watermelon is already a symbol
- Djamela is there to buy a "cake" she's disappointed in
- Jean Luc offers something better

**The New Scene:**
> Djamela and Daudi are at the fruit market. Djamela's father, D'juma, is selling watermelons. Djamela is disappointed. A man in a BMW pulls up. He's buying fruit. He sees Djamela. He offers her a ride. He calls her Chantal.

## The Rule of Ten

> If your finished screenplay contains every scene you've ever written, if you've never thrown an idea away, if your rewriting is little more than tinkering with dialogue, your work will almost certainly fail.

### The Takeaway
- Generate ten ideas for every one you use
- Throw away the nine that are less than your best
- The choices make the writer
