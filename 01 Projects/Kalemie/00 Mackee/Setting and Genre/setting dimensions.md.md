# MCKEE — SETTING DIMENSIONS

## Definition

> A story's SETTING is four-dimensional—**Period, Duration, Location, Level of Conflict.**

---

## The Four Dimensions

### 1. PERIOD
> PERIOD is a story's place in time.

**Questions to Ask:**
- Is the story set in the contemporary world?
- In history?
- A hypothetical future?
- Or is it a fantasy where time is unknowable?

**Examples:**
- **Animal Farm:** Unknowable time
- **The Godfather:** 1940s-1950s
- **Blade Runner:** Future dystopia
- **The Last Emperor:** Historical epic

---

### 2. DURATION
> DURATION is a story's length through time.

**Questions to Ask:**
- How much time does the story span?
- Decades? Years? Months? Days?
- Is it real-time (screentime = storytime)?

**Examples:**
- **My Dinner with Andre:** 2 hours = 2 hours
- **The Godfather:** Decades
- **The Battleship Potemkin:** Screentime > Storytime (Odessa Steps sequence)

**Key Insight:**
> It's possible for screentime to surpass storytime. The "Odessa Steps" sequence expands 2-3 minutes into 5 minutes of screen terror.

---

### 3. LOCATION
> LOCATION is a story's place in space.

**Questions to Ask:**
- What is the specific geography?
- What town? What streets? What buildings?
- What rooms inside those buildings?
- Up what mountain? Across what desert?

**Examples:**
- **Casablanca:** One city, multiple locations within it
- **Die Hard:** One building
- **Lawrence of Arabia:** The desert

---

### 4. LEVEL OF CONFLICT
> LEVEL OF CONFLICT is the story's position on the hierarchy of human struggles.

**The Three Levels:**

1. **INNER CONFLICT** — Within the character's mind, body, emotions
2. **PERSONAL CONFLICT** — With family, friends, lovers
3. **EXTRA-PERSONAL CONFLICT** — With society, institutions, environment

**Questions to Ask:**
- Does your story focus on inner conflicts?
- On personal conflicts?
- On battles with institutions?
- On struggles against the environment?

## For Kalemie

### Period
- Contemporary DRC (Democratic Republic of Congo)
- Post-colonial, post-war setting
- Kalemie, Lake Tanganyika region

### Duration
- Approximately one month
- From Djamela's disappearance to Daudi's realization

### Location
- **Kalemie**, DRC
- **Lake Tanganyika** (the lake is a character)
- **Mihogoni** (Daudi's neighborhood)
- **Rue Saint Michel** (the ghost kids' neighborhood)
- **Ave de Bobozo** (police station)
- **Lakeside hotel** (Jean Luc's territory)
- **The road between the lake and the mines** (where Daudi finds debris)

### Level of Conflict
- **Primary:** Inner (Daudi's passivity)
- **Secondary:** Personal (Djamela leaving, D'juma, Morisho)
- **Tertiary:** Extra-Personal (Police system, Jean Luc, class structure)

## The Relationship Between Structure and Setting

> A story must obey its own internal laws of probability. The event choices of the writer, therefore, are limited to the possibilities and probabilities within the world he creates.

**For Kalemie:**
- What is possible in Kalemie?
- What is probable?
- What is impossible?

**The Rules of Kalemie:**
1. Poverty is inescapable
2. The system is corrupt
3. Those with money have power
4. Daudi watches, but doesn't act
