# MCKEE — SETTING & CHARACTER

## The Relationship

> The relationship between setting and character is intimate. A character is defined by the world they inhabit, and the world is defined by the characters who inhabit it.

---

## How Setting Reveals Character

### 1. Choices of Home
- Where does the character live?
- What does their home say about them?

### 2. Choices of Dress
- What do they wear?
- What does their clothing reveal?

### 3. Choices of Objects
- What do they own?
- What do they carry?

### 4. Choices of Routine
- How do they spend their day?
- What rituals do they have?

### 5. Choices of Relationships
- Who do they surround themselves with?
- Who do they avoid?

## For Kalemie

### Daudi's Setting Reveals Him

**His Room:**
- Small room in Mihogoni
- Wall covered with debris (motherboards, hub caps, antelope heads)
- The debris is his theology
- The room reveals: He collects what others discard. He finds meaning in dead things.

**His Neighborhood:**
- Mihogoni (the down part of town)
- "The founder of dead things"
- The neighborhood reveals: He's poor, he's an outsider, he's a witness

**His School:**
- Physics class
- Learning about entropy
- The school reveals: He's smart, he thinks in terms of science, he applies physics to life

### Djamela's Setting Reveals Her

**Her Home:**
- She lives with her father D'juma, a fruit seller and healer
- The home reveals: She's poor, she's desperate to escape

**Her Desire:**
- To be like the ghost kids from Rue Saint Michel
- To be "Chantal"
- The desire reveals: She wants to escape her class, she wants to be seen, she wants to be someone else

## Setting as Antagonism

> Setting can be a force of antagonism.

**For Daudi:**
- The lake (cold, dark, swallowing things)
- The police station (corrupt, indifferent)
- The BMW (Jean Luc's power)
- The class structure (he can't escape)

**For Djamela:**
- Poverty (she's trapped)
- The lake (her father's wisdom, also her fate)
- The police station (the system fails her)
- The BMW (seduction and trap)
