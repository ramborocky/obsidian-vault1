# MCKEE — REINVENTING GENRES

## The Principle

> Genre conventions are not carved in stone; they evolve, grow, adapt, modify, and break apace with the changes in society.

---

## Why Genres Evolve

> For genres are simply windows on reality, various ways for the writer to look at life. When the reality outside the window undergoes change, the genres alter with it. If not, if a genre becomes inflexible and cannot bend with the changing world, it petrifies.

---

## Example 1: The Western

### Stage 1: Morality Plays
- Set in the "Old West"
- Allegories of good versus evil

### Stage 2: Satire (1970s)
- **Blazing Saddles** exposed the Western's fascist heart
- The genre went into hibernation

### Stage 3: Social Drama (1980s)
- Quasi-Social Drama
- Corrective to racism and violence
- **Examples:** Dances with Wolves, Unforgiven, Posse

---

## Example 2: The Psycho-Drama

### Stage 1: Freudian Detective Story
- Psychiatrist played "detective"
- Investigated hidden "crime" (repressed trauma)
- Victim restored to sanity
- **Examples:** Sybil, The Snake Pit, The Three Faces of Eve

### Stage 2: Psycho-Thriller
- Merged Psycho-Drama with Detective Genre
- Cops became lay psychiatrists
- **Examples:** The First Deadly Sin, Manhunter, Seven

### Stage 3: Cop Becomes the Psycho
- Detective suffers from modern maladies
- Key to justice = cop's psychoanalysis of himself
- **Examples:** Tightrope, Lethal Weapon, Angel Heart

### Stage 4: Psychopath is Close to You
- In your spouse, psychiatrist, surgeon, child, nanny
- Communal paranoia
- **Examples:** The Hand That Rocks the Cradle, Sleeping with the Enemy, Dead Ringers

---

## Example 3: The Love Story

### Stage 1: Parents Oppose
- The parents of the girl block the love
- From 2300 B.C. to the 20th century

### Stage 2: New Blocking Forces
- **The Graduate:** Parents for unconventional reasons
- **Witness:** Culture (Amish vs. modern)
- **When Harry Met Sally:** Belief that friendship and love are incompatible
- **The Crying Game:** Sexual identity
- **Ghost:** Death

### Stage 3: Down-Endings
- **Dangerous Liaisons, The Bridges of Madison County, The Remains of the Day**
- Speaks to hopelessness of lasting love

### Stage 4: The Longing Story
- **Sleepless in Seattle, Red**
- End on boy-meets-girl
- Replaces difficulty of love with difficulty of meeting

---

## The Lesson

> Social attitudes change. The cultural antenna of the writer must be alert to these movements or risk writing an antique.

### For Kalemie

**What is the contemporary reality outside the window?**

- Colonialism's legacy
- Corruption in the DRC
- Class division
- The power of money
- The vulnerability of girls

**What genre conventions need reinventing?**

- The Education Plot: Daudi doesn't get a happy ending
- The Crime Story: The criminal isn't caught
- The Love Story: The lovers don't end up together

**What does Kalemie say about today?**

> The system is corrupt. The powerful get away with it. The powerless watch and do nothing.
