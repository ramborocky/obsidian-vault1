# MCKEE — THE PRINCIPLE OF ANTAGONISM

> A protagonist and story can only be as intellectually fascinating and emotionally compelling as the forces of antagonism make them. When a story is weak, the cause is almost always that the negative side is weak — not that the protagonist needs to be more likable.

## The Core Principle

- Human nature is conservative — nobody does more than necessary. What forces a character into full, deep, multidimensional life is **pressure from the negative side**, not charm engineered into the positive side.
- "Forces of antagonism" ≠ a single villain. It means the *total* sum of everything opposing the character's will: inner weakness, personal relationships, institutions, environment — all of it, together.
- At the Inciting Incident, weigh the protagonist's total capacity against the total antagonism: they should look like an underdog. Some conflict may look solvable in isolation, but the **totality should feel overwhelming.**

## The Declension: Positive → Contrary → Contradictory → Negation of the Negation

For any primary value at stake, there are degrees of negativity, not just a flip switch:

- **Positive**: e.g. Justice.
- **Contrary**: not the full opposite, just *negative-ish* — unfairness, nepotism, bureaucratic delay, bias. Not illegal, just wrong.
- **Contradictory**: the actual opposite — Injustice. A law has been broken.
- **Negation of the Negation**: not just "more negative" but a **different order** of negative — qualitatively, not just quantitatively, worse. For Justice, this is **Tyranny**: "might makes right," where the powerful *make* the law rather than break it. (McKee: most TV detective shows never go past the Contradictory — a crime is solved. *Missing* reaches the Negation of the Negation — the murder was state policy, and there's no court to appeal to.)

> Life doesn't work like arithmetic — two negatives don't make a positive. Things can simply get worse, and worse again, in kind, not just degree.

- Most weak, forgettable stories stall at the Contrary or Contradictory (love/hate, truth/lie, freedom/slavery as flat opposites). **Reaching the Negation of the Negation is what separates the satisfying from the sublime.**

## Worked Examples of the Ladder

| Value | Contrary | Contradictory | Negation of the Negation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Justice | unfairness | injustice (a crime) | tyranny — the powerful *make* the law |
| Truth | white lies | knowing lies | lying to yourself and believing it (Blanche DuBois) |
| Love | indifference | hatred | hating your own being — or being hated by someone who *pretends* to love you (harder to fight than open hatred) |
| Courage | temporary fear | cowardice | an act that *looks* brave but is cowardice at its limit (shooting the officer to avoid the minefield) |
| Communication | misunderstanding/alienation | isolation | losing communication with your *own mind* — insanity |

## Practical Use

- If a story feels thin, don't reach for more likability on the protagonist's side — **push the antagonism further down this ladder.** Ask: what's the Contrary here? The Contradictory? Have I actually reached a Negation-of-the-Negation-level force, or did I stop one rung too early?
- Most stories progress Positive → Contrary (Act One) → Contradictory (middle acts) → Negation of the Negation (final act), landing tragic or circling back to Positive transformed. Some open already at the Negation of the Negation (*Casablanca* opens with Rick already living in cynical, self-loathing exile) and climb back up instead.
