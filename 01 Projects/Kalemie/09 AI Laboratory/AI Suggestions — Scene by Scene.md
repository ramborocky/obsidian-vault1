# AI SUGGESTIONS — SCENE BY SCENE

**This file is not canon.** Nothing here is part of the manuscript. It's a visible, tagged, appendable scratch space — every suggestion below is something I noticed or proposed, tied to a specific McKee or Cleland principle, kept separate from `KALEMIE-Draft_with_AI_Questions.md` on purpose. If something here is worth using, **you rewrite it in your own words into the actual draft** — nothing gets copy-pasted across.

## How to read an entry

- **Tag** — the specific principle in question, as a hashtag (filterable in Obsidian's tag pane) plus a link to the source note.
- **Where** — the exact line in the current draft this responds to.
- **Suggestion** — what I noticed or a possible direction. Written out fully so you can see it clearly, but it is a *proposal*, not a replacement line.
- **Status** — `proposed` until you mark it `adopted (rewritten)` or `declined`.

## OPENING (Option C)

### O-1
**Tag:** #show-dont-tell / #labeling-words — [[Grammar]]
**Where:** *"That morning I knew something was wrong before I knew what it was."*
**Suggestion:** "knew" is on Cleland's banned-labeling-words list (see [[Grammar]]) and it appears twice in one sentence. The rest of the paragraph already shows this brilliantly through absence (the dala dala not there, Morisho's voice not there) — the stated "I knew" undercuts the showing that follows it. Consider cutting the sentence entirely and letting the next three sentences (the absences) carry the dread on their own.
**Status:** proposed

### O-2
**Tag:** #labeling-words — [[Grammar]]
**Where:** *"I was beginning to understand what he meant."*
**Suggestion:** "understand" is also a banned label. This is the closing line of the whole opening — high-value real estate. Worth testing whether a physical beat (something Daudi does with his hands, the debris on his wall, anything concrete) could close the section instead of a stated realization.
**Status:** proposed

## SCENE 1 — D'juma's Words / The Lake

### S1-1
**Tag:** #labeling-words — [[Grammar]]
**Where:** *"I realised how the lake pulls things down whilst engaged in..."*
**Suggestion:** "realised" is banned. This whole sentence is Daudi explaining his own insight rather than the insight arriving through the physical act of skimming pebbles. Consider letting the pebble-skimming action itself carry the idea, and cutting the explanatory frame around it.
**Status:** proposed

### S1-2
**Tag:** #symbolic-ascension — [[Composition]]
**Where:** D'juma's lake speech + the entropy/physics-class paragraph, back to back.
**Suggestion:** Composition's Symbolic Ascension principle says a motif should start neutral and only *accrue* meaning gradually — right now the lake-as-metaphor and entropy-as-metaphor are both fully explained back to back in the same short scene, which risks announcing the symbol before it's earned. Worth considering whether the entropy connection could wait a beat (a scene or two) before it's made this explicit.
**Status:** proposed

## SCENE 2 — The Neighbourhood / Djamela's Introduction

### S2-1
**Tag:** #specificity — [[Writing Constitution]] ("beauty emerges from specificity")
**Where:** *"Djamela took her mother's figure, she was tall and beautiful just like her mother."*
**Suggestion:** Already flagged in this scene's own `[!AI]` question as "thin." The Writing Constitution's rule is specificity over generic description — "tall and beautiful" could be almost any character. Worth asking: what is the one physical detail Daudi notices about Djamela that he wouldn't say about anyone else?
**Status:** proposed

## SCENE 3 — The Dala Dala / Morisho / The Ghost Kids

### S3-1
**Tag:** #sentence-check — [[Sentence Check]]
**Where:** *"which often led to their skirts stained with a frothy fluid of sorts on the back of their skirts"*
**Suggestion:** "of sorts" plus repeating "skirts" twice in one clause reads as a hedge rather than a precise image — Cleland's rule is to name the specific thing rather than gesture near it. Also a candidate for tightening under the 20-word average.
**Status:** proposed

### S3-2
**Tag:** #character-contradiction — [[Character Dimension and Cast Design]]
**Where:** Morisho's whole introduction in this scene.
**Suggestion:** Per the McKee note on dimension, Morisho needs a *consistent* contradiction to read as a genuine red herring later — currently he's warm/generous (money for school) with no counter-note. Worth considering one small, early detail that could read as suspicious in hindsight without tipping into obvious guilt.
**Status:** proposed

## SCENE 4 — Djamela's Birthday / The Watermelon

### S4-1
**Tag:** #labeling-words / #show-dont-tell — [[Grammar]]
**Where:** *"To me it felt as though it was Djamela's father's desperate attempt to stave off the inevitable rot of the whole situation."*
**Suggestion:** "felt" is banned, and the sentence also explains the metaphor (rot, desperation) rather than letting the watermelon scene itself carry it. This is a good candidate for cutting the explanatory sentence entirely — the coagulating smoke and the melon "preserving itself in sugar" a paragraph earlier already do this work.
**Status:** proposed

## SCENE 5 — The Dala Dala Continued / The Dagaa Stop / Jean Luc Appears

### S5-1
**Tag:** #image-system — [[The Text]]
**Where:** *"The car radio was blazing soft rhumba of Fally Ipupa."*
**Suggestion:** This is the one place in the draft where real Congolese music vocabulary (from [[Language Lexicon]] — terms like *sebene*, the instrumental breakdown of a rumba song, or *atalaku*, the hype man) could replace generic description ("soft rhumba") with something a reader who knows the music would recognize as authentic. Worth considering — but write the actual sentence yourself; the point isn't the specific word, it's whether it's true to how Daudi would hear it.
**Status:** proposed

### S5-2
**Tag:** #the-gap / #character-contradiction — [[The Substance of Story]]
**Where:** *"'Sawa shemeji,' said Morisho, giving Djamela a five-hundred-shilling coin."*
**Suggestion:** Already an open question in this scene's own callout — why does Morisho give her the coin? If it's read as a small, habitual protectiveness (rather than nothing, or something sinister), it sharpens the tragedy of his later arrest per McKee's dimension principle — the contradiction between his warmth here and how the story treats him later.
**Status:** proposed

## SCENE 6 — The Rain / The Mattress / The Twenty Seconds

### S6-1
**Tag:** #whisper-dont-shout — [[Wisper but don't Shout]]
**Where:** *"the bioluminescent stream of pure everything"*
**Suggestion:** Already flagged in this scene's own callout as possibly overwritten. Cleland's whisper-not-shout principle argues that at peak emotional intensity, quieter and more physically specific beats the register-breaking abstract phrase. Worth testing a version that stays inside Daudi's established physics vocabulary instead of reaching outside it.
**Status:** proposed

### S6-2
**Tag:** #labeling-words — [[Grammar]]
**Where:** *"My mind told me that she felt it and had wanted me to do something."*
**Suggestion:** "felt" is banned, and this sentence tells us Daudi's inference rather than showing the physical evidence for it. A specific, observable detail (something in her hand, her breath, anything Daudi could actually perceive) would let the reader draw the same conclusion without being told to.
**Status:** proposed

## SCENE 7 — The Police Station / Jean Luc / The Envelope

### S7-1
**Tag:** #labeling-words — [[Grammar]]
**Where:** *"He looked up and saw Djamela's face, but couldn't help himself from making a grunting sound."*
**Suggestion:** "looked" and "saw" both banned in one sentence. The grunting sound itself is a strong, specific detail — it could likely open the beat on its own without the two label-verbs in front of it.
**Status:** proposed

### S7-2
**Tag:** #the-gap — [[The Substance of Story]]
**Where:** The scene's final image — the thin envelope.
**Suggestion:** Already an open question in this scene's own callout (#6): the scene cuts on the envelope with no reaction beat. Per the Gap principle, the bigger the gap between what's expected (justice) and what happens (a thinner envelope), the harder the scene should land — right now the cut denies the reader Daudi's reaction to that gap entirely. Worth considering whether at least one more sentence of him processing it belongs before the scene ends.
**Status:** proposed
