# CLELAND — LAYER IN TWO SUBPLOTS

> The art of simplicity is a puzzle of complexity.

## Subplots Need a Job, Not Just Interest

- The two legitimate purposes for a subplot: add intricacy to the primary plot, or reveal nuanced character information the main plot has no room for.
- Lincoln's riddle applies: calling a digression a subplot doesn't make it one. A subplot that gets more attention than it's worth, or that never pays back into the main plot, reads to readers as a broken promise — they were following it in good faith.

## The Subplot Checklist

A real subplot: has a full beginning/middle/end; features developed characters; reveals something about the *main* characters that the primary plot can't show; resolves by story's end; mirrors or contradicts your theme; never overtakes the primary plot.

## In Series or Shorter Forms

- In a series, subplots become thematic and recurring — same underlying issue (isolation, romantic loyalty), different incidents illuminating it book to book.
- In a single short story, a "subplot" often compresses to a single mirroring incident or secondary character rather than a full service road — the *purpose* test still applies at any length.

## For Kalemie

- Kalemie is short-form, so it can't sustain two full service roads, but Cleland's purpose test still applies to its secondary character material. Anitha's plotline (money from Morisho, "chips kuku," dropped at Ecole Secondaire) functions exactly like a Cleland subplot: it mirrors Djamela's arc in miniature — a schoolgirl accepting money and attention from an older man who has power over her — and it's already doing the job of building suspicion around Morisho as a red herring (see [[The Devil Made Me Do It]]). That's a subplot earning its keep, even at short-story scale.
- The "ghost kids" motif (Rue Saint Michel, Monseigneur André Kaseba Catholic School) functions as a second miniature subplot: it's not a full plotline with its own characters, but it recurs, it mirrors the theme (wanting to be pulled into a different, richer world), and it pays off directly in Djamela renaming herself Chantal. Cleland would call this thematic mirroring done correctly, if compressed.
- Watch for scope creep: the Craft Log itself already flagged and reverted an attempt to add lexicon insertions across multiple scenes — a caution that any expansion of secondary material (more Anitha, more ghost-kids texture) needs to earn its page count against Cleland's subplot checklist, not just add color.
