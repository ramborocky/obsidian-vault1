# BARRETT — THE UNITIES IN MINIATURE

> As nearly as possible: a single person, in a single action, at a single place, in a single time.

## The Claim

More than any other modern form, the short story requires the old Greek unities of **time, place and action**. Not as a classical affectation but as arithmetic: its brevity does not admit proper treatment of changes wrought by passing time, the influence of different scenes, or the complications of many interrelated characters.

The plot must be **simple and complete**. It admits **no side issues and no second or under plot** — and it must never be the synopsis of a novel, or a novel's complicated plot compressed into a short story's compass.

## The Diagnostic

> If the plot requires the passage of ten years, if it shifts scene from New York to Timbuctoo, or if it introduces two or three sets of characters, it may by some miracle of ingenuity make a readable story, but it will never be a model one.

The model he returns to throughout: *The Ambitious Guest* runs less than three hours, occupies a single room, and its action is the development of one man's ambition.

## The Exception He Allows

Detective stories, where plot is all-important because the interest is entirely in unravelling. Even there it must contain **a single idea**, however involved — and Barrett notes such stories are simpler than they look, their apparent complexity being the effect of telling the story backwards.

## For Kalemie

**Kalemie violates all three unities, and the violation is the source of its word-budget problem** — which makes this note less a rule to obey than a bill to read.

| Unity | Barrett's standard | Kalemie |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Time** | A single span; hours, ideally | Months — birthday in March, a rainy April, the station, the death |
| **Place** | A single place | The stop, the dala dala, the porch, Daudi's room, the station, the hotel by implication |
| **Action** | One action | Two: the investigation, and the memory of the friendship |

**The action count is the one that matters.** Barrett's ban on a second or under plot is not aesthetic — a short story has no room to develop two lines to a satisfying end. Kalemie currently runs a mystery engine (who killed Djamela; Morisho wrongly arrested; the clues) *alongside* a love-and-complicity story (the rain, the silence, the choosing). The [[Scenario — Arcs, Beats & Scenes|Scenario]] is candid that the fusion is deliberate — mystery for forward pull, social drama for the refusal to resolve — and that is a defensible modern choice.

But the bill comes due exactly where [[Kalemie — Standing Diagnosis|the diagnosis]] found the draft breaking. **The narrative question is doubled** — the draft's own Structural Notes ask which of the two should be foregrounded, and has never answered. Barrett's answer is that you cannot foreground both in 5,000 words.

**The concrete test:** the mystery line requires Morisho's arrest to be resolved, Jean Luc's culpability established, and the fob key followed. The memory line requires only that Daudi choose. **The Scenario's ending serves the memory line and abandons the mystery line** — Morisho is still in a cell, unmentioned. That is the under-plot going unpaid, precisely as Barrett predicts. See [[Climax and Conclusion — The Story in Six Words]].

**Two ways out, and they are genuinely different stories:**

1. **Demote the mystery to a frame.** Keep the arrest as the opening's pressure and never return to it as a puzzle. The clues stop being clues and become [[All the Small Things — Grief Through the Minor Object|small things that only hurt]]. Cheapest in words, and consistent with the drafted ending.
2. **Pay the mystery off.** One sentence disposing of Morisho, one acknowledging what the fob leads to. Costs perhaps 60 words and keeps both lines — but keeps the draft at two actions, which at 5,000 words will always feel slightly crowded.

**Where "Chantal" is cleaner.** [[2026-07-26 — Chantal (Djamela POV Draft)|The Djamela draft]] has one action — a girl doing arithmetic on her own life until the sum comes out — and no mystery at all. It violates time and place as freely as Kalemie does, but the single action is what makes it feel unified despite that. **Unity of action is the load-bearing one; the other two are negotiable.**

## Questions for AI

1. State Kalemie's action in one clause. If it takes two clauses joined by *and*, that is the under-plot.
2. Trace each of the two lines separately through all seven scenes. Which scenes serve only one? Which serve neither?
3. Cost the two ways out in words: demoting the mystery vs paying it off. Which fits the remaining headroom?
4. If the story kept only one place, which would it be, and what would have to be reported rather than shown?
5. Compare against "Chantal": what specifically does single-action unity buy it that Kalemie lacks?
