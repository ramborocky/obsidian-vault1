# BARRETT — THE ART OF OMISSION

> It is seldom necessary to tell the novice what to put in; but it is frequently necessary to tell him — and oh! so hard to persuade him — that to introduce an irrelevant idea is worse than to omit a necessary detail.

## Unity Of Form

Beyond unity of plot, the short story needs **unity of form**. The novel may leave gaps of time and scene between chapters; the short story admits no such chasms. **Parts and chapters in a short story are uncanonical.**

The banned divisional signs: diary entries, letters, **stars or blank spaces to indicate a hiatus**, and numbered parts. His diagnosis of each is the same and it is severe — either the plot belongs to a larger form, in which case the fault is radical and the remedy is reconstruction, or the divisions are unnecessary and the writer has simply imported a novel's furniture.

Some passage of time and change of scene is inevitable. The tyro spans the gap with stars; **the master ignores the gap and dovetails so deftly the join is invisible.** Hawthorne needed to move his guest through supper and did it in a single sentence that acknowledges the elision and carries on.

## Padding

Any material that does not advance the action: scenes, persons, episodes, conversations, general observations — and, more insidious, occasional words and phrases that lengthen a sentence without adding to it.

**Local colour is the chief cause.** Details introduced to make a story smack of the soil must be *eminently* local — applicable only to the specific community — or they are padding. Barrett's quoted standard is exact: the atmosphere, the feeling, the idiosyncrasy, a word or phrase that reveals character — *these* are the only true local colour, not passing phrases of unkempt speech.

## Against Didacticism

Any attempt to make the story a medium for ethical or spiritual instruction "will entail ridicule upon the writer and failure upon his work." Didacticism in literature is always inartistic. A story saddled with a moral or tailed with one ceases to be a story and becomes an argument — at which point it stops concerning us.

But the qualification matters as much as the rule: **under the surface of every story is something corresponding to the moral, which we shall call the soul of the story.** And religion, or any conviction, has a place — treated for the way it influences human life and action, never from the ethical standpoint. Barrett's model is Wilkins, whose stories are saturated in New England Puritanism with no attempt to preach.

## Proportion

Essential parts differ in importance and must be handled according to their influence on the plot. **No scene, however cleverly done, may monopolise space beyond what is needed to understand what follows; no incident that furthers the plot, however trivial it seems to you, may be slighted.**

Generally the reader values a part by the words given to it — with one exception: the climax, which is judged by its position and by the preparation leading to it, not its length.

## And The Sentence That Frames The Whole Chapter

> The story proper is really only the preparation for the climax.

## For Kalemie

**The draft has no chapter divisions, and that is a real if unglamorous strength** — it reads as one continuous movement, which is more than most drafts of this length manage. But it does have scene headings, and while those are working-document scaffolding rather than published structure, they are worth testing: **remove all seven headings and read it through.** Where the story stops making sense without a heading, there is a gap Barrett would say must be dovetailed rather than signposted. The transition from the birthday porch to the dagaa stop is the first place to look.

**Padding, specifically located.** By his local-colour standard — *only that which is applicable to this community alone* — the draft is mostly clean and has one clear failure. The Scene 2 neighbourhood paragraph describes fishermen, boda boda drivers and a lake conferring visa-free movement. That is regional colour, true and interesting, and it would describe any lake town on Tanganyika. Contrast it with *mia tano mia tano*, the 16-seater Hiace, or nails on Friday and off on Monday — details that could not be transplanted. **The first is padding by his definition; the others are the real thing.**

**The didacticism warning has one live instance and one near-miss.**

- Djamela's *"we can beat them at their own game… just followers"* states the story's postcolonial argument in dialogue. Barrett's line about a story becoming an argument applies, and [[The Funhouse Mirror — AI Output as Cliché Detector|the mirror test]] flags the same speech from a different angle.
- The Scenario's ending contains *"It is not justice."* That is the moral, stated. See [[Climax and Conclusion — The Story in Six Words]].

The counterweight is that Kalemie's **soul** — the thing under the surface that corresponds to the moral — is genuinely present and does not need stating. The thin envelope argues the whole case without a word of commentary. Trust it.

**Proportion is where the draft is furthest out, and it is measurable.** Using [[Kalemie — Standing Diagnosis|the measured word counts]] against Barrett's rule that reader-perceived importance tracks word space:

- Scene 2 receives words and contains no incident.
- The rain scene (TRD2, the emotional midpoint) receives substantial space — correct.
- **The ending receives zero.** By Barrett's own arithmetic, a reader values what gets space, so the current draft tells its reader that the ending does not matter.
- Daudi's reaction to the pregnancy reveal receives nothing, though it is an incident that furthers the plot — the precise fault Barrett names as slighting.

**The sentence to hang over the whole revision:** *the story proper is only the preparation for the climax.* Kalemie has an accomplished story proper preparing for a climax that has not been written. Every scene currently reads as an end in itself, which is why the draft feels like a collection of strong scenes rather than one story — and why fixing the ending will retroactively improve scenes nobody touched.
