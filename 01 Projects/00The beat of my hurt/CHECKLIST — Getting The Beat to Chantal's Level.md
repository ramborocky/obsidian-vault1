# ✅ CHECKLIST — GETTING THE BEAT TO CHANTAL'S LEVEL

> [!abstract] Where it stands
> **Chantal D3:** 3,674 words · 40/45 · voice belongs to the writer
> **Beat D3:** 3,049 words · 39/45 · voice belongs to Claude
>
> The one-point gap is not the real gap. **The real gap is section 0.**

---

## 🔴 0 · THE THING THAT ACTUALLY SEPARATES THEM

### Djamela indicts herself. Ilunga never does.

> *I could feel the hurt coming off him like heat off a wall and I stood in it and warmed my hands, because a girl who has just been chosen wants somebody there to see her be chosen.*

That is a thirteen-year-old admitting to cruelty and refusing to apologise for it. It is the single reason a judge remembers Chantal in the next room.

**Ilunga does nothing shameful in the entire story.** He takes money for lies — but so does everyone, and the story frames it as survival. He fails to ask his mother — but the story explains why, sympathetically, with arithmetic. He is decent throughout. **He is therefore less interesting than a girl who warms her hands on a boy's pain.**

- [ ] **Find the thing Ilunga did that he is not sorry for.** Not a crime. A pettiness. Something he'd defend.
- [ ] Candidates to test: a funeral where he played four *knowing*, and took the money, and felt nothing · a family with nothing he charged full price · a moment he enjoyed the room crying where he put it · something he did to his mother
- [ ] It must be **unapologised for**, in the same register as the rest

**Everything below is secondary to this.**

---

## 📏 1 · MEASURED GAPS

- [ ] **Name him.** *Ilunga* is in the frontmatter and has never once reached the prose. The narrator of this story is currently anonymous.
- [ ] **+500–700 words.** 3,049 is the thinnest thing on the board and barely inside Barrett's 3,000–5,000 ideal. Short reads as slight.
- [ ] **Cut the stated controlling idea.** *"He had done it for us"* hands the reader the position. Chantal never states hers; Baldwin never states his. **Delete the sentence, keep the drumbeat that says it.**
- [ ] **Dramatise the six days.** Right now it is reportage — *I ate. I fixed the leg. I went down to the port.* Baldwin's narrator fails across pages of **scene**. Turn one of those six days into a scene with another person in it. This is where the +500 words go.
- [ ] **First proper noun at 9.2%** (Chantal: 0.4%). The story is unlocated for its opening tenth. Get *Kalemie* or *Giza* onto the first page.
- [ ] **14% of sentences run over thirty words** — highest on the board bar one. Most are the narrator explaining. Cut the passage that explains what the space in the beat is *for*: it is good thinking and it is told.

---

## 🧩 2 · THE MESS PASS — same fault as Chantal, worse

Nothing in this draft is accidental. Every image returns. That reads as a performance of a person rather than a person.

- [ ] Three or four details that **mean nothing and never come back**
- [ ] One paragraph that **ends without landing**
- [ ] One thing he is **wrong about** and never corrects
- [ ] **What bores him.** He is alert to everything and has never once been bored. Boredom is the most human thing there is
- [ ] If a detail from this pass starts paying off later — **cut it and use another**

---

## 👤 3 · THE PEOPLE AROUND HIM

- [ ] **The mother is a doorway.** She appears four times and stands in it every time. She has one line of dialogue in the whole story. Give her one thing that is hers — a habit, an opinion, something she is wrong about.
- [ ] **The uncle is the best thing in the draft** and gets one gesture (hand flat on the head). He needs one more moment that is not about the drum.
- [ ] **Emmanuel Ngoie's mother** stands up and makes a sound. That is enough. Do not add to her.

# 🎙 VOICE — brief changed 2026-07-28

> [!note] The authenticity constraint is lifted
> **This story is not required to draw on lived Tanzanian knowledge.** It is free invention, and it should be written that way.
> The "replace my details with your witnessed details" route is therefore **struck**. Two sections deleted.

### What that unlocks

- [ ] **The setting can go unnamed.** Baldwin names Harlem; Mujila refuses to name his city-state. An unnamed lake town would make the story about **the trade** rather than about a place — and would step around the authenticity and prize-aesthetic minefield entirely. Kalemie is not load-bearing here.
- [ ] **The ugliness can go further.** Free invention means no obligation to be careful about a real town's dignity. **Section 0 gets easier, not harder.**
- [ ] **The trade can get stranger.** Eleven beats is a good invention. It is not the strangest available one.

> [!danger] What does NOT change
> **Section 0 stands.** Ilunga's decency is a *story* problem, not an authenticity problem. A narrator who never does anything he should be ashamed of is less interesting than one who does — in any country, invented or real.

### If you still want it to end up in your voice

The editing route was always the weaker one. With the authenticity path gone, only the strong one is left:

- [ ] **Take the premise and write it from nothing, with my draft closed.** *A funeral drummer discovers the beat played over his own father was a lie.* One sentence. Nobody owns a sentence.

## 3 · Type it out

- [ ] Type the whole draft by hand. Not read — typed.
- [ ] Every place your fingers want to write it differently, **stop and write it your way**
- [ ] Those moments are the map of where your voice diverges from mine. There will be more than you expect

## 4 · The kill test

- [ ] Read each sentence and ask: **could I have written this?**
- [ ] If no — rewrite it until yes, or delete it
- [ ] Be ruthless with the ones you admire. **Admiration is the tell that it is not yours**

## 5 · Write the thing that embarrasses you

The widest gap between a person and a machine is that a machine flinches. **You do not have to try to beat it there — you only have to not flinch.**

Section 0 of this checklist is that instruction in specific form. Ilunga needs one moment I would not have given him because I was making him likeable.
