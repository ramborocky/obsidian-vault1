# PART 1 · THE HONEST NUMBERS

**Read this part before the plan.** The plan is worth doing; it is not worth doing for the reason people usually do it.

| Fact | Figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-published authors earning **under $1,000/year** | **75%** | ALLi / industry surveys |
| Authors with 1–3 books earning **under $100/month** | **~80%** | 2026 indie income data |
| Median annual income, *committed* indie authors | $13,500 | ALLi 2025 survey |
| Same survey's **mean** | above $80,000 | a handful of six-figure earners drag it up — **median is the honest number** |
| Authors with **25+ books**, median | ~$3,000/month | catalog size is the single strongest predictor |

**And the category is the hardest one.** Short story collections are widely reported as the most difficult fiction format to sell, self-published or traditional — and when they do sell to a publisher, the advance is smaller than for a novel. The collections that do work self-published are the ones **anchored in a single genre** (romance, horror, fantasy, historical), not general literary fiction.

> **So: a debut literary short story collection by an author with no audience is close to the worst-case combination in self-publishing.** That is not a reason to skip it. It is a reason to size the expectation correctly and to build for the thing that actually pays, which is **catalog**, not this book.

# PART 2 · UNIT ECONOMICS

Assume nine stories, ~4,000 words each — **~36,000 words, ~150 print pages, ~1 MB ebook file.**

### Ebook, 70% tier

70% applies only between **$2.99 and $9.99**, and Amazon deducts a **$0.15/MB delivery fee** first.

```
List $4.99
  (4.99 − 0.15) × 0.70            = $3.39 per sale
```

### The Tanzania tax bite — this is material

Tanzania has **no royalty tax treaty with the United States**, so **US sales are withheld at 30%** after you complete the W-8BEN tax interview. Non-US sales are not withheld (except Brazil).

```
US sale        $3.39 × 0.70       = $2.37 net
Non-US sale    $3.39              = $3.39 net
Blended (assume ~60% US)          ≈ $2.78 per sale
```

**Skipping the tax interview does not avoid this — it makes it worse**, because 30% is withheld on everything regardless.

### Kindle Unlimited (KDP Select)

Paid per **page actually read**, from a shared monthly pool. The rate floats: **~$0.0042–$0.0050** through 2026 (April 2026: $0.004820; May 2026: ~$0.004888).

```
~200 KENP pages, full read-through
  200 × $0.0048                   = $0.96 gross
  less 30% withholding            = $0.67
```

**But collections have heavy drop-off** — readers stop after two or three stories. At a realistic 50% read-through it is **~$0.34 net.** A KU borrow is worth roughly **one-eighth of a sale.** KU is a discovery tool here, not a revenue line.

### Paperback

60% royalty, minus print cost. B&W over 108 pages: **$1.00 fixed + $0.012/page.**

```
Print cost   1.00 + (150 × 0.012)  = $2.80
List $9.99
  (9.99 × 0.60) − 2.80             = $3.19 gross
  less 30% withholding (US)        = $2.24 net
```

**Paperback nets about the same as the ebook and sells far less.** Do it for legitimacy and for the copies you hand to people, not for income.

# PART 3 · THREE SCENARIOS, ARITHMETIC SHOWN

**Setup cost assumed: $150** — premade cover ~$100, ISBN free from KDP, self-edited with beta readers. See Part 5 for what it costs to do it properly instead.

| | Copies, year 1 | Gross | Net of setup | Reality |
| --- | --: | --: | --: | --- |
| **Modal** *(the 75% outcome)* | 40 | $111 | **−$39** | Friends, family, a few strangers. **This is the most likely single outcome** |
| **Base** *(real cover, one genre, consistent posting)* | 200 | $556 | **+$406** | Achievable with work, no advertising spend |
| **Good** *(top decile, active marketing)* | 1,000 | $2,780 | **+$2,630** | Requires either an audience you don't have yet or paid ads that eat most of it |

**Breakeven is ~54 copies.** That is the one genuinely encouraging number in this document — it is a low bar and you will probably clear it.

**Now the number that matters more:** at the base case, **$406 for roughly nine months of writing.** Per hour, that is worse than almost anything else you could do with the time. **Anyone who tells you a first anthology is a business is selling you a course.**

# PART 4 · WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY HAVE TO BE TRUE

The plan in Part 5 is built around these five, in order of how much they matter.

**1 · Catalog, not book.** Nothing in the data says one book earns. Everything says 10+ earns. **Nine stories is one book.** Treat it as unit one of five, or the arithmetic never improves.

**2 · One genre, named on the cover.** The single most repeated finding about self-published collections. "Literary short fiction" is not a genre a reader shops for. *Nine ghost stories from the lake* is.

**3 · Serialise, then bundle.** Publish the nine as individual 99¢ singles or in twos as you finish them, then release the collection. Each single is a separate storefront listing, a separate chance to be found, and a reason to publish something every six weeks instead of once.

**4 · KDP Select for the first 90 days.** Exclusivity in exchange for Kindle Unlimited visibility. KU money is negligible here; KU *reach* is not, for an unknown name.

**5 · The cover is most of the marketing.** More than the blurb, more than the price. A $100 premade cover that looks like the genre beats a $0 cover absolutely.

# PART 5 · THE PLAN

> **Hard constraint: this does not touch the prize run.** Submission is 25 October and the Atelier runs to 21 August. Nothing below competes for those blocks.

### Phase 0 · Now → 25 October — free, invisible, no extra desk time

**The Atelier's Block D drills are already disposable 25–30 minute pieces that nobody will ever read.** Stop throwing them away. Keep them in `KDP Biz/Seeds/`.

- Nine stories need nine seeds. The Atelier generates one most days.
- **Do not write anthology stories in Block E.** Block E is the manuscript's, until 25 October.
- Decide the **genre** and the **through-line** by 30 September. One decision, made on paper.

**Cost so far: nothing. Time so far: nothing you weren't already spending.**

### Phase 1 · 26 October → 31 January — write four

Prize is submitted; the desk is free. Four stories, ~4,000 words each, at the Atelier's pace.

- **Publish two as 99¢ singles** in this window. The point is to learn KDP's machinery — upload, metadata, categories, keywords, the tax interview, the payment setup — on something small, not on the book.
- **Set up the KDP account and complete the W-8BEN tax interview here**, not later. It takes a fortnight to clear and it gates every payment.

### Phase 2 · 1 February → 31 May — write five, release the collection

- Five more stories. Nine total.
- Order the collection deliberately: strongest first, second strongest last, weakest at position three.
- Commission the cover in April, not May.
- **Release: June 2027.** Ebook at $4.99 in KDP Select, paperback at $9.99.

### Phase 3 · From June 2027 — the part that is the actual business

- Book two starts the week book one ships.
- Keep the singles up; they feed the collection.
- Reinvest anything the first book earns into the second book's cover.

**Target by end of 2028: five books.** That is where the income data stops being discouraging.

# PART 6 · COSTS

| Item | Cheap | Proper | Notes |
| --- | --: | --: | --- |
| Cover | $0 (Canva) | **$100–150** premade | Don't do it yourself. This is where money buys the most |
| Editing / proofread | $0 (beta readers) | **$360–720** at $0.01–0.02/word | The single biggest optional cost |
| ISBN | **$0** | $0 | KDP issues one free. You don't need your own unless you go wide later |
| Formatting | $0 (KDP's tool) | $50 | KDP's built-in formatter is adequate for straight prose |
| **Minimum viable** | **~$100–150** | | Breakeven ~54 copies |
| **Done properly** | | **~$500–900** | Breakeven ~180–320 copies — **base-case territory, not modal** |

**Recommendation: minimum viable for book one.** Buy the cover, skip paid editing, use readers. Spend properly on book two *out of book one's earnings*, once you know whether anyone buys.
