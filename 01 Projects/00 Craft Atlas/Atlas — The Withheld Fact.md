# 🔒 ATLAS — THE WITHHELD FACT

> **Every finished story on the board withholds one central fact and never releases it.**
> **Seven for seven.** The one draft that withholds nothing scores 24.

## What makes it work, and what makes it coyness

**The withheld fact must be the thing the reader most wants.** Withholding something trivial is not omission, it is teasing, and readers punish it.

Three tests each of the six passes:

1. **Is it the question the story raises?** Sonny's recovery, Djamela's death, the hotel — each is the thing the whole story is *about*.
2. **Is something put in its place?** Chantal substitutes the inventory. Uwazuruike substitutes the puddles. **The gap is furnished, never left empty.**
3. **Does the story know it is refusing?** Chantal says so outright. Baldwin has Sonny say it. **The refusal is authored, not accidental.**

> [!warning] The master draft fails all three
> Its vaccination scar is *explained* — *vaccinations like that are usually done six months before giving birth.* The one fact that could have been withheld is annotated for the reader.
>
> **That single habit is worth several points across axes 5, 7 and 9**, and it is the clearest difference between the 24 and the 40.

## Which fact should the next story withhold?

The pattern across all six: **the withheld fact is always the one that would assign blame.**

- Whether Sonny relapses would settle whether the narrator failed.
- Whether anyone died would settle whether the protest was worth it.
- Whether it was murder would settle what the grandfather is.
- What happened in the hotel would settle whether Djamela was a victim or a participant — **and the story's whole position is that this is the wrong question.**

**So: withhold the fact that would let the reader stop thinking.** That is the operational rule, and it is the same instruction as [[Writing That Learns, Not Teaches]] arrived at from the other end.
