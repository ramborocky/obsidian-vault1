# THE ART OF BREVITY — GRANT FAULKNER

**The sixth craft base, and the first one that is not about how to build a story but about how much of it to leave out.**

> [!info] What this base is and where it sits
> Notes taken from a 58,000-word book so the ideas are usable without rereading it. **These are my notes, not the book** — short quotes attributed, everything else compressed and applied. Copyright remains Faulkner's.
>
> It lives in [[x-factor]] rather than in `Kalemie/` because it is **general training**, not a Chantal application. Everything here connects out to the five Kalemie bases and to the three annotated stories in `Annotated/`.

## Why this book matters to work that is not flash fiction

Faulkner writes about stories of 100 to 1,000 words. Chantal is 3,900 and the gallery stories run 3,400–5,100. **The book still transfers, for one reason:**

> **Every other base in this vault tells you what a story needs. This one tells you what a story survives losing.**

McKee builds. Cleland tensions. Gardner theorises. Barrett excludes — and Barrett is the closest cousin here, a century earlier. Kiteley prescribes. **Faulkner is the only one whose subject is the space where the words are not**, and that turns out to be where two of this project's live findings already were.

> [!success] It independently confirms two things found empirically in this folder
> **1 · The section break is a prose instrument.** [[Chantal × Beat — The Twenty-Eight Houses (AI Draft, Not Canon)|Experiment 2]] measured that hard breaks shorten sentences by deleting the transitional sentence. Faulkner has a whole chapter arguing white space is *"a different type of punctuation, with its own rules of grammar."* → [[White Space as Grammar]]
>
> **2 · Instruction-as-epigraph.** Found in [[Dite — ANNOTATED|*Dite*]] and used in [[MABANGA (New Story — AI Draft, Not Canon)|MABANGA]] as a price list. Faulkner has a chapter on exactly this — the recipe, the outline, the flow chart, the paint catalogue as story forms. → [[Found Objects and the Infraordinary]]
>
> Two independent arrivals at the same technique. That is worth more than either alone.

## Where it agrees and where it fights

The value of a sixth base is not more advice. It is **the disagreements**, because a disagreement between two good sources is where a real decision lives.

| Question | Faulkner | The other bases |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Plot** | Freytag's pyramid *"kidnaps young writers each year."* Momentum can come from image, mood or voice | **[[Story structure by robert mackee.md\|McKee]] is built on the pyramid.** Direct conflict — see [[Plot as Yearning, Not Pyramid]] |
| **Omission** | The iceberg. Ellipsis as the flash aesthetic | **[[The Art of Omission — Unity, Padding, and Proportion\|Barrett]] agrees, from 1900.** Strongest agreement in the vault |
| **Endings** | *"End it two paragraphs earlier"* | **[[Climax and Conclusion — The Story in Six Words\|Barrett]]: end within a paragraph of the climax.** Same rule, 120 years apart |
| **Character** | Flatness is a tool. Kundera: a character need only *"fill the whole space of the situation"* | **[[Character Dimension and Cast Design\|McKee]] wants dimension; [[Interest, Free Will, and the Active Protagonist\|Gardner]] wants free will.** Real tension, resolvable by length |
| **Show don't tell** | The fairy tale says *beautiful* and does not show it — and works | **[[Show, Don't Tell — Needless Explanation\|Gardner]] forbids exactly this.** Faulkner's exception is narrow; see [[Characters in Fleeting Profile]] |
| **Silence** | Silence is never empty; it has subtext | **[[Whisper Dont Shout\|Cleland]] and [[Reveal Answers Slowly\|Cleland]] agree** |
| **Constraint** | Restriction produces material | **[[The Method — Restrict and Liberate\|Kiteley]] agrees exactly** |

## The single most quotable thing in the book

> *"Omission is a form of creation."* — David Mamet, quoted by Faulkner

Everything else in this base is a consequence of that sentence.
