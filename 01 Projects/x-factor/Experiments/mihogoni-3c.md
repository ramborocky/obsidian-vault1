# THE TERMINUS
### Mihogoni cycle · #3, third pass

> [!danger] AI DRAFT · NOT CANON · NOT THE USER'S WORK
> Written by Claude as a technique experiment. **Never submit, never merge into `01 Story Brain`, never present as the author's.** → [[12 The Rules — Source of Truth]]

> [!abstract] What the author asked for, and what changed
> **The complaint was correct.** [[mihogoni-3b]] had the narrator everywhere, and everywhere licensed noticing that moved nothing — *she had drunk about a cup at the house that morning, boiled the night before.* That sentence does no work. It exists because the narrator could reach the kettle.
>
> **The fix is a boundary, not a trim.** The narrator is no longer water-as-substance. It is **the lake — a place, with an edge.** It knows its shore, its surface, what enters it, and its own history. It **cannot** go to the school, the kitchen, the police station or the hotel. Those scenes must come back to the water or they do not exist. A narrator that cannot follow her cannot pad.
>
> **The refusal is now structural.** Draft 3's *I am not going to describe the hotel* was a choice. Here the narrator physically cannot get there — and the police station is handled the same way.
>
> **What the boundary bought back: Uvira is on this lake.** The crossing happens on the narrator's own surface, and it still cannot follow her ashore.
>
> **Five epigraphs, climbing** — [[Dite — Story Sheet|Dite]]'s device with [[MABANGA (New Story — AI Draft, Not Canon)|MABANGA]]'s hostility: **Radjabu's instruction → Radjabu's instruction → an explorer's register → an imperial notice → a fare table.** Cosmology to price. Nobody says colonialism.

> [!warning] One line deliberately left where it is
> [[Supporting - D_Juma|Radjabu's file]] holds the author's own sentence for the ending — *I was wrong. The lake doesn't put things back in order. It keeps them. There's a difference.* [[The X-Factor]] recorded it as unspent and untaken. **It is still unspent.** Nothing in this file uses it, echoes it, or writes around it, and the close deliberately goes somewhere else so the position stays open. It is one of the best lines in the vault and it is not mine to cash.

> *Stand side-on. Throw flat, not down — it will not take a stone that comes at it straight. Count the touches out loud. If it goes under on the first one that is your wrist and not the water. And do not throw at the lake. Throw along it. A man who throws at a thing has already decided what it is.*

---

The road comes down out of the town and stops being a road about forty metres before the water, and that is the edge of what can be seen from here.

Past that there are the first houses and the charcoal woman's fire, and behind them nothing. What happens up there arrives late and secondhand, the way anything arrives at a shore — carried down by somebody, or not carried down at all.

Four hulls stand off the point. Three are going over and one is upright and the mud is taking all four at the same rate. The one furthest out has a hold that filled a long time ago and the water in it is colder than the water around it and turns once a year, slowly, and the fish do not go in.

Nothing has ever been at the bottom of the middle of this lake. Men came and let a line down at four places and found no bottom at three hundred fathoms and wrote that down, and it is still true, and they are gone.

---

In the evenings Radjabu came down to the water with the boy from two houses along.

He would take a stone out of his pocket — he carried them, he did not look for them at the shore like a man who had not planned to teach — and he would stand side-on and throw it flat, and it would go four, five, six.

*Count them out loud,* he said. *If you count in your head you will lie.*

The boy threw and it went under on the first touch.

*Your wrist,* said Radjabu. *Not the water. Never the water.*

Then he talked while the boy threw, which is how he did all of it. He said the lake is cold, like some women. He said people come and they go and it stays the same, dark and deep, and it swallows things down and holds them, and that a man who understands that will not be surprised by anything in his life.

The boy listened. The boy has always listened. He is the one who stands still while an older man talks, and there is a use for such a boy, and Radjabu had found it.

The girl was on the porch behind them, closer to the house than to the water. She heard it the way you hear a thing you have heard since before you could count. She was not throwing anything.

---

Radjabu is not from the lake side originally. He sells fruit on Ave de Tanganyique in the mornings and lays hands on people in the evenings, and the second one is not a thing anybody says out loud, and everybody in the down part of town knows the way to his door.

He has never been able to give her anything except sayings and fruit. He decided a long time ago that if he refused the outside hard enough she would stop wanting it, and it did not work like that, it only made the wanting private, and he does not know this and there is nobody to tell him.

He has also seen a car outside places it should not be, more than once, and has said nothing to her, and has increased the talking about the lake instead.

That is the whole of what can be reported about that. He came down and he threw stones and he talked, and what he did not say went back up the road with him each time.

---

> *In the sebene the guitar does not stop, so do not listen for the stopping. Listen for the man who is not playing — he is the one who knows where it ends. Clap on two and four; on one and three you are clapping at the music and not inside it. And when the shouting starts, do not follow the loudest. The loudest is being paid.*

---

He taught the boy that too, on the same shore, out of the same pocket.

He would put his phone on the sand face up and let a long one run, and it would go into the sebene and stay in the sebene, and he would hold up a hand at the exact place the thing turned, before it turned, every time.

*There,* he would say. *You heard it after. I heard it before. That is the whole difference and it is not a gift, it is forty years.*

The boy could not do it. The boy claps on one and three, which Radjabu told him is a Protestant's clap and can be unlearned but not quickly.

*Do not follow the loudest,* he said. *The loudest is being paid.*

The girl on the porch heard that one too, and did not ask what it meant, and it did not come up again for two years.

---

There is one way out of this lake and its mouth is at the north end of the town.

The river goes out past the cement works and under the bridge where the boys jump off in December, and it runs down through the forest until it finds the Congo, and the Congo takes it to the sea. Everything else that comes in stays in. That is not a large river for a lake this size and it is the only one there is.

The railway ends at the shore as well. It was built to go further and it does not, and it has not moved since before the men who built it were old, and in the afternoons men sit at the station the way men sit at a bedside.

The town divides in a way that is visible from the water. There is a part of it reached by pipe and a part reached by bucket, and the buckets come down to the standpipe or to the shore and go back up the road, spilling at every third step, leaving a line on the road for as long as the sun takes. When the power goes on the carried side the queue at the standpipe gets longer and quieter. The pipes on the other side stay full.

The girl came down for water in the afternoons like everybody on that side, and stood in the queue, and did not talk much in it.

---

In March she came down to the porch in the evening with woodsmoke in her clothes and sat with the boy.

Something had happened up at the house and it did not come down. What came down was the smoke, which was candle smoke and not cooking smoke, and a mood, and one sentence in the middle of a lot of nothing.

*He thinks I want things,* she said.

The boy said nothing, which is what the boy does.

*I do want things.* And then, after a while: *I want to control them. I want to conquer like the Europeans did, except now you do not need weapons or degrees. You only need followers.*

She had turned thirteen that day and she believed all of it and she had worked it out herself, from the evidence in front of her, which was a town where the people with cars had taken things and the people without cars had been taken from.

Behind them the four hulls sat where they sit. She counted them without appearing to. She has done that since she was small, in the evenings, one two three four, pointing with her chin, and she always gets four, and it has never once looked as though the answer pleased her.

---

> *13 February. Reached the water at Ujiji after eight months. Soundings taken at four points; no bottom found at three hundred fathoms. It is not laid down on any chart. The natives have no name for the whole of it, only for the parts they use. We are satisfied it is the source, or near it. Named this day.*

---

They have come and measured it four times.

The first two came overland and were carried the last part of the way because they could not walk, and one of them could not see, and they argued about what it was a source of, and they were both wrong, and their names are on it.

Another came later and was looked for by a man with a good hat who found him at Ujiji, and that meeting is in books.

Then a government came and built the railway down to the shore, and put a ship on it in five thousand pieces, and the ship was carried here by the railway in crates and screwed together on the sand. That one is still afloat. It was sunk on purpose by the navy that owned it, and raised by the navy that took the country off them, and it carries goats and mattresses and people's aunties from one end of this water to the other, and it is older than the country and older than the last two names of the country.

The four hulls off the point are not that ship. Nobody remembers what they are. They went down in a season nobody wrote down, and the men who could have said are dead, and that is a different kind of record from three hundred fathoms and a date.

The lake has been named four times by people who had just arrived. It answered to none of it and it did not refuse either.

---

The car came down to the dagaa stop, which is at the water, which is why any of this can be told at all.

It was black and it had been washed that morning somewhere on the other side of town and it was still beaded along the top of the door. When the window went down the beads ran into the mechanism.

She was standing in the road in the heat with the boy beside her.

A horn, different from the boda boda men's. The window came down with a swish, not the clink of a handle going round, like a word said quietly on purpose.

*Je m'appelle Jean Luc. Tu es d'où, ma belle.*

*Rue Saint Michel,* she said.

She lied immediately and easily and without deciding to, and it came out as though it had been sitting behind her teeth for years, waiting. Rue Saint Michel is where the children with the yellow windows live and she has never set foot on it.

The boy started the thing about strangers. *Mama alisema —*

*We shouldn't talk to strangers,* said the man, finishing it for him. And he smiled.

He said he was a director. Consul général de Belgique. He said it in French with Lingala underneath and his French was worse than he thought it was, and she noticed, and she decided not to have noticed. That is the part she would like written down somewhere. Not that she was fooled. That she did the deciding.

He wrote a number on a piece of paper and put a ten thousand note under it.

Then he asked her something short, and she gave him a name that was not the one she came with.

*Chantal.*

She said it out clear, into a road, over the noise, the way you say a thing you want carried. It went across the water and it is out there. Almost nothing said on that road ever gets this far. To reach the water a thing has to be shouted, or said to somebody who is walking away.

The boy did not say one word after *mama alisema*. He stood there while it happened and he was still standing there after the car had gone, which is longer than anybody stands in the road in that heat.

She was thirteen. She is not asking to be forgiven for that. She is asking for it to be counted correctly.

---

Then a long time in which she did not come down much.

She came for water and went straight back up. On the evenings she did come she counted the hulls and went on walking, and she was carrying something back and forth up that road that never once came down to the shore, and there is no way from here to say what it was.

What can be said is that the boy came down alone that season, often, and threw stones badly, and that Radjabu stopped bringing the phone.

---

> *The line terminates at the lake. Beyond the terminus, traffic proceeds by steamer. The vessel is delivered in sections by rail — five thousand pieces — and assembled upon the shore. She is armed. Her draught permits her to make any port upon either coast. Journey time is not guaranteed in the season of storms.*

---

She went up the road with the boy on a morning in the dry season and they did not come back down until evening.

The road goes up past the second bend and after that it cannot be seen from here. What is up there is a building with dust on everything and a tap in the yard that has not run in a while, and what happened in it happened out of sight, the way most of what happens to people happens out of sight of the thing they were told would look after them.

They came down at evening. She came down bent over. The boy came down beside her, half a step behind, and he is fifteen and good and he thinks shame is the only thing that can bend a person.

That is the whole of the account and it is not a refusal. **It cannot be seen from the water.** The road bends.

What came down with them: an envelope that had been fat that morning and was not, and a number that had been agreed on by three men out loud in two languages, and a second smaller number for a doctor at Uvira.

Uvira is on this lake. That part is not out of sight.

---

The women wash at the edge, downshore of the boats, where the stones are flat.

Her mother came down on a Thursday as she has come down every Thursday since before the girl could count. She had the basket and she had the soap and she worked through the pile in the order it came.

Then she stopped, and put one thing to the side, and finished everything else first.

There are things that come out of cloth and things that do not, and the list of the second kind is short and nothing has ever been added to it or taken off it. Dirt, blood, fish, smoke, sweat, a mango a child has been holding — all of those go. An oil made to sit down in the fibre and stay does not. Soap gets everything around it and lifts and leaves it. It goes eventually, but by air and by time. Four days. Five.

She had gone up the hill again and come back down with something else in a bottle, sharp and thin, and she put that in and worked it through with her hands, and it came out in one go, four months of it, and went off the flat stones into the shallows and was gone in about the time it takes to notice.

She did not say anything while she did it. There was nobody there to say it to. She wrung the two of them out and put them on the top of the basket instead of the bottom and carried them up the road.

---

> *MV LIEMBA · Kigoma – Kalemie – Uvira – Mpulungu.*
> *FIRST CLASS, cabin, two berths. SECOND CLASS, cabin, four berths. THIRD CLASS, deck.*
> *Deck passengers should provide their own covering. Livestock by weight. Children under three carried free.*
> *The vessel does not wait. Fares payable before boarding, never after.*

---

She crossed in the third month, on the deck, at night, which is when it goes.

That part is on the water and can be given properly. She sat against the rail on the north side with her back to the town and she did not sleep and she did not go inside. There were women asleep in a row with their covering over their faces and a man with two goats tied to the same rail and the goats did not settle either. The engine is under everything and you stop hearing it after an hour and then you hear it again whenever it changes.

She had a bag with almost nothing in it, which is what people carry when they are going somewhere they intend to leave the same day.

She counted the lights on the shore going by. There is nothing else to do on that deck at night and she is a girl who counts, and the lights on the down side of any town go out early and the lights further up stay on, so the count goes wrong in the same way at every town, and she did it anyway, at four of them.

The boat came in at Uvira before it was light.

She went up the gangway and off the end of it and onto the shore, and after that there is nothing. The shore at Uvira is a shore. It has a road going up from it and the road bends.

She came back down it about eleven hours later and stood in the queue for the deck and crossed again with the light behind her.

Whatever happened up that road happened where it could not be seen, the same as the other road, and the two of them are the only places in this account that matter and neither one is visible from the water.

She came home with the second number spent and the small thing that had been getting bigger in her no longer getting bigger.

*The vessel does not wait.* It went on south to Mpulungu the same night with the goats still on the rail.

---

School again on the Monday.

She came down to the water at the end of that week and counted the hulls and got four.

The boy was on the shore and she did not go over to him and he did not come over to her, and they were both on that shore for about twenty minutes, and then she went up.

Radjabu was on the point later throwing stones with nobody there. He is a man who does not stop doing a thing because the person he did it for is not present. Six touches, then four, then six. He counted them out loud to the water the whole time, which is what he had told the boy to do, and there is nobody who has ever heard him do it except from here.

---

There is a thing she worked out and has not said to anybody.

When this finishes, and it is going to finish, somebody will be taken for it, and it will not be him. They will take somebody visible and poor who cannot afford an envelope, and the town will agree to be satisfied, and she thinks it will be the one who hangs off the side of the dala dala and shouts the fare every morning where everyone can see him.

*Do not follow the loudest,* Radjabu told the boy on this shore, with the phone face up in the sand. *The loudest is being paid.*

He meant a man in a song. He said it two years before it was any use to anybody and he said it to the wrong child.

The one who shouts the fare gave her a coin off it once and called her a name that means family. That is the whole of what he did.

---

She came down the shore in the evening, walking home, counting.

Four. It came out even, the way it does.

The river goes out at the north end of the town, past the cement works, under the bridge where the boys jump off in December, down through the forest until it finds the Congo, and the Congo takes it to the sea. It is the only way out there is and it has been there longer than the town and longer than any of the names.

She walked the whole length of that shore with it on her right hand, counting the hulls on her left, and she did not once look north.

# ⊹ WHAT THE THIRD PASS FOUND

**1 · A bounded narrator cannot pad. That was the whole complaint and the boundary is the whole fix.**
The author's objection to [[mihogoni-3b]] was exact: *she had drunk about a cup at the house that morning* moves nothing, and it exists only because an omnipresent narrator could reach the kettle. **You cannot trim your way out of that** — the filler is generated by the access. Give the narrator an edge and the sentences die at the source. Everything in this draft is at the shore, on the surface, or in the record, and there was nowhere to put a cup of water.

**2 · The refusal became geography.** Draft 3 says *I am not going to describe the hotel* — a choice, and a strong one. Here **the road bends and the station cannot be seen from the water**, and the same sentence covers the hotel, the school, the kitchen and the back room. *"That is the whole of the account and it is not a refusal. It cannot be seen from the water."* **A narrator that is not permitted to be there does not have to be tasteful about not being there.**

**3 · The boundary paid for itself once, and that is the crossing.** Uvira is on this lake. The termination journey happens on the narrator's own surface and it still cannot follow her ashore — the deck, the goats on the rail, the lights of four towns counted wrong the same way each time, eleven hours, and *the vessel does not wait.* **This scene does not exist in Chantal and could not be written by any narrator that was allowed indoors.**

**4 · Five epigraphs, and the climb is the argument.**
**Radjabu's stone instruction → Radjabu's sebene instruction → an explorer's register → an imperial railway notice → a fare table.** The first two are a father's knowledge, in the imperative, given freely. The last is a price. In between, the lake gets measured, named, and put on a timetable by people who had just arrived. **Nobody says colonialism, and the word does not appear.** → [[Dite — Story Sheet]]

**5 · MABANGA's contribution is the hostility, not the material.** *Dite*'s recipes are neutral. [[MABANGA (New Story — AI Draft, Not Canon)|MABANGA]]'s tariff is the antagonist speaking in commercial language above a child who cannot read it. The fare table here does the same job — **children under three carried free**, and *fares payable before boarding, never after* (a deliberate echo of MABANGA's *on paie avant le studio, jamais après*) — sitting above the section where a child is crossing water on a number three men agreed on.

**6 · Radjabu is the largest gain and he is the author's character, not mine.**
He was a name in [[Supporting - D_Juma|a character file]] and a man with a watermelon. Here he is on the shore twice, teaching a boy who is not his son, and **both lessons detonate later without him present**: *do not throw at the lake, a man who throws at a thing has already decided what it is*, and *do not follow the loudest, the loudest is being paid* — which turns out to be the exact mechanism by which Morisho is taken, said two years early, to the wrong child. His file's Contradiction reads *a wise man whose wisdom is also a form of avoidance.* **That is now on the page instead of in a note.**

**7 · The motif is the Lukuga, and it is an absence again — but the lake's own.**
[[mihogoni-3b]] used the dry board under the third bucket; that room is inland and is gone. The replacement is better because it belongs to the narrator: **one outflow, its mouth at this town, and everything else that comes in stays.** Six placements, ending on *she did not once look north.* Paired motif: **counting** — Radjabu's *count them out loud or you will lie*, the soundings at three hundred fathoms, the berths in the tariff, the lights of four towns, and her four hulls. **Everyone in this story measures the lake, and the lake was never the thing being measured.**

**8 · What it costs, stated plainly.** Daudi's room, the buckets, the motherboard and the fob under the loose board are **all gone** — they are inland and the narrator cannot reach them, so the story loses Chantal's best closing gesture and gets a walk along a shore instead. **That is a real loss and I have not disguised it.** The kiss is gone for the same reason. If the bucket room matters more than the boundary, the boundary is the wrong instrument and 3b is the better file.

**9 · It came out at 3,416 words of prose, against 4,879 for [[mihogoni-3b]] — 1,463 shorter for the same events.** That is the measurement that confirms finding 1. **Nothing was cut to get there.** No trimming pass was run; the boundary simply refused to generate the sentences, and the two scenes that vanished (the room, the kiss) left with the access rather than with an edit. Rhythm sits closer to *Dite*'s profile than to either winner's, and is not optimised toward any number — per finding 7 in [[x-factor]] the metric predicts nothing.

**10 · The author's line is still unspent** and the ending was routed away from it on purpose. The close lands on the river and the direction she does not look, so **the position Radjabu corrects is left standing and uncorrected** — which is what leaves room for his sentence, in his mouth, in the author's hand.
