# THE THIRD BUCKET
### Mihogoni cycle · #3 · the water

> [!danger] AI DRAFT · NOT CANON · NOT THE USER'S WORK
> Written by Claude as a technique experiment. **Never submit, never merge into `01 Story Brain`, never present as the author's.** → [[12 The Rules — Source of Truth]]

> [!abstract] What is being tested
> [[mihogoni-2|Mihogoni 2]] took the *Dite* object-chorus to a **new** incident so the voice could be heard without risking Chantal. This one does the opposite: **the same events as [[2026-07-28 — Chantal (Draft 3, Prize Pass)|Chantal Draft 3]], narrated by the water.**
>
> **Why water and not the ships.** The ships can only see the shore. Water is in the lake, the rain, the buckets, the wash, the watermelon, the breath, and the body — so it has access to every scene Djamela has, and to one she does not narrate. It is the only narrator in this world that is *inside* the pregnancy.
>
> **The constraint that keeps it naive: the water has no eyes.** It knows weight, temperature, what dissolves and what does not, and sound. It cannot read a face. It reports chemistry with total precision and understands none of it.
>
> **The existential frame is already in Chantal and is never adjudicated here.** Her father: *the lake puts things back in order, and it remembers everything in its right place.* Daudi: *it is hydrogen and oxygen, and it has no intelligence.* Both men say this over water. The water does not answer either of them, because it does not know it has been asked.

---

We are the lake, and we are also in the fruit, and in the smoke off the charcoal, and in every person on that road, and while it is happening we have no way of telling those apart.

We should say at the start that we have no eyes, because everything after this depends on it. What we have is weight. Temperature. What dissolves in us and what will not. A person reaches us as a warm displacement with salt in it. A pot on a fire reaches us as a place we are being driven out of. Rain is ourselves coming back down at speed, and it is the loudest thing that happens to us, and it happens every year, and we have never got used to it, if getting used to a thing means anything.

Sound we have. Sound goes further in us than it goes in air, which is why the shouting from the road arrives out at the hulls before it arrives at the far end of the road itself. We have four hulls. They are iron and they are going over slowly and we are inside three of them. The fourth has water in her hold that came in a long time ago and has not been out since. It is colder than we are. It does not move except in a slow circle when we change our mind about the season, and there are things on the floor of it, and we have no use for them, and the fish do not go in.

Below a certain depth we do not turn over at all. The top of us goes up and comes down with the year. The bottom of us has not been at the top since before any of this, and nothing lives down there, and it is not dark to us because nothing is dark to us, it is only still.

That is our own arrangement and we mention it once. The rest of this is about the road.

---

The town divides in a way that is plain to us and we have never heard anybody say it.

There is a part of the town we reach through a pipe, and a part of the town we reach in a bucket on a head. That is the whole of the division. In the piped part we arrive under pressure, warm from the roof tanks in the afternoon, and we come out of a tap in a room with a door on it. In the carried part we are lifted out of a standpipe or out of us, and we go up a road in a plastic bucket with a lid that does not fit, and we spill at every third step, and the road there is wet in a line for as long as the sun takes.

When the power goes on the carried side, the pump for that side stops, and we stop arriving, and the queue at the standpipe gets longer and quieter. The pipes on the other side stay full. We are in both, at the same time, at different pressures, and we have counted the difference for years without ever finding a use for the number.

The girl's family is on the carried side. We know their house from the inside of a kettle and from a gutter that came off the wall the year before last and is lying in the yard where it landed, and when it rains we go into the gutter out of habit and run the length of it and come out the far end into the dirt, because it is still shaped the way it was shaped, and shape is most of what decides where we go.

The other thing that decides where we go is downhill.

We should also say, because it matters later and will not matter to anybody else: we take most things out of cloth. Dirt, blood, fish, smoke, sweat, the mango a child has been holding. There is a short list of things we do not take out. We have never been able to add to the list or take anything off it.

---

She was at the school gate on a Tuesday and we were in her.

We are always in her. A person is mostly us and the proportion drifts a little across the day. What we had that morning was this: she had drunk about a cup at the house, we were coming off her at the back of the neck faster than usual for the hour, her hands were cold at the fingers, and she was standing still with her hands moving.

We could not hear what she was counting. Counting is done inside a head and the inside of a head is dry to us, or dry enough. We only had the hands.

Then the other girl took her arm, and there was warmth passing between two arms, and they went in through a gate.

What we can report from that Tuesday, which we could report from any of about ninety days before it, is that there was a second water in her by then.

It is small and it is closed and it is warmer than the rest of her and it does not mix with the rest of her. We have been in a great many women and it happens in some of them and not others. It arrives, and it gets bigger, and then in most cases it comes out all at once and there is a lot of noise. We have no name for it. We would not know what to do with a name.

We noted that it was there. We note it every time. It is the same to us as the thermocline moving, which is to say it is a thing that changes and that we can measure, and measuring is what we do instead of anything else.

---

There are children in the piped part of town who go to the school with the wall.

We are on those children twice a day, under pressure, warm, in a room with a door. We are in a machine in their houses that turns their clothes over in us for forty minutes and then wrings us out of them and sends us down a pipe. We come off them clean and we go back to us through a channel with a grating on it.

The girl has stood on the road at night and looked at their windows. We were on her face at the time, a thin film of us, the way there is on everyone in the evening, and we can tell you it did not change temperature while she stood there, and that she stood a long time for somebody not doing anything.

The man on the side of the bus shouts the same two words all morning until his voice goes. His voice goes because he has dried out at the throat and does not stop to drink. We are in the shout — every shout is a small amount of us leaving a person — and we are what he is running out of, and he does not know that, and it would not change anything if he did, because the alternative to shouting is not being paid.

Once he handed her a coin out of her own fare and said a word to her. It was a short word and it was said close to her face, so it did not carry, and we did not get it.

Names reach us badly. People say them at each other from a foot away and the sound goes into the sand. To get a name out to us somebody has to shout it, or write it, or put it on a hand.

---

In March her father bought a watermelon and we were the watermelon.

A watermelon is us with a rind on it. We had been in the ground under a field two districts over, and we came up through a stem, and we sat in the dark inside that rind getting sweet, which is a thing that happens to us and about which we have no view. Then a man carried us on a bicycle for eleven kilometres and stood us in a pyramid on Ave de Tanganyique with our brothers, and one of us went home under his arm.

He pushed a candle into the top of us. It went in about a thumb's depth and the wax was cold and we closed round it.

They talked over us for some time. We were the loudest thing in the room and nobody looked at us, which is normal. Then the girl leaned in and there was a wave of warm damp air across the rind, which is a person breathing out, and she said something with a laugh at the end of it, and the man said something back with no laugh in it at all, and the mother said something very fast.

Then the flame went out and the smoke came up. Smoke off a candle is mostly us. We went up to the iron roof and hit it and spread out flat and stayed there, because iron in the evening is cooler than the room and we go where it is cooler, and there was nowhere else for us to be. We hung under that roof for about an hour.

Later she was on the porch with the boy and the lake was in front of them. She talked and he did not. We had them both from below, as two warm places on a step, and the four hulls between them and the far side.

We would like to be accurate about the hulls, since she was looking at them. Three of them are going over and one is upright, and the mud is taking all four at about the same rate, and none of that has anything to do with anybody on the porch.

---

The boy's room is one room with a roof that has given up in four places.

We come through those four places when it rains, at four different speeds, and there are buckets under three of them and the fourth is over the door where it does not matter. The buckets are plastic and they were different colours once. We know them by the note they make. The first one is nearly empty most of the time and rings. The second is squat and takes the heaviest of the four leaks and does not ring at all. The third stands on a board that is not nailed down.

We have never been under that board.

That is not a thing we have decided. The board sits flat and the floor under it is packed earth and it is above the run of the yard, so nothing gets in from the side, and the bucket on top of it takes whatever comes down. We have been all over that room. We have been in the mattress and in the physics papers and in the mortar between the bricks and in both of them, many times, for years. Under the board is the one place in that house we have never been.

He has a wall in there with things nailed to it. A hubcap. A skull. A bicycle wheel. Between the skull and the hubcap there is a green board with gold lines on it, and we know that one, because we are the reason it is on the wall.

It was in a heap behind a café by the market and it rained on that heap for two seasons. We got into it and stayed in it and we are still in it. That is all it takes. A thing like that has a very small amount of us in it in the wrong places and it stops working and does not start again.

She picked it out of the heap and gave it to him. We were on both their hands at the time.

---

It rained hard in April, which it does, and she came in wet.

We were on the outside of her, in her clothes, running out of her hair onto the floor, and we were also in the buckets and coming through the roof and hitting the buckets, and the noise of us filled that room completely. The note of the second bucket got lower as it filled. That is the only clock that room has.

They sat on the mattress. Her knee was against the edge of his knee, and we were between the two knees as a film of sweat, and we can tell you that the film was there before the knees touched.

They talked. Some of it we had.

He said a word we have heard him say many times. He said order needs energy, and disorder is what happens when you stop.

Then she said her father says we are the opposite. She said her father says we put things back in order and remember everything in its right place.

The boy said her father was a wise man. Then he waited, and then he said that we are hydrogen and oxygen and have no intelligence.

Both of those were said in a room where we were coming through the roof into three buckets, and we went on doing that at the same rate through both of them.

Then something happened between the two of them for about twenty seconds. What we had: both temperatures went up at the surface, both breathing stopped and started again, and the film between the knees got wider. Then it ended and she said something short and there was a laugh in it.

We filled the third bucket twice that afternoon. The board under it stayed dry.

---

The car came down to the dagaa stop and it had been washed that morning on the other side of town, in a yard, out of a hose, by a man who was paid to do it. We were still on it in beads along the top of the door. When the window went down we ran down inside the door and into the mechanism, which is where we always end up on a car like that, and which is what will finish that car in eleven years.

We were on her too. It was hot and she was standing in the road and we were coming off her at the temples and at the back of the knees.

A man in the car said some words in one language. She said three words back. He said more. The boy started a sentence and the man finished it for him. We had the sound of all of it and about half the shape of it, and we do not hold shapes well, and we could not tell you now which of them said what.

Then something was handed out of the window and taken, and there was paper in it, and paper is a thing we ruin, so she was careful with it, and we came off her palm onto it and made it soft in one corner.

Then he asked her something short and she gave him a name.

We got that one. She said it out clear, into a road, over the noise of the boda bodas, the way people say a thing they want carried. **Chantal.** It came to us across sixty metres of hot air and we have it, and it is one of two names out of that whole year that ever reached us, and it is not the name she is called in the house.

The boy said nothing else. We were on him as well. His temperature at the surface went up and stayed up for a long time after the car had gone, which is a thing we see in people who have been running, and he had not been running.

She went home and put the paper under a mattress. We are in a mattress the way we are in everything, and the paper went soft there over the week, at the folds first, the way paper does.

---

We were not in the room where she decided things, if there is such a room.

We go downhill. We go up when it is hot. We go into whatever shape is offered and we take the shape and we do not have another way. We are saying this because it is the only account we have of ourselves and it seems to be a different kind of account from the one she was giving.

---

We are not going to be able to tell you much about the hotel, and it is not out of any delicacy. We were not in that room in any quantity.

Here is what we have.

There is a tap on the third floor that we come out of hard for the first three seconds and then evenly. It was run twice each time, once short and once long. There is a drain in that floor that we go down slowly because of what is in it, and what is in it is hair and soap and us.

There is a thing that goes into cloth there that we do not take out.

We said there was a short list. This is on it. It is oil and it is made to sit in the fibre and not come out, and soap gets everything else and lifts around it and leaves it, and it will go eventually but only by air and time, four days, five. We have no way to remove it. In sixty years of that lake we have not found a way to remove it, and it is not the kind of thing we could work at, because we do not work at things.

She learned about the four days. We know she learned it because the clothes started going to the bottom of the basket instead of the top, and staying there, and the basket is in a corner where we are in the wall, and we could feel the pile change order.

Her mother does the wash on Thursdays and has done since before the girl could count. We are the wash. That is us, in a plastic tub, at the temperature of the yard, with soap in us.

---

The station had no water in it except what was in them.

It is a dry building. There is a tap in the yard that does not run and has not run in a while, and dust on everything, and dust is what is there when we are not. We were only in that place as breath, and as the film on the men, and as what was in the officer's mouth, which had onion and whiskey in it, and both of those come to us as things dissolved and we report them without prejudice, because we have no way to have any.

We were in her the whole time she stood at that counter. The small closed water was still in her and had got bigger and had got its own temperature by then, about half a degree over hers, which is normal, and which happens to all of them.

At one point she said something to the man across the counter and the room went quiet. We do not have the words. We have that four men stopped moving at once, and the air in a room where four men have stopped moving gets damper very fast, and we were the damp.

Then there was a back room, and we were in five people in the back room, and nothing happened to anybody's body in there. There was talking. It went on about twenty minutes and it had numbers in it. When they came out her temperature at the surface had dropped, which is a thing that happens after a fright and also happens after sitting still in the shade, and we cannot tell those apart and have never been able to.

Outside, the lake was on her left the whole way home, with the four hulls in it. We were flat that evening. We are flat most evenings in that season.

---

The house was empty and the clothes were folded on the end of the bed.

All of them. Including the two from the bottom of the basket that still had four days to go.

They had been washed twice. We know this from the fibre, which is the only thing we know anything from. Once with soap, in the usual way, in the tub in the yard. Then again, after, with something else in us — sharp, thin, either the vinegar or the paraffin, we could not tell which by then because we had already carried it and let it go.

The second wash took the thing out. We had not been able to take it out in four months and somebody put the right thing in us on a Tuesday afternoon and it came out in one go and went down into the yard and into the ground and, in about nine months, back to us in the lake, in a quantity too small to find.

We want to be accurate here because it is the part of this we can be accurate about. **We were the instrument.** We did not do it and we could not have declined to do it. Somebody who had worked out what was in that cloth put a sharp thing in us and used us on it, and we took it out, and we have no way to know who worked it out or when or what it cost them to say nothing.

She put the clothes in the drawer. Then she stood in the kitchen doorway for a while, and there was nobody in the kitchen, and the kettle in there had gone cold about two hours before.

---

In the third month after the back room, the small closed water was not in her any more.

We do not know where it went. There was a journey in it — she was upright and moving for a long time and drank very little, which we notice — and after that it was not there, and her own water came back to the ordinary amount over about a week, and her temperature came down half a degree to where it had been.

Things leave us all the time. Every day, off the whole surface of the lake, more of us goes up into the air than goes out by the river, and the river is the only way out there is. Almost none of us leaves by the river. Almost all of us leaves by going up, and comes down again somewhere, and is back inside a week or a hundred years.

We put the two of those things next to each other because they happened in the same season. We have no other reason.

---

At the end of a school day she was at a locker in an empty corridor and there was dust on the inside of the door.

Dust is our opposite and we know its edges the way you know a wall in the dark. She wrote a name in it with one finger. It was five letters. We were on the finger — everybody's finger has us on it — and we came off onto the dust and made the line of the letters darker than it would have been, which is why it was legible at all.

**Daudi.**

Then she put the side of her hand flat on it and took it off, and the dust and the letters came onto the heel of her hand, and we were in that too, and it stayed on her palm damp for about ninety seconds and then dried.

That is the second name that reached us in that year. We had it for ninety seconds. The first one we got across a road at sixty metres in a shout and we have it still.

---

She went to the boy's house last and he was out.

She went into the room with the buckets. It was the dry season and we were not coming through the roof, and the three buckets were empty and had a ring of us dried in the bottom of each, which is the mineral that is in us and stays when we go.

She took the green board with the gold lines off its nail. Then she knelt at the third bucket and moved it, and lifted the board.

We were in that room, in the mortar and in the mattress and in her, and we have never been under that board, and we were not under it then either. She put something into the space. Then she put the board back and stood on it with her weight and it did not move, and she put the bucket back on top of it, and she hung the green board on its nail between the skull and the hubcap and straightened it.

Whatever is under there is in the only dry place in that house. It will keep. We are the reason things do not keep, and we cannot get to it.

She walked home along the shore. We were on her left the whole way, running up the sand about four inches and going back, which we do about eleven times a minute in that weather. She was counting.

We have watched her count the hulls in the evening since she was small. She always gets four. We have never known what she is checking.

---

Her father says we put things back in order and remember everything in its right place. He says it standing in front of us with his hands out. He has said it for years and he will go on saying it, and men on that shore were saying it before he was born, and we were flat for that too.

Here is our account, and it is the only one we have.

Everything that has ever been put into us is still in us. The gutter run-off and the soap and the sharp thing from the second wash and the fish and the rain off the iron roof and the ninety seconds of a name off a palm. None of it has left. Some of it went up and came down and is back. Some of it is at the bottom where we have not turned over since before the town, and it is not going anywhere.

And none of it is where it was put.

# ⊹ WHAT THIS EXPERIMENT FOUND

**1 · Water solves the object-narrator's access problem without solving its ignorance problem.**
The ships in [[mihogoni-2]] can only watch a shore, which forced a *new* incident. Water is in the kettle, the watermelon, the buckets, the wash, the breath and the body, so it can reach every scene in Chantal — but it still cannot see, read a face, or follow a thought. **Total access, zero comprehension.** That is the [[Mothers Not Appearing in Search — ANNOTATED|Lubwama]] engine with the range of an omniscient narrator, and it is the most useful thing this experiment produced.

**2 · The pregnancy is the load-bearing discovery, and it is only available to this narrator.**
*"There is a second water in her. It is small and it is closed and it is warmer than the rest of her and it does not mix with the rest of her… We have no name for it."* The reader arrives instantly; the narrator never does. And the termination is reported as **the small closed water not being there any more, and things leaving all the time.** Djamela cannot narrate that scene — she isn't in it in Draft 3 — so the water covers the one gap in her account without adding a word of interiority. → [[15 The Chair Test — Scoring the Hidden Factor|axis 3]]

**3 · The motif is an absence, and absences count.**
Today's lesson from [[Dite — Story Sheet]] is *one thing, placed six times, never explained.* Here the thing is **the dry board under the third bucket** — placed at the room description, the rain, the kiss, the dried mineral rings, the hiding, and the close. **A narrator that is everywhere has exactly one blind spot, and the entire secret of the story goes into it.** *"We are the reason things do not keep, and we cannot get to it."* Paired motif: **what soap will not take out**, planted flat at 15% before it can mean anything, detonating at the mother's second wash.

**4 · The mother's wash is the best thing here and it is pure Chantal, just relit.**
Draft 3 gives it in one line — *soap and then something after the soap, vinegar, or paraffin.* From inside, it becomes: the mother worked out what was in the cloth, said nothing, and used the water as a solvent. **The narrator is the instrument and reports the chemistry without knowing it is testimony.** *"We did not do it and we could not have declined to do it."*

**5 · The class system is delivered as plumbing and the words rich and poor never appear.**
The water sorts the town into **piped and carried** — its own vocabulary, not a human one — and the délestage becomes a pump that stops on one side and not the other. This is posthumanist decentring earning its keep: the narrator's private taxonomy happens to be the story's argument. Cheaper and harder than *délestage* doing it in Djamela's voice.

**6 · The existential frame is quoted and refused.**
Both men state a position over water — *it remembers everything in its right place* / *it is hydrogen and oxygen and has no intelligence* — and the water answers neither, because it does not know it has been addressed. The close grants the father his memory and takes his order: **everything is still in us and none of it is where it was put.** A statement of mixing, not of theme. Had one clause said *we do not care*, [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes|axis 7]] would cap at 3.

**7 · The freedom problem, placed and not connected.**
Chantal's claim is *that I did the deciding.* Sixty words earlier the water gives its own account of motion — *we go downhill, we go up when it is hot, we take the shape that is offered* — as a one-paragraph section that touches nothing around it. **The two are never joined and the reader joins them.**

**8 · Cycle payoff, two ways.** The fourth hull's hold appears once, flat, with the cold water and the things on the floor — a reader of [[mihogoni-2]] knows what those are and Chantal's reader does not. And Djamela's evening count is here from *outside* her, so *"we have never known what she is checking"* answers a question mihogoni-2 asked about the same girl.

**9 · Length. 4,469 words**, measured from the file, prose only. Inside the 2,000–5,000 gate with **531 of headroom** — tighter than [[mihogoni-2]] (3,302) and close to [[chantal-3rd]] (4,961). **The claim in mihogoni-2 that an object-narrator is cheaper than free indirect discourse does not survive this.** It held for the ships because they could only see a shore; water has access to everything and therefore has to *decline* things, and declining costs words — *"we were not in that room in any quantity"*, *"we have no way to check"*. **An omnipresent narrator pays a toll on every scene it isn't in.** Rhythm not optimised: per finding 7 in [[x-factor]], the metric predicts nothing, and this voice wants long cyclical sentences beside flat short ones.

**10 · The cost, stated honestly.** Every scene loses its dialogue. The water gets *"we had the sound of all of it and about half the shape of it"* — which protects the naive engine and throws away the single strongest thing in Draft 3, the Writing Constitution's *dialogue reveals class*. **This is a lens, not a replacement.** Nothing here should go near the manuscript; what is portable is the *device* — an absence as motif, and a narrator whose ignorance is structural rather than chosen.
