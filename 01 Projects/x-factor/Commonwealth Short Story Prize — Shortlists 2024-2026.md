## Africa region: writers with confirmed anthology/magazine credits beyond the prize itself

Every shortlisted story publishes in *adda* by default and every regional winner publishes in *Granta* by default — that's the prize mechanism, not a separate credit. Below is what turned up for writers with placements *outside* that guaranteed pipeline. No Africa-region CSSP writer turned up with a confirmed Paris Review credit in this search — flagging that as an open gap rather than a no, since Paris Review rarely publicizes contributor prize histories.

**Mbozi Haimbe** (Zambia) — 2026 longlist, "Give Them Names." Already won the CSSP Africa region outright in 2019 ("Madam's Sister"). Two stories — "Madam's Sister" and "Shelter" (1st runner-up, Short Story Day Africa Prize 2019/20) — appear together in *Disruption: New Short Fiction from Africa*, the Short Story Day Africa anthology (Catalyst Press). Strongest anthology case of the group.

**Jarred Thompson** (South Africa) — 2026 longlist, "Upon the Rack." Shortlisted for the Gerald Kraak Award and Anthology (2019); story "What We Ride In On" appears in the *Short.Sharp.Stories* anthology *Fluid*. Debut novel *The Institute for Creative Dying* (Picador Africa / Afritondo UK, 2023) won the 2024 UJ Debut Prize for South African Writing in English.

**Ola W. Halim** (Nigeria) — 2026 shortlist, "Shock Me I Shock You" (his 2nd CSSP shortlisting). Widely published outside the prize: *Isele Magazine*, *Lolwe*, *Brittle Paper*, *Iskanchi*, *The African Writer*, *SmokeLong Quarterly*, *Forge Literary Magazine*. Pushcart Prize–nominated. No Granta/Paris Review credit found, but the most prolific magazine presence of the group.

**Hussani Abdulrahim** (Nigeria) — 2026 shortlist, "Arewa Girls." Previously longlisted for CSSP in 2023. Work has appeared in *Boston Review* and *Evergreen Review* — both notable US literary magazines, comparable prestige tier to Granta/Paris Review even if not those titles specifically. Also in *Ubwali Lit Mag*, *ZamaShort*, and *The Flame Tree Writers Workshop Anthology*.

**Renée Akitelek Mboya** (Kenya) — 2026 longlist, "Four Fish, Five People." Writer/curator/filmmaker; short story "Brave New Worlds" published in *Kwani?* (the influential Nairobi literary journal), and collaborative work with the *Chimurenga Chronicle* (South Africa) / *Kwani?* (Kenya) / Cassava Republic Press (Nigeria) pan-African project. Not Granta, but Kwani? and Chimurenga are the African-continent equivalents in terms of gatekeeping prestige.

**Iman Verjee** (Kenya) — 2026 longlist, "The Shed." Established novelist rather than short-story-magazine writer: two novels via mainstream trade publishers — *In Between Dreams* (Oneworld, 2014, won the Peters Fraser & Dunlop/City University Prize) and *Who Will Catch Us As We Fall* (Simon & Schuster, 2016).

### Reading this list correctly

None of these are Granta or Paris Review credits directly — the honest finding is that Africa-region CSSP long/shortlistees mostly build reputations through African-continent venues (adda, Kwani?, Chimurenga, Brittle Paper, Isele, Short Story Day Africa) and adjacent US journals (Boston Review, Evergreen Review), not the two magazines you named. If a Granta or Paris Review credit specifically matters for your comparison (e.g., benchmarking Chantal against what "gets into Granta" looks like), the cleanest source is still the CSSP regional winners themselves, since Granta publication is guaranteed for that group — Lisa-Anne Julien (2026), Joshua Lubwama (2025), and going back further, past Africa winners like Mary Rokonadravu, Iryn Tushabe, and Kwame McPherson-adjacent cohorts. Worth a follow-up search on that older cohort if useful.

## Repeat appearances in the Africa region, 2021–2026

Only two writers repeat within this 6-year window on the shortlist itself:

**Ola W. Halim** (Nigeria) — shortlisted 2021 ('An Analysis of a Fragile Affair') and 2026 ('Shock Me I Shock You'), a 5-year gap. Between the two, he racked up adjacent recognition rather than more CSSP shortlists: shortlisted for the Gerald Kraak Prize and the Kendeka Prize for African Literature, Pushcart-nominated, and published across *adda*, *Isele Magazine*, *Lolwe*, *Brittle Paper*, *Iskanchi*, *SmokeLong Quarterly*, and *Forge Literary Magazine*. Pattern: sustained mid-tier journal presence rather than a second big prize, then a return to the same shortlist half a decade later.

**Franklyn Usouwa** (Nigeria) — shortlisted 2021 ('A for Abortion') and 2022 ('Lifestyle Guide for The Discerning Witch'), back-to-back. At the time he was an undergraduate engineering student in Lagos; published in *The Kalahari Review* and *Writer's Space Africa* — smaller regional venues. Pattern: rapid consecutive shortlisting early in a career, no confirmed sustained magazine trajectory after in this search.

## The more important pattern: CSSP Africa shortlist as a farm team for the Caine Prize and trade publishing

The single biggest finding isn't repeat CSSP shortlisting — it's that several Africa-region CSSP alumni went on to bigger, more international recognition elsewhere, specifically the **AKO Caine Prize for African Writing** (the continent's most prestigious short story prize) and trade-published books:

**Rémy Ngamije** (Namibia) — 2021 CSSP Africa **winner**. Also **shortlisted for the AKO Caine Prize twice**, 2020 and 2021. Founder and editor-in-chief of *Doek!*, Namibia's first literary magazine (published 100+ writers across six issues). Has a confirmed contributor page on granta.com. Debut novel *The Eternal Audience of One* published by Scout Press (Simon & Schuster). Longlisted for the Afritondo Short Story Prize in both 2020 and 2021. Came back in **2023 as the Africa-region judge** — prize alumnus judging the same category two years later, a full-circle pattern worth naming if you're mapping how this ecosystem recycles its own talent.

**Vincent Anioke** (Nigeria/Canada) — 2021 CSSP Africa shortlist. Also **shortlisted for the AKO Caine Prize in 2020** (a year before his CSSP shortlisting — so the Caine recognition came first). Won the Austin Clarke Prize in Literary Excellence in 2021. Debut story collection *Perfect Little Angels* (Arsenal Pulp Press, 2024) was shortlisted for the Dayne Ogilvie Prize for LGBTQ Emerging Writers and the Danuta Gleed Literary Award, and named a CBC Books Writer to Watch. Published in *Callaloo* — a major African-diaspora literary journal, roughly Paris-Review-tier within its niche. Clearest case in the sample of a CSSP shortlisting converting into a published book with further prize traction.

**Mubanga Kalimamukwento** (Zambia) — 2022 CSSP Africa shortlist ('Thandiwe'). Already the first and only Zambian writer to win the **Dinaane Debut Fiction Award** (2019) for her novel *The Mourning Bird*, listed among Brittle Paper's top 15 debuts of 2019. Also won the Kalemba Short Story Prize, shortlisted for the Bristol Short Story Prize, and is a Miles Morland Foundation scholar. Pattern: arrived at CSSP already an established novelist, not built up by it.

**Mbozi Haimbe** (Zambia) — won the CSSP Africa region outright in 2019 (just outside this window), then reappeared on the **2026 longlist** with 'Give Them Names' — a 7-year return. Her two other stories, 'Madam's Sister' and 'Shelter' (1st runner-up, Short Story Day Africa Prize 2019/20), both appear in the Short Story Day Africa anthology *Disruption*.

### What this means for the anthology

None of this is Granta or Paris Review credit earned independently of the prize mechanism — the closest is Ngamije's Granta contributor page, and that traces back to his CSSP win, not a separate submission. The real ladder these writers climb is CSSP or Caine shortlisting → African-continent journals (Brittle Paper, Isele, Lolwe, adda, Doek!) → a debut novel or story collection from a trade or African-focused press (Simon & Schuster, Arsenal Pulp Press, Bridge Books) → further Anglo-Canadian prize attention (Dayne Ogilvie, Danuta Gleed, Austin Clarke). If Chantal's trajectory is meant to mirror a realistic path rather than a fantasy of landing in Granta on the first try, Anioke's sequence (Caine shortlist → CSSP shortlist → story collection → CBC recognition) is the most legible template in this set.
