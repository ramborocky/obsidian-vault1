### The ten rules

1. **The shape never changes.** You never decide what happens next. That is the whole design.
2. **One target per block, written on an index card before the block starts.** Not in your head.
3. **The timer runs the day, not you.** Hard stops. **Stop mid-sentence** — it removes tomorrow's starting friction.
4. **Copywork is the anchor.** If a day collapses, do Block B and nothing else. It counts as a done day.
5. **Mornings are paper.** No screen until Block E.
6. **Nothing carries over.** A missed day is deleted, not made up. Fridays absorb.
7. **Tick the box on the wall.** Visible completion is not decoration.
8. **Hyperfocus clause:** you may extend **one** block by 30 minutes, **once per day.** It costs you the drill. Never the rest.
9. **The start ritual is identical:** water, timer, card, pen. Two minutes. **No phone first.**
10. **The rest window is protected.** Breaking it is the fastest way to lose week 2.

## 🌙 NIGHT SALAWAT — the recovery protocol

*Salawat runs at night, up to 3 hours. The next morning is the problem, not the night.*

Short sleep takes executive function first — which is exactly what a structured plan runs on. **So do not plan to be sharp. Plan to be mechanical.**

| If salawat ended…   | The next day                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Before midnight** | Normal day.                                                                                                                                              |
| **Midnight–01:00**  | **COPYWORK MORNING.** Blocks A and C are cancelled. Do **three copywork passages** instead, 09:00–11:45, from that day's story. Afternoon runs normally. |
| **After 01:00**     | **The day is deleted.** Do not make it up. Friday absorbs it.                                                                                            |

**Why copywork:** it requires no judgement, cannot be failed, and transfers more craft per hour than reading does. It is the correct tired-day activity, not a consolation.

**Never** put a scoring block or a generative block the morning after salawat. Scoring tired produces numbers you will trust and shouldn't.

## Gaiman's prediction read — the method

> **Read the first page or two. STOP.**
> **Write down what you think the ending will be, and why.**
> **Then finish it and compare.**
>
> *"What elements led you to believe in a certain ending? How did the author surprise you?"*

It measures **your own expectations**, not the story's quality — and your expectations are your reader's. Five minutes of writing, and it is the single best use of twenty minutes with an unfamiliar story.
