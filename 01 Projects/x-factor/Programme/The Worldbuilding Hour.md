# 🌍 THE WORLDBUILDING HOUR
### 40 minutes a day · 16:00–16:40 · Sat 1 – Fri 21 August

**Bolts onto [[The Three-Week Atelier]] as a fourth block.** Everything else in that programme stays exactly as it is.

> [!important] Why this track exists and why it is worth 40 minutes a day
> **[[15 The Chair Test — Scoring the Hidden Factor|The Chair Test]] scores Chantal 2 on The World** — the lowest single score anywhere in the vault. Against the confirmed overall winner it is **2 against 5**.
>
> **That gap is 3 of the 6 points between Chantal and the story that beat 7,805 others.** It is entirely additive — nothing gets cut, nothing gets restructured — and it is the only axis where 40 minutes a day for three weeks is obviously the right instrument.

## 🚶 THE RULE FOR THIS BLOCK — it is not desk work

**The morning blocks are paper. This one is legs.**

> Gaiman: *"So a lot of what you do in world building is you do your homework. You do your research. **Go on walks. See things.** All of that — and then take notes."*

Wherever a day below can be done outside, **do it outside.** It is at the end of the day on purpose: you have already written, the pressure is off, and movement is the correct mode for collecting. It is also the block most likely to survive a bad day, which is why it is last and not first.

**One notebook. Back pocket. It never comes to the desk.**

# WEEK 1 · COLLECT
### You do not think about Chantal at all this week. Not once.

**Why:** you cannot invent surplus. Surplus is *noticed*, then transplanted. A week of pure collection gives you a heap to draw from in week 3 — Gaiman's compost heap, rotting away usefully.

| Day | 16:00–16:40 |
| :-: | --- |
| **1** · Sat 1 | **Three details from today.** Things you remember from the last week for no reason. Describe each with **the senses**, not the meaning. *Smuggling from your own life* |
| **2** · Sun 2 | **Ten objects in one real room** — someone else's if you can. List all ten. **Circle the most interesting.** Rewrite the room through that one object only |
| **3** · Mon 3 | **Go somewhere you have never been. Record nothing.** No phone, no notes, no photographs. Stand in it. Come home and write it from memory — what it *smelled* and *sounded* like |
| **4** · Tue 4 | **Where does the water come from?** Answer it for where you actually live — the route, the container, who carries it, what it costs, who argues about it. Then answer it for Mihogoni |
| **5** · Wed 5 | **Ten places you have loved · ten you want to see.** Then **confluence**: take one from each list and combine them into a third place that is neither |
| **6** · Thu 6 | **What does poverty look like here?** Not the word. Ten physical facts. *Dite* does it in nine words: **"his pay is swallowed by the previous month"** |
| **7** · Fri 7 | **Walk. No notebook.** Float day — or absorb whatever the week lost |

# WEEK 2 · INTERROGATE
### Now you study how the three published stories did it — and you build the rules of Mihogoni

| Day | 16:00–16:40 |
| :-: | --- |
| **8** · Sat 8 | 🏆 **Serpent · the wells.** *"People said the British had dug those wells… older people said the British found a hole the island had dug for itself."* **Two histories of one object, held by two generations.** Find an object in Mihogoni that two generations would explain differently. Write both explanations |
| **9** · Sun 9 | **Dite · the boutikier's notebook.** Rice, oil, flour, exercise books, all on tick. **An entire household economy in one object** — which returns 79 points later as a colonial ledger. What is Mihogoni's equivalent object, and where could it return? |
| **10** · Mon 10 | **Mothers · the driving school.** An institution with its own agenda, negotiating with children, over a page and a half that serves no plot. **Name three institutions in Mihogoni that want something.** Not people — institutions |
| **11** · Tue 11 | **The rules of Mihogoni. Write five, as rules.** Then find the place in Chantal where somebody grapples with one. *Dite* has Rule 2 — *only French allowed* — and the narrator's mouth cannot obey it |
| **12** · Wed 12 | 🌑 **NEW MOON — no writing.** Re-read the worldbuilding clauses in [[Worldbuilding — The Soap Bubble]]. Read only |
| **13** · Thu 13 | 🌑 **NEW MOON — no writing.** Walk. Look. Nothing in the notebook |
| **14** · Fri 14 | **Gaiman's weird questions, all of them, for Mihogoni.** Toilet · food · where it comes *in* · who has power · rituals · languages · **what was different fifteen years ago.** None of these answers goes in the story |

# WEEK 3 · INSTALL
### This is the week it reaches the manuscript

| Day | 16:00–16:40 |
| :-: | --- |
| **15** · Sat 15 | **Twenty surplus details for Mihogoni.** Write them fast. **Cut-test each as you write:** *delete it — does the plot notice? No, and the story is poorer → keep* |
| **16** · Sun 16 | **One person whose life the story does not follow.** A full sketch: name, hat, what they want, what they owe, where they will be next year. **The story will use four lines of this** |
| **17** · Mon 17 | **The four hats** — the mother, Jean Luc, Morisho, Alicia. Five candidates each, twenty total, most of them bad. *(Pairs with day 17 of the main programme)* |
| **18** · Tue 18 | **INSTALL SIX.** From the twenty. At least one must be the person from day 16. *(Pairs with day 18 of the main programme)* |
| **19** · Wed 19 | 🔵 **THE BRADBURY TEST.** Write **one** unexplained detail into Mihogoni and state it flat, with total confidence, and **never explain it.** *"lava bubbled silvery and hushed"* — Bradbury never says why lava is silver on Mars. **The confidence is what convinces, not the detail** |
| **20** · Thu 20 | **Re-score The World axis alone.** Count the surplus. Cut-test each one honestly. Write the new number next to the old **2** |
| **21** · Fri 21 | **Walk. Nothing.** Done |

## ⛔ THE FOUR RULES OF THIS BLOCK

**1 · Never describe. Always state.**
> Surplus is not scenery. *"There is a gutter that came off our side of the house the year before last and is still lying where it landed"* is already the best worldbuilding sentence in Chantal — flat, specific, unresolved, unexplained, and it serves nothing. **You need five more of those, not five more paragraphs.**

**2 · Anything you explain, you have wasted.**
The Bradbury rule. Explanation tells the reader you were worried they wouldn't believe it. **Confidence tells them you know a hundred more things you aren't saying.**

**3 · 90 under, 10 on the page.**
> *"Always know more than you tell."* Weeks 1 and 2 build the 90%. Week 3 spends the 10%. **If you skip to week 3 you will invent, and invented surplus reads as invented.**

**4 · Never at the desk.**
If the block happens in the chair you already worked in, it becomes a fifth writing block and it will be the first thing you drop. Legs.
