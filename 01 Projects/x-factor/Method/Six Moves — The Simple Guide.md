# SIX MOVES

**Small steps. One at a time. Tick the boxes.**

> [!tip] How to use this page
> Do **ONE** move per sitting. Not two. Not all six.
> Set a timer. When it rings, stop — even if you are mid-sentence.
> Tick the box. Close the file. Walk away.

**Prize opens 1 September 2026. You send it 25 October 2026.**
Today is 9 August. That is plenty. It is not spare.

---

# MOVE 1 · BUT / THEREFORE

### What it is
Your scenes must be joined by the word **BUT** or the word **THEREFORE**.
They must **never** be joined by **AND THEN**.

### Why
"And then" = a list. Lists are boring.
"But" = trouble. "Therefore" = a result.
Trouble and results are a story.

### Do this — 15 minutes ⏱️

- [ ] Open a blank file
- [ ] Write your scenes down the page. One line each
- [ ] Between each one, write **BUT** or **THEREFORE**
- [ ] Any place you had to write **AND THEN**, mark it red
- [ ] Stop. Do not fix anything today. Just look at the red

### Done when
You can see the red marks. **The red marks are where the story is dead.**

### To fix them later
Ask one question: **"What does this force?"**

# MOVE 2 · THE QUESTION LADDER

### What it is
One person asks questions. One person gives short flat answers.
Each answer takes away something the asker assumed was there.

> "He knows what his sentence is?" — **No.**
> "He knows he has been judged?" — **Not even that.**

### Why
It makes the reader angry. The story never has to be angry.

### The secret
**The person answering must be polite and helpful.**
A calm, helpful monster is scary. A shouting monster is not.

### Do this — one sitting ⏱️

- [ ] Pick two characters. One has power. One does not
- [ ] Write **4 questions**
- [ ] Write **4 short answers**. Some can be one word
- [ ] The powerful one must stay **kind and patient** the whole time
- [ ] Two pages. Stop

### Done when
You have a scene where nobody shouts and it still hurts.

# MOVE 3 · THE OBJECT RELAY

### What it is
Pick **one small object**. Make it move from hand to hand. **Never explain it.**

Kafka's example: two handkerchiefs.
Gift → sweat rag → given to a dying man → stolen → fought over.
**Four moves. Zero explaining.** They carry the whole story.

### Why
A thing you explain means one thing.
A thing you never explain means everything.

### Do this — one sitting ⏱️

- [ ] Pick **one object already in your story.** Do not invent a new one
- [ ] Make it change hands **4 times**
- [ ] Write **only the handover.** Who gives. Who takes. Nothing else
- [ ] Delete any sentence that says what it means
- [ ] **Make the 4th handover your last image in the story**

### Done when
The story ends on somebody **doing** something, not somebody **realising** something.

> [!success] This move fixes your ending for free
> → [[The Resonant Close]] · [[All the Small Things — Grief Through the Minor Object]]

# MOVE 4 · ADD A PERSON. ADD A WORLD.

### What it is
Stop fixing sentences. **Add stuff.**

### Why
Judges praise **feeling**, **voice**, and **having something to say** — every single year.
They almost never praise structure.

And your own [[15 The Chair Test — Scoring the Hidden Factor|Chair Test]] already found the hole:
**9 of your 11 missing points are in The Person and The World.**
Both are things you **add**. Neither needs a cut.

### Do this — 2 hours, one sitting ⏱️

**Part A — The Person**
- [ ] Write **3 lines** where somebody wants something **the plot does not need**

**Part B — The World**
- [ ] Add **5 small extra things**: a price · a rumour · a rule · a smell · a nickname

**The test for each one:**
> **Cut it. Does the plot notice?**
> If the plot does NOT notice, but the story feels **poorer** without it — **keep it.**
> That is leakage. → [[The X-Factor]]

### Done when
Three wants. Five surplus things. Nothing deleted.

---

# MOVE 5 · PUT IN THE THING YOU DON'T WANT TO

### What it is
Every writer keeps one thing out. Readers feel the hole.

### Why
"Moral force" is not a fancy idea.
It just means: **the writer did not flinch.**

### Do this — 10 minutes, nobody sees it ⏱️

- [ ] On paper, write: *"The thing I am keeping out of this story is ______."*
- [ ] Now put it in. One paragraph is enough. One line is enough

### Done when
Sending the story makes you feel a little bit sick.
**That is the correct feeling.** → [[What You Are Censoring]]

---

# MOVE 6 · THE FIRST 300 WORDS

### What it is
Your first page is not the start of the story. **It is the whole competition.**

### Why
Thousands of people enter. A reader decides fast. → [[The First-Page Cut]]

### Do this — do it LAST ⏱️

- [ ] Copy your first 300 words into a **brand new blank file**
- [ ] Write them again. From scratch. **10 times**
- [ ] Not edits — **10 whole different versions**
- [ ] Read all ten **out loud**
- [ ] Keep one. Paste it back

### Done when
You chose the opening with your ears, not your eyes.
