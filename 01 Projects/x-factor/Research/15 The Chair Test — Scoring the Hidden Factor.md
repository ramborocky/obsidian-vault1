# 15 · THE CHAIR TEST

**An instrument that scores what six Chairs of Judges actually said, instead of what craft books say.**

Built from [[14 The Judges — Six Years of Chair Statements]] and calibrated against [[The Serpent in the Grove — ANNOTATED]], [[Mothers Not Appearing in Search — ANNOTATED]] and [[Dite — ANNOTATED]].

---

## Why the Nine Axes cannot find this

[[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes]] is a good instrument and it is measuring the wrong object. The fault is precise and it is not a matter of weighting:

> **The Nine Axes score what the writer did. Every chair statement in six years describes what happened to the reader.**

*Scene turn · the gap · structural economy · resonant close* are all descriptions of construction. *Moves us · leaves you breathless · heartbroken and humbled · deeply moving · brims with humanity · changes us* are all descriptions of an event in a person.

A story can be perfectly constructed and cause nothing. **That is exactly Chantal's situation** — 40/45, and its own dashboard says *the rubric rewards architecture and this panel forgives architecture.*

So this instrument is built the other way round: **from effect back to the textual fact that caused it.** Each axis is a chair's word; each band is a test you can perform on the page.

---

## The five axes

Each 1–5. **25 total.** Bands are cumulative — you cannot score 4 without meeting 3.

---

### 1 · THE MOVE
> *"moves us, causes us to think, and sometimes changes us"* — Hereniko 2025
> *"deeply moving"* — Doughty 2026 · *"heartbroken and humbled… leaves you breathless"* — Makumbi 2024 · *"brims with humanity"* — Wicomb 2021 · *"real heart"* — Tanweer 2023

**The test: point at it.** Name the percentage position of the paragraph that moves. If you cannot name one, you do not have one.

| | Band |
| :-: | --- |
| **1** | You cannot locate a paragraph. The story is competent and inert |
| **2** | You can locate one, but it moves you because of what it *describes* (a death, a child, a cruelty) rather than what it *does*. **Subject mistaken for effect** |
| **3** | One locatable paragraph that survives a second reading |
| **4** | Two or more, at different positions, and at least one arrives **through an object or an action, not a statement of feeling** |
| **5** | As 4, and the strongest one lands **on material the reader was given earlier for another reason.** The move is a payoff, not an event |

> **Disqualifier:** if the paragraph names the emotion it wants you to feel, cap the axis at 2. Gardner's sentimentality — see [[Three Faults of Soul — Sentimentality, Frigidity, Mannerism]].

---

### 2 · THE VOICE
> *"the prose assured"* — Doughty · *"tight prose and stanza-like paragraphs"* — Makumbi · *"the quiet voice of a seer"* — Hereniko · *"the varied tones of fiction"* — D'Aguiar · *"mastery of craft… simplicity, humility"* — Tanweer

**The test: the blind paragraph.** Take any paragraph from the middle at random. Could it have come from any competent literary short story, or only from this one?

| | Band |
| :-: | --- |
| **1** | Register lurches. The narrator's vocabulary is not their own |
| **2** | Clean and anonymous. The blind paragraph could be anybody's |
| **3** | Identifiable from a whole page, not from a paragraph |
| **4** | Identifiable from any paragraph. **A signature sentence exists and you can quote it** |
| **5** | As 4, and the signature sentence is inside the **first 10%** — the panel meets the voice before it meets the plot |

> **Note the trap.** This axis is not about beauty and not about sentence length. [[Dite — ANNOTATED\|*Dite*]] runs 17.5 words average with 20% short sentences; both confirmed winners run near 11. **All three score high here.** → [[14 The Judges — Six Years of Chair Statements]]

---

### 3 · THE POSITION
> *"the author has something important to say"* — Doughty · *"aware of history, while never sacrificing story"* — D'Aguiar · *"difficult metaphysical and historical questions"* — Tanweer · *"geography matters in storytelling"* — Hereniko

**The test, in two parts, in this order:**
1. State the story's position in one sentence, in your own words. Not its subject — its *argument*.
2. Then search the text for that sentence. **Find where the story says it.**

| | Band |
| :-: | --- |
| **1** | You cannot state a position. The story has a subject and no argument |
| **2** | You can state it, and it is a platitude anyone would agree with |
| **3** | A real position — **and the text states it somewhere.** Hard cap |
| **4** | A real position, never stated in the narrator's own voice, but **delivered by a proxy** — an epigraph, an object-narrator, a minor character, a title |
| **5** | Never stated anywhere by anyone, and **impossible to miss** |

> **This is the axis that separates the three calibration stories**, and the only axis where the confirmed overall winner scores badly.

---

### 4 · THE PERSON
> *"the characters are utterly believable"* — Doughty · *"illuminate many aspects of human nature"* — Hereniko · *"moments of character illumination"* — D'Aguiar · *"brims with humanity"* — Wicomb

**The test: the surplus want.** Does anyone in the story want something the plot does not need? Count the people it is true of.

| | Band |
| :-: | --- |
| **1** | Characters are functions. Each exists to move the plot |
| **2** | The protagonist is real. Everyone else is a function |
| **3** | The protagonist has a want the plot does not need |
| **4** | **Two or more people do**, including at least one minor character |
| **5** | As 4, **and at least one character refuses the category the story could have put them in** — the villain who is not a villain, the victim who is not a victim, the woman the plot blames who is given her own morning |

> **The cheapest 5 available anywhere in this instrument**, and the one Chantal fails worst. See [[Character by Salient Detail]] and [[Interest, Free Will, and the Active Protagonist]].

---

### 5 · THE WORLD
> *"memorable and urgent stories that captured the concerns of their respective communities"* — D'Aguiar · *"Each tale shows that geography matters"* — Hereniko · *"lead them into a world"* — Doughty

**The test: the cut test.** List details that serve no plot function. For each: *cut it — does the plot notice?* If no, and the story is poorer, it is leakage.

| | Band |
| :-: | --- |
| **1** | Every detail is load-bearing. The world begins and ends with the story |
| **2** | One or two decorative details, doing atmosphere only |
| **3** | Three to five items of true surplus |
| **4** | Six or more, **including at least one person with a life the story does not follow** |
| **5** | As 4, and at least one surplus item is **the most memorable thing in the story** while serving nothing |

> This is the axis [[The Underground Rubric — Risk, Afterlife and the Room|Gate 3]] calls Leakage, and the only axis the 2026 winner scored a 5 on there. → [[The X-Factor]]

## The sixth axis, which you cannot score

> ### 6 · THE AFTERLIFE — *unscoreable by the author. Leave blank.*
>
> *"sometimes changes us"* — Hereniko. **What survives 24 hours after the reader has put it down, with the book closed.**
>
> This is the largest single component of what a chair means and **there is no honest way to self-score it.** You cannot forget your own story. Every attempt to score it from inside is the [[The ELIZA Effect and the Vestments of Plausibility|ELIZA problem]] — you supply the effect and then detect it.
>
> **It requires a cold reader, 24 hours, and three questions asked without prompting:**
> 1. What do you remember? *(Score what they name first, not most.)*
> 2. Whose story was it? *(If they name the wrong person, that is data, not error.)*
> 3. What was it about? *(If they answer with the subject, axis 3 is a 3 or lower.)*
>
> **[[Home]] has said since 2026-07-28 that four instruments depend on a human who is not you, and there is nobody.** This is the fifth. It is now the single largest hole in the campaign and it is a phone call, not a craft problem. → [[10 The Three Moves — Reader, Winners, Record]]

### Reading the calibration

**All three published stories land 21–24. Chantal lands 16.** The instrument separates the prize band from the accomplished band by six clear points, which is what an instrument has to do to be worth running.

**And here is the result that justifies building it:**

| | Nine Axes | The Chair Test |
| --- | :-: | :-: |
| Nazir (won, from 7,806) | **31 / 45** = 17.2/25 | **22 / 25** |
| Chantal Draft 4 | **40 / 45** = 22.2/25 | **16 / 25** |
| **Gap** | **Chantal +5.0** | **Nazir +6.0** |

**The two instruments disagree by eleven points of relative position, and the new one agrees with the panel.** The Nine Axes rank Chantal comfortably above the story that beat 7,805 others. The Chair Test ranks it comfortably below. Only one of those can be describing the thing that happened in June 2026.

### Where the losses actually are

Chantal loses on **The World (2)** and **The Person (3)** — nine of its eleven missing points sit in two axes that have nothing to do with prose or structure:

- **The World — 2.** Almost every detail is load-bearing. Nobody in Mihogoni wants anything the plot doesn't need. There is no Pinto, no driving school, no letter for a mother whose son was held for cussing a policeman.
- **The Person — 3.** Djamela is real. The mother is furniture, Jean Luc has no object, Morisho is a function, Alicia is a device. **Not one character refuses their category.**

Both are additive fixes. Neither is a rewrite, neither touches the architecture, and neither requires cutting a word — which matters, because [[Home]] already forbids cutting for tightness again.

## Protocol

Run it **cold, after seven days**, per [[The Cold Read Protocol]], and in this order — the order is load-bearing:

1. **Axis 3 first, before rereading.** State the position from memory. If you have to reread to find it, that is already a finding.
2. **Axis 5 second**, with a pen — list surplus, cut-test each. Do this before you reread for beauty or you will score atmosphere as leakage.
3. **Axis 4** — list every named character, then ask the surplus-want question of each.
4. **Axis 1** — read once through and mark the percentage where you feel something. Mark nothing else.
5. **Axis 2 last**, because by now you have read it four times and can no longer hear it. Use the blind-paragraph test on a paragraph you have not just been staring at.
6. **Axis 6 — hand it to someone else.** Do not read their answers for 24 hours.

**Never add this total to the Nine Axes total.** They measure different objects; a combined number would be meaningless. Run both, log both, and when they disagree, **write down which one the disagreement favours** — that record is the instrument's own calibration over time.

## Known limitations — read before trusting a number

1. **It cannot rank within the prize band.** Lubwama scores 24 and Nazir 22; Nazir won overall and Lubwama did not. **The instrument identifies prize-level; it does not predict a winner.** Anything above ~20 should be read as *"in the room"*, not as a ranking.
2. **Three calibration points, one unverified.** Thin. It gets better with every story sheet added — [[Baldwin — Sonny's Blues|Baldwin]], Uwazuruike and Giorgis are already in the vault and should be scored next.
3. **Axis 6 is 0% complete** and is probably the largest real component. Until a cold reader exists, the total is a partial score and should always be written **/25, no afterlife**.
4. **The chair rotates.** The three shared criteria held 6/6 across six chairs, but taste on top of them did not — Makumbi rewarded satire, Hereniko rewarded allegory. **This instrument measures the stable part. It cannot measure the 2027 chair, who has not been named.**
5. **Self-scoring is the weakest evidence in the campaign.** Every number in the calibration table above was produced by me, including my own experiments' scores. Treat the *relative order* as the finding and the absolute numbers as provisional.
