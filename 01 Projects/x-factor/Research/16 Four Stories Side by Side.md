> [!warning] Read these three caveats before any number

Where the two disagree, **the earlier score was made by the person who wrote it and this one was not.**

### Where the disagreements actually are

**Axis 1 — Chantal 4, not 5.** *"Nobody tells you the exact day your body stops being only yours"* is strong, and it is an aphorism-first opening, which is the single most common shape in any submission pile. It also is not the best line in the story; *It was not shame. It was arithmetic* is. MABANGA gets **3** because it opens on an explanation and takes four paragraphs to reach an image.

**Axis 3 — Chantal 3.** Djamela is completely realised, but she narrates her own reframes, and the reader almost never knows something she does not. The Craft Log itself records the one exception — the folded clothes at 84.9% — as *"first and only place in the draft where the reader gets there before the narrator."* One instance is a 3. *Dite* has six (the throat) and *Mothers* has the entire story.

**Axis 7 — Chantal capped at 3, and this is the important one.** *I am not asking to be forgiven for that. I am asking for it to be counted correctly.* is magnificent and it **states the controlling idea**. The rubric's own 5-band requires *never stated*. **The two best lines in the story are the reason it cannot score above 3 here.** That is not a fault to fix — it is a price, and it should be paid knowingly.

**Axis 6 — MABANGA 3.** I know some of my sentences are unmotivated; the Ngaba and trade sections could lose a fifth of their length without losing anything.

### Two scores I revised upward, with the reason

Scoring four texts side by side rather than three changed my calibration, and re-reading the bands strictly:

- **Chantal, The Move: 4 → 5.** The band requires the strongest moment to land *on material the reader was given earlier for another reason.* The four-days-at-the-bottom-of-the-basket is planted at 58% as an inventory item about perfume; at 85% the clothes come back washed early, with something stronger than soap. **That is a payoff, not an event.** It qualifies. Three object-borne moves in total — the clothes, the name wiped from the dust, the fob under the board.
- **Mothers, The Voice: 4 → 5.** I had docked it for having no signature sentence at the level of *Sun on galvanise*. That was wrong: **the opening sentence is the signature sentence**, it sits at 1%, and the voice is identifiable from any paragraph in the story.

### The three that matter for your draft

**The Position — 3, hard cap, same as the Nine Axes.** The story says what it is about. Twice. Beautifully.

**The Person — 3.** Djamela is real. **The mother is furniture, Jean Luc has no object, Morisho is a function, Alicia is a device — and not one character refuses the category the story could have put them in.** Compare *Mothers*, where Fatima refuses both victim and predator, and the mother refuses antagonist at the 25% mark.

**The World — 2, and it is the lowest single score anywhere on this page.** Almost every detail in Chantal is load-bearing. There is no Pinto, no driving school, no Jjaja Reagan on a porch she never leaves. The world begins and ends with the story.

> **Person + World hold 5 of Chantal's 8 missing points, and both are additive.** No cutting, no restructuring, no risk to a draft that already works — which matters, because [[Home]] forbids cutting for tightness again.

### And the ceiling problem, now demonstrated

***Mothers* scores 25/25.** A perfect score, on a **regional** winner — while Nazir, the overall winner, scores 22.

**The Chair Test cannot discriminate at the top.** Above about 20 it says *in the room*, and nothing more. That limitation was written into the instrument as a caveat; this is the first time it has been shown rather than predicted.

## The most useful thing on this page, and it is about my story, not yours

**MABANGA's worst Chair Test score is The Voice, at 3 — and the reason is contamination.**

My narrator is a flat-declarative counting fourteen-year-old girl who reports without understanding. **So is Djamela.** I have spent this entire session inside Chantal's register, and when I sat down to invent something from nothing, that register is what came out. On the blind-paragraph test — *could this be anyone's?* — a middle paragraph of MABANGA could plausibly be a paragraph of Chantal, and that is the definition of a 3.

Two consequences, and the second one matters more:

1. **The story is worth less as a benchmark than its total suggests.** It is not an independent data point; it is Chantal's voice wearing a different city.
2. **This is exactly what the quarantine rule in [[x-factor]] exists to prevent, running in the direction nobody watches for.** The rule protects your manuscript from my prose. It says nothing about my prose absorbing yours — and it just did, invisibly, in a single session. **If a machine that has read your draft two hundred times produces a narrator who sounds like yours, then anything it hands you will feel like your own voice when you read it back.** That is the real hazard, and it is not solved by folder structure.
