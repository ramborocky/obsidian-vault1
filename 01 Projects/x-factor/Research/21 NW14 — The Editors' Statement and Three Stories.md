# 21 · NW14 — THE EDITORS' STATEMENT AND THREE STORIES

**A British Council anthology, 1,000+ submissions from 43 countries, cut to fourteen stories.** One of the two editors is **Helon Habila — Commonwealth Writers Prize (Africa, Best First Book) and Caine Prize winner.** He is precisely the kind of reader who will hold your entry.

> [!important] The most valuable thing in this book is not a story
> **It is the four-page introduction, and it functions as a chair statement.** Two editors, having read a thousand submissions, wrote down what failed and why. Their vocabulary for failure is sharper than anything in the Nine Axes, because it was produced under the same conditions as a prize sift. → [[14 The Judges — Six Years of Chair Statements]]

---

## PART 1 · THE STATEMENT

### What they were looking for

Writing that **surprised, provoked and impressed** in roughly equal measure. Two criteria beneath that:

1. **"Authenticity of sensation."**
2. **The writer's ability to keep the original impulse alive in the finished work.**

They state plainly that it had **nothing to do with first or third person**, and nothing to do with whether the material was real or invented. Only whether it was deployed *"rigorously and artfully."* — which retires a question this vault has spent real time on. → [[19 The Third-Person Test — What Close Third Bought and Cost]]

### The four failure modes — and this is the instrument

| # | Their phrase | What it names | The test you perform |
| --: | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **"Made by remote control"** | Proficient, genre-literate, technically correct — and **nothing lingered** | Close the draft. Twenty-four hours. **What image comes back unprompted?** If none, this is you |
| **2** | **"Cruise-control lyrical prose"** | *"A smooth ride along a perpetual present tense"*, language *"so cushioned you could barely feel the bumps in the road"* | Read one page aloud. **Where does the reader get jolted?** If nowhere, the prose is anaesthetic |
| **3** | **"Realism worn down to an 'ism'"** | Default realism as an unexamined setting rather than a choice | Ask: **why is this story realist?** If the only answer is "that's how stories are," it is mode 3 |
| **4** | **"Tourist fiction — jaunts into the exotic"** | Setting supplied as content. **The named trap for your material** | **Cut every proper noun of place. Does the story still know where it is?** If not, the place was decoration |

> [!tip] The second instrument this book produced
> Set NW14's introduction beside NW12's sign-off — *love, loss, sex, hunger, laughter, justice, a sense of place* — and the difference yields a general test for any judge's statement. **Negate it; if the negation is absurd, the statement is empty.** Applied to six years of Commonwealth chairs in → [[22 The Negation Test — Which Chair Statements Survive]]

> [!success] The NW14 Test — four axes, all failure-side
> Every other instrument in this vault scores what a story *has*. **This one scores what it hasn't got rid of.** Run it before the Nine Axes, not after — mode 1 cannot be fixed by adding, and modes 2–4 get harder to see the longer you have lived with the draft.

### The finding that should change a plan

> **"The dominant perspective was that of the child."**

Across a thousand entries from forty-three countries, the child narrator was **the default position.** The editors took some, and say so explicitly: they were impressive *only because they rose out of a sea of ones that didn't work, or which were no more than freshness for its own sake.*

**This is the human proof of the funhouse mirror.** Whatever the field generates by default is the cliché — and the field, not just the model, defaults to the child. → [[All the Small Things — Grief Through the Minor Object]]

**Consequence for this campaign:** the vault's finding #2 — *the reader must know before the narrator does* — is sound, and [[Mothers Not Appearing in Search — ANNOTATED|Lubwama]] is a real proof of it. But **the child is a saturated position, not a clever one.** Any story leaning on narratorial naivety needs a second engine underneath it, or it arrives looking like the other four hundred.

### Two more observations from the sift

- **They wanted the fantastic and barely got it** — they call the good instances *"a trapdoor in the real."* An almost-empty lane.
- **They stopped noticing names and origins** within days. *"We were soon taking no notice of who the author was or where they came from."* Corroborates what the Commonwealth's own process claims.

## PART 2 · THE THREE STORIES THAT MATTER HERE

### 1 · Abdulrazak Gurnah — *My Mother Lived on a Farm in Africa* (p. 49)

**The most directly relevant story in the anthology, and the best.** Swahili coast. A woman in England overhears her fourteen-year-old daughter tell her friends the title sentence, just after they have watched *Out of Africa*. The memory that follows: sent at fourteen to an uncle's farm, and a neighbour at the window.

**Architecture: two panels and a hinge.** Present (kitchen, England) → the farm → back to the kitchen. Nothing explains the join. This is the compressed, story-length version of the adjacency engine. → [[The Letter to Zadie at Eighteen]]

**Four things to take:**

| Device | How it runs |
| --- | --- |
| **Argue with a famous text without arguing** | Blixen's film plays in the next room. The rebuttal is one clause — *she was the subject people* — and the film is never mentioned again |
| **The assault is never named** | A man at a barred window; his breathing; and next morning **a small mound of hard earth** he must have stood on. The mound arrives after the fact, as evidence |
| **Two lines carry the whole politics** | The aunt says *don't say anything to Omar*, then lies to the uncle to explain the girl's sudden removal. **The man's reputation protected over the girl's experience — enacted, never stated** |
| **The ending is speech, not realisation** | The daughter breezes in and says she was only winding her friends up, trying to make them jealous. The mother has just relived the worst weeks of her childhood. **Nobody in the story notices the gap** |

> [!warning] A calibration point against our own rubric
> [[15 The Chair Test — Scoring the Hidden Factor|The Chair Test]] says: *state the position and cap at 3.*
>
> **Gurnah states his.** Once, mid-story, in free indirect: not "Africa" but a real place with names for everything, from the smell of grass and leaves to the smallest change in the weather. He then won the Nobel Prize.
>
> **Revise the rule, don't abandon it.** A statement survives when it is *a character's argument with someone else* — here, with a film — rather than the author's summary for the reader. The cap applies to summary, not to rebuttal.

### 2 · Shereen Pandit — *Dying to See the Sea* (p. 109)

Cape Town under the state of emergency. The narrator moves in with her cousin Freddie, a kindergarten teacher, in a house on the border between the "Coloured" and "African" townships. Three children who have never seen the sea. A beach trip that is planned and postponed.

**The engine is the best single piece of construction in the anthology.** Paragraph one establishes a comic verbal tic: Freddie is *always dying* — dying for a drink, dying for a cigarette, dying to get out of her shoes. It reads as characterisation. It is the title. It is a loaded gun.

The eldest girl is shot on her way to visit. Freddie throws a brick at the informer's car. The last clause of the story is **a new red flower on the gay floral print dress** — and then nothing. No sentence after it.

- **A comic motif detonated as literal.** Eight pages of jokes cashed in one image. This is the unexplained-motif device with a *funny* surface, which is harder and better.
- **The narrator is Kafka's Traveler.** She can act, never does, and names it herself: *quiet islands in the midst of the river.* She survives. The one who acted does not.
- **Leakage as aside:** dogs riding in the cab while labourers ride in the open back; good meat for the dogs, scraps for the workers. Two sentences, an entire political economy, and the plot needs neither. → [[The X-Factor]]
- **End two paragraphs earlier, executed.** Death arrives as an image in a subordinate clause and the story refuses to comment. → [[End Two Paragraphs Earlier]] · [[The Resonant Close]]

*(Unresolved: the girl is called Mpandikaze when Freddie introduces her and Nosizwe in the mother's account. Possibly two names, possibly a slip. If deliberate it is savage — the child they were fond of was not known by her right name.)*

### 3 · C. D. Rose — *The Neva Star* (p. 84)

A ship abandoned in the port of Naples, unmoved for three years. Opens in a flat encyclopaedic register, cataloguing other abandoned ships — Genoa five years, Venice seven — and repeating that this is *not an entirely unusual situation* until the repetition turns menacing.

**This is [[mihogoni-2]] — four rusted ships, no body, no agency — written by someone else and selected.** Read it as a competitor and as a permission slip: the shape works, and it does not need a protagonist.

## PART 3 · STANDOUTS ON PROSE ALONE

| Writer | The move |
| --- | --- |
| **Kirsty Gunn** — *Now I Can See How It Was, I Think* | The family arrives *smelling of blood*, smiling big white smiles *like they had knives in them.* Best sentence-level voice in the book |
| **Desmond Hogan** — *Belle* | Opens on a wall of objects — a Y-fronts advert based on *The Last of the Mohicans*, Cardinal Tien, Margaret Mitchell. **Pure surplus as an opening move**, with no plot attached |

## PART 4 · THE GAP

Fourteen stories. **No East African coast. No lake. No Kiswahili on the page.** Gurnah is the only writer of the Swahili world in the book, and he writes from England about a remembered farm — his ground is memory, not present-tense place.

And the editors name the trap directly: **tourist fiction, jaunts into the exotic.** That is the charge waiting for our material if the setting is doing the work.

> **The defence is Gurnah's own clause.** Not "Africa" — *a real place with names for everything, down to the smallest change in the weather.* Specificity is the entire difference between a lake town and somebody's holiday.

## WHAT TO DO WITH THIS

1. **Run the NW14 Test before the Nine Axes.** Four failure modes, four physical tests. Mode 1 cannot be fixed by addition; find out early.
2. **Steal Pandit's engine.** A comic verbal tic in the narrator's voice, planted on page one, made literal at the end. **This is a Person and World addition** — exactly where the eleven missing Chair Test points live.
3. **Find the counter-text.** One famous thing the story argues with in a single clause, then drops. Gurnah gets a whole postcolonial position out of a video playing in another room.
4. **Copy the Gurnah out by hand — once.** Seven pages. It is the closest published story to our material in structure, world and temperature. → [[The Apprenticeship — Learning By Hand]]
