# GAIMAN ADVICE

**Three MasterClass chapters, compressed. The seventh craft base, and the only one written by a working storyteller rather than a critic, a teacher or a theorist.**

## Why it earns a place next to the other six

McKee analyses. Cleland systematises. Gardner theorises. Barrett legislates. Kiteley prescribes. Faulkner subtracts.

**Gaiman describes what he actually does at the desk** — and on three questions he is more useful than any of them:

| --- | --- |
| [[Voice Is What You Cannot Help Doing]] | *"Your voice is the stuff you can't help doing."* And the enemy is named: **writerly voice** |
| [[The Short Story as an Ending]] | Write the short story as if it were the **last chapter of a novel you already wrote** |
| [[Dialogue Is Character]] | *"Dialogue is character."* Plus **funny hats** — the cheapest character fix in this vault |

## The three things worth stealing immediately

**1 · "Funny hats."** Gaiman gives every secondary character one memorable tag — a physical quirk, a verbal style, a name, an extended metaphor, a past event. **[[15 The Chair Test — Scoring the Hidden Factor|The Chair Test]] scores Chantal 3 on The Person** because the mother is furniture, Jean Luc has no object and Morisho is a function. **Funny hats is the direct fix and it costs one detail per person.** → [[Dialogue Is Character]]

**2 · The characters existed before the story started.**

> *"You want to feel like these characters didn't just start to exist the moment the story began. You want to know they've all been in existence all along."*

**That is leakage, described by a practitioner without the word.** It arrives at the same place as [[The X-Factor|the surplus test]], Nazir's letter for the mother whose son was held for cussing a policeman, and Perec's *infraordinary*. Four independent routes to one idea.

**3 · "Instead of being paid by the word, I am paying by the word."** The single best reframe of economy in any of the seven bases, and it is one sentence.

## Where he fights the other bases

| Question | Gaiman | The others |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Character method** | *Doesn't make lists of personality traits.* Finds character **by listening to speech** | **[[Character Dimension and Cast Design\|McKee]] designs the cast first.** Opposite methods, both work — see [[Dialogue Is Character]] |
| **Must scenes turn?** | Notes the debate openly: *"Sometimes your protagonist will resist all change, or decline a critical moment of transformation"* | **[[Scene Design\|McKee]] requires the turn; [[Plot as Yearning, Not Pyramid\|Faulkner]] denies it.** Gaiman refuses to adjudicate, which is honest |
| **Where to start** | *In medias res.* Cut the descriptive opening, start on dialogue or action, **let the reader be confused for a moment** | **[[The Beginning — Three Paragraphs at Most\|Barrett]] agrees.** [[The First-Page Cut]] agrees |
| **Overwriting** | **"Writerly voice"** — complex syntax, too many adjectives, rare words | **[[Three Faults of Soul — Sentimentality, Frigidity, Mannerism\|Gardner's mannerism]]**, and [[The De-Slop Pass — English Prose\|the de-slop pass]] Tier 2 |
| **When to compress** | **In editing, never in drafting.** Banish the inner critic first | [[The Five Passes]] agrees; [[The Method — Restrict and Liberate\|Kiteley]] disagrees — he restricts *during* |

## Orwell's four questions, quoted by Gaiman as his own edit pass

Applied to every sentence:

1. **What am I trying to say?**
2. **What words will express it?**
3. **What image or idiom will make it clearer?**
4. **Is this image fresh enough to have an effect?**

**Question 4 is the one nobody runs**, and it is the whole of [[The Funhouse Mirror — AI Output as Cliché Detector|the funhouse mirror]] in seven words.

## The exercises worth keeping

Gaiman's are unusually well designed for anyone who struggles to start, because **most of them are timed and disposable**:

- **60 seconds** to answer one personal question, then **30 minutes** to write the whole story. *"You don't have much time, so just keep writing. Don't worry about craft or structure."*
- **Condense a page by a third.** Rewrite completely if necessary.
- **Change the setting.** Move the same scene somewhere hostile and see what survives.
- **The prediction read** — read the first page or two of an unfamiliar story, **stop**, write down what you think the ending is, then finish it and compare. *"What elements led you to believe in a certain ending? How did the author surprise you?"*
- **Write one whole story in a single sitting.** Not allowed to leave until there is a complete draft.
- **Observe a stranger.** Invent a name, a reason for being there, a feeling. One page. One distinct detail.

> [!important] The sentence to keep above the desk
> **"You learn more from finishing a failure than you do from writing a success."**
