# VOICE IS WHAT YOU CANNOT HELP DOING

## The principle

> **"After you've written 10,000 words, 30,000 words, 60,000 words, 150,000 words, a million words, you will have your voice because your voice is the stuff you can't help doing."**

Voice is not chosen. **It is the residue left after you stop trying.** Technically it is the sum of your choices about emotional tone, character, setting, diction, sentence structure and punctuation — but the operative word is *can't help*. You find it by writing past the point where you are still performing.

And the corollary, which is the whole reason this note matters:

> **"If style is made up of 'the things you get wrong'…"**

### Voice vs persona — the distinction most writers never make

**Your voice** is yours across everything you write. **The persona** is the voice a particular story demands, and it is not the same thing. Gaiman's three:

| Persona | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **American transparent** | So clean the author disappears. *"Technically perfect, clean, and conservative."* You forget someone is telling the story |
| **Formal / old-fashioned** | Syntax or vocabulary borrowed from an earlier period. Uses its own stiltedness on purpose |
| **The cicerone** | *"Informal, close, and often not very literary."* Mimics an actual person explaining their story to you |

> **You find the persona the same way you find a setting or a character** — it is a decision about the story, not a fact about you.

### The enemy has a name

> **"Writerly" voice** — *"an attempt to sound literary that can come off as overwritten. This voice involves unnaturally complex sentence structures, too many adjectives or rare words, and lengthy, unnecessary descriptions."*

And the exemption, which is precise: **unless the character genuinely is pompous and sees the world that way.**

> [!warning] The three bases that name the same fault
> **Gaiman: writerly voice.** **[[Three Faults of Soul — Sentimentality, Frigidity, Mannerism|Gardner: mannerism]].** **[[The De-Slop Pass — English Prose|The de-slop pass]]: Tier 2 lexical tells.**
>
> Three sources, one fault, and the vault's own version already has the diagnosis: *AI-ness is not grammatical error. It is over-smoothness, over-symmetry, over-explanation.*
>
> **Gaiman adds the cause the others don't.** It is not carelessness. It is **an attempt to sound literary** — which means it appears most in the sentences you are proudest of.

## Where it shows in the gallery

**[[Dite — ANNOTATED|*Dite*]] runs three personas in one story** and switches between them without announcement: the object-chorus of the teas (*we*), the close first-person child, and the four epigraph registers. **Persona as structure.**

**[[Mothers Not Appearing in Search — ANNOTATED|Lubwama]] is a pure cicerone** — informal, close, unliterary, a boy explaining what happened. And it is exactly why the story can be funny and devastating in the same sentence.

**[[The Serpent in the Grove — ANNOTATED|Nazir]] is the cautionary case.** *Sun on galvanise is a cruel instrument* is voice. The coda — *A story is a well. It eats sound until somebody throws a rope* — is **writerly voice**, and it is where the story stops being told and starts being performed. [[The Judging Rubric — Nine Axes]] docks it there. The panel did not.

## The exercises

**1 · Describe yourself as a writer, in the third person**, as if explaining your work to someone who has never read it. If stuck, reread your own pieces and write down what they have in common — the overt (genre, character type) and the quiet (attitude, emotional register).

**2 · Give a short selection to someone else and ask for three adjectives.** *"Do they recognize you in the writing?"* — a cold-read instrument that takes ninety seconds and does not require a literary reader.

**3 · Write a whole short story in one sitting.** Not allowed to leave until there is a complete draft. Any length. *"Don't do too much editing while you write."*

**4 · Imitate deliberately.** Read a few pages of an admired writer, then write a passage in their voice with your own characters and problem. **Then write a paragraph in the voice that feels natural to you and compare.**

**5 · The five-book sweep.** Faulkner's advice, quoted by Gaiman: *"Read everything: Trash, classics, good and bad, and see how they do it."* Five authors you have never read, random chapter each, and **list what you liked and what you disliked.** *"Part of your apprenticeship is learning what you love and hate."*

> [!important] The sentence for the wall
> **"You learn more from finishing a failure than you do from writing a success."**
>
> And: *"Writing is one of the places where your weirdness is welcome."*

## Against the other bases

**[[Point of View — From Barbaric First Person to Authorial Omniscient|Gardner]]** classifies POV technically. **Gaiman's persona is a different axis** — not who is speaking but *what kind of voice the story demands* — and the two can be crossed.

**[[Point of View — The Reluctant I|Kiteley]]** is the closest ally: both treat the narrating voice as a constraint you choose rather than a default you inherit.

**[[Style — Simple, Easy, Concise|Barrett]]** would call writerly voice a failure of style. **Gaiman calls it a failure of nerve**, which is more useful because it tells you where to look.
