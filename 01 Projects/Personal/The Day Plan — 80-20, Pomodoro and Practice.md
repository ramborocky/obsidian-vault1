## Part 2 · The Practice — one hour across the day

Sixty minutes, split five ways, each one attached to something that already happens. **Nothing here is my ruling on anything — it is a timetable. Take the content from your own knowledge or your imam.**

| After | Minutes | What |
| --- | --: | --- |
| **Fajr** | 15 | Morning adhkar · **first 33 salawat** |
| **Dhuhr** | 5 | 33 salawat |
| **Asr** | 10 | 34 salawat — the day's hundred is complete |
| **Maghrib** | 15 | Evening adhkar. **No screen between Maghrib and Isha** |
| **Before sleep** | 15 | Qur'an, even a short portion. Phone already charging in another room |
| | **60** | |

**One hundred salawat a day, counted on beads, split three ways.** It is measurable, it is small enough to actually happen, and it is the same discipline as the pomodoro — a fixed number, at a fixed time, attached to something you were doing anyway.

> **Friday is 31 July — the day after tomorrow.** Salawat is traditionally increased on Fridays. Two days is enough time to build the habit before the day it matters most. That is the deadline to aim the practice at, not next month.

**The pairing that does the real work:** every long break in the timetable above is a tasbih break. The screen-off system and the practice are **the same system**. You are not adding an hour of worship to a full day — you are putting something in your hands during the hours you would otherwise be holding a phone.

## Part 3 · Getting off the screen

The honest problem: the phone is not a habit, it is a **default**. It fills every gap. So it does not get removed, it gets **replaced** — every rule below swaps one object for another.

| Rule | Instead of |
| --- | --- |
| **Phone in a different room** during every pomodoro. Not face down. Another room | Willpower |
| **Tasbih in the pocket, always.** When the hand reaches, it finds beads | The reach reflex |
| **Greyscale on the phone.** Settings → Accessibility. It makes everything boring | Feeds designed to be bright |
| **Maghrib to Isha is screenless.** One fixed hour, same time daily | "I'll stop soon" |
| **Print Chantal and read it aloud on paper** | Reading your own draft on a screen |
| **Paper first.** The five tasks go on paper before the laptop opens | Opening the laptop to "check something" |

### The one that matters most

**Print the draft.** [[09 The Practice — Calendar, Ritual and Ilmu Huruf|The full-moon station]] is a read-aloud, on paper, no pen — and the next full moon is **~27–28 August**. Printing today means the most valuable craft pass available to you is also completely screen-free, and it is booked.

**The screen problem and the writing problem have the same solution.** Chantal will not be improved by more hours at this laptop. It will be improved by you reading it out loud in a room with the phone somewhere else.

## Part 4 · Why these rules and not others

| Rule | What it says | Where it lands here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **80/20 (Pareto)** | A small input produces most of the output | The ACPA status question unblocks six tasks |
| **Eat the Frog** | Do the avoided thing first | School fees, six days overdue |
| **Ivy Lee** | Six tasks maximum, in order, one at a time | Five tasks, numbered, no jumping |
| **Parkinson's Law** | Work expands to fill the time given | The 16:00 hard stop is the point |
| **Two-minute rule** | If it takes under two minutes, do it now | Writing three lines into the ACPA file |
| **Implementation intention** | *At this time, in this place, I will do this* | The timetable, not a to-do list |

**The one to keep if you keep nothing else:** *one task, one number, one deadline.* Everything above is a variation on it.
