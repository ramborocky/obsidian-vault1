Part one

Two campaigns, one set of hours

They have never competed openly, because both are real work and both feel like progress. The hours are the only thing that makes them rivals.

The vault holds a nine-axis judging rubric, six craft knowledge bases, an empirical campaign measuring real prize winners, and a cross-index pinning each craft concept to the percentage point where it occurs. It also holds a credential file that has moved once in six weeks.

Neither of those facts is about laziness. Avoidance does not look like idleness — it looks like your best work, because relief arrives the instant you turn toward the safe task, every time, with no delay. Nothing you deliberately build will ever be reinforced that cleanly.

The blocker on the credential shrank from a phone call to a login, and still did not move. If effort were the obstacle, halving it would have worked.

Part two

The number, divided

A million dollars over thirty-six months, and what the division rules out before anything else gets decided.

The line that closes a whole category

At $30 an hour, $27,778 a month is 926 billable hours — 214 hours a week. A week contains 168. Even at $100 an hour it is 64 billable hours every week for three years, saving every cent. Selling hours cannot reach it at any rate, ever.

And what is actually being sold

Kevin Kelly's argument, from the essay you supplied: when copies are abundant they become worthless, and what can't be copied becomes valuable. AI made competent copies free. So the bookkeeping output is now the free copy — reconciliations, schedules, a set of management accounts, all produced in seconds and heading toward zero.

What survives are his generatives. Interpretation — what the numbers mean and what to do, which is his Red Hat example exactly. Authenticity — a signed, warranted set of accounts with a named human behind it. Immediacy — a close on day five instead of day eighteen.

You are not selling bookkeeping. Bookkeeping is now the free copy. You are selling interpretation with a signature on it, delivered faster than anyone else can manage.

The re-ranking

Morland is roughly 47× the Commonwealth per application — and that understates it, because the Commonwealth pays one lump sum to one person while Morland pays £1,500 a month for twelve months. It is not a prize. It is a salaried commission with a delivery schedule, and it is the only thing on either side of this vault that pays the monthly bill in 2027.

Part six

Five acts, all small

Each one returns a verdict you don't control. That is the only property that matters — an act that cannot tell you you're wrong has not exposed you to anything, however much work it contained.

  1. Log into MEMS and read three fields.Grade, registration date, years logged. Ten minutes, cannot be done wrong, and every plan in the vault is currently guessing at the answers.
  2. Email NBAA one question.Does self-employed practice count toward the logbook, and can the mentor be someone other than my employer? It may reorder the entire sequence.
  3. Email the Commonwealth Foundation one question.Does work in a personal public code repository count as previously published? Asked neutrally, without naming the story, now rather than in October.
  4. Move the AI material to a private repository.Not deletion — git keeps history and deleting destroys the labelling that currently protects you. An hour's work.
  5. Write the offer in one sentence and send it to one person.Not ten. One. The market is the only thing that can tell you what the offer should have been, and it will not speak until asked.

Scored at the end of the week: how many acts did I take that could have come back badly? Zero is a week of hiding, whatever it produced. One is a working week. Three is a good one.
