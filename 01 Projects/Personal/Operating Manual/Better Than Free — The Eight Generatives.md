# BETTER THAN FREE — THE EIGHT GENERATIVES
### What you sell when the output is free

Part of [[Operating Manual]]. From the document you supplied. **This is a better model than the one I built**, and the honest thing is to say so and replace mine.

---

## The argument in three lines

Copies used to be scarce, so creators sold copies. The internet made copies free. **AI made competent *variations* free** — words, images, code, advice, in seconds. So the thing to sell is whatever cannot be copied.

Kelly's word for those is **generatives**: qualities that must be generated in place, in time, in relationship. They cannot be cloned, stored, faked, banked or reproduced. Trust is the archetype — you cannot download it.

## The eight

| # | Generative | What the buyer is paying for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Immediacy** | Having it first, or now, or in beta before anyone |
| 2 | **Personalization** | Fitted to them specifically, which requires an ongoing conversation |
| 3 | **Interpretation** | *"Software, free; the software manual, $10,000."* What it means and what to do |
| 4 | **Authenticity** | Warranted, reliable, signed. The artist's stamp |
| 5 | **Accessibility** | Organised, at hand, dependable, tended |
| 6 | **Embodiment** | In a body, in a room, on paper, in person |
| 7 | **Patronage** | People genuinely want to pay creators, if it is easy and it clearly reaches them |
| 8 | **Findability** | Being found at all, out of millions |

> Kelly's closing note is the one that matters for us: money does not follow the path of the copies. **It follows the path of attention** — and these are very human skills, which AIs are not good at yet.

## Why this replaces my model

[[Five Models I Can't Unsee]] had a **Signature Test** — *does it keep working without me, and could anyone else have signed it?* That was right and thin. Kelly names **eight** uncopyable values where I had found one, and my one turns out to be his fourth. Where a source does the job better, the source wins.

## The practice, read as a generative stack

The bookkeeping output is a copy. AI makes it free. So which of the eight is actually being sold?

| Generative | How it shows up in the practice | Priced? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Interpretation** | *This is what your numbers mean and what to do about it.* Kelly's Red Hat example is literally the accounting business | **The core sale** |
| **Authenticity** | The ACPA-PP signature. Warranted, professionally liable, signed by a named human | **The moat** |
| **Immediacy** | Close by day 5 instead of day 18. A documented AI-native firm did exactly this | **Real premium** |
| **Personalization** | Fitted to their chart of accounts, their reporting, their board. Iterative, sticky, uncopyable | Retention |
| **Accessibility** | Numbers at hand, tidy, dependable, always current | Retention |
| **Findability** | Being the person they can name when the need appears | The weekly publish |
| **Embodiment** | A physical review, a printed pack, a room | Later |
| **Patronage** | Not applicable to a B2B practice | — |

> [!important] The reframe worth keeping
> **You are not selling bookkeeping. Bookkeeping is now the free copy.** You are selling interpretation with a signature on it, delivered faster than anyone local can manage. That sentence sets the price, writes the offer, and explains why $350 a month is cheap for the buyer and excellent for you.

## And the same lens on the writing

The prose is a copy; AI can make competent variations of it. What can't be copied is **Immediacy** (readers who get it first), **Authenticity** (it is yours, from that lake town, and nobody else's), **Embodiment** (a printed thing, a reading) and **Patronage** (people paying because they want you to keep going). Kelly's own 1,000 True Fans is the model — and every one of those is built by publishing, not by perfecting. → [[The Second Curriculum]] IV.4
