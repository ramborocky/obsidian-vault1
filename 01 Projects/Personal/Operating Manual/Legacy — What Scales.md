# LEGACY — WHAT SCALES
### The shifts that move you past your own hours

Part of [[Operating Manual]]. Tactics stop working at the point where your hours become the ceiling; this is what replaces them.

---

**From doing the work to designing what does the work.** The first ceiling is attention. Anything requiring yours is capped at your waking hours and degrades when you are tired, ill or grieving. Impact scales only through things that keep running when you look away — systems, trained people, published forms, institutions. This is the difference between a career and a body of work.

**Income tracks decision rights, not effort.** Payment scales with the size of the uncertainty you are trusted to absorb and how expensive your judgement is to replace. The ladder: hands → skill → judgement → responsibility for others' judgement → capital. Most people climb the first two and are confused about why the money stalls.

**From hero to multiplier.** A hero solves it themselves, is admired, and caps permanently at one person's capacity. A multiplier makes five people better and compounds. The hero role is more emotionally rewarding, which is why talented people rarely leave it.

**Legacy is a transferable form, not a memory of you.** Affection lasts about a generation. What lasts is something others can pick up and use: a body of work with a shape, a trained successor, a way of doing it that outlives your name on it. A cycle of stories set in one town by one lake **is** a form — the town outlives you if other people can write in it. → [[Collection Tracker]]

**Give away credit at every opportunity.** Credit hoarded buys one round of recognition. Credit given away buys loyalty and referral, which buy recognition permanently and at a better rate. This feels wrong when you are hungry and is correct anyway.

**Your standard is what you tolerate, not what you announce.** Every stated value is tested the first time enforcing it is expensive. Whatever you let pass that day becomes the real standard, for you and everyone watching.

**Time horizon is the last real edge.** Nearly everyone optimises the next quarter, so ten-year positions are cheap and uncontested. A long horizon lets you do things that look irrational for two years and obvious in year six. Same logic with people: play long games with long-term people, and drop anyone whose behaviour only makes sense if you'll never meet again.

**Reputation compounds, and is built almost entirely on the occasions when keeping your word cost you something.** Nobody remembers the promises kept when they were convenient.

## The parked idea belongs here, not in year one

Training mama ntilie and small traders on records and credit is a **legacy-shaped idea in a business-shaped disguise**, and reading it correctly is what saves it.

It fails as a first business for reasons that are documented rather than arguable — Kippa reached 500,000 traders and could not monetise them; the training is already given away free by Vodacom, the BOT Academy and CRDB's Malkia programme; Ramani.io is running the funded version of it in Dar with a BOT lending licence and $34M. → [[Due Diligence — What the Market Says]]

But the insight is provably right: **NMB's own loan criteria require "a proper recording system", and 80–85% of Tanzania's 3.5 million SMEs never get bank finance.** Records are the gate to credit.

So place it where it works. A year-three product, with a bank or FSDT as the customer rather than the trader, built on a practice that already has revenue and a credential behind it. That is the form that outlives you — not a course, a **method other people can run without you.** Everything on this page above says why that distinction is the whole game.

---

> [!warning] The uncomfortable one
> Most legacy talk is fear of death in a good suit. The honest test: **would the work still matter to you with your name removed?** If not, you are building a monument, and monuments have a poor record of outliving the people who commissioned them.
