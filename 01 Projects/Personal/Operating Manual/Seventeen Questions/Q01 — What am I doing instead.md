# 1 · What am I doing instead?

**22 July 2026.** The ACPA status question went onto [[Home]] as blocker #1. It needed one phone call. Six weeks later it had become *easier* — the answer sits on your MEMS profile now, three fields, readable while logged in — and it still had not been done.

The instinct is to ask *why haven't I done it.* That question has never once produced an answer that changed behaviour, because it invites a story and you will write a flattering one.

Ask the other question instead. **In those six weeks, what did I do?**

A nine-axis judging rubric. Six craft knowledge bases. An empirical campaign that measures real prize winners and corrects the rubric against them. A cross-index pinning each craft concept to the exact percentage point where it occurs. That is not nothing — it is months of genuinely skilled work, and every hour of it was real.

Which is the point. **Avoidance does not look like idleness. It looks like your best work.** Relief arrives the instant you turn toward the safe task, every time, with zero delay, and nothing you deliberately try to build will ever be reinforced that cleanly. So the substitute activity gets stronger and more impressive with each round, and the alibi gets better.

The tell is never effort. It is the pairing: **sustained effort, no exposure.**

> **The question:** not *why haven't I done it*, but *what did I do instead, and what verdict does that thing protect me from?*

The honest answer here is that the rubric cannot tell you that you can't write, and the MEMS profile can tell you that you are further from qualified than you hoped. One of those is safe. That is the whole selection criterion, and it operates below the level of decision.

**And the diagnostic that settles it:** the blocker shrank from a phone call to a login and did not move. If effort were the obstacle, halving the effort would have solved it.
