# 9 · Can I Shepardize this?

**From your own document.** Martine Rothblatt's youngest daughter was diagnosed with pulmonary arterial hypertension. No approved medicines. All patients died within two to three years. Rothblatt had not taken a life-science course since high school — she was a lawyer running SiriusXM.

So she went down to the hospital library at night, after her daughter fell asleep, and read. Most of it she did not understand. She looked the words up in dictionaries. When a paper was too hard she went **backwards** — college anatomy textbooks, then high-school ones — until something explained it, then climbed back up.

And she used one specific technique from law school, called **Shepardizing**: take a paper, read every reference it cites, then read every reference in *those*, and keep going until three or four levels down the citations start circling back on themselves. That is the point of diminishing returns, and it means you have mapped the field.

It worked. She found a molecule that had failed its congestive-heart-failure trial but had, in the charts, lowered pressure between lung and heart while leaving the rest of the body alone. She tracked down its owner, was told no, built a company so she could be told yes, got fifteen executives to sign, and paid $25,000 plus 10%. That molecule has since paid Glaxo Wellcome over a billion dollars in royalties, and there are now 50,000 Americans alive with the disease.

**A lawyer taught herself pulmonary medicine from a hospital library.** The method transfers exactly.

To learn a client niche — US e-commerce sellers, UK creative agencies, whatever it turns out to be — find three people who already serve it well, read everything they have published, then read every source *they* cite, then those sources' sources. Three levels down it starts repeating. **That is two weeks, not two years**, and at the end you can hold a conversation with a buyer.

> **The question:** where are the citations, and how far down do I have to go before they start circling back?

Note also what she did when told no: she did not conclude the door was shut. She asked *what would it take*, and then went and became that.
