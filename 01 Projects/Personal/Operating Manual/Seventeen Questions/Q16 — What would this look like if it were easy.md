# 16 · What would this look like if it were easy?

**Ferriss's, and the most quietly useful of his seventeen.** The mind's default when a goal is important is to make the plan *proportionally* elaborate — big goal, big apparatus. The question interrupts that with an absurd premise: suppose this were easy. What would it look like then?

Run it on everything in this folder.

**Getting out of debt.** The elaborate version: build an app for informal traders, raise money, get a Bank of Tanzania licence, scale. The easy version: **four clients at $350 a month.** Four. That covers the entire $1,228 household-and-debt figure. The gap between those two plans is not effort, it is a thousand times the risk.

**Learning a niche.** Elaborate: a course, a certification, six months. Easy: read three practitioners and everything they cite, three levels down, in two weeks. → [[Q09 — Can I Shepardize this]]

**Getting the credential moving.** Elaborate: a project plan across seven tasks. Easy: log in, read three fields, ask NBAA one question about mentors.

**Building an audience.** Elaborate: a content strategy, a brand, a schedule. Easy: one useful thing a week, same place, aimed at one kind of reader.

**Pricing.** Elaborate: market analysis. Easy: say $350 without flinching and see what happens.

Notice what the easy versions have in common. They are all **smaller, sooner, and more exposed.** The elaborate versions are all bigger, later and safer — and that is not a coincidence, it is the same circuit as every other question here. Complexity is often avoidance with a project plan attached. → [[Q01 — What am I doing instead]]

The question also does something to feelings, which is why Ferriss keeps it. Difficulty is largely a story about the plan, not about the world. Ask what easy looks like and the anxiety attached to the elaborate version simply does not transfer to the small one.

> **The question:** if this were easy, what would it be? Then ask what it would cost to just do that version first.

Not because easy is always right. Because the elaborate version should have to **win** against it, and usually it can't.
