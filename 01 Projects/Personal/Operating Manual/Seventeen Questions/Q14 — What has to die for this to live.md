# 14 · What has to die for this to live?

**Steve Young, in your document, on the day after he retired.** He had been known worldwide for one thing. The next morning it was gone.

What he says about it is more useful than any advice about goals:

> Everyone needs to recognise it and **treat it like a death** — to mourn it and go through all the steps of mourning it and burying it, and actually have it as a place you can keep referring to, almost like a grave site. Because otherwise you carry it around and it never gets — you never transition.

And Roger Staubach's advice to him, when asked for tips, was one word: **run.** Run where? *Just run away. The game will never leave you, but you need to leave it.*

Read carefully, this is not about giving anything up. Young keeps referring to the grave site. The thing is buried and it is still *there*, and it can be visited. What dies is not the football. It is the **primacy** — the arrangement where that one thing is the answer to who you are and everything else is a hobby.

Applied without softening it: the identity that has to be buried is not *writer*. It is **"writer, and one day the writing will pay, and until then the accounting is admin."** That arrangement has been running the allocation, and it is why a nine-axis rubric exists and the credential file has moved once in six weeks.

Suleika Jaouad, in the same document, supplies the other half. *Moving on is a myth.* You do not compartmentalise the hard passage and stow it in the past — you learn to move **forward with it**, integrated into the present. And she notes that our culture has rituals for birth, marriage and death and none at all for the transitions in between, so she had to invent her own rite of passage. The road trip, the twenty-two strangers, the driving licence she'd had for a month.

> **The question:** what arrangement has to be buried, properly and with some ceremony, for the new thing to have room — and what would I do to mark it?

Not *what do I give up.* Nothing is given up. The lake town, Djamela, the cycle: all of it survives. What ends is the ordering.
