# 17 · What's the smallest exposure I can take today?

**The last one, and the only one that has to be answered before tomorrow.**

Everything in this folder converges on a single finding: the obstacle was never ability, information, tooling or time. It is that **the exposing act goes second**, and the safe act — which is real work, and good, and defensible — goes first and absorbs the week.

Insight does not fix this. That is the clinical finding and it is unusually blunt: understanding why you avoid something has close to zero effect on whether you keep avoiding it. **Only doing it, while still afraid, and letting the fear decay, works.** Which means every one of the sixteen questions before this is worthless on its own, and this booklet is the most sophisticated avoidance device yet built if it is read and nothing follows.

So the question is deliberately small. Not *what's the right strategy.* Not *am I ready.* **What is the smallest act available today that could return a verdict I don't control?**

Today's answers, in ascending order of exposure:

1. **Log into MEMS. Read three fields.** Grade, registration date, years logged. Ten minutes. Cannot be done wrong.
2. **Ask NBAA one question:** does self-employed practice count toward the logbook, and can a mentor be someone other than my employer? One email. It may reorder the entire plan. → [[Unknown Unknowns — What Could Kill This]]
3. **Write the offer in one sentence** and send it to one person who might buy it. Not ten. One.

Each is small. Each returns something reality decides rather than something you decide. And that is the only property that matters — an act that cannot tell you you're wrong has not exposed you to anything.

The scoring is behavioural, not emotional. At the end of a week: **how many acts did I take that could have come back badly?** Zero is a week of hiding, whatever it produced. One is a working week. Three is a good one.

> **The question, every morning:** what is the smallest thing I can do today that puts a verdict outside my own control?

Identity follows the evidence of what you watch yourself do. You do not become the person who does this by deciding to be them. You become them by accumulating instances, and the first instance is available in the next ten minutes.
