# 7 · What did the last person who tried this find out?

**Before market research, before a business plan, before a single spreadsheet: find the corpse and read the autopsy.**

Almost every idea a person has has already been attempted by somebody better funded. That is not discouraging, it is a gift — they paid for the experiment and the results are public. Kippa spent $14 million discovering that half a million African small traders will use a free bookkeeping app and will not pay for one. That finding cost them everything and costs you an afternoon of reading.

The trick is knowing what to search. Three queries, in this order:

1. **The idea plus "shut down" / "layoffs" / "pivot".** Failures are written up more honestly than successes, usually by the trade press within weeks.
2. **The idea plus "raises" or "Series A", restricted to your own region.** If someone local is funded and running, you are not early — you are late, and you need a reason why you win anyway. Ramani.io was three miles away.
3. **Who currently gives this away free**, and who funds them. If a telco or a donor is doing it for customer acquisition, there is no price above zero.

Note what all three have in common: none of them asks whether the idea is good. **They ask what already happened.** Base rates over stories, applied to businesses rather than prizes.

The same method points forward, not just backwards. The reason the practice survives this test is that the autopsies come back *alive* — Basis at a $1.15bn valuation, Modus buying firms, a solo practice in Texas running 37 client engagements with no staff accountants. When you go looking for the corpse and find a category being funded instead, that is the strongest signal available.

> **The question:** who has already run my experiment, what did it cost them, and what did they learn that I can have for free?
