# 2 · Who pays, out of what money, instead of what?

**31 August 2026,** researching whether to build bookkeeping training for mama ntilie and small traders.

Kippa, in Nigeria, built exactly that. They raised **over $14 million**. They reached roughly **500,000 small businesses**. The payments arm shut with 40 layoffs, a co-founder left, and by 2025 the app was inaccessible — users could not retrieve their own sales records.

Half a million people used it. It still died.

That is the most expensive lesson available for free, and it has a precise shape: **adoption is not demand.** People will happily accept something useful without ever intending to pay for it, and their enthusiasm is real, and it is not a business.

The question has three parts and all three have to answer in one sentence each:

1. **Who writes the cheque?** A named role, not a category. "Small businesses" is not an answer; "the partner at a 4-person US accounting firm" is.
2. **Out of which budget line?** Money that already exists and is already allocated. New budget is ten times harder to win than reallocated budget.
3. **Instead of what?** What do they stop buying to buy you? If nothing, you are asking them to spend more, which is a different and much harder sale.

Run the practice through it: the partner at a small US firm writes the cheque, from a bookkeeping-outsourcing line that runs $400–800 a month per client, instead of the Manila provider currently doing it at $10–25 an hour. **Three sentences, all specific, no scale required.**

Run the training idea through it and the third sentence has no answer, the second has no budget, and the first is someone with a few thousand shillings of daily margin.

> **If you cannot name all three, you have a project, not a business** — however many people would use it for free, and however right the underlying insight is.
