# 8 · What am I not allowed to do?

**This is the question that pays only if asked before you start**, which is why almost nobody asks it. It has no upside in the moment. It produces no plan, no revenue, no artefact — just a list of walls you would otherwise have hit at speed in month nine.

Asked once, in one afternoon, it returned four things nobody in this vault had considered:

**The logbook needs a mentor.** NBAA requires three years under a supervising CPA or CPA-PP, with the logbook signed by mentor and employer. Self-employment may not count. The cash plan and the moat plan may be in conflict, and every note here was written assuming they weren't.

**US tax data offshore is a criminal statute.** IRC §7216 — up to a year's imprisonment for disclosing taxpayer information without specific written consent, and sending 1040 data outside the United States needs standalone consent language saying so. The fix is easy once you know: sell bookkeeping and management accounts, not tax preparation.

**AI on client data has to be named.** The 2026 standard says "AI tools we may use from time to time" does not meet the consent bar — engagement letters are expected to name the specific tools, with data-processing terms covering retention, training and deletion.

**Professional services must VAT-register regardless of turnover** in Tanzania, even though exported services are zero-rated.

None is fatal. Every one of them is cheap to handle at the start and expensive to discover late — and the third is not even a cost. **A supplier who arrives with §7216 already understood and a named-tools AI clause already written is instantly a different category from the ones who have never heard of either.** The compliance answer is the sales pitch.

> **The question:** what are the rules of this game that I would not know to look up — licensing, professional standards, data, tax, cross-border? Who already had to learn them the hard way, and what did it cost them?

**And the asymmetry worth staring at.** This vault contains a nine-axis rubric for judging a short story and, until this afternoon, contained nothing at all about whether its own income plan was legal, licensed or loggable.
