# UNKNOWN UNKNOWNS — WHAT COULD KILL THIS
### Four things nobody in this vault had thought to ask

Part of [[Operating Manual]]. [[Due Diligence — What the Market Says]] answered the questions we knew to ask. This note is the other kind: **rules that were always there, that nobody had gone looking for, and that decide whether the plan works at month nine.**

---

## 1 · The logbook may not accept self-employment — and this is the big one

> [!danger] This one can break the plan as written
> NBAA's requirement is three years of post-qualifying experience **under the supervision of a mentor** who is a CPA, CPA-PP, or holder of a practising certificate from a recognised body. The practical-experience logbook is signed **by the mentor and by the employer.**

Read plainly, that means freelancing alone may not count toward ACPA at all.

**Why this matters more than anything else in the folder.** Every plan here says: go independent, land clients, build the practice. Every plan here also says the credential is the moat ([[Ten Things I Was Blind To]] §7). If the logbook needs a supervising mentor and an employer signature, **those two moves are in direct conflict** — three years of self-employed practice could produce income and zero logged experience.

**Three ways it might resolve, in order of preference:**
1. **Employment plus a side practice.** A job at a firm with a CPA-PP mentor logs the years while the practice is built in the evenings on your own clients. Slower on cash, keeps the moat.
2. **A supervising mentor over your own work.** If NBAA accepts a mentor who is not your employer, the practice itself can be the experience. **This is the question to ask.**
3. **Years already logged.** If the MEMS profile shows the clock already running or complete, the conflict evaporates entirely.

**None of this is decidable from a website.** It needs one direct question to NBAA — the very call the vault decided was no longer necessary. It is necessary again, for a different reason.

## 2 · US tax data offshore is a criminal statute, not a preference

**IRC §7216** makes it a misdemeanour — up to a year's imprisonment and a fine — for a US tax return preparer to disclose taxpayer information without specific written consent. Sending 1040 information to a preparer **outside the United States** requires a standalone consent with mandatory language stating that the data goes overseas and that US privacy protections may not apply. §6713 adds civil penalties with no intent requirement.

**What it changes:** aim the offer at **bookkeeping, month-end close and management accounts, not tax return preparation.** Same buyer, same budget, none of the criminal exposure. When a firm does want tax work offshore, the consents are theirs to obtain — ask to see them before touching anything.

**This is a selling point, not just a risk.** A candidate who raises §7216 unprompted in the first call is instantly a different category of supplier from the ones who don't know it exists.

## 3 · Using AI on client data now needs naming in the engagement letter

The 2026 professional standard has moved. "AI tools we may use from time to time" **does not meet the consent standard** — the letter is expected to name the specific tools, and firms are being told to require data-processing agreements covering retention, model training, subprocessors and deletion for anything that touches client financials. And feeding tax return information into a third-party AI can itself be a §7216 disclosure.

**What it changes:** the AI-native practice has a **consent architecture**, not just a technology stack. Written into your own engagement letter — named tools, no-training terms, retention and deletion, where data sits — it stops being a liability and becomes the thing that makes you safe to hire. Most one-person offshore competitors will not have this.

## 4 · Professional services must VAT-register regardless of turnover

Tanzania's general VAT registration threshold is TZS 200M in twelve months — **but professional service providers, accounting firms included, are named as requiring mandatory registration regardless of turnover.**

The sting is smaller than it looks: **exports of services to non-residents consuming them outside Tanzania are zero-rated**, so you charge 0% and can still claim input VAT. But registration means filing obligations from day one. Separately: a TIN is mandatory and free (issued off your NIDA ID), and as a resident you are taxable on **worldwide income** at up to 30%, with credit for foreign tax paid.

**What it changes:** the practice needs a TIN, probably a registered sole proprietorship, and a VAT position taken deliberately at the start rather than discovered in year two. Budget for an hour with a Tanzanian tax practitioner before invoice one. You will be able to read their answer better than most clients can.

## What this note is really demonstrating

None of these four came from asking *how do I make money.* They came from asking **what am I not allowed to do**, which is a question that only pays when you ask it before you start. → [[Seventeen Questions/Q08 — What am I not allowed to do]]

**And note the shape of the miss.** The vault has a nine-axis rubric for a short story and had nothing at all on whether its own income plan is legal, licensed or loggable. That asymmetry is the whole diagnosis in one line.

## The five questions to put to NBAA, in one call
1. Does experience gained in **self-employment or my own practice** count toward the three years?
2. Can the mentor be someone other than my employer, and what is required of them?
3. Am I currently a registered Graduate Accountant, and from what date?
4. Are any years already recorded against my logbook?
5. For **ACPA-PP**, what changes — referees, practising certificate, fees?
