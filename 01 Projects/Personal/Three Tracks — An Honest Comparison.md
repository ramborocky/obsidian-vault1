# TRACK A · ACPA(T)

**What it is:** the NBAA professional grade. A **legal gate**, not a market — you don't have to beat competitors, you have to satisfy a board.

### The money

Accountant salaries in Tanzania run roughly **TSh 462,000 to 3,957,000/month**, junior roles clustering **411,000–1,624,000**. Dar pays materially more than Arusha. Qualified-and-registered sits in the upper half.

```
TSh 1,200,000/mo  =  $453
TSh 2,500,000/mo  =  $944
TSh 4,000,000/mo  =  $1,511
```

**Sources disagree with each other on this** — some conflate monthly and annual. Treat the range as indicative, not precise.

### What has to be true

1. You are already a **registered Graduate Accountant**, or can become one
2. You have **three years of logged practical experience**, or a route to them
3. **Three logbooks**, one per year
4. **Two ACPA/FCPA referees** willing to sign

### Next action, and its cost

**The phone call. `+255 22 2 211890` · `info@nbaa.go.tz`.** Twenty minutes, free.

It has to establish exactly two things: **are you a registered GA**, and **how many years are already logged.** Nothing else in this track can be costed until those are answered — I could not find the GA registration fee, the ACPA application fee or the annual subscription published anywhere, and the fee page is not publicly readable. *(Firm registration is published at TSh 100,000 + 400,000, but that is for firms, not individuals.)*

> **This is why the call is blocker #1 on [[Home]] and why it has been sitting since 22 July.** It is the difference between Track A being a six-month plan and a three-year one, and there is no way to find out except to ask.

### Failure mode

The three years can't be shortened by wanting it. If none are logged, **this track cannot produce income inside 2027** — but the credential is permanent once earned, which no other track here offers.

# TRACK B · REMOTE FREELANCE, PAID IN USD

**What it is:** bookkeeping and finance/business writing for clients abroad. **The whole advantage is arbitrage** — earn in dollars, spend in shillings. It is the only structural edge in your situation and it does not expire.

### The money

Open-market freelance writing runs **$15–40/hr**; finance and technical specialists command more; the January 2026 per-word average across 500 working freelancers was **$0.42**. **Generic content writing has fallen 18% since 2020 — specialist work has not.** An accountant who writes clean English is not a common combination and should not be priced as a generic writer.

```
10 hrs/wk @ $20/hr  =  $800/mo    =  TSh 2,117,600   → covers the household running cost
15 hrs/wk @ $25/hr  =  $1,500/mo  =  TSh 3,970,500   → covers running cost AND the 1.2M debt service
20 hrs/wk @ $30/hr  =  $2,400/mo  =  TSh 6,352,800   → covers everything, including school fees
```

**That middle line is the one that matters.** Fifteen hours a week at a specialist rate clears the entire monthly problem. No other track on this page does that in year one.

### What has to be true

1. Reliable internet and power for client hours *(the délestage question is real and I don't know your situation)*
2. A payment rail that works from Tanzania — this needs checking before anything else
3. Getting through the cold-start problem: no reviews, no history. **The first three clients are the hard part; after that it compounds**
4. Pricing as a specialist rather than competing with generic content mills, which is a race you'd lose

### Next action

Pick the **narrowest** offer you can defend — not "writer", but *bookkeeping and financial reporting for small businesses*, or *finance writing for accounting software companies*. Narrow is what lets a beginner charge a specialist rate.

### Failure mode

You spend six weeks pitching and get nothing, because the cold start is genuinely hard and global competition on price is brutal at the generic end. **Mitigated entirely by narrowness** — and even a failed attempt leaves you with samples, a rate card and a profile.

# TRACK C · THE WRITING

**What it is:** the Commonwealth entry, and the KDP anthology.

### The money, and it is not a business

**Prizes:** £5,000 for the overall winner plus £2,500 each for four regional winners = **£15,000 across 7,806 entries** = **£1.92 expected value per entry.** Free to enter, so the EV is positive and negligible.

**KDP:** Part 9 of [[KDP Business Plan]] models it — **$280 profit across year one**, cash breakeven in month 4, peak exposure $120. In shillings: **TSh ~741,000 for a year**, or about **TSh 62,000/month**. That is *less than one day* of your household running cost.

**First KDP money lands around August 2027** — a June 2027 sale paid 60 days later.

### But the value isn't the money, and this is not a consolation prize

A regional win buys a **credential**: an agent, publication (Nazir went to Granta), a route to a book. It is a lottery ticket where the payout is access, and **your ticket is free** because the craft work is happening anyway.

**And the 25 October submission is very nearly free right now.** Draft 3 exists, Draft 4's insertions are written, the apparatus is built. Abandoning it saves you almost nothing and forfeits the whole option — **rule 3g means there is no second attempt with Chantal, ever.**

### Failure mode

It doesn't sell and doesn't place. **That is the modal outcome, not the worst case**, and the honest expectation.

# THE INTERACTIONS — this is the part that decides it

**1 · Only B produces cash inside 90 days.** With ~1.2M/month of debt service, that isn't a preference, it's arithmetic. A track that pays in 2029 does not answer a bill that arrives in September.

**2 · B funds A and C.** Fifteen freelance hours a week covers the household and the lenders, which is precisely what buys the calm to do a three-year qualification and to write. Right now the writing is competing with survival, and survival wins every time — which is most of why the ACPA call has gone unmade for two weeks.

**3 · A has the highest ceiling and the longest lag, and the phone call is free.** There is no version of this where making that call is the wrong move. Twenty minutes, no money, and it converts the biggest unknown on this page into a fact.

**4 · C costs almost nothing to keep alive — but only at low intensity.** The Atelier is currently ~25 hours a week. **That is the real cost of Track C, and it is being paid in the hours Track B needs.** Finish the Atelier to 21 August, submit on 25 October, then drop to maintenance.

**5 · A and B are not rivals — they're the same skill.** Bookkeeping work for overseas clients *is* practical accounting experience. Whether NBAA will count it toward the logbook is a question for the phone call, and if the answer is yes, then B pays you while completing A.

# THE RECOMMENDATION

**B first and hardest, A immediately and free, C at reduced intensity.**

Make the NBAA call this week — it's twenty minutes, it costs nothing, and it resolves whether A is a six-month path or a three-year one. In parallel, spend the next 90 days building one narrow freelance offer and landing three clients, because that is the only thing on this page that pays a bill in 2026. Keep the Atelier to 21 August and submit Chantal on 25 October, because that work is already bought and paid for and there is no second attempt. Then let the writing go quiet until the money is not an emergency.

**The uncomfortable version:** you have built a genuinely serious apparatus — nine-axis rubric, six craft bases, an empirical campaign measured against real winners — and pointed it at the single least reliable income source available, while a qualification that pays has been stalled on one unmade phone call and two lenders bill you monthly. **The problem was never ability. It's allocation.**
